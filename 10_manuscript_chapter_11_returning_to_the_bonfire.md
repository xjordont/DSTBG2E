# 11. Returning to the Bonfire

The *bonfire* is a safe haven in a treacherous world. Two strangers wait beside it. The *Firekeeper*, blindfolded and silent, strengthens the characters in exchange for *souls*. Blacksmith Andre, toiling at his forge, sells equipment and fits upgrades.

## 11.1 The Bonfire

The *bonfire* is represented by the bonfire tile, which is placed at setup (see §6.4). The bonfire tile has no nodes, and no *encounter* is fought on it. A model on the bonfire tile stands anywhere on the tile. The *Firekeeper* (see §11.3) and Blacksmith Andre (see §11.4) wait at the bonfire, and the characters may visit them only while the party rests (see §11.2).

The characters begin the game on the bonfire tile (see §6.4). The party returns to it in the following cases.

- After an encounter is *cleared*, the party may return to the bonfire tile and rest instead of advancing to the next tile (see §7.5, Encounter Recovery).
- When an encounter is *failed*, the party must return to the bonfire tile and rest (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution). The death of a *hollow* character fails the encounter at once (see §7.2).
- When a boss is defeated, the characters return to the bonfire tile and rest (see §16.5).

When the party returns, the characters' models are placed on the bonfire tile. The characters do not pass back through the encounter tiles, and no encounter is fought on the way.

Returning to the bonfire tile and resting there are one act. The party returns only to rest, and the rest begins when the models are placed on the tile (see §11.2). The party cannot choose to return while the *spark* dial shows zero (see §7.4).

## 11.2 Resting

*Resting at the bonfire* is an action of the whole party: the characters rest together, and no character rests alone. The party may rest whenever the characters are on the *bonfire* tile and the *spark* dial shows one or more (see §7.4). When an *encounter* is *failed*, the party must rest; if the spark dial shows zero, the party cannot rest and the players lose the game (see §7.1).

Resting costs one spark. A rest that follows the defeat of a boss costs no spark (see §7.4). If a player versus player (PvP) encounter follows the boss, that free rest is taken after the PvP encounter (see §20.9). In campaign play, a rest costs no spark only when it follows the completion of a *section* (see §18.3).

The party rests as follows.

1. Turn the spark dial down by one, unless the rest costs no spark.
2. Revive each dead or *lost* character: stand the character's model upright on the bonfire tile (see §7.2).
3. Remove every *damage cube* and every *stamina cube* from each character's endurance bar (see §3.4).
4. Remove every *hollow cube* from each character's endurance bar (see §7.2).
5. Remove every *condition* token from each character (see §8.5).
6. Flip each character's *heroic action*, *luck*, and *estus flask* tokens to the ready side (see §3.5).
7. The characters may visit the *Firekeeper* (see §11.3) and Blacksmith Andre (see §11.4), in either order and any number of times.
8. *Reset* every encounter (see §11.5).

A rest restores every character in the same way, whether the character was living, dead, or lost when the rest began. After a rest no character is *hollow*, and every endurance bar is empty.

Resting neither places nor removes an *ember token*. An *embered* character stays embered through the rest, and a character who is not embered does not become embered by resting (see §7.3).

Resting does not restock the *shop*. Cards bought from the shop are not replaced, and unsold cards stay in the shop, until the shop is next restocked during the setup that follows a boss's defeat (see §6.3, Restocking After a Boss). In campaign play the shop is restocked during the setup that follows the completion of a section (see §18.6, Blacksmith Andre's Shop).

While the party rests, each character may rearrange their *inventory* and equipment slots and may trade cards with other characters, as at any time outside an encounter (see §3.2).

*Example.* The Knight, the Herald, and the Assassin clear the second encounter, a Level 2 encounter. The Herald died in it holding three souls, which lie on the second tile, and has been revived hollow. The Knight and the Assassin hold seven souls each, and the spark dial shows three. The party returns to the bonfire tile and rests. The spark dial is turned down to two. The Knight's four damage cubes and the Herald's two hollow cubes are removed, and the Knight's used estus flask token and the Assassin's used luck token are flipped to the ready side. No boss has been defeated, so the shop is not restocked. The Knight pays the Firekeeper two souls to raise strength from the base tier to stat tier one and holds five souls. The Assassin pays Andre two souls for a Rapier from the tier one shop and places it in the inventory, then sells a Morning Star for one soul and holds six souls. The Morning Star is placed face down at the bottom of the tier one treasure deck. The party then resets every encounter: the first and second encounter cards are turned face down and keep their black cubes, and the Herald's three souls stay on the second tile.

## 11.3 The Firekeeper

The *Firekeeper* raises a character's stats in exchange for that character's *souls*. A character may visit the Firekeeper only while the party rests at the *bonfire* (see §11.2).

Each purchase raises one of the character's four stats by one *stat tier* (see §3.3). Stat tiers are bought in sequence: a stat rises from the base tier to stat tier one, then to stat tier two, then to stat tier three, and then to stat tier four, and no tier is passed over. The character pays the cost of the stat tier bought, as the following table shows.

| *Stat tier* bought | Cost in *souls* |
|---|---|
| Stat tier one | 2 |
| Stat tier two | 4 |
| Stat tier three | 8 |
| Stat tier four | 10 |

A character may make any number of purchases during one rest, for one stat or for several, and pays for each purchase separately. When a stat rises, the character moves its *stat cube* as the stat progression track directs; the value of a stat at stat tier four and the way that tier is marked are stated there (see §3.3). Campaign play uses its own costs (see §18.6, Stat Tier Costs).

Each character pays with their own souls. Souls cannot be pooled or transferred between characters (see §7.6). Outside a rest, souls are spent only to call a *summon* at the *fog gate* (see §12.1).

*Example.* The Herald's strength stat cube is in the base space, and the Herald holds six souls. During a rest the Herald pays the Firekeeper two souls for stat tier one and then four souls for stat tier two. The Herald moves the strength stat cube two spaces to the right and holds no souls.

## 11.4 Blacksmith Andre

Blacksmith Andre keeps the *shop* and fits upgrades. A character may visit Andre only while the party rests at the *bonfire* (see §11.2). Andre trades *treasure cards* for *souls*, and each character pays and is paid in their own souls (see §7.6).

### Shop Tiers

The shop is two piles of face-up treasure cards: the tier one shop and the tier two shop (see §6.3). The tier one shop is open from the start of the game. The tier two shop opens when the *mini-boss* is defeated (see §6.6); in campaign play it opens when the *bonfire ascetic* is burnt (see §18.7).

The shop is restocked only after a boss is defeated, and its unsold cards are returned to their treasure decks before it is restocked (see §6.3, Restocking After a Boss). In campaign play the shop is restocked only when a *section* is completed (see §18.6, Blacksmith Andre's Shop). A card bought from the shop is not replaced until the shop is next restocked.

### Purchases

A character may buy any card in the shop. A card in the tier one shop costs two souls, and a card in the tier two shop costs four souls. The character returns the souls to the supply and places the card in their *inventory* (see §3.2). A character may buy any number of cards during one rest.

An *ember card* is a tier one shop card and costs two souls. A character who is not permitted to carry an ember card cannot buy one (see §4.4). *Boss treasure* and *legendary treasure* are not placed in the shop and cannot be bought (see §6.2).

### Sales

A character may sell to Andre any treasure card they hold, whether it is *equipped*, in the *back-up slot*, or in the inventory. *Starting equipment* cannot be sold. Andre pays one soul for a tier one or tier two treasure card, one soul for an *invader*'s treasure card or a Titanite Scale card, and six souls for a boss treasure card, and the character takes the souls from the supply. Legendary treasure and *transposed treasure* are *tier two treasure* cards (see §6.2).

A sold card goes to one of the following places.

- A sold tier one or tier two treasure card that came from a treasure deck or from the shop is placed face down at the bottom of the treasure deck of its tier (see §6.2).
- A sold boss treasure card is returned to the game box, and so is any other sold card that came from no treasure deck: an *invader*'s treasure card or a Titanite Scale card.
- A sold ember card is returned face up to the tier one shop.

When a *weapon* with *Titanite* attached is sold, the Titanite is sold with it, and every other *upgrade card* attached to a sold card returns to the character's inventory (see §4.2, Titanite). The sell value of the weapon is paid once; attached Titanite adds nothing to it. The weapon and the Titanite are separated, and each goes to the place stated for a sold card of its kind.

### Prices

| Card | Purchase cost in *souls* | Sell value in *souls* |
|---|---|---|
| Tier one card | 2 | 1 |
| Tier two card | 4 | 1 |
| *Invader*'s treasure card or Titanite Scale card | - | 1 |
| *Boss treasure* card | - | 6 |

An ember card is priced as a tier one card. Boss treasure cannot be purchased. Campaign play uses its own prices (see §18.6, Shop Price Table).

### Upgrades

Andre attaches an upgrade card bearing the weapon upgrade icon {{icon:ast-0044-weapon-upgrade}} or the armour upgrade icon {{icon:ast-0046-armour-upgrade}} from a character's inventory to a weapon or *armour* card equipped by that character. Andre also removes an attached upgrade card other than Titanite and returns it to the character's inventory. Andre charges no souls for either service, and a character may have any number of upgrade cards attached or removed during one rest.

The *upgrade slots* that limit the number of attached cards, the *stat requirements* of upgrade cards, the permanence of Titanite, and the attaching of *upgrade spells* are stated with the upgrade rules (see §4.2).

## 11.5 Resuming Exploration

The last step of a rest *resets* every *encounter* (see §11.2). The characters then set out again from the *bonfire* tile; their visits to the *Firekeeper* and Blacksmith Andre are over until the party next rests.

The encounters are reset as follows.

1. Turn every face-up *encounter card* face down.
2. Leave the black cube on each encounter card that carries one (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution).
3. Remove every enemy model from the encounter tiles.
4. Leave every *terrain* token and model on its node, and restore each kind of terrain as its rules direct (see §5.5).
5. Leave all *dropped souls* on their tiles (see §7.6).

A *cleared* encounter keeps its black cube through the reset, and a *failed* encounter has none (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution). A cleared *Level 4 encounter* is the exception to the reset: it is not reset and is not fought again (see §6.7, Level 4 Encounters). A failed boss encounter is reset as the boss rules direct (see §15.2). An *invader* that has appeared does not return when the encounters are reset (see §12.2).

After the reset the characters leave the bonfire tile and enter the first encounter tile of the route, where that tile's encounter begins (see §7.5, Encounter Start). The characters then advance one tile at a time towards the boss tile. If the *fog gate* token is on the bonfire tile, the party may instead pass through it to the boss encounter without entering any encounter tile (see §15.1).

When the characters enter a tile whose encounter card carries a black cube, the party may skip the encounter or fight it again. Skipping costs each character stamina; the cost and its limit are stated with the encounter rules (see §7.5, Encounter Start). An encounter fought again awards the *reduced reward* and no treasure (see §7.6). An encounter whose card carries no black cube, because it was failed or has not yet been cleared, cannot be skipped and must be fought.

*Example.* The Knight, the Herald, and the Assassin have rested after clearing the first and second encounters, and they set out again. The first and second encounter cards carry black cubes, and the party skips both encounters. The characters enter the third tile, each placing two stamina cubes on their endurance bar, and the third encounter begins. The Herald's three dropped souls stay on the second tile, because the Herald has not survived a cleared encounter there since dropping them.
