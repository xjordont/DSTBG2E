# 6. Setup

The fire is lit, and the dark waits beyond it. Before the first step is taken, the party readies its characters, builds the treasure decks, stocks Blacksmith Andre's shop, kindles the bonfire, and lays the tiles that lead to the bosses it has chosen to face. Campaign play alters parts of this setup (see §18.1).

## 6.1 Character Setup

Each player chooses one character (see §2.1). The number of characters in the party sets the scaling of enemy health and attack values (see §8.3) and the starting number of *sparks* (see §6.4, §7.4).

1. Place each character's dashboard (see §3.1) in front of its player.
2. Equip each character with its *starting equipment*, the equipment cards that bear the character's class icon, in the slots the cards show (see §3.2, §4.1).
3. Place one *stat cube* on the base tier space of each of the character's four stat progression tracks (see §3.3).
4. Place the character's *luck*, *heroic action*, and *estus flask* tokens on the dashboard, ready side up (see §3.5).
5. Place the *ember* tokens, *damage cubes*, *stamina cubes*, *hollow cubes*, soul tokens, *condition* tokens, *trap tokens*, and *wound tokens* in a supply within reach of every player (see §2.8).
6. Place the *aggro token* on the character with the highest *threat* value (see §7.5, Encounter Start).
7. If the party consists of one character, place six *souls* on that character's dashboard (see §7.6).

## 6.2 Treasure Deck Setup

Two treasure decks are built at setup: the *tier one treasure* deck and the *tier two treasure* deck. Every *treasure card* belongs to one of six types (see §2.5), and its type and its stat requirements (see §4.1) decide which deck, if any, it enters.

### Treasure Card Types

**Starting equipment.** The three or four equipment cards a character begins with, marked with that character's class icon. Starting equipment is equipped during character setup (see §6.1). It is not shuffled into either treasure deck.

**Common treasure.** A treasure card that is not starting equipment, class-specific, transposed, legendary, or boss treasure. A common treasure card with any stat requirement of 25 or more is tier two. A common treasure card whose stat requirements are all below 25 is tier one.

**Class-specific treasure.** A treasure card marked with a small white class emblem in its top-right icon. The emblem has no rules effect. Class-specific treasure is sorted into tier one or tier two by its stat requirements exactly as common treasure is.

**Transposed treasure.** A class-specific treasure card marked with a blue light in the top-right of the card. All transposed treasure is tier two.

**Legendary treasure.** A treasure card marked with an orange light beneath a chest in the top-right of the card. Legendary treasure cannot be purchased, and no legendary treasure card enters a treasure deck at the start of the game. The first time the tier two shop is stocked, five legendary treasure cards drawn at random are shuffled into the tier two treasure deck and the rest are returned to the game box (see §6.3, Restocking After a Boss).

**Boss treasure.** A treasure card marked with a boss's silhouette over an orange light in the top-right of the card. Boss treasure is not shuffled into a treasure deck. The boss treasure cards of each selected boss are placed with that boss during encounter setup (see §6.5), and one of them is awarded at random when the boss is defeated (see §16.5). The boss treasure cards of bosses that are not selected are returned to the game box.

*Ember cards* are not shuffled into either treasure deck. One ember card per character is placed in the tier one shop at the start of the game (see §6.3), and the rest are returned to the game box (see §4.4).

### Tier One Treasure Deck

The tier one treasure deck holds every common treasure card and every class-specific treasure card whose stat requirements are all below 25. It holds no starting equipment, ember cards, transposed treasure, legendary treasure, or boss treasure.

### Tier Two Treasure Deck

The tier two treasure deck holds every common treasure card and every class-specific treasure card with at least one stat requirement of 25 or more, and every transposed treasure card. It holds no starting equipment, ember cards, or boss treasure. Legendary treasure enters the deck only after the tier two shop is first stocked (see §6.3, Restocking After a Boss).

### Building the Treasure Decks

1. Set aside every boss treasure card, every legendary treasure card, every ember card, and every starting equipment card that is not equipped.
2. Return the starting equipment cards that are not equipped to the game box.
3. Sort the remaining treasure cards into two piles: cards whose stat requirements are all below 25, and cards with at least one stat requirement of 25 or more.
4. Add every transposed treasure card to the second pile.
5. Shuffle the first pile to form the tier one treasure deck.
6. Shuffle the second pile to form the tier two treasure deck.

### Expansion Treasure

Treasure cards from every expansion in the party's collection are included and are sorted into the tier one and tier two treasure decks by the rules of this section.

## 6.3 Shop Setup

Blacksmith Andre's *shop* holds two piles of face-up *treasure cards*: the tier one shop and the tier two shop. The tier one shop is stocked at the start of the game. The tier two shop is first stocked when the *mini-boss* is defeated (see §6.6), and both shops are restocked after each boss is defeated (see §6.6, §6.7); in campaign play the shops are restocked when a *section* is completed (see §18.6). Purchase and sale prices are set out in Andre's rules (see §11.4).

### Stocking Categories

Each shop is stocked by category: three weapons, three armour cards, two shields, two upgrades, and two spells. For stocking, a treasure card belongs to the first of the following categories that describes it (see §4.1).

- An upgrade is a card with the weapon-upgrade icon {{icon:ast-0044-weapon-upgrade}} or the armour-upgrade icon {{icon:ast-0046-armour-upgrade}}, including rings and *titanite*.
- A spell is a card whose art shows a scroll.
- A shield is a weapon card that shows a *block* or *resist* value and has no *attack option*.
- An armour card is a card with the *armour* icon {{icon:ast-0047-armour}}.
- A weapon is any other card that is equipped in a weapon slot, including *catalysts* and throwable cards.

### Stocking at the Start of the Game

1. Draw one card from the tier one treasure deck.
2. If the card's category is not yet full, place it face up in the tier one shop; otherwise set it aside.
3. Repeat steps 1 and 2 until every category is full or the deck is exhausted. A category still unfilled when the deck is exhausted stays short.
4. Shuffle the set-aside cards back into the tier one treasure deck.
5. Add one *ember card* per character to the tier one shop, and return the remaining ember cards to the game box.

### Restocking After a Boss

The shops are restocked when a boss is defeated (see §6.6, §6.7) and at no other time in the standard game. Ember cards remaining in the tier one shop stay there. No ember card is added to a shop after the start of the game.

1. Place every card remaining in the tier one shop, other than ember cards, on the bottom of the tier one treasure deck.
2. Stock the tier one shop from the tier one treasure deck by steps 1 to 4 of Stocking at the Start of the Game, without adding ember cards.
3. If the tier two shop has been stocked before, place every card remaining in it on the bottom of the tier two treasure deck.
4. Stock the tier two shop from the tier two treasure deck in the same way.
    4.1. If a *legendary treasure* card is drawn, shuffle it back into the tier two treasure deck and draw again.
5. If the tier two shop has been stocked for the first time, draw five legendary treasure cards at random without revealing them, shuffle them into the tier two treasure deck, and return the remaining legendary treasure cards to the game box.

## 6.4 Bonfire Setup

1. Place the *bonfire* tile on the table, leaving room for the encounter tiles and the boss tile (see §6.5).
2. Set the *spark* dial to six minus the number of characters (see §7.4).
3. Place every character's model on the bonfire tile.
4. Place the *tier one treasure* and *tier two treasure* decks face down beside the bonfire tile.
5. Place the tier one *shop* face up beside the bonfire tile, leaving room beside it for the tier two shop.

*Example.* The Knight, the Herald, and the Assassin set out together. The party sets the spark dial to three, six minus three characters, and every enemy they meet is scaled for three characters.

## 6.5 Encounter Setup

Encounter setup lays the route from the *bonfire* to the first boss: the bosses are chosen, the tiles are laid, and each encounter tile receives its encounter card and its treasure.

### Boss Selection

1. The party chooses one *mini-boss* and one *main-boss* from the bosses in its collection (see §2.3).
2. The party may add a *mega-boss* as the *final boss* (see §6.7).
3. Take each selected boss's model, *data card*, *behaviour cards*, *health dial*, and *boss treasure* cards, and return the boss treasure cards of the other bosses to the game box.
4. Keep the main-boss's components, and the mega-boss's if one is in play, aside until they are needed (see §6.6, §6.7).

Random boss selection is an optional rule. It requires no expansion. The party decides during setup whether random boss selection is in play.

When random boss selection is in play, each boss is chosen at random in step 1: shuffle the data cards of every boss of that rank in the party's collection and reveal one. A mega-boss added in step 2 is chosen in the same way.

### Encounter Tiles

1. Draw four of the six encounter tiles at random.
2. Place each encounter tile with either side face up, as the party chooses.
3. Arrange the bonfire tile, the four encounter tiles, and the mini-boss tile, which is one of the two *boss tiles* (see §15.3), in a grid of two rows of three tiles, with the bonfire tile and the mini-boss tile in opposite corners.
4. Turn each encounter tile so that exactly two of its doorways open on to adjacent tiles, forming a single route from the bonfire tile through all four encounter tiles to the mini-boss tile.
5. Place the *fog gate* token on the doorway between the fourth encounter tile and the mini-boss tile (see §15.1).
6. Place the mini-boss's model, data card, health dial, and boss treasure cards on the mini-boss tile.
7. Build the mini-boss's *behaviour deck* (see §15.5) and place it face down on the mini-boss tile.

### Encounter Cards

The *encounter levels* of the four encounters on the route to a boss are printed on that boss's data card. Encounter levels increase from the tile adjacent to the bonfire tile towards the boss tile.

1. Build the encounter decks: shuffle separately the Level 1 {{icon:ast-0007-encounter-level-1}}, Level 2 {{icon:ast-0011-encounter-level-2}}, and Level 3 {{icon:ast-0015-encounter-level-3}} *encounter cards* of the set that supplies the mini-boss (see §6.5, Expansion Encounters).
2. Deal one encounter card face down to each encounter tile, of the level the mini-boss's data card gives for that tile's position on the route.
3. Deal one *tier one treasure* card per character face down to each encounter tile. These cards are the encounter's treasure reward and are awarded when the encounter is first *cleared* (see §7.7).
4. If *mimics* are in play, deal one *ambush card* face down beside each encounter tile (see §13).
5. If *invaders* are in play, place the *invader tokens* on the encounter cards (see §12.2).

### Expansion Encounters

Each encounter deck is built only from the encounter cards of the set that supplies the next boss. Encounter cards of other sets are not used on that leg of the exploration.

Mixed encounter decks are an optional rule. They require at least one expansion that supplies encounter cards. The party decides during setup whether mixed encounter decks are in play. When they are in play, each encounter deck is built from the encounter cards of its level from every set in the party's collection.

*Example.* The party selects the Gargoyle as its mini-boss and Great Grey Wolf Sif as its main-boss. The decks for the four encounters before the Gargoyle are built from the core set's encounter cards. After the Gargoyle is defeated, the decks for the four encounters before Sif are built from the Darkroot expansion's encounter cards.

### Optional Rules

The party decides during setup whether each of the following optional rules is in play. Each is stated in full in its own section.

- Random boss selection replaces the party's choice of bosses with a random draw (see §6.5, Boss Selection).
- Mixed encounter decks draw encounter cards from every set in the party's collection (see §6.5, Expansion Encounters).
- A mega-boss adds a Level 4 encounter and a final boss after the main-boss (see §6.7).
- Mimics require the Explorers expansion (see §13).
- *Crystal lizards* require the Iron Keep expansion (see §14).
- *Phantoms*, both *summons* and invaders, require the Phantoms expansion (see §12).
- Player versus player (PvP) encounters follow the defeat of each boss (see §20.1).

## 6.6 Setup After the Mini-Boss

When the *mini-boss* is defeated (see §16.5), the route is rebuilt for the second leg of the exploration, which leads to the *main-boss*. If player versus player (PvP) encounters are in play, a PvP encounter follows the mini-boss's defeat (see §20.1).

1. Return any *dropped souls* remaining on the tiles to the characters who dropped them (see §7.6).
2. Return the four *encounter cards* to the game box.
3. Clear every remaining token from the encounter tiles.
4. If *mimics* are in play, gather the *ambush cards* and shuffle them (see §13).
5. Flip the four encounter tiles to their other sides and shuffle them.
6. Arrange the *bonfire* tile, the four encounter tiles, and the main-boss tile in a straight line, with the bonfire tile and the main-boss tile at opposite ends. The main-boss tile is the two *boss tiles* joined as one (see §15.3).
7. Turn each encounter tile so that a single route runs from the bonfire tile through all four encounter tiles to the main-boss tile.
8. Move the *fog gate* token to the doorway between the fourth encounter tile and the main-boss tile (see §15.1).
9. Place the main-boss's model, *data card*, *health dial*, and *boss treasure* cards on the main-boss tile.
10. Build the main-boss's *behaviour deck* (see §15.5) and place it face down on the main-boss tile.
11. Build the encounter decks from the set that supplies the main-boss (see §6.5, Expansion Encounters).
12. Deal one encounter card face down to each encounter tile, of the level the main-boss's data card gives for that tile's position on the route (see §6.5, Encounter Cards).
13. Deal one *tier two treasure* card per character face down to each encounter tile. These cards are the encounter's treasure reward (see §7.7).
14. If mimics are in play, deal one ambush card face down beside each encounter tile (see §13).
15. If *invaders* are in play, place the *invader tokens* on the encounter cards (see §12.2).
16. Restock the tier one *shop* and stock the tier two shop (see §6.3, Restocking After a Boss).
17. Add one *spark* to the spark dial (see §7.4).
18. Place every character's model on the bonfire tile. The characters rest at the bonfire without spending a spark (see §11.2) and may visit the *Firekeeper* (see §11.3) and Blacksmith Andre (see §11.4).
19. Exploration resumes from the encounter tile adjacent to the bonfire tile (see §11.5).

## 6.7 Setup After the Main-Boss

A *mega-boss* is an optional rule. It requires a mega-boss expansion. The party decides during setup whether a mega-boss is in play (see §6.5, Boss Selection). If no mega-boss is in play, the *main-boss* is the *final boss*, and its defeat wins the game (see §7.1).

When a mega-boss is in play, the defeat of the main-boss (see §16.5) is followed by one *Level 4 encounter* and then by the mega-boss encounter. Both are fought on the *mega-boss game board* supplied with the mega-boss (see §2.9). If player versus player (PvP) encounters are in play, a PvP encounter follows the main-boss's defeat (see §20.1).

1. Return any *dropped souls* remaining on the tiles to the characters who dropped them (see §7.6).
2. Return the *encounter cards*, the encounter tiles, and the *boss tiles* to the game box.
3. Place the mega-boss game board adjacent to the *bonfire* tile, with its Level 4 encounter side face up, the side that shows enemy spawn nodes {{icon:ast-0006-primary-spawn-node}} and terrain nodes {{icon:ast-0014-primary-terrain-node}}, and with one of its doorways aligned with a doorway of the bonfire tile.
4. Shuffle the mega-boss's four Level 4 encounter cards and deal one face down to the game board.
5. Shuffle the mega-boss's *boss treasure* cards and deal one face down to the game board. This card is the Level 4 encounter's treasure reward (see §6.7, Level 4 Encounters). No tier two treasure cards are dealt to the game board.
6. Place the mega-boss's model, *data card*, *health dial*, *behaviour cards*, and remaining boss treasure cards beside the game board.
7. Restock the tier one and tier two *shops* (see §6.3, Restocking After a Boss).
8. Add one *spark* to the spark dial (see §7.4).
9. Place every character's model on the bonfire tile. The characters rest at the bonfire without spending a spark (see §11.2) and may visit the *Firekeeper* (see §11.3) and Blacksmith Andre (see §11.4).
10. The characters enter the Level 4 encounter from the bonfire tile (see §7.5, Encounter Start).

### Level 4 Encounters

**Level 4 encounter.** The single encounter fought on the Level 4 encounter side of a mega-boss game board, after the main-boss is defeated and before the mega-boss encounter.

A Level 4 encounter is set up and fought as any other encounter (see §7.5), with the following differences. All four basic nodes along the wall beside the doorway are *entry nodes*. The encounter card places more enemies, *terrain*, and *trap tokens* than a Level 1 to 3 card, and the card is followed as printed.

The encounter's soul reward is the Level 4 reward (see §7.6). Its treasure reward is the single mega-boss treasure card dealt to the game board, which is awarded when the encounter is *cleared* in the same way as a chest's treasure card (see §7.7).

If a revealed Level 4 encounter card requires a set the party does not own, return it to the game box, deal another of the mega-boss's Level 4 encounter cards face down to the game board, and reveal that card instead.

A Level 4 encounter that is cleared is not fought again and does not reset when the characters rest at the bonfire. A Level 4 encounter that is *failed* resets as any other encounter does (see §11.2).

In campaign play, a Level 4 encounter listed in a section is fought on a mega-boss game board placed in the section's tile sequence (see §18.1).

### The Mega-Boss Encounter

Clearing the Level 4 encounter grants no free rest. Before passing through the fog gate the party may rest at the bonfire at the cost of one spark (see §11.2); the cleared Level 4 encounter does not reset.

1. Remove every token, model, and card from the game board.
2. Flip the game board to its mega-boss side and align one of its doorways with a doorway of the bonfire tile.
3. Place the *fog gate* token on that doorway (see §15.1).
4. Set up the mega-boss encounter as a boss encounter (see §15.2), with the mega-boss's model on the mega-boss *spawn node* and its data card, health dial, and *behaviour deck* (see §15.5) beside the game board.
5. Apply the mega-boss's special rules (see §17).

The mega-boss encounter begins when the characters pass through the fog gate (see §15.1). Death and hollowing are resolved as in any encounter (see §7.2), and a failed mega-boss encounter is resolved as any other failed boss encounter (see §15.2). Defeating the mega-boss wins the game (see §7.1). In campaign play, a mega-boss that is not the campaign's final boss is fought under the same rules, and the campaign continues when it is defeated (see §18.1).
