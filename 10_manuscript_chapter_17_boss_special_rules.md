# 17. Boss Special Rules

No two bosses fight alike. One hunts as a pair, one brings the pillars down upon its prey, one burns the ground from the air, and one cannot be wounded while its servants stand. This chapter sets out, boss by boss, what each adds to the boss encounter.

## 17.1 Rules Common to Several Bosses

Every *boss* is played from its *data card* and the general boss rules (see §15 and §16). The special ability printed on the data card applies throughout the boss's *encounter* (see §15.4). This chapter states the further rules that particular bosses require. A boss with no entry in this chapter has no rules beyond its data card.

Where a rule in this chapter and a data card disagree, the rule in this chapter governs. Each entry states, where the boss has them, its components, its setup, its special ability, its *heat-up*, and its unique icons.

### Zero Dodge Difficulty

Some *behaviour cards* show a *dodge difficulty* of 0. A character targeted by an attack on such a card may pay one stamina to *dodge* it without rolling, and the dodge succeeds even if the character has no dodge dice. The character then moves to an *adjacent node* as after any successful dodge (see §9.5). A character whose equipment states that the character cannot dodge may not dodge the attack.

The character may instead *block* or *resist* the attack and pay no stamina (see §9.4).

### Several Boss Models in One Encounter

Some bosses are fought with more than one boss model. The rules under this heading apply to a boss whose models stand on separate *nodes*: Ornstein & Smough (see §17.2) and the Four Kings (see §17.10, The Four Kings). The two models of the Executioner's Chariot share one node and act as a single model (see §17.9).

The models are one boss, fought in one encounter with one *behaviour deck*. Each boss model has its own *health dial*, its own four *boss arcs* (see §15.6), and its own *condition* tokens. A character who shares a node with a boss model is placed in the arcs of that model.

A node holds at most one boss model (see §5.3). When a boss model moves onto a node that holds another boss model, the model already on the node is *pushed* to a *valid node* chosen by the players and keeps its facing (see §8.4). Characters on either node are placed in the arcs of the boss model that now shares their node, as when a boss moves onto a character's node (see §16.2). No character's movement or attack pushes a boss model (see §8.4).

A character's attack is made against one boss model. The attack is resolved against the block and resist values of that model, its damage lowers that model's health dial, and any condition it inflicts is placed on that model. Damage inflicted on any of the boss's models is damage inflicted on the boss when the aggro token moves (see §16.4).

On each boss *turn* one behaviour card is drawn, as for any boss (see §16.1), and every boss model in play acts on it, one model after another, in the order that the boss's entry states. Each model resolves its icons from its own node: its movement, its facing, the *range* of its attack, its attack arcs, and the direction of any push are taken from that model. The target of each icon is determined afresh for each model.

The *weak arcs* {{icon:ast-0041-weak-boss-arc}} of each boss model are read from the current behaviour card (see §15.6). A character gains the weak-arc bonus when attacking a model from an arc of that model that the card shows as weak.

A boss model whose health dial is reduced to zero is defeated. It is removed from the tile at once, with its condition tokens, and resolves no further icon. The encounter does not end, and Boss Death is not resolved, until the boss's entry states that the boss is defeated (see §16.5).

### Twin Gargoyles

**Twin Gargoyles.** The boss encounter set by a campaign *section* that lists the Gargoyle as fought twice (see §19).

The Twin Gargoyles are one *mini-boss* encounter in two parts, fought on the mini-boss tile against one Gargoyle and then, at once, against a second. The one Gargoyle model, data card, health dial, and set of behaviour cards serve for both parts. In the standard game the Gargoyle is selected once and is fought once.

When the first Gargoyle's health dial reaches zero, the encounter does not end, and Boss Death is not resolved (see §16.5).

1. Remove the Gargoyle model from the tile and place it on the mini-boss *spawn node*, facing as at the start of a boss encounter (see §15.2).
2. Place any character on that node on the model's base, in an arc chosen by that character's player.
3. Set the health dial to the Gargoyle's starting health.
4. Gather all of the Gargoyle's behaviour cards and build a new behaviour deck (see §15.5).
5. Continue the encounter in the same turn order (see §16.1).

The second Gargoyle takes its first turn after the turn of the character who defeated the first. Nothing else changes between the two parts. Each character stays on their node and keeps their *damage cubes*, *stamina cubes*, condition tokens, and used tokens. A character who died stays dead, a *summon* in play stays in play, and the *aggro token* stays with its holder.

The characters do not rest at the *bonfire* between the two parts, and no *souls*, no treasure, and no *spark* are awarded. The second Gargoyle heats up at its own heat-up point {{icon:ast-0030-heat-up-point}}, as any boss does (see §15.5). When its health dial reaches zero, the boss is defeated and Boss Death is resolved once (see §16.5).

The soul reward, one Gargoyle *boss treasure* card, and one spark are awarded, every character becomes *embered*, and the characters rest at the bonfire, at that time and not before. If player versus player (PvP) encounters are in play, the *PvP encounter* follows the second Gargoyle's defeat (see §20.1).

If the encounter is *failed* while either Gargoyle is in play, the whole encounter is failed and is resolved as any failed boss encounter (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution; §15.2). A first Gargoyle defeated before the failure earns no reward. When the characters next enter the encounter, both Gargoyles are fought again: the first is placed with its health dial at its starting health and a newly built behaviour deck, and the second follows as this heading describes.

*Example.* The Knight and the Herald fight the Twin Gargoyles. The Knight defeats the first Gargoyle while holding four damage cubes. The Gargoyle model returns to the mini-boss spawn node, its health dial is set to its starting health, and a new behaviour deck is built. The Knight still holds four damage cubes, and no reward is given. The second Gargoyle then kills the Herald, who is *hollow*, and the encounter is failed. After the characters rest at the bonfire and return, they face the first Gargoyle again at its starting health.

## 17.2 Core Set

The Gargoyle, the Titanite Demon, the Boreal Outrider Knight, and the Winged Knight each have the rank of *mini-boss*, and the Dancer of the Boreal Valley is a *main-boss*. These five bosses have no rules beyond their *data cards*. A campaign *section* that lists the Gargoyle as fought twice sets the Twin Gargoyles *encounter* (see §17.1, Twin Gargoyles).

### Dragon Slayer Ornstein and Executioner Smough

Ornstein & Smough is one main-boss fought with two boss models, Dragon Slayer Ornstein and Executioner Smough (see §2.3). Both models are in play from the start of the encounter, and the boss is defeated only when both have been defeated. The rules for several boss models in one encounter apply (see §17.1, Several Boss Models in One Encounter).

**Components.** Each model has its own data card and its own *health dial*. The boss has 15 *behaviour cards*: five paired standard cards, each of which shows one behaviour for Ornstein and one for Smough; five *heat-up cards* for Ornstein; and five heat-up cards for Smough. The two models share one set of *boss treasure* cards.

**Setup.** The joined *boss tiles* of a main-boss encounter show two boss *spawn nodes*: the main-boss spawn node and the mini-boss spawn node (see §15.3). Ornstein's model is placed on the main-boss spawn node and Smough's model on the mini-boss spawn node, each facing as a boss faces at the start of its encounter (see §15.2). Each health dial is set to the starting health on that model's data card. The five paired cards are shuffled to form the *behaviour deck*, and each model's heat-up cards are shuffled and placed face down as a pile of their own.

**Special ability.** The special abilities printed on the two data cards state one rule. On each boss *turn* one behaviour card is drawn, and both models act on it: Ornstein resolves the behaviour shown for Ornstein, and Smough then resolves the behaviour shown for Smough. The range, the *dodge difficulty*, the icons, and the arc diagrams printed with a behaviour apply only to the model that resolves it. A model's *weak arcs* are those shown with its own behaviour on the current behaviour card (see §15.6).

**Heat-up.** Neither data card shows a *heat-up point*. Ornstein & Smough heats up when the first of its two models is defeated. The defeated model is removed from the tile at once (see §17.1, Several Boss Models in One Encounter). The *heat-up* is carried out at the start of the boss's next turn, before a card is drawn (see §15.5, Heat-Up), by these steps in place of the general ones.

1. Return the behaviour deck and its discard pile to the game box.
2. Shuffle the surviving model's heat-up cards and place them face down as the new behaviour deck.
3. Raise the surviving model's health dial by 10 if the survivor is Ornstein, or by 15 if the survivor is Smough, to no more than the starting health on its data card.

From the heat-up the encounter continues against the surviving model alone (see §16.1). The defeated model's heat-up cards take no part in the encounter, and the boss does not heat up again. When the surviving model's health dial is reduced to zero, the boss is defeated, and Boss Death is resolved once (see §16.5). If the second model is defeated before the heat-up is carried out, the boss is defeated at once.

*Example.* The Knight's attack reduces Ornstein's health dial to zero while Smough's health dial shows 22 of a starting health of 30. Ornstein's model is removed from the tile. At the start of the next boss turn the paired behaviour cards are returned to the game box, Smough's five heat-up cards are shuffled to form the new behaviour deck, and Smough's health dial is raised to 30, not to 37. Smough then draws the top card of the new deck.

## 17.3 Asylum Demon and Stray Demon

The Asylum Demon is a *mini-boss* and the Stray Demon is a *mega-boss*; both are supplied by the Asylum Demon expansion (see §2.3). The rules of this section apply to both. The Stray Demon is fought after a *Level 4 encounter*, as any mega-boss is (see §6.7).

### Components

- 4x Pillar tokens
- 1x Mega-boss game board, whose mega-boss side shows four pillar nodes

**Pillar node.** One of the four *terrain nodes* printed on the mega-boss side of the Asylum Demon expansion's *mega-boss game board*, on each of which a *pillar token* is placed.

### Setup

The Asylum Demon is fought as any mini-boss, except that its *encounter* takes place on the mega-boss side of the expansion's mega-boss game board in place of the mini-boss tile (see §15.3). During setup that game board is placed where the mini-boss tile is placed, with its mega-boss side face up and the *fog gate* token on its doorway (see §6.5), and the Asylum Demon's model is placed on the mega-boss *spawn node* when the encounter is set up. The Stray Demon's encounter is set up as any mega-boss encounter (see §6.7).

At the start of every *boss* encounter fought on the mega-boss side of that game board, one pillar token is placed on each of its four pillar nodes, before the boss takes its first *turn*. A *node* that holds a pillar token is closed to every model (see §5.5, Pillars).

### Special Ability

The Asylum Demon and the Stray Demon share the special ability Pillars of Woe. When either demon makes a physical *area attack*, that is, a physical attack that shows the area icon {{icon:ast-0038-area-attack-icon}}, every pillar within the area of the attack is destroyed before the attack is resolved. A pillar is within the area of the attack if its node is within the attack's *range* of the demon and lies in one of the attack arcs marked on the *behaviour card* (see §16.3).

1. Find every pillar token within the area of the attack.
2. For each such pillar token, deal one damage to each character within range 1 of the pillar's node (see §5.4), and give each of those characters a *stagger* {{icon:ast-0066-stagger}} token.
3. Remove each such pillar token from the game board.
4. Resolve the area attack, including its *dodges* and its pushes (see §16.3).

The damage of a destroyed pillar is not an attack, and it cannot be *blocked*, *resisted*, or dodged. One attack may destroy several pillars. A character within range 1 of several destroyed pillars suffers one damage for each of them and holds one stagger token (see §8.5, Stagger). A magical attack, or an attack that does not show the area icon, destroys no pillar.

Once a pillar is destroyed, its node is used as a *basic node* for the rest of the encounter (see §5.5, Pillars). When the characters rest at the *bonfire*, every destroyed pillar token is returned to its pillar node.

*Example.* The Stray Demon resolves a behaviour card with a physical area attack whose attack arcs are its front arc and its left arc. Two pillar tokens stand within range in the front arc. The Knight stands on a node adjacent to both pillars, and the Herald on a node adjacent to one of them. The Knight suffers two damage and gains one stagger token; the Herald suffers one damage and gains one stagger token. Both pillar tokens are removed. The area attack is then resolved, and the Knight, who is within range in the front arc, blocks it with one subtracted from the block total for stagger.

### Unique Icons

The *Wind Up* icon appears on behaviour cards of the Asylum Demon and of the Stray Demon. A Wind Up icon printed on any other behaviour card is resolved in the same way.

When a Wind Up icon is resolved, each character may spend one stamina to *move* to an *adjacent node*. Each player decides for their own character. The movement counts as a *Run* (see §10.2), and every rule that applies to a Run applies to it. The characters complete the movement before the next icon on the behaviour card is resolved (see §16.1).

## 17.4 Darkroot

Great Grey Wolf Sif and Artorias each have the rank of *main-boss*. Several *behaviour cards* of each show a *dodge difficulty* of 0 (see §17.1, Zero Dodge Difficulty).

### Great Grey Wolf Sif

**Special ability.** Sif's special ability is Mournful Howl. The cool-down card Limping Strike, which shows Sif weakened and close to death, is neither a standard behaviour card nor a *heat-up card*. It is set aside when the *behaviour deck* is built. When Sif's health is reduced to three or less, the behaviour deck and its discard pile are returned to the game box at once, and Limping Strike alone forms the behaviour deck: Sif draws and resolves it on each of its *turns* until the *encounter* ends (see §15.5, Deck Renewal). Sif has no other rules beyond its *data card*.

### Artorias

**Heat-up.** Artorias does not heat up as other bosses do (see §15.5). When Artorias's health is reduced to or below its *heat-up* point {{icon:ast-0030-heat-up-point}}:

1. Gather the *behaviour deck* and its discard pile.
2. Remove two of the gathered cards at random and return them to the game box.
3. Add all three of Artorias's *heat-up cards* to the gathered cards.
4. Shuffle the cards to form the new behaviour deck.

## 17.5 Iron Keep

The Smelter Demon and Sir Alonne each have the rank of *main-boss*. Sir Alonne has no rules beyond its *data card*.

### Smelter Demon

**Heat-up.** The Smelter Demon does not heat up as other bosses do (see §15.5). When the Smelter Demon's health is reduced to or below its *heat-up* point {{icon:ast-0030-heat-up-point}}:

1. Return the *behaviour deck* and its discard pile to the game box.
2. Shuffle the Smelter Demon's *heat-up cards* and draw five.
3. Place the five drawn cards face down as the new behaviour deck.
4. Return the undrawn heat-up cards to the game box.

## 17.6 Explorers

### Old Dragonslayer

The Old Dragonslayer is a *mini-boss*.

**Components.** The Old Dragonslayer uses the Dragon Slayer Ornstein model of the core set, with its own *data card*, *health dial*, eight *behaviour cards*, and two *boss treasure* cards.

**Special ability.** The Old Dragonslayer's special ability is Corrupted Dragonslayer. The first three times the Old Dragonslayer loses four or more health to a single attack, one of its *heat-up cards*, chosen at random, is placed face down on top of its *behaviour deck*. The deck is not shuffled, and the card is the next one the Old Dragonslayer draws (see §16.1). The Old Dragonslayer's data card shows no heat-up point, and the Old Dragonslayer does not heat up in any other way.

### Pursuer

The Pursuer is a *main-boss*. Several of its behaviour cards show a *dodge difficulty* of 0 (see §17.1, Zero Dodge Difficulty).

**Special ability.** The Pursuer's special ability is Ceaseless Pursuit. On each of its *turns* the Pursuer draws and resolves two behaviour cards, one after the other. If the behaviour deck is empty when either card is due, the deck is renewed before that card is drawn (see §16.1). The *weak arcs* {{icon:ast-0041-weak-boss-arc}} that apply to character attacks are those of the second card, the card most recently resolved (see §15.6).

**Heat-up.** When the Pursuer's health is reduced to or below its *heat-up* point {{icon:ast-0030-heat-up-point}}, one random heat-up card is shuffled into its behaviour deck, as for any *boss* (see §15.5). From that moment until the *encounter* ends, the *attack value* of every attack on the Pursuer's behaviour cards is increased by one.

### Hungry Mimic and Voracious Mimic

The Hungry *Mimic* and the Voracious Mimic are fought as mini-boss encounters when a *chest* proves to be a mimic. Their rules, including their lack of arcs, their heat-up, and their treasure, are stated with the mimic rules (see §13).

## 17.7 Tomb of Giants

The Black Knight is a *mini-boss* and Gravelord Nito is a *main-boss*. Both are played with the replacement *data cards* and *behaviour cards* listed in the card amendments (see Appendix B). Neither has rules beyond its data card.

## 17.8 Black Dragon Kalameet

Black Dragon Kalameet is a *mega-boss*. The *Level 4 encounter* that precedes it and the placing of its *mega-boss game board* are stated with the setup rules (see §6.7). The campaign *section* that ends with Kalameet is set out with the campaigns (see §19.1).

### Components

- 1x Black Dragon Kalameet model
- 1x Data card
- 1x Health dial
- 13x Behaviour cards: 10 standard cards, 2 signature cards (Mark of Calamity and Hellfire Blast), and 1 heat-up card (Hellfire Barrage)
- 8x Strafe cards
- 4x Calamity tokens
- 2x Mega-boss treasure cards
- 4x Level 4 encounter cards
- 1x Mega-boss game board

**Signature card.** A *behaviour card* that is included in every *behaviour deck* built for its boss. Kalameet's two signature cards are Mark of Calamity and Hellfire Blast.

### Setup

The mega-boss *encounter* is set up as any mega-boss encounter (see §6.7), with the following differences. Kalameet's behaviour deck is built by this procedure in place of the general one (see §15.5).

1. Place Kalameet's model on the mega-boss *spawn node* with its front arc facing the centre of the game board.
2. Set aside the *heat-up card*, Hellfire Barrage.
3. Shuffle the 10 standard behaviour cards and draw four.
4. Return the six undrawn standard cards to the game box.
5. Add the two *signature cards* to the four drawn cards, and shuffle the six cards to form the behaviour deck.
6. Shuffle the eight *strafe cards* and place them face down beside the behaviour deck as the strafe deck.

### Special Ability

Kalameet's special ability is Calamity & Ruin. Every behaviour deck built for Kalameet includes both signature cards, Mark of Calamity and Hellfire Blast.

### Heat-Up

Kalameet has one heat-up card, Hellfire Barrage. When Kalameet's health is reduced to or below its *heat-up* point {{icon:ast-0030-heat-up-point}}, Hellfire Barrage is shuffled into the behaviour deck as the general rule directs (see §15.5).

### Unique Icons

Kalameet's behaviour cards show two icons of their own: the calamity icon and the strafe icon.

An attack that shows the calamity icon inflicts the *calamity* *condition*. Each character *hit* by the attack gains one calamity token (see §8.5, Calamity).

The strafe icon marks a strafe attack. A strafe attack is a magical attack {{icon:ast-0042-deal-magical-damage}} that targets the characters on particular *nodes*, in place of the characters within an arc and a *range*.

**Strafe card.** A card that shows the nodes a strafe attack targets and the landing node on which Kalameet's model is placed afterwards.

When a strafe icon is resolved:

1. If the strafe deck is empty, turn its discard pile face down without shuffling to form the new strafe deck.
2. Remove Kalameet's model from the game board. Every other model stays on its node.
3. Reveal the top card of the strafe deck.
4. Resolve the attack against every character on a node that the strafe card shows as targeted.
5. Discard the strafe card.
6. Place Kalameet's model on the landing node shown on the strafe card, facing as it faced before it was removed.
7. *Push* each character on the landing node to an *adjacent node* chosen by that character's player (see §8.4).

The strafe attack uses the *attack value* and the *dodge difficulty* of the behaviour card that shows the strafe icon. Each targeted character *resists* or *dodges* the attack separately (see §9.4; §9.5).

*Example.* Kalameet resolves a behaviour card that shows the strafe icon. Kalameet's model is removed from the game board, and the top strafe card is revealed. The Knight stands on a targeted node and resists the attack. The Herald stands on the landing node, which the card does not show as targeted, and is not attacked. The strafe card is discarded, Kalameet's model is placed on the landing node, and the Herald is pushed to an adjacent node chosen by the Herald's player.

## 17.9 Executioner's Chariot

The Executioner's Chariot is a *mega-boss* fought in two parts. Before its *heat-up* it follows a fixed pattern and cannot be damaged; after its heat-up it is fought as any *boss*. The *Level 4 encounter* that precedes it and the placing of its *mega-boss game board* are stated with the setup rules (see §6.7). The campaign that includes the Chariot is The Undead Colosseum (see §19.9).

### Components

- 2x Executioner's Chariot models: the skeletal horse and the chariot
- 1x Data card, double-sided, with the Executioner's Chariot on the front and the Skeletal Horse on the heat-up side
- 1x Health dial
- 4x Track behaviour cards, named Death Race and numbered 1 to 4
- 9x Standard behaviour cards
- 3x Heat-up cards
- 1x Mega-boss setup card
- 2x Mega-boss treasure cards
- 2x Black Hollow Mage and 4x Falchion Skeleton models, with their two enemy data cards
- 4x Level 4 encounter cards and 6x encounter cards of Levels 1 to 3
- 1x Mega-boss game board

**Mega-boss setup card.** The card that shows the enemy *spawn nodes* on which the Black Hollow Mages and the Falchion Skeletons are placed at the start of the mega-boss *encounter*.

The two models are one boss. Until the heat-up they share one *node*, *move* together, and count as one boss model for the *node model limit* (see §5.3).

The centre of the Chariot's game board is a wall, not a node. Nodes on opposite sides of the centre are not adjacent, and *range* is counted around it (see §5.1; §5.4). No model may move, *dodge*, or be *pushed* into the centre.

### Setup

The mega-boss encounter is set up as any mega-boss encounter (see §6.7), with the following differences. Two decks are built in place of the general *behaviour deck* (see §15.5).

1. Place the *data card* with its front side face up.
2. Place both Chariot models together on the mega-boss spawn node.
3. Place the two Black Hollow Mages and the four Falchion Skeletons on the enemy spawn nodes {{icon:ast-0006-primary-spawn-node}} shown on the *mega-boss setup card*.
4. Stack the four *track behaviour cards* face down in numerical order, with the card numbered 1 on top and the card numbered 4 at the bottom, to form the track deck. Do not shuffle it.
5. Shuffle the nine standard behaviour cards and draw four.
6. Shuffle the three *heat-up cards* and draw one.
7. Shuffle the five drawn cards together and set them aside face down as the heat-up deck.
8. Return the undrawn behaviour cards to the game box.

The Black Hollow Mages and the Falchion Skeletons are enemies, not bosses. They use their own data cards and are scaled for the number of characters (see §8.3). The *health dial* is not used until the heat-up.

### Special Ability

The front side of the data card shows the special ability Crash and shows no starting health {{icon:ast-0029-starting-health}}, no *block* value, and no *resist* value. Until the Chariot heats up, it cannot be attacked or damaged, and no attack or effect of a character affects it. Crash is the Chariot's heat-up (see §17.9, Heat-Up).

Until the heat-up, each boss *turn* of the encounter (see §16.1) is taken by every enemy model on the game board. The Chariot, each Black Hollow Mage, and each Falchion Skeleton take one turn each in descending *threat* order, with ties settled as in any encounter (see §7.5, Encounter Turn Order). One character then takes a turn, and the enemy models take their turns again. The Black Hollow Mages and the Falchion Skeletons act as their data cards direct (see §9.1).

**Track behaviour card.** One of the four behaviour cards, named Death Race and numbered 1 to 4, that direct the Chariot's turns in fixed order until it heats up. Each card shows the node to which the Chariot moves and the nodes that its attack targets.

On each of its turns before the heat-up, the Chariot resolves one track behaviour card:

1. If the track deck is empty, turn its discard pile face down without shuffling to form the new track deck.
2. Reveal the top card of the track deck.
3. Move both Chariot models together to the node shown on the card.
4. Turn both models so that their front arc faces directly away from the node they left.
5. Resolve the card's physical attack against every character on a node that the card shows as targeted.
6. Push each character *hit* by the attack to an *adjacent node* that the card does not show as targeted. The players choose the node.
7. Push each enemy model on a targeted node to an adjacent node that the card does not show as targeted. The players choose the node.
8. Discard the card.

The attack of a track behaviour card targets nodes in place of arcs and range, and the targeted nodes include the node the Chariot left and the node it entered. Each targeted character blocks or dodges the attack separately, using the *attack value* and the *dodge difficulty* on the card (see §9.4; §9.5). Enemy models pushed in step 7 suffer no damage. A model with no untargeted adjacent node to enter stays on its node (see §5.2).

The track deck is not shuffled at any time. The four cards are resolved in numerical order, and the card numbered 1 follows the card numbered 4.

### Heat-Up

The Chariot's heat-up is Chariot Crash. The Chariot does not heat up at a heat-up point {{icon:ast-0030-heat-up-point}} (see §15.5). It heats up at once when no Black Hollow Mage and no Falchion Skeleton remains on the game board.

1. Remove the chariot model from the game board. The skeletal horse stays on its node and keeps its facing.
2. Flip the data card to its heat-up side, the Skeletal Horse.
3. Set the health dial to the starting health shown on the heat-up side.
4. Return the track behaviour cards to the game box.
5. Place the heat-up deck built at setup face down beside the game board as the behaviour deck.

From the heat-up, the encounter continues as any boss encounter against the skeletal horse (see §16.1). The skeletal horse and the characters alternate turns, the skeletal horse draws from its behaviour deck, and attacks against it are resolved against the block and resist values on the heat-up side. The skeletal horse does not heat up again. The boss is defeated when the health dial reaches zero (see §16.5).

The heat-up side shows the special ability Crush. When a character's block or resist roll reduces the damage of an attack on one of the skeletal horse's behaviour cards to zero, that character suffers one damage. Crush does not apply to an attack that the character dodges.

### Unique Icons

The Raise Skeleton icon appears on the Black Hollow Mage's data card. It is resolved in the same way in every encounter that contains a Black Hollow Mage.

When a Black Hollow Mage resolves the Raise Skeleton icon, the players count the enemy models on the tile whose names include the word "Skeleton". If fewer are on the tile than at the start of the encounter, the players choose one such enemy killed during the encounter and place its model, with no *wound tokens*, on the enemy spawn node nearest to that Black Hollow Mage. If two or more enemy spawn nodes are equally near, the players choose among them. If the count is not lower than at the start of the encounter, the icon has no effect.

*Example.* The Knight kills the fourth Falchion Skeleton while one Black Hollow Mage remains, and the Chariot does not heat up. On its turn the Black Hollow Mage resolves the Raise Skeleton icon. No Falchion Skeleton is on the game board and four were there at the start, so the players place one Falchion Skeleton, with no wound tokens, on the enemy spawn node nearest to the Black Hollow Mage. The Warrior kills the Black Hollow Mage, and the Knight kills the returned Falchion Skeleton. No Black Hollow Mage and no Falchion Skeleton remains, and the Chariot heats up at once.

## 17.10 Bosses of Other Expansions

Each *mega-boss* of this section is fought on the *mega-boss game board* supplied with it, after a *Level 4 encounter* (see §6.7). Where an entry gives a procedure for building the *behaviour deck*, that procedure takes the place of the general one (see §15.5, Deck Construction).

### The Four Kings

The Four Kings is a mega-boss fought with four *boss* models, the kings numbered one to four, who enter the *encounter* one at a time. The rules for several boss models in one encounter apply (see §17.1, Several Boss Models in One Encounter).

**Components.**

- 4x Four Kings models
- 4x *Health dials*, numbered one to four
- 4x Identifier tokens, numbered one to four
- 1x *Data card*
- 20x *Behaviour cards*: 8 standard cards, 4 second king cards, 4 third king cards, and 4 fourth king cards
- 2x Mega-boss treasure cards
- 4x Level 4 encounter cards
- 1x Mega-boss game board

Each identifier token is placed on the base of one model and marks it as the king whose health dial bears the same number.

**Setup.** The mega-boss encounter is set up as any mega-boss encounter (see §6.7), with the following differences.

1. Place the model of king one on the mega-boss *spawn node*, and set health dial one to the starting health on the data card. The other three models are kept beside the game board.
2. Shuffle the eight standard behaviour cards and draw four as the behaviour deck.
3. Return the four undrawn standard cards to the game box.
4. Shuffle the four second king cards, the four third king cards, and the four fourth king cards as three separate face-down piles beside the game board.

**Special ability.** The Four Kings' special ability is Royal Summons. The data card shows no *threat* and no *heat-up point* {{icon:ast-0030-heat-up-point}}. On each boss *turn* one behaviour card is drawn, and every king in play resolves the whole card, in numerical order. A king whose health dial is reduced to zero is removed from the game board (see §17.1, Several Boss Models in One Encounter). The boss is defeated when all four kings have been defeated (see §16.5).

The first three times the behaviour deck is empty at the start of a boss turn, a royal summons is carried out in place of the deck renewal (see §15.5, Deck Renewal).

1. Place the model of the next king in numerical order on the mega-boss spawn node, facing as a boss faces at the start of its encounter (see §15.2), and set that king's health dial to the starting health on the data card.
2. *Push* every other model on that *node* to an *adjacent node* chosen by the players, as after a *leap* (see §16.2, Leap).
3. Turn the discard pile face down, remove one of its cards at random without looking at it, and return that card to the game box.
4. Add two cards, taken at random and unseen, from the pile of the king placed in step 1.
5. Shuffle these cards to form the new behaviour deck.

The boss turn then continues: the top card of the new deck is drawn, and every king in play resolves it. A royal summons is carried out whether or not the kings placed before it have been defeated. Once king four has been placed, an empty behaviour deck is renewed as for any boss.

If no king is in play at the start of a boss turn and no royal summons is due, the top card of the behaviour deck is drawn and discarded without effect, each living character removes one *damage cube*, and the boss turn ends.

**Heat-up.** The Four Kings does not heat up. The royal summons takes the place of the *heat-up*.

*Example.* The Knight and the Herald fight king one, whose behaviour deck holds four cards. At the start of the fifth boss turn the deck is empty. King two is placed on the mega-boss spawn node, and the Herald, who stands there, is pushed to an adjacent node. The four cards of the discard pile are turned face down, one is removed at random, two second king cards are added, and the five cards are shuffled. The top card is drawn, king one resolves it, and king two then resolves it.

### Gaping Dragon

The Gaping Dragon is a mega-boss.

**Components.**

- 1x Gaping Dragon model
- 1x Data card
- 1x Health dial
- 14x Behaviour cards: 7 standard cards, 2 signature cards (both named Stomach Slam), 4 heat-up cards, and 1 Crawling Charge card
- 4x Corrosion tokens
- 2x Mega-boss treasure cards
- 4x Level 4 encounter cards
- 1x Mega-boss game board

**Setup.** The mega-boss encounter is set up as any mega-boss encounter (see §6.7), with the following differences.

1. Place the Crawling Charge card face up beside the data card. It is never part of the behaviour deck.
2. Shuffle the seven standard behaviour cards and draw three.
3. Shuffle the four *heat-up cards* and draw one.
4. Add the two *signature cards* to the four drawn cards, and shuffle the six cards to form the behaviour deck.
5. Return the four undrawn standard cards to the game box, and place the three undrawn heat-up cards face down beside the game board.

**Special ability.** The Gaping Dragon's special ability is Devouring Maw. Every behaviour deck built for the Gaping Dragon includes both signature cards and one heat-up card taken at random.

**Heat-up.** When the Gaping Dragon's health is reduced to or below its heat-up point, it heats up as the general rule directs: one of its three remaining heat-up cards, taken at random, joins the behaviour deck (see §15.5, Heat-Up).

**Unique icons.** The Gaping Dragon's behaviour cards show two icons of their own: the corrosion icon and the crawl icon.

An attack that shows the corrosion icon inflicts the *corrosion* *condition*. Each character *hit* by the attack gains one corrosion token (see §8.5, Corrosion).

When the crawl icon is resolved, the Gaping Dragon at once resolves every icon on the Crawling Charge card, from left to right, with the *range* and the *dodge difficulty* printed on that card. Its turn then ends: no further icon of the drawn behaviour card is resolved, and the drawn card is discarded. The Crawling Charge card stays beside the data card. The drawn card, not the Crawling Charge card, remains the Gaping Dragon's current behaviour card and shows its *weak arcs* (see §15.5, Card Anatomy).

### Guardian Dragon

The Guardian Dragon is a mega-boss.

**Components.**

- 1x Guardian Dragon model
- 1x Data card
- 1x Health dial
- 11x Behaviour cards: 9 standard cards and 2 heat-up cards, both named Cage Grasp Inferno
- 4x Fiery Breath cards
- 2x Mega-boss treasure cards
- 4x Level 4 encounter cards
- 1x Mega-boss game board, whose mega-boss side shows four cage nodes

**Cage node.** One of the four nodes at the corners of the mega-boss side of the Guardian Dragon's game board. No model may *move*, *dodge*, or be pushed into a cage node. The Guardian Dragon's model is placed on a cage node only when it resolves an Inferno icon.

**Fiery Breath card.** A card that shows the cage node on which the Guardian Dragon's model is placed, the nodes that an Inferno attack targets, and the *attack value* and dodge difficulty of that attack.

**Setup.** The mega-boss encounter is set up as any mega-boss encounter (see §6.7), with the following differences.

1. Shuffle the nine standard behaviour cards and draw five as the behaviour deck.
2. Return the four undrawn standard cards to the game box.
3. Set aside the two heat-up cards.
4. Shuffle the four *Fiery Breath cards* and place them face down beside the behaviour deck as the Fiery Breath deck.

**Special ability.** The Guardian Dragon's special ability is Caged Beast. When the Guardian Dragon heats up, both of its heat-up cards join the behaviour deck.

**Heat-up.** When the Guardian Dragon's health is reduced to or below its heat-up point, it heats up as the general rule directs, except that both Cage Grasp Inferno cards are added to the gathered cards in place of one card taken at random (see §15.5, Heat-Up).

**Unique icons.** The Cage Grasp Inferno cards show two icons of their own: the Inferno icon and the Bonus Behaviour icon.

The Inferno icon marks an Inferno attack. An Inferno attack is a magical attack {{icon:ast-0042-deal-magical-damage}} that targets the characters on particular nodes, in place of the characters within an arc and a range. When an Inferno icon is resolved:

1. If the Fiery Breath deck is empty, turn its discard pile face down without shuffling to form the new Fiery Breath deck.
2. Remove the Guardian Dragon's model from the game board. Every other model stays on its node.
3. Reveal the top card of the Fiery Breath deck.
4. Place the Guardian Dragon's model on the *cage node* shown on the card, turned to face the centre node of the game board.
5. Resolve the attack against every character on a node that the card shows as targeted.
6. Discard the Fiery Breath card.

The Inferno attack uses the attack value and the dodge difficulty printed on the Fiery Breath card. Each targeted character *resists* or dodges the attack separately (see §9.4; §9.5). The Guardian Dragon stays on the cage node until one of its behaviour cards moves it.

When the Bonus Behaviour icon is resolved, the Guardian Dragon at once draws the next card of its behaviour deck and resolves it in full, in the same turn. If the behaviour deck is empty, it is renewed before the card is drawn (see §15.5, Deck Renewal). The second card is the Guardian Dragon's current behaviour card from the moment it is drawn, and the two cards are discarded in the order in which they were resolved.

*Example.* The Guardian Dragon draws Cage Grasp Inferno. Its model is removed from the game board, and the top Fiery Breath card is revealed. The model is placed on the cage node that the card shows, turned to face the centre node. The Knight and the Herald stand on targeted nodes: the Knight resists the attack, and the Herald dodges it and moves to an adjacent node. The Fiery Breath card is discarded. The Bonus Behaviour icon is then resolved, and the Guardian Dragon draws and resolves the next card of its behaviour deck in the same turn.

### The Last Giant

The Last Giant is a mega-boss.

**Components.**

- 1x Last Giant model
- 1x Data card
- 1x Health dial
- 16x Behaviour cards: 5 standard cards, 5 Arm cards, 5 heat-up cards, and 1 Falling Slam card
- 2x Mega-boss treasure cards
- 4x Level 4 encounter cards
- 1x Mega-boss game board

**Setup.** The mega-boss encounter is set up as any mega-boss encounter (see §6.7), with the following differences.

1. Shuffle the five standard behaviour cards and draw three.
2. Shuffle the five Arm cards and draw three.
3. Shuffle the six drawn cards together to form the behaviour deck.
4. Return the undrawn standard cards and the undrawn Arm cards to the game box.
5. Set aside the Falling Slam card, and shuffle the five heat-up cards face down beside the game board.

**Special ability.** The Last Giant's special ability is Armed and Dangerous. When the Last Giant heats up, the Falling Slam card joins its behaviour deck, and heat-up cards take the place of its Arm cards.

**Heat-up.** The Last Giant does not heat up as other bosses do (see §15.5, Heat-Up). When its health is reduced to or below its heat-up point, the heat-up is carried out at the start of its next turn by these steps in place of the general ones.

1. Gather the behaviour deck and its discard pile.
2. Remove the three Arm cards from the gathered cards and return them to the game box.
3. Add the Falling Slam card and three heat-up cards taken at random.
4. Shuffle the seven cards to form the new behaviour deck.

**Unique icons.** The *Wind Up* icon appears on behaviour cards of the Last Giant. It is resolved as it is for the Asylum Demon (see §17.3, Unique Icons).

### Manus, Father of the Abyss

Manus, Father of the Abyss is a mega-boss. Its behaviour deck is built by the general procedure (see §15.5, Deck Construction).

**Components.**

- 1x Manus, Father of the Abyss model
- 1x Data card
- 1x Health dial
- 14x Behaviour cards: 9 standard cards and 5 heat-up cards
- 2x Mega-boss treasure cards
- 4x Level 4 encounter cards
- 1x Mega-boss game board

**Special ability.** Manus's special ability is Overextended. At the end of each of Manus's turns, each character may spend one stamina to move to an adjacent node. Each player decides for their own character. The movement counts as a *Run* (see §10.2), and every rule that applies to a Run applies to it. The characters complete the movement before the next character's turn begins (see §16.1).

**Heat-up.** Manus does not heat up as other bosses do (see §15.5, Heat-Up). When Manus's health is reduced to or below its heat-up point, the heat-up is carried out at the start of its next turn by these steps in place of the general ones. The behaviour deck is not shuffled.

1. Take two of Manus's heat-up cards at random, without looking at them.
2. Place one of the two cards face down on top of the behaviour deck.
3. Place the other card beneath the discard pile, turned as the cards of that pile are turned.

The first card is the card that Manus draws in that turn. The second card is the first card drawn after the behaviour deck is next renewed (see §15.5, Deck Renewal). If the discard pile is empty, the second card starts it.

**Unique icons.** Many of Manus's behaviour cards show the *shaft* icon beside an attack. An attack that shows the shaft icon does not target a character on Manus's own node (see §10.3, Shaft). A character on Manus's node is not targeted by such an attack even when the character is placed in one of its attack arcs.

*Example.* The Warrior's attack reduces Manus's health below its heat-up point. At the start of Manus's next turn two heat-up cards are taken at random: one is placed on top of the behaviour deck, and the other beneath the discard pile. Manus draws the first heat-up card at once. Two turns later the behaviour deck is empty: the discard pile is turned face down, and Manus draws the second heat-up card.

### Old Iron King

The Old Iron King is a mega-boss that stands in lava and is placed only on three nodes of its game board.

**Components.**

- 1x Old Iron King model
- 1x Data card
- 1x Health dial
- 12x Behaviour cards: 6 standard cards, 3 signature cards (each named Fire Beam), and 3 heat-up cards
- 6x Beam cards
- 2x Mega-boss treasure cards
- 4x Level 4 encounter cards
- 1x Mega-boss game board, whose mega-boss side shows three Iron King nodes

**Iron King node.** One of the three nodes, printed on the mega-boss side of the Old Iron King's game board, on which the Old Iron King's model is placed. The Old Iron King never stands on any other node.

**Beam card.** A card that shows the Iron King node on which the Old Iron King's model is placed and the nodes that a Beam attack targets.

The area behind the Iron King nodes is lava. It holds no nodes and is treated as a wall (see §5.1). No character on the Old Iron King's node may be placed in its back arc or move to it (see §15.6, Characters and Arcs).

**Setup.** The mega-boss encounter is set up as any mega-boss encounter (see §6.7), with the following differences.

1. Place the Old Iron King's model on the *Iron King node* opposite the doorway.
2. Shuffle the six standard behaviour cards and draw three.
3. Add the three signature cards to the three drawn cards, and shuffle the six cards to form the behaviour deck.
4. Return the three undrawn standard cards to the game box, and shuffle the three heat-up cards face down beside the game board.
5. Shuffle the six *Beam cards* and place them face down beside the behaviour deck as the Beam deck.

**Special ability.** The Old Iron King's special ability is Old Iron Rage. From its heat-up until the encounter ends, the attack value and the dodge difficulty of every Beam attack are each increased by one.

**Heat-up.** When the Old Iron King's health is reduced to or below its heat-up point, it heats up as the general rule directs (see §15.5, Heat-Up), and Old Iron Rage takes effect when the heat-up is carried out.

**Unique icons.** The Fire Beam cards show the Beam icon, which marks a Beam attack. A Beam attack is a magical attack that targets the characters on particular nodes, in place of the characters within an arc and a range. When a Beam icon is resolved:

1. If the Beam deck is empty, turn its discard pile face down without shuffling to form the new Beam deck.
2. Reveal the top card of the Beam deck.
3. Place the Old Iron King's model on the Iron King node shown on the card, turned to face the centre of the game board.
4. Push every other model on that node to an adjacent node chosen by the players, as after a leap (see §16.2, Leap).
5. Resolve the attack against every character on a node that the card shows as targeted.
6. Discard the Beam card.

The Beam attack uses the attack value and the dodge difficulty of the behaviour card that shows the Beam icon. Each targeted character resists or dodges the attack separately (see §9.4; §9.5). A character pushed in step 4 onto a targeted node is attacked in step 5.

*Example.* After its heat-up the Old Iron King resolves a Fire Beam card that shows attack value 5 and dodge difficulty 1. The top Beam card is revealed, and the Old Iron King's model is placed on the Iron King node that the card shows. The Assassin, who stands on that node, is pushed to an adjacent node, which the Beam card shows as targeted. The attack is resolved against the Assassin with attack value 6 and dodge difficulty 2.

### Vordt of the Boreal Valley

Vordt of the Boreal Valley is a mega-boss that draws from two behaviour decks and heats up twice.

**Components.**

- 1x Vordt of the Boreal Valley model
- 1x Data card
- 1x Health dial
- 17x Behaviour cards: 6 standard movement cards, 2 heat-up movement cards, 6 standard attack cards, 2 heat-up attack cards, and 1 Frostbreath card
- 2x Mega-boss treasure cards
- 4x Level 4 encounter cards
- 1x Mega-boss game board

**Setup.** The mega-boss encounter is set up as any mega-boss encounter (see §6.7), with the following differences. Two decks are built in place of the general behaviour deck.

1. Shuffle the six standard movement cards and draw four as the movement deck.
2. Shuffle the six standard attack cards and draw three as the attack deck.
3. Place the attack deck face down to the right of the movement deck. The two decks and their discard piles are kept apart.
4. Return the undrawn standard cards to the game box.
5. Shuffle the two heat-up movement cards and the two heat-up attack cards as two separate face-down piles.
6. Place the Frostbreath card face down to the left of the movement deck.

**Special ability.** Vordt's special ability is Icy Blows. When a character *blocks* or resists one of Vordt's attacks and prevents four or more damage, that character gains a *frostbite* token (see §8.5, Frostbite). The damage prevented is the attack value or the character's *defence total*, whichever is lower. A character who dodges the attack gains no token.

On each of its turns Vordt draws and resolves two behaviour cards: first the top card of the movement deck, then the top card of the attack deck. Each card is discarded to the discard pile of its own deck. When either deck is empty as its card is due, that deck's discard pile is turned face down without shuffling to renew it (see §15.5, Deck Renewal). The attack card, the card most recently resolved, is Vordt's current behaviour card and shows its weak arcs (see §15.6).

**Heat-up.** Vordt's data card shows two heat-up points, and Vordt heats up once at each. Each heat-up is carried out at the start of Vordt's next turn (see §15.5, Heat-Up), in place of the general steps. At the higher heat-up point the attack deck and its discard pile are gathered, one heat-up attack card taken at random is added, and the cards are shuffled to form the new attack deck; the movement deck is not disturbed. At the lower heat-up point the movement deck and its discard pile are gathered, one heat-up movement card taken at random is added, and the cards are shuffled to form the new movement deck; the attack deck is not disturbed.

In each turn in which Vordt carries out a heat-up, Vordt resolves the Frostbreath card once, after the heat-up and before it draws its movement card. The Frostbreath card is then turned face down again beside the movement deck and joins neither deck.

*Example.* One of Vordt's attacks has attack value 6. The Knight blocks it with a defence total of 4 and suffers two damage. The Knight has prevented four damage and gains a frostbite token. The Herald blocks the same attack with a defence total of 3, suffers three damage, and gains no token.

### Crossbreed Priscilla

Crossbreed Priscilla is a *main-boss* of the Painted World of Ariamis expansion. It is played from its data card and the general boss rules (see §15 and §16). Apply the special rules supplied with that expansion. Its data card and behaviour cards are the replacement cards listed in the card amendments (see Appendix B).

### Heavy Knight
