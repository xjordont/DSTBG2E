# 17. Boss Special Rules

## 17.1 Rules Common to Several Bosses

Every boss is played from its *data card* and its *behaviour cards* (see §15.4). This chapter gives only what those cards do not say. A boss not named here has no rule of its own.

### Setup

The bosses in this table are set up as it shows. A behaviour deck given here takes the place of the usual one (see §15.5). "4 of 10" means four cards drawn at random from the ten; the cards not drawn go back to the box. Every deck and pile is shuffled unless the table says otherwise. A set shown as Own is the expansion that bears the boss's name.

| Boss | Set | Behaviour deck | Also set out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ornstein and Smough | Core | The 5 paired cards | Each model's 5 *heat-up* cards, as a pile of its own |
| Great Grey Wolf Sif | Darkroot | As the data card | Limping Strike, set aside |
| Asylum Demon, Stray Demon | Asylum Demon | As the data card | 4 pillar tokens, one on each pillar *node* |
| Black Dragon Kalameet | Own | 4 of 10 standard cards, Mark of Calamity and Hellfire Blast | Hellfire Barrage, set aside; the 8 strafe cards as a strafe deck |
| Executioner's Chariot | Own | Track deck: Death Race 1 to 4 in order, 1 on top, not shuffled | Heat-up deck: 4 of 9 standard cards and 1 of 3 heat-up cards, shuffled together and set aside; 2x Black Hollow Mage and 4x Falchion Skeleton |
| The Four Kings | Own | 4 of 8 standard cards | The second, third and fourth king cards, as three piles of 4 |
| Gaping Dragon | Own | 3 of 7 standard cards, 1 of 4 heat-up cards and both Stomach Slam cards | Crawling Charge, face up beside the data card; the other 3 heat-up cards |
| Guardian Dragon | Own | 5 of 9 standard cards | Both Cage Grasp Inferno cards, set aside; the 4 Fiery Breath cards as a Fiery Breath deck |
| The Last Giant | Own | 3 of 5 standard cards and 3 of 5 Arm cards | Falling Slam, set aside; the 5 heat-up cards |
| Old Iron King | Own | 3 of 6 standard cards and the 3 Fire Beam cards | The 3 heat-up cards; the 6 Beam cards as a Beam deck |
| Vordt of the Boreal Valley | Own | Movement deck: 4 of 6 standard movement cards. Attack deck: 3 of 6 standard attack cards | The 2 heat-up movement cards and the 2 heat-up attack cards, as two piles; Frostbreath, face down |

### Dodge Difficulty 0

Some behaviour cards of Great Grey Wolf Sif, Artorias and the Pursuer show a *dodge difficulty* of 0. A *dodge* against such a card costs its one *stamina* and succeeds without a roll. No dodge dice are needed.

### Marked-Node Attacks

**Marked-node attack.** An attack that strikes the nodes marked on a card, in place of an *arc* and a *range*. Every character on a marked node is attacked, and each defends on their own.

- The cards that mark the nodes form a deck of their own. Each attack reveals the top card and discards it once resolved. When that deck is empty, turn its discard pile face down without shuffling.
- Where a rule of this chapter places a boss model on a node, the other models on that node are *pushed* off it as after a *leap* (see §16.2).

### Ornstein and Smough

One *main-boss*, two models. Dragon Slayer Ornstein and Executioner Smough each have a data card and a health dial of their own.

- Ornstein starts on the main-boss *spawn node* and Smough on the *mini-boss* spawn node.
- Each boss turn draws one card. Ornstein resolves the behaviour printed for Ornstein, then Smough the one printed for Smough. The range, the dodge difficulty, the arcs and the *weak arcs* {{icon:ast-0041-weak-boss-arc}} printed with a behaviour apply to its model alone.
- A character's attack is made against one model. A character on a model's node stands in that model's arcs.
- A model that moves onto the other's node pushes it to an adjacent node of the players' choice.
- Neither data card shows a heat-up point {{icon:ast-0030-heat-up-point}}. When the first model falls, remove it and return the deck and its discard pile to the box. Shuffle the survivor's heat-up cards as the new deck, and raise its health by 10 if it is Ornstein or by 15 if it is Smough, never above its starting health.
- The boss is defeated when both models have fallen.

### Twin Gargoyles

Where a campaign *section* lists the Twin Gargoyles, two Gargoyles are fought one after the other as one mini-boss encounter. The one model and health dial serve for both.

- When the first Gargoyle falls, return its model to the spawn node, set the dial to its starting health and build a new behaviour deck. Play goes on in the same order.
- The characters keep their nodes, cubes and tokens, and the dead stay out.
- Each Gargoyle heats up at its own heat-up point.
- The rewards of a defeated boss are given once, when the second Gargoyle falls (see §16.5).
- If the encounter is *failed*, both Gargoyles are fought again.

## 17.2 Core Set

The Gargoyle, the Titanite Demon, the Boreal Outrider Knight, the Winged Knight and the Dancer of the Boreal Valley have no special rules. Ornstein and Smough and the Twin Gargoyles have rules of their own (see §17.1).

## 17.3 Asylum Demon and Stray Demon

The Asylum Demon is a *mini-boss* and the Stray Demon a *mega-boss*. Both are fought on the mega-boss side of the Asylum Demon board. For the Asylum Demon that board takes the place of the mini-boss tile, with the *fog gate* on its doorway, and the model starts on the mega-boss *spawn node*.

### Pillars of Woe

When either boss encounter is set up, place one pillar token on each of the four pillar *nodes* of the board. Pillars block movement: no model may enter a pillar's node or be *pushed* onto it.

When the Asylum Demon or the Stray Demon makes a physical attack with the area icon {{icon:ast-0038-area-attack-icon}}, check for pillar tokens within the area of the attack: within its *range* and in one of its attack *arcs* (see §16.3).

1. For each such pillar, every character within one node of it suffers one damage and the *stagger* {{icon:ast-0066-stagger}} *condition*. This damage cannot be *blocked*, *resisted* or dodged.
2. Remove those pillar tokens from the board.
3. Resolve the attack itself, with its dodges and pushes.

One attack may smash several pillars, and one character may be hit by several shattering pillars at once.

The node of a destroyed pillar is a *basic node* for the rest of the encounter. When the party rests at the *bonfire*, return the destroyed pillars to their nodes.

### Wind Up

When the Wind Up icon is resolved, each character may spend one *stamina* to move at once to an adjacent node. This movement counts as a *run*. The icon works the same on the cards of any other boss.

## 17.4 Darkroot

Some *behaviour cards* of Sif and of Artorias show a *dodge difficulty* of 0 (see §17.1).

### Great Grey Wolf Sif

Limping Strike is neither a standard card nor a *heat-up* card. When Sif's health falls to three or less, return the behaviour deck and its discard pile to the box. Limping Strike alone is now the deck: Sif draws and resolves it every turn until the encounter ends.

### Artorias

Artorias has a heat-up of its own. When its health reaches its heat-up point:

1. Gather the behaviour deck and its discard pile.
2. Remove two of the gathered cards at random and return them to the box.
3. Add all three of Artorias's heat-up cards.
4. Shuffle the cards to form the new behaviour deck.

## 17.5 Iron Keep

### Smelter Demon

The Smelter Demon has a *heat-up* of its own. When its health reaches its heat-up point:

1. Return the behaviour deck and its discard pile to the box.
2. Shuffle the heat-up cards and draw five as the new behaviour deck.
3. Return the other heat-up cards to the box.

## 17.6 Explorers

### Old Dragonslayer

The Old Dragonslayer is played with the Dragon Slayer Ornstein model of the core set. Its *data card* shows no *heat-up* point. The first three times it loses four or more health to a single attack, place one of its heat-up cards, chosen at random, face down on top of its behaviour deck. Do not shuffle: it is the next card drawn. The Old Dragonslayer heats up in no other way.

### Pursuer

- Some of the Pursuer's *behaviour cards* show a *dodge difficulty* of 0 (see §17.1).
- Each turn the Pursuer draws and resolves two behaviour cards, one after the other. If the deck is empty when either card is due, turn the discard pile over first. The *weak arcs* are those of the second card.
- From its heat-up until the encounter ends, every attack on its behaviour cards has +1 to the attack value.

### Mimics

The Hungry *Mimic* and the Voracious Mimic are fought by the mimic rules (see §13).

## 17.7 Black Dragon Kalameet

Kalameet's model starts on the *mega-boss* *spawn node*, facing the centre of the board.

- Mark of Calamity and Hellfire Blast are in every behaviour deck built for Kalameet. Hellfire Barrage is its only *heat-up* card.
- An attack with the *calamity* icon gives one calamity token to each character it hits (see §8.5).
- The strafe icon is a magical {{icon:ast-0042-deal-magical-damage}} marked-node attack (see §17.1). It uses the attack value and the *dodge difficulty* of the *behaviour card* that shows the icon.

When a strafe icon is resolved:

1. Remove Kalameet's model from the board. Every other model stays on its *node*.
2. Reveal the top strafe card and attack every character on a node it marks.
3. Place Kalameet's model on the landing node shown on the strafe card, facing as it faced before it was removed. Characters on that node are *pushed* off it.

A character on the landing node is attacked only if the card also marks that node.

## 17.8 Executioner's Chariot

The Chariot is a *mega-boss* fought in two parts. Until its *heat-up* it follows a fixed track and cannot be harmed. Afterwards it is fought as any boss.

- Its two models, the skeletal horse and the chariot, share one *node*, move together and count as one boss model. They share one double-sided *data card* and one health dial.
- The centre of the Chariot's board is a wall. Nodes on opposite sides of it are not adjacent, *range* is counted around it, and no model may move, *dodge* or be *pushed* into it.
- At setup the data card lies front side up and both models stand on the mega-boss *spawn node*. The Black Hollow Mages and the Falchion Skeletons stand on the enemy spawn nodes {{icon:ast-0006-primary-spawn-node}} shown on the mega-boss setup card. They are enemies and act from their own data cards (see §8.2). The health dial is not used until the heat-up.

**Before the heat-up.** The front of the data card shows no starting health {{icon:ast-0029-starting-health}}, no *block* and no *resist*. The Chariot cannot be attacked or damaged, and nothing a character does affects it.

Before each character's turn, every enemy model on the board takes a turn, in descending *threat* (see §7.5): the Chariot, each Black Hollow Mage and each Falchion Skeleton.

On its turn the Chariot resolves the top card of the track deck. The track deck is never shuffled: the four cards are resolved in order, and card 1 follows card 4.

1. Move both models together to the node shown on the card.
2. Turn both models so that their front *arc* faces directly away from the node they left.
3. Make the card's physical marked-node attack (see §17.1), with the attack value and the *dodge difficulty* on the card. The marked nodes include the node the Chariot left and the node it entered.
4. Push each character hit by the attack to an adjacent node that is not marked. The players choose the node.
5. Push each enemy on a marked node in the same way. It suffers no damage.

A model with no unmarked adjacent node to enter stays where it is.

**Chariot Crash.** The Chariot has no heat-up point. It heats up at once when no Black Hollow Mage and no Falchion Skeleton remains on the board.

1. Remove the chariot model from the board. The skeletal horse stays on its node and keeps its facing.
2. Flip the data card to its heat-up side, the Skeletal Horse.
3. Set the health dial to the starting health shown on that side.
4. Return the track cards to the box.
5. Take the heat-up deck built at setup as the behaviour deck.

From then on the skeletal horse is fought as any boss, against the block and resist values of the heat-up side. It does not heat up again.

**Crush.** When a character's block or resist roll reduces the damage of one of the skeletal horse's attacks to zero, that character suffers one damage. A dodged attack causes none.

**Raise Skeleton.** This icon is printed on the Black Hollow Mage's data card and is resolved the same way in every encounter. Count the enemies on the tile whose names include the word "Skeleton". If there are fewer than at the start of the encounter, the players choose one such enemy killed during the encounter and place its model, with no wound tokens, on the enemy spawn node nearest to that Black Hollow Mage. The players choose between nodes that are equally near. Otherwise the icon has no effect.

## 17.9 Bosses of Other Expansions

### The Four Kings

The Four Kings are one *mega-boss* with four models, the kings numbered one to four, who enter the encounter one at a time. Each king has a health dial of its own, and the identifier token on a model's base shows which dial that is. The data card shows no *heat-up* point: the Four Kings never heat up.

- King one starts on the mega-boss *spawn node*. The other three models wait beside the board.
- Each boss turn draws one behaviour card, and every king in play resolves the whole card, in numerical order.
- A character's attack is made against one king, and a king that moves onto another's *node* pushes it, as for Ornstein and Smough (see §17.1).
- A king whose health dial reaches zero is removed from the board. The boss is defeated when all four kings have fallen.

**Royal Summons.** The first three times the behaviour deck is empty at the start of a boss turn, the next king arrives. Do this in place of turning the discard pile over:

1. Place the next king in numerical order on the mega-boss spawn node, facing as a boss faces at the start of its encounter, and set its health dial to its starting health. The models already on that node are pushed off it (see §17.1).
2. Turn the discard pile face down, remove one of its cards at random without looking at it, and return that card to the box.
3. Add two cards, taken at random and unseen, from the pile of the king just placed.
4. Shuffle these cards to form the new behaviour deck.

The boss turn then goes on: draw the top card of the new deck, and every king in play resolves it. A summons does not wait for the earlier kings to fall.

If no king is in play at the start of a boss turn and no summons is due, draw the top card of the behaviour deck and discard it without effect. Each living character removes one damage cube, and the boss turn ends.

### Gaping Dragon

- Both Stomach Slam cards and one random heat-up card are in every behaviour deck built for the Gaping Dragon. When it heats up, one of the three remaining heat-up cards joins the deck in the usual way.
- An attack with the *corrosion* icon gives one corrosion token to each character it hits (see §8.5).
- Crawling Charge is never part of the behaviour deck. When the crawl icon is resolved, the Gaping Dragon at once resolves every icon on Crawling Charge, from left to right, with the *range* and the *dodge difficulty* printed on that card. Its turn then ends: no further icon of the drawn behaviour card is resolved. The drawn card, and not Crawling Charge, still shows the *weak arcs*.

### Guardian Dragon

- The four corner nodes of the Guardian Dragon's board are cage nodes. No model may move, *dodge* or be pushed into a cage node. The Guardian Dragon stands on one only when an Inferno icon places it there.
- When the Guardian Dragon heats up, both Cage Grasp Inferno cards join the behaviour deck, in place of one heat-up card taken at random.
- The Inferno icon on those cards is a magical marked-node attack (see §17.1). It uses the attack value and the dodge difficulty printed on the Fiery Breath card.
- When the Bonus Behaviour icon is resolved, the Guardian Dragon at once draws the next card of its behaviour deck and resolves it in full, in the same turn. If the deck is empty, turn the discard pile over first. The second card shows the weak arcs from the moment it is drawn.

When an Inferno icon is resolved:

1. Remove the Guardian Dragon's model from the board. Every other model stays on its node.
2. Reveal the top Fiery Breath card.
3. Place the model on the cage node shown on the card, facing the centre node of the board.
4. Attack every character on a node the card marks.

The Guardian Dragon stays on the cage node until one of its behaviour cards moves it.

### The Last Giant

The Last Giant has a heat-up of its own. When its health reaches its heat-up point:

1. Gather the behaviour deck and its discard pile.
2. Remove the three Arm cards and return them to the box.
3. Add Falling Slam and three heat-up cards taken at random.
4. Shuffle the seven cards to form the new behaviour deck.

The Wind Up icon on the Last Giant's cards is resolved as for the Asylum Demon (see §17.3).

### Manus, Father of the Abyss

- **Overextended.** At the end of each of Manus's turns, each character may spend one *stamina* to move to an adjacent node, before the next character's turn begins. This movement counts as a *run*.
- An attack with the *shaft* icon does not target a character on Manus's own node (see §10.3), whichever *arc* that character stands in.
- Manus has a heat-up of its own. When its health reaches its heat-up point, take two of its heat-up cards at random, without looking at them. Place one face down on top of the behaviour deck and the other beneath the discard pile. Do not shuffle. The first is the next card Manus draws; the second is the first card drawn after the discard pile is next turned over.

### Old Iron King

- The Old Iron King stands only on the three Iron King nodes of its board. It starts on the Iron King node opposite the doorway.
- The lava behind the Iron King nodes holds no nodes and is a wall. A character on the Old Iron King's node cannot stand in its back arc.
- The Beam icon on the Fire Beam cards is a magical marked-node attack (see §17.1). It uses the attack value and the dodge difficulty of the Fire Beam card.
- **Old Iron Rage.** From its heat-up until the encounter ends, every Beam attack has +1 to the attack value and +1 to the dodge difficulty.

When a Beam icon is resolved:

1. Reveal the top Beam card.
2. Place the Old Iron King's model on the Iron King node shown on the card, facing the centre of the board. The other models on that node are pushed off it.
3. Attack every character on a node the card marks. A character pushed onto a marked node in step 2 is attacked.

### Vordt of the Boreal Valley

Vordt draws from two behaviour decks and heats up twice. The attack deck lies to the right of the movement deck and Frostbreath to its left. The two decks and their discard piles are kept apart.

- Each turn Vordt draws and resolves two cards: the top card of the movement deck, then the top card of the attack deck. Each card goes to the discard pile of its own deck. When a deck is empty as its card is due, turn its own discard pile face down without shuffling. The attack card shows the weak arcs.
- **Icy Blows.** A character who *blocks* or *resists* one of Vordt's attacks and prevents four or more damage gains a *frostbite* token (see §8.5). The damage prevented is the attack value or the character's block or resist sum, whichever is lower. A character who dodges gains no token.
- Vordt's data card shows two heat-up points. At the higher, gather the attack deck and its discard pile, add one heat-up attack card taken at random, and shuffle them as the new attack deck. At the lower, do the same for the movement deck with one heat-up movement card.
- After each heat-up Vordt resolves Frostbreath once, at the start of its next turn, before it draws its movement card. Frostbreath is then turned face down again and joins neither deck.
