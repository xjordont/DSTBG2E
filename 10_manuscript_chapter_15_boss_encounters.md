# 15. Boss Encounters

Boss encounters are the heart of the adventure. A boss is a foe of great health and power, with a behaviour deck in place of fixed behaviours. There are three types: mini-boss, main-boss, and mega-boss.

Bosses follow the enemy rules of §8 and §9 except as stated in this chapter and §16.

## 15.1 The Fog Gate

The *fog gate* token marks the *doorway* through which the characters enter a *boss* *encounter*. During setup it is placed on the doorway between the last encounter tile of the route and the *boss tile* (see §6.5, §6.6), or on the doorway of the *mega-boss game board* (see §6.7).

The characters enter a boss encounter only by passing through the fog gate, and the party passes through it together. The boss encounter begins when the characters pass through (see §15.2). The party may pass through the fog gate when it advances from the last encounter tile of the route (see §7.5, Encounter Recovery). Before the characters pass through, an *embered* character may call a *summon* (see §12.1).

The first time the characters pass through the fog gate of a boss, the fog gate token is moved from the doorway to the *bonfire* tile. The token stays on the bonfire tile until that boss is defeated. The setup that follows the boss's defeat moves the token to the doorway of the next boss (see §6.6, §6.7).

While the fog gate token is on the bonfire tile, it is a shortcut to that boss. When the characters set out from the bonfire tile (see §11.5), the party may pass through the fog gate on the bonfire tile and begin the boss encounter at once. The encounters on the route are then neither fought nor skipped: no *stamina cube* is placed for them (see §7.5, Encounter Start). An encounter on the route that is not marked *cleared* does not bar the shortcut.

The party may instead leave the shortcut unused and travel the route from the first encounter tile. Each encounter on the route is then fought or skipped as in any exploration (see §7.5, Encounter Start), and the boss encounter begins when the party advances from the last encounter tile.

In campaign play the fog gate token is aesthetic only and is not a shortcut (see §18.2).

*Example.* The Knight and the Herald clear the fourth encounter and pass through the fog gate for the first time. The fog gate token is moved to the bonfire tile, and the *mini-boss* encounter begins. The Herald, who is *hollow*, dies, and the encounter is *failed*. The party spends one *spark* and rests at the bonfire. The characters then pass through the fog gate on the bonfire tile and begin the mini-boss encounter again. The four *reset* encounters are not fought, and no stamina cubes are placed for them.

## 15.2 Overview

A *boss* encounter is an *encounter* fought against a boss on a *boss tile* or on a *mega-boss game board* (see §15.3). It follows the rules for encounters (see §7.5) except as this chapter and §16 state. No *encounter card* is dealt to a boss tile, and the boss is the only enemy in the encounter unless its special rules add others (see §17).

A *mini-boss* is fought at the end of the first leg of the exploration and a *main-boss* at the end of the second (see §6.5, §6.6). A *mega-boss*, when one is in play, is fought after the *Level 4 encounter* (see §6.7). A boss is not scaled for the number of characters (see §8.3).

The boss's *behaviour deck* is built during setup, before the boss encounter begins, and is not built again at this point (see §15.5). When the characters pass through the *fog gate*, the boss encounter is set up as follows.

1. Move the fog gate token to the *bonfire* tile, if this is the first time the characters pass through this boss's fog gate (see §15.1).
2. Place each character model on an *entry node* of the boss tile (see §15.3), no more than three models to a node (see §5.3).
3. Place on each character's *endurance bar* the *stamina cubes* owed for encounters skipped on the route (see §7.5, Encounter Start).
4. If the party called a *summon* at the fog gate, place the summon's model (see §12.1).
5. Set the boss's *health dial* to the starting health shown on its *data card* (see §15.4).
6. Place the boss model on the boss *spawn node*, with the centre of its front arc facing the *adjacent node* closest to the entry nodes (see §15.6). If two or more adjacent nodes are equally close, the players choose one of them.
7. Carry out any setup that the boss's special rules require (see §17).
8. Place the *aggro token* on the character with the highest *threat* (see §7.5, Encounter Start).
9. Begin the boss's first *turn* (see §16.1).

If a boss's special rules state where the boss is placed or how it faces, those rules replace that part of step 6, and the rest of the step applies (see §17).

The boss takes the first turn of the encounter. The boss and the characters then alternate turns: one character takes a turn after each boss turn (see §16.1). A summon takes its turns as §12.1 describes. No *invader* joins a boss encounter (see §12.2).

When the boss's health is reduced to or below the *heat-up point* on its data card, the boss heats up, and a *heat-up card* joins its behaviour deck (see §15.5).

A boss encounter ends in one of two ways. The boss is defeated when its health dial is reduced to zero (see §16.5). The encounter is *failed* when every character has died, or when a *hollow* character dies (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution).

A failed boss encounter is resolved as any failed encounter (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution). This rule applies to every boss, a mega-boss included: a character who dies in a mega-boss encounter is revived as after any other encounter (see §7.2).

When the characters rest at the bonfire after a failed boss encounter, the boss encounter is *reset* with every other encounter (see §11.5). The boss model is removed from the boss tile and kept with its data card, and its behaviour deck is built again (see §15.5, Deck Reset). The boss's health dial is returned to its starting health when the encounter is next set up. *Souls* dropped on the boss tile stay there (see §7.6). The encounter is set up again from step 2 when the characters next pass through the fog gate.

## 15.3 Boss Tiles

**Boss tile.** One of the two tiles on which *mini-boss* and *main-boss* *encounters* are fought. One boss tile is printed with the mini-boss *spawn node* and is the mini-boss tile. The other is printed with the main-boss spawn node.

A mini-boss encounter is fought on the mini-boss tile alone, unless the mini-boss's special rules name another tile (see §17.3).

A main-boss encounter is fought on both *boss tiles*. The two tiles are joined along one edge to form a single rectangular tile, the main-boss tile (see §6.6). The joined tiles are one tile for every rule. No wall runs along the join, and each *node* beside the join is adjacent to the nodes that face it across the join (see §5.1). Models *move*, are *pushed*, and count *range* across the join as within any tile.

A *mega-boss* encounter is fought on the mega-boss side of the *mega-boss game board* supplied with that mega-boss (see §6.7). A wall printed across a game board separates the nodes on either side of it (see §5.1).

**Boss spawn node.** The spawn node of the boss's type: the mini-boss spawn node, the main-boss spawn node, or the mega-boss spawn node. The boss model is placed on it at the start of every boss encounter, unless the boss's special rules name another node (see §15.2; §17).

The mini-boss spawn node of the main-boss tile is used as a *basic node* during a main-boss encounter (see §5.1), unless the main-boss's special rules place a model on it (see §17).

The *entry nodes* of a boss encounter are the basic nodes adjacent to the *doorway* on which the *fog gate* token is placed during setup (see §5.1). They are the same nodes whether the characters arrive along the route or by the fog gate shortcut (see §15.1).

No *encounter card* and no *trap tokens* are dealt to a boss tile, and no *terrain* is placed on it, unless the boss's special rules place some (see §17).

## 15.4 Boss Data Cards

A *boss*'s *data card* lists no behaviours. A boss acts according to its *behaviour deck* (see §15.5). Every boss data card shows the following parts.

- **Name.** The boss's name is printed across the top of the card.
- **Threat.** The value beside the threat icon {{icon:ast-0026-threat-level}} is the boss's *threat* (see §8.2).
- **Behaviour deck size.** The value beside the behaviour deck size icon {{icon:ast-0027-behaviour-deck-size}} is the number of standard *behaviour cards* that form the boss's behaviour deck (see §15.5).
- **Starting health.** The value beside the starting health icon {{icon:ast-0029-starting-health}} is the health at which the boss begins every attempt at its encounter.
- **Heat-up point.** The value beside the heat-up point icon {{icon:ast-0030-heat-up-point}} is the health at or below which the boss heats up (see §15.5).
- **Block and resist.** The two values on the shield icon {{icon:ast-0028-block-resist-shield}} are the boss's *block* and *resist*. Block is subtracted from each physical attack against the boss and resist from each magical attack (see §8.2).
- **Special ability.** A rule unique to that boss. It applies for the whole encounter (see §17).
- **Encounter levels.** The *encounter levels* of the four encounters on the route to that boss, in order from the *bonfire* tile (see §6.5, Encounter Cards). The route to a *mega-boss* is the single *Level 4 encounter* fought on its game board (see §6.7).
- **Boss type icon.** An icon marks the boss as a *mini-boss*, a *main-boss*, or a mega-boss.
- **Set symbol.** A symbol shows the core set or expansion the boss belongs to.

Threat, block, and resist are used exactly as on an enemy data card (see §8.2). A boss's values are not scaled for the number of characters (see §8.3).

Boss health is tracked on that boss's *health dial*, not with *wound tokens* (see §8.2). The dial is set to the boss's starting health when the boss encounter is set up (see §15.2). Each point of damage the boss suffers lowers the dial by one. A boss whose health dial is reduced to zero is defeated (see §16.5).

*Example.* A mini-boss has starting health 24, block 2, and resist 1, and its health dial is set to 24 when the encounter is set up. The Knight makes a physical attack and rolls an *attack value* of 7. The boss's block of 2 is subtracted, the boss suffers five damage, and the health dial is turned to 19.

## 15.5 Boss Behaviour Cards

A *boss* has no fixed behaviours. Its *behaviour cards* take the place of the *behaviour icons* on an enemy *data card* (see §8.2). One behaviour card is drawn at the start of each of the boss's *turns*, and the boss does what the card shows (see §16.1).

### Card Anatomy

Every boss behaviour card shows the following parts.

- **Name.** The name of the behaviour is printed across the top of the card.
- **Range.** The value beside the range icon {{icon:ast-0060-attack-range}} is the *range* of every attack on the card (see §5.4; §16.3).
- **Dodge difficulty.** The value beside the dodge icon {{icon:ast-0062-dodge}} is the *dodge difficulty* of every attack on the card (see §9.5).
- **Heat-up symbol.** The heat-up symbol marks the card as a *heat-up card*. A card without the symbol is a standard behaviour card.
- **Behaviour icons.** A row of movement icons {{icon:ast-0033-enemy-movement-composite-symbol}} and attack icons {{icon:ast-0037-enemy-attack-composite-symbol}}, resolved one at a time from left to right (see §16.2; §16.3).
- **Arc diagram.** A diagram beneath each attack icon shows which of the boss's arcs the attack is made into and which arcs are weak (see §15.6, Arc Diagrams).
- **Repeat.** A card that bears the *repeat* icon {{icon:ast-0061-repeat-behaviour-or-attack}} is resolved the number of times the icon shows (see §16.1).

A boss has no single range or dodge difficulty of its own. The card a boss drew most recently is its current behaviour card until the next card is drawn: the card being resolved during the boss's turn, and at every other time the card it resolved most recently. The range and dodge difficulty of the current behaviour card apply, and its arc diagram shows the boss's *weak arcs*, for that whole time (see §15.6, Arc Diagrams).

### Deck Construction

The *behaviour deck* of each boss is built during setup, at the step that places the boss's components on its tile (see §6.5, §6.6, §6.7). It is not built at the start of the boss *encounter*. The deck is built as follows.

1. Set aside the boss's heat-up cards.
2. Shuffle the boss's standard behaviour cards face down.
3. Deal a number of standard behaviour cards equal to the behaviour deck size on the boss's data card {{icon:ast-0027-behaviour-deck-size}} into a face-down pile, without looking at them. This pile is the behaviour deck.
4. Set aside the standard behaviour cards that were not dealt, face down, without looking at them. They take no part in the encounter.
5. Shuffle the heat-up cards and place them face down beside the *boss tile*.

The behaviour deck is kept face down on the boss tile, or beside the game board of a *mega-boss*, until the boss encounter begins. The players do not look at its cards, except that a *scry* may reveal its top card (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution). A boss whose special rules build its deck differently follows those rules (see §17).

### Deck Renewal

At the start of each boss turn the top card of the behaviour deck is drawn. When the card has been resolved, it is placed face up on top of the boss's discard pile (see §16.1). The discard pile is kept in the order in which its cards were resolved.

When the behaviour deck is empty at the start of a boss turn, the discard pile is turned face down, without shuffling, and becomes the behaviour deck. The cards are then drawn again in the order in which they were first drawn.

### Heat-Up

**Heat-up point.** The health value, printed on a boss's data card, at or below which the boss heats up.

A boss heats up when its health is reduced to or below its *heat-up point* {{icon:ast-0030-heat-up-point}}. A boss heats up once in each attempt at its encounter. The heat-up is carried out at the start of the boss's next turn, before a card is drawn (see §16.1), as follows.

1. Gather the behaviour deck and the discard pile.
2. Take one card at random from the boss's heat-up cards, without looking at it, and add it to the gathered cards.
3. Shuffle the gathered cards face down to form the new behaviour deck.

The boss then draws the top card of the new deck. Until that card is drawn, the boss's current behaviour card and its weak arcs do not change (see §15.5, Card Anatomy). The boss's other heat-up cards stay face down beside the boss tile and take no part in the encounter.

Some bosses heat up in other ways. The special rules of such a boss replace the steps of this sub-section. Their steps are also carried out at the start of the boss's next turn, unless those rules state another time (see §17).

*Example.* A *mini-boss* has a heat-up point of 12, and its *health dial* shows 14. Two cards remain in its behaviour deck and three lie in its discard pile. The Warrior's attack inflicts three damage, and the dial is turned to 11. At the start of the boss's next turn the players gather the two cards of the deck and the three cards of the discard pile, add one of the boss's heat-up cards, taken at random and unseen, and shuffle the six cards to form the new behaviour deck. The boss then draws the top card.

### Deck Reset

When a *failed* boss encounter is *reset* (see §15.2), the boss's behaviour deck is built again as follows.

1. Gather all of the boss's behaviour cards: the behaviour deck, the discard pile, the heat-up cards, and the standard behaviour cards set aside when the deck was built.
2. Build the behaviour deck from them (see §15.5, Deck Construction).
3. Place the behaviour deck face down on the boss tile, or beside the game board of a mega-boss.

Each attempt at a boss is fought against a newly built behaviour deck.

## 15.6 Arcs and Defence

### Boss Arcs

**Boss arc.** One of the four sectors into which the base of a *boss* model is divided: front, left, right, and back.

**Facing.** The direction in which the centre of a boss's front arc points. A boss faces directly towards an *adjacent node*, so that the centre of its front arc lines up with the centre of that node.

The lines that divide the base of a boss model, extended across the tile, divide the tile into four areas, one for each *boss arc*. Every *node* on the tile is in at least one arc. A node on the line between two arcs is in both arcs, and a model on that node is in both arcs at once.

When a boss faces a node in the same row or column as its own, the lines between its arcs run along the diagonals through its node. When a boss faces a node diagonally, the lines run along the row and the column through its node.

A character on the boss's own node is placed against the boss's base in one of the four arcs and is in that arc only (see §15.6, Characters and Arcs).

*Mimics*, *invaders*, and *summons* have no arcs. The rules of this section for arcs do not apply to attacks against them or to movement on their nodes (see §12; §13).

### Facing and Turning

A boss changes its *facing* only when a movement icon turns it towards or away from a *target* (see §16.2) or when it resolves a turn icon. A boss does not change its facing when it attacks (see §16.3).

A boss resolves a turn icon by turning in place on its node.

- The turn 90° right icon {{icon:ast-0034-turn-90-right}} turns the boss 90° to its right.
- The turn 90° left icon {{icon:ast-0035-turn-90-left}} turns the boss 90° to its left.
- The turn around icon {{icon:ast-0036-turn-around}} turns the boss 180°, so that its front arc points where its back arc pointed.

A turn icon may leave a boss facing a wall. The boss keeps that facing until it next turns.

When a boss turns, no other model moves. A character on the boss's node stays in place while the boss model turns beneath them, and the character is then in the arc of the base that they touch. The arcs of every other node are read from the boss's new facing.

### Arc Diagrams

Beneath each attack icon on a *behaviour card* is an arc diagram. It shows each of the boss's four arcs in one of three states.

- A neutral arc {{icon:ast-0039-neutral-boss-arc}}, shown in black, has no effect.
- An *attack arc* {{icon:ast-0040-attack-boss-arc}}, shown in green, is an arc into which an *area attack* is made (see §16.3, Area Attacks).
- A *weak arc* {{icon:ast-0041-weak-boss-arc}}, shown in red, is an arc from which the boss is open to attack (see §15.6, Weak Arcs).

The attack arcs of a diagram apply only to the attack icon beneath which the diagram is printed. The boss's weak arcs are those shown on its current behaviour card (see §15.5, Card Anatomy). If that card bears more than one arc diagram, the diagram beneath its last attack icon shows the boss's weak arcs.

### Weak Arcs

A character attacks a boss as any other enemy (see §10.3). The boss's *block* or *resist* is subtracted from the attack (see §8.2), and the damage lowers the boss's *health dial* (see §15.4).

When a character attacks a boss from a weak arc, the attack gains one additional black die {{icon:ast-0051-black-dice}}. A character attacks from the arc in which their node lies or, on the boss's own node, from the arc of the base in which they are placed. The die is gained at any *range*.

A character on a node that lies in two arcs attacks from both arcs, and the attack gains the die if either arc is weak. The same character is targeted by an area attack made into either arc (see §16.3, Area Attacks). The attack gains one black die only, however many weak arcs the character is in.

The die is added to every attack roll made from a weak arc. This includes each roll of an *attack option* that bears the *repeat* icon (see §10.3, Repeat) and the roll of a *riposte* (see §9.6). A summon that attacks a boss from a weak arc gains the die as a character does (see §12.1).

*Example.* The boss faces the node directly ahead of it, on which the Herald stands. The Herald is in the front arc. The Knight stands on the node directly behind the boss, in the back arc. The Assassin stands on the node diagonally ahead of the boss and to its left, which lies on the line between the front arc and the left arc, and is in both arcs. The boss's current behaviour card shows the left arc and the back arc as weak arcs. The Knight attacks from the back arc and adds one black die. The Assassin attacks from the front arc and the left arc and adds one black die. The Herald attacks from the front arc and adds no die.

### Characters and Arcs

A character who shares a node with a boss must be placed against the boss's base in one of its four arcs.

When a character moves onto a boss's node, the character is placed in the arc in which the node they left lies. If that node lies in two arcs, the character's player chooses one of the two.

A character on a boss's node may, in place of moving one node, move to an adjacent arc of the base. The front arc and the back arc are each adjacent to the left arc and to the right arc. Each node of movement granted by the *Walk* or *Run* *minor action* (see §10.2) or by a *shift* icon (see §10.3, Shift) may be used in this way.

When a character moves off a boss's node, the character must move to a *valid node* that is adjacent and that lies in the arc in which the character is placed. A node that lies in two arcs lies in each of them for this purpose. If that arc holds no such node, the character cannot move off the boss's node from that arc.

A character who is *pushed* off a boss's node must be pushed to a valid adjacent node that lies in the arc in which the character is placed. This limit applies whatever causes the push: a *push-move* (see §8.4; §16.2), a *push-attack* (see §16.3), or the *node model limit* (see §5.3). The players choose among the valid adjacent nodes in that arc.

If that arc holds no valid adjacent node because of a wall, the character is pushed to any valid adjacent node that touches that wall. If no valid node exists, the character is not pushed (see §8.4). The push that follows a *leap* is not limited by arcs (see §16.2, Leap).

*Example.* The Knight is on the boss's node, in the front arc. The Knight takes the Walk minor action and moves to the left arc, which costs no stamina. The Knight then takes the Run minor action, places one *stamina cube*, and moves to the back arc.

### Defence

A character targeted by a boss's attack defends as against any enemy attack: by block or resist (see §9.4), by *dodge* (see §9.5), or by *parry* (see §9.6). The *dodge difficulty* of the attack is the one printed on the boss's current behaviour card.

The movement that follows a successful dodge ignores arcs. A character who dodges while on a boss's node must move either to any valid adjacent node, whatever arc it lies in, or to any other arc of the boss's base. A character who dodges onto a boss's node from another node is placed in an arc of their player's choice.

A character may parry a boss's attack only when the attack targets one character and the conditions for a parry are met (see §9.6). An area attack, a *node attack*, and a push-attack cannot be parried. A boss that is parried is *staggered*, and the character may riposte, as against any enemy (see §9.6).

A boss model cannot be pushed by a character's attack or by a character's movement (see §8.4). The push icon on a character's attack option has no effect on a boss, and a boss is not displaced under the node model limit (see §5.3). A boss model is pushed only when another boss model moves onto its node or is placed on it (see §17.1; §17.10, The Four Kings).
