# 21. Glossary

Terms set in *italics* in this book are defined here. Where a definition and a chapter disagree, the chapter governs.

**Adjacent node.** A node directly next to another node horizontally, vertically, or diagonally. Nodes separated by a wall, including a wall printed across a tile or a mega-boss game board, are not adjacent, and nodes on different tiles are not adjacent (see §5.1).

**Aggro.** The state of being the enemies' preferred target. Enemy movement icons and attack icons marked with the skull icon target the character who holds the aggro token (see §9.2, §9.3).

**Aggro token.** The single token that marks the character with aggro. It is placed on the character with the highest threat at the start of each encounter and, after each round of character turns, moves to the character who inflicted the most damage on enemy models during that round (on a tie, or if no damage was inflicted, the holder keeps it). If the holder dies it moves to the living character with the highest threat. In boss encounters it moves to any character who damages the boss during their turn (see §7.5, Encounter Turn Order; §16.4).

**Ambush card.** A card from the Explorers expansion that shows Treasure or Mimic. When mimics are in play, one is dealt face down beside each encounter tile and is turned face up only when the party chooses to open that tile's chest (see §13.1).

**Area attack.** A boss attack whose icon bears the area icon. It is made into the attack arcs of the arc diagram beneath it and targets every character within the card's range who is in one of those arcs. Each targeted character defends separately, and an area attack cannot be parried (see §16.3; §15.6).

**Area-of-effect (AoE).** A character attack option marked with the area-of-effect icon. It targets one node within range: one attack roll is compared with the block or resist of every enemy on that node, and every enemy there is hit (see §10.3, Area-of-Effect).

**Armour.** An equipment card bearing the armour icon. One armour card may be equipped in the armour slot. After a failed dodge, only the block or resist dice of the armour card and of the upgrade cards attached to it are rolled (see §9.5).

**Armour slot.** The dashboard slot that holds one equipped *armour* card (see §3.2).

**Attack arc.** A *boss arc* shown in green on the arc diagram beneath an *area attack*. The area attack targets every character within range who is in that arc (see §15.6; §16.3).

**Attack (major action).** The major action in which a character makes up to one attack with each weapon or spell equipped in a weapon slot, each attack using one attack option and paying its stamina cost (see §10.3).

**Attack option.** One line of a weapon or spell card, showing a stamina cost, dice and modifiers. Using an attack option is the *Attack* major action.

**Attack value.** The total of the values rolled on an attack's dice after rerolls, with any fixed modifier applied once to the total, plus one for a *scaled* weapon and minus one for a *staggered* attacker, to a minimum of zero (see §10.3). For enemies the attack value is the fixed number printed in the attack icon, scaled for the number of characters (see §9.3).

**Back-up slot.** The dashboard slot that holds up to two treasure cards of any kind, carried but not equipped (see §3.2). During an encounter, cards in the back-up slot may be exchanged with the weapon and armour slots only by the *Equip* minor action; an *ember card* must be in the back-up slot to be used.

**Barrel.** A terrain token or model with an intact side and a destroyed side. No model may *move* into a node containing an intact barrel; a model that *dodges* or is pushed into the node destroys the barrel. Barrels are restored when the characters rest at the *bonfire* (see §5.5, Barrels).

**Base health.** The health printed on an enemy's data card, before enemy scaling (see §8.2).

**Basic node.** The most common node type. Basic nodes adjacent to a doorway are also entry nodes.

**Behaviour card.** A card drawn from the behaviour deck of a boss, mimic, invader, or summon at the start of that model's turn. Its icons are resolved from left to right. A boss's current behaviour card sets the range and the dodge difficulty of its attacks and shows its weak arcs until the next card is drawn (see §15.5; §16.1).

**Behaviour deck.** The face-down deck of behaviour cards from which a boss, mimic, invader, or summon draws one card at the start of each of its turns. A boss's behaviour deck is built during setup and built again each time its failed encounter is reset (see §15.5). When the deck is empty at the start of a turn, the discard pile is turned face down without shuffling to form the new deck.

**Behaviour icon.** A movement or attack icon on an enemy data card or a behaviour card. Icons are resolved left to right; an icon that has no valid target, or that cannot be resolved, is skipped (see §9.1).

**Blacksmith Andre.** The smith at the *bonfire* who keeps the *shop*, buying and selling treasure cards for *souls*, and who attaches and removes *upgrade cards* at no cost. He is visited only while the party rests at the bonfire (see §11.4).

**Bleed.** A condition. A model holds at most one bleed token. When a bleed token is to be placed on a model that already holds one, the model instead suffers three damage that block and resist do not reduce, and its bleed token is removed. A bleed token is otherwise removed only when the encounter ends (see §8.5, Bleed).

**Block.** The defence rolled against an attack that deals physical damage. The defence total is the sum of the two highest block dice rolled from all equipped cards, with the fixed modifiers of those cards then applied (see §9.4). For enemies, block is the fixed value subtracted from a physical attack value.

**Bonfire.** The characters' safe haven, represented by the *bonfire* tile, which has no nodes. The Firekeeper and Blacksmith Andre wait there and are visited only while the party rests. Returning to the bonfire tile costs nothing; resting there costs one *spark* (see §11.1, §11.2).

**Bonfire ascetic.** In campaign play, a one-time communal payment of 16 *souls* made while resting at the *bonfire*, the one exception to the rule that souls cannot be pooled. It opens tier two treasure and the tier two shop and increases by two the base health of every enemy subject to enemy scaling for the rest of the campaign, cumulative with the health scaling for the number of characters. Enemy attack values are not changed (see §18.7).

**Boss.** A single powerful enemy whose turns are driven by a behaviour deck and whose base is divided into four arcs. There are three ranks: *mini-boss*, *main-boss*, and *mega-boss*. A boss is not scaled for the number of characters and cannot be pushed by characters (see §15; §16).

**Boss arc.** One of the four sectors (front, left, right, back) into which a boss's base is divided. The arcs extend across the tile: each node lies in at least one arc, and a node on the line between two arcs lies in both (see §15.6).

**Boss spawn node.** The spawn node of a boss's type: the mini-boss spawn node, the main-boss spawn node, or the mega-boss spawn node. The boss model is placed on it at the start of every boss encounter (see §15.3).

**Boss tile.** One of the two tiles on which mini-boss and main-boss encounters are fought. A mini-boss encounter is fought on the mini-boss tile alone; a main-boss encounter is fought on both boss tiles joined as one tile, the main-boss tile. A mega-boss is fought on the mega-boss game board supplied with it (see §15.3).

**Boss treasure.** A treasure card supplied with a boss. One of a boss's treasure cards is awarded at random when the boss is defeated (see §16.5). Boss treasure cannot be bought; it sells for six *souls* and is returned to the game box when sold (see §11.4; for campaign play, see §18.6).

**Calamity.** A condition from the Black Dragon Kalameet expansion. A model holds at most one calamity token, which reduces the total of each of its block, resist, and dodge rolls by one. A calamity token is removed only when the encounter ends (see §8.5, Calamity).

**Campaign.** A game played through a fixed list of *sections*, taken in order, in which the characters keep what they have gained from one section to the next. The book supplies 12 campaigns; campaign play follows the standard game except as its own chapter states (see §18.1, §19).

**Catalyst.** A weapon card that stores up to three spells of a matching school: a pyromancer flame stores pyromancies, a staff or catalyst stores sorceries, and a talisman or chime stores miracles. A stored spell occupies no equipment slot; its attack options are used as if it were equipped, and its block, resist, and dodge dice are ignored (see §4.3).

**Character dashboard.** The board that records a character's class: the heroic action, the four equipment slots, the stat progression track, the endurance bar, the threat value, and the four token slots (see §3.1).

**Chest.** A terrain token or model that blocks movement, dodging, and pushes. A chest holds one face-down treasure card of the current tier, revealed when the encounter is *cleared* and awarded to the party (see §7.7). With mimics in play, opening a chest requires revealing the tile's *ambush card* (see §13).

**Class-specific treasure.** A treasure card marked with a small white class emblem. It is sorted into the tier one or tier two deck by its stat requirements exactly as common treasure is.

**Cleared.** The result of an encounter in which every enemy model is killed and at least one character survives. A *cleared* encounter card is marked with a black cube, keeps the cube through a *reset*, and may thereafter be skipped until the encounter is *failed*. The first clear rewards full *souls* and treasure; every later clear rewards the *reduced reward* and no treasure (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution).

**Common treasure.** A treasure card that is not starting, class-specific, transposed, legendary or boss treasure. Common treasure with any stat requirement of 25 or more is tier two; all other common treasure is tier one.

**Condition.** A lasting effect marked by a condition token on a model: *bleed*, *poison*, *frostbite*, *stagger*, *calamity*, or *corrosion*. A condition token is placed on each model hit by an attack that bears the matching icon, after the attack is resolved. A model holds at most one token of each condition, except poison (see §8.5).

**Corrosion.** A condition from the Gaping Dragon expansion. A model holds at most one corrosion token, which reduces the total of each of its block rolls by one. The token is removed when the affected model next suffers damage (see §8.5, Corrosion).

**Crystal lizard.** An optional enemy from the Iron Keep expansion, placed on a terrain node that the encounter card marks with the crystal lizard symbol. It flees rather than fights, is not scaled, does not count towards clearing the encounter, and escapes without award when the encounter is *cleared*. The character who kills one takes a Titanite Scale card, and once one has been killed in an encounter none is placed there again (see §14).

**Damage cube.** A red cube. Each point of damage a character suffers places one damage cube on the endurance bar, filling from the right. A character who must place a damage cube and has no space dies.

**Data card.** The card that defines an enemy, boss, mimic, or phantom: name, threat, range, dodge difficulty, block and resist, behaviour icons or behaviour deck size, health, and special ability (see §8.2 for enemies; §15.4 for bosses).

**Defence total.** The sum of the two highest block or resist dice rolled, to which the fixed modifiers of the equipped cards are then applied; a single die counts alone, no dice count as zero, and a total of less than zero is zero. Damage suffered equals the attack value minus the defence total, to a minimum of zero (see §9.4).

**Distract.** An icon found only on summon behaviour cards. During the boss's next turn the summon is treated as holding the aggro token; the token itself is not moved (see §12.1).

**Dodge.** A defence against an attack that targets the character. The character pays one stamina, may pay two further stamina for one extra green die to a maximum of four dice, and succeeds if the dodge icons rolled equal or exceed the attack's dodge difficulty. A successful dodge avoids the hit and the character must move to an adjacent valid node; after a failed dodge only the armour card's block or resist dice are rolled (see §9.5).

**Dodge difficulty.** The number of dodge icons a dodge or parry roll must equal or exceed. It is printed on an enemy data card, a boss behaviour card, or a trap token (see §9.5). In player versus player it is derived from the stamina cost of the attacker's attack option.

**Doorway.** A gap in a tile's wall through which characters pass between tiles. Basic nodes beside a doorway are entry nodes.

**Drink Estus (major action).** The major action in which a character flips the *estus flask* token to its used side and removes every damage cube from their endurance bar. It may be taken only while the token shows its ready side (see §3.5, Estus Flask; §10.1).

**Dropped souls.** *Souls* placed on an encounter tile by a character who died there or, when an encounter was *failed*, by a living character. Only the character who dropped them may reclaim them, by surviving to clear that encounter. They are returned to the supply if that character dies first (see §7.6).

**Ember.** An *ember card* consumed by a character as the *Ember* major action, or the state of being *embered* that results (see §4.4, §7.3).

**Ember card.** A tier one shop card. Discarding an *ember* card from the back-up slot (the *Ember* major action, or outside an encounter) makes the character *embered* (see §7.3). A character may carry only one *ember* card and cannot carry one while *embered*; a card held when the character becomes *embered* passes to another character (see §7.3).

**Ember token.** The token that marks an *embered* character. It begins the game beside the dashboard, is placed in the dashboard's ember slot when the character becomes embered, and is removed when the character dies (see §3.5, Ember; §7.3).

**Embered.** The state of a character with an *ember token*. An embered character is not *hollow*, reduces by one the damage of any attack that deals three or more, may call a *summon* before a boss encounter, and provokes *invasions*. The state ends when the character dies (see §7.3).

**Encounter.** A battle fought on one or more tiles against the enemies set out by an encounter card, or against a boss. An encounter ends *cleared* or *failed* (see §7.5); a PvP encounter ends in neither way (see §20.7).

**Encounter card.** The card dealt face down to each tile that lists the encounter's level, enemies, terrain and traps. It is revealed when the characters enter the tile.

**Encounter level.** The difficulty of an encounter, from 1 to 4. The levels of the encounters leading to a boss are printed on that boss's data card and increase from the tile adjacent to the bonfire tile towards the boss tile (see §6.5). In the standard game Level 4 encounters precede mega-bosses only (see §6.7). In campaign play each section lists the levels of its encounters (see §18.1).

**Endurance bar.** The track of 10 spaces on the dashboard that holds both stamina cubes (filling from the left) and damage cubes (filling from the right). A *hollow* character's two hollow cubes block the two rightmost spaces, leaving eight (see §3.4).

**Enemy scaling.** The adjustment of every enemy to the number of characters fixed at setup: more health with two or more characters, movement values of 1 resolved as 2 with two or more characters, and +1 attack value with four characters. Bosses, mimics, phantoms, and crystal lizards are never scaled (see §8.3).

**Entry node.** A basic node beside the doorway through which the characters entered the tile. Characters are placed on entry nodes at the start of an encounter, no more than three to a node. In a Level 4 encounter all four nodes along the wall beside the doorway are entry nodes (see §6.7).

**Equip.** A minor action, once per turn, in which a character exchanges any number of cards among the back-up slot, the two weapon slots, and the armour slot. It is the only way to change equipped cards during an encounter; the inventory cannot be accessed (see §10.1).

**Equipment card.** A treasure card that a character equips in a weapon or armour slot, stores in a catalyst, or attaches to an equipped card: weapon, shield, catalyst, spell, armour, and upgrade cards (see §4.1).

**Equipped.** The state of a card in the armour slot or a weapon slot of the dashboard. Cards in the back-up slot and the inventory are not equipped (see §3.2).

**Estus flask.** A character token. Flipping it as the Drink Estus major action removes all damage cubes from the endurance bar; stamina cubes, hollow cubes, and condition tokens are not removed. It is readied only by resting at the *bonfire* (see §3.5, Estus Flask).

**Facing.** The direction in which the centre of a boss's front arc points. A boss faces directly towards an adjacent node and changes its facing only when a movement icon or a turn icon turns it (see §15.6).

**Failed.** The result of an encounter in which every character dies, or in which any *hollow* character dies. A *failed* encounter awards nothing; every living character drops their *souls* on the tile, its card is turned face down and its black cube removed, and the characters must rest at the *bonfire* (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution).

**Final boss.** The last boss of the game: the main-boss, or the mega-boss if the players chose to add one. Defeating the final boss wins the game (see §7.1). In campaign play the final boss is the last boss listed in the campaign's last section (see §18.1).

**Firekeeper.** The figure at the *bonfire* who raises a character's stats, one *stat tier* per purchase, in exchange for that character's *souls*. Stat tiers one to four cost 2, 4, 8, and 10 souls (see §11.3; for campaign play, see §18.6).

**Fog gate.** The token that marks the doorway of a boss encounter. A boss encounter begins when the characters pass through it. The first time they pass through, the token is moved to the *bonfire* tile, where it is a shortcut to that boss until the boss is defeated (see §15.1). In campaign play it is aesthetic only (see §18.2).

**Frostbite.** A condition. A character with a frostbite token cannot recover stamina and cannot take the *Rest* major action; any other model with a frostbite token reduces each of its movement values by one. The token is removed at the end of the model's next complete turn (see §8.5, Frostbite).

**Gravestone.** A terrain token or model. No model may *move*, *dodge*, or be pushed into a node containing a gravestone. When an encounter that contains a gravestone is *cleared*, the players may *scry* one card (see §5.5, Gravestones; §7.5, Encounter Resolution).

**Health dial.** The dial that records the health of a boss, summon, invader, or mimic. Enemies record damage with wound tokens instead (see §15.4).

**Heat-up.** The event in which a boss, mimic, or invader becomes more dangerous. When a boss's health is reduced to or below its heat-up point, one heat-up card taken at random is shuffled into the cards that remain in its behaviour deck (see §15.5); a mimic or an invader adds one of its unused behaviour cards instead. Some bosses heat up differently (see §17).

**Heat-up card.** A behaviour card marked with the heat-up symbol. Heat-up cards are set aside when the behaviour deck is built, and one of them joins the deck when the boss heats up (see §15.5).

**Heat-up point.** The health value, printed on a boss's data card, at or below which the boss heats up (see §15.5).

**Heroic action.** The once-per-rest ability printed on the dashboard, resolved as a minor action by flipping the *heroic action* token to its used side. Its printed wording overrides other rules. The token is readied only by resting at the *bonfire* (see §3.5, Heroic Action).

**Hit.** A target is hit by an attack when it is within the attack's range and does not avoid the attack by a successful *dodge* or *parry*, even if the attack deals no damage. Pushes and conditions carried by an attack apply only to models that the attack hits (see §8.1).

**Hollow.** The state of a character revived after dying. Two *hollow cubes* block the two rightmost spaces of the endurance bar, leaving eight. Hollow is removed only by resting at the *bonfire* or by becoming *embered*. A hollow character who dies is *lost*, and the encounter is *failed* (see §7.2).

**Hollow cube.** One of eight cubes of a colour distinct from the damage, stamina, and stat cubes. Two hollow cubes block the two rightmost spaces of a *hollow* character's endurance bar (see §7.2).

**Invader.** A hostile phantom brought into one encounter by an *invasion*. An invader acts after each character's turn using its own behaviour deck, is not scaled, counts towards clearing the encounter while it is on the tile, vanishes without award if a character dies, and awards *souls* and its treasure card when killed (see §12.2).

**Invader token.** A token from the Phantoms expansion that shows an invader or is blank. When an *invasion* is provoked, one token is placed face down on each face-down encounter card, one of them showing an invader; a token is revealed when the characters fight that encounter (see §12.2).

**Invasion.** The placing of invader tokens that brings one invader into the game. An invasion is provoked when the encounter cards are dealt while a character is *embered*, or when a character becomes embered by discarding an *ember card* while a face-down encounter card lies on a tile; no more than one is provoked before the mini-boss is defeated and no more than one after it (see §12.2).

**Inventory.** A character's unlimited stack of unequipped treasure cards, kept face down beside the dashboard. Outside an encounter it may be rearranged freely and cards may be traded one for one between inventories; during an encounter it cannot be accessed (see §3.2).

**Leap.** A boss movement marked with the leap icon. The boss is removed from its node and placed on the node of its target, at any distance, without changing its facing. Every character on that node is pushed to a valid adjacent node chosen by that character's player, and the push is not limited by arcs (see §16.2).

**Legendary treasure.** A rare treasure card marked with an orange light under a chest. It cannot be purchased; five are shuffled into the tier two deck when the tier two shop is first stocked, and one drawn while stocking a shop is shuffled back (see §6.2, §6.3). In campaign play legendary treasure enters neither treasure deck, is obtained only by *transposition*, and sells for four *souls* (see §18.6, §18.8).

**Level 4 encounter.** The single encounter fought on the Level 4 encounter side of a *mega-boss game board* after the main-boss is defeated and before the mega-boss encounter. All four nodes along the wall beside the doorway are entry nodes; once *cleared* it is not fought again and does not reset (see §6.7). In campaign play a Level 4 encounter is fought where its section lists it, on a mega-boss game board placed in the section's route (see §18.1).

**Lost.** The state of a *hollow* character who has died (see §7.2). A *lost* character is removed from play, the encounter is *failed* at once, and the character is revived only when the characters rest at the *bonfire*.

**Luck.** A character token. Flipping it allows the character to reroll any or all dice of one of their own attack or defence rolls; the dice are chosen together and the new results stand. It is readied only by resting at the *bonfire* (see §3.5, Luck).

**Magical damage.** Damage from an attack marked with the white circle icon. Magical damage is reduced by resist.

**Main-boss.** The second boss of the standard game, fought on both boss tiles joined as one after the mini-boss is defeated. Its defeat wins the standard game unless a mega-boss is in play (see §6.6; §15.3).

**Major action.** The one principal action of a character's turn: *Attack*, *Rest*, *Drink Estus*, or *Ember*. A character may take one major action per turn (see §10.1).

**Mega-boss.** An optional final boss from a mega-boss expansion, fought on its own game board after a Level 4 encounter. Death and hollowing in a mega-boss encounter follow the usual rules (see §7.2). In campaign play a mega-boss is faced only where the campaign lists one, and a mega-boss that is not the campaign's final boss is fought under the same rules as any other boss (see §18.1).

**Mega-boss game board.** The double-sided board supplied with a mega-boss expansion. Its Level 4 encounter side is the tile of the Level 4 encounter and its mega-boss side is the tile of the mega-boss encounter (see §6.7, §15.3).

**Mega-boss setup card.** The card of the Executioner's Chariot expansion that shows the enemy spawn nodes on which the Black Hollow Mages and the Falchion Skeletons are placed at the start of the mega-boss encounter (see §17.9).

**Mimic.** An optional enemy from the Explorers expansion that masquerades as a chest. When the party opens a chest whose *ambush card* shows Mimic, the Hungry Mimic (before the mini-boss is defeated) or the Voracious Mimic (afterwards) is fought in a *mimic encounter*. Killing it awards two treasure cards in place of the chest's one (see §13).

**Mimic encounter.** The encounter fought against a *mimic* on the tile of a *cleared* encounter whose chest the party has opened. It is fought as a mini-boss encounter without arcs, with the characters on the nodes they occupy and before any recovery or reward (see §13.2).

**Mini-boss.** The first boss of the standard game, fought on the mini-boss tile (see §6.5; §15.3).

**Minor action.** An action a character may take during their turn before the major action, after it, or both: *Walk*, *Equip*, and *Heroic Action* once each per turn, and *Run* any number of times (see §10.1).

**Move.** A change of node made by a model through the *Walk* or *Run* minor action, a *shift* icon, or an enemy or boss movement icon. The movement after a successful *dodge* and the movement of a *push* are not moves (see §5.2).

**Nearest character.** The living character at the shortest range from an enemy. If two or more characters are tied, the nearest character is the one who holds the aggro token, otherwise the one with the highest threat, and otherwise the one the players choose (see §9.2).

**Node.** A circular space on a tile. Every model occupies exactly one node. Nodes are basic, spawn or terrain.

**Node attack.** An enemy attack that bears the node icon. It targets every character on the node of the character named by its skull icon or nearest-character icon; each character defends separately, and a node attack cannot be parried (see §9.3).

**Node model limit.** At most three non-boss models and one boss model may occupy a node. A model entering a full node forces one occupant to be pushed to a valid node chosen by the players (see §5.3).

**Parry.** A defence available to a character with a shield equipped in the left weapon slot when the attacker is on the character's node; an attack that pushes, a node attack, and a boss attack outside §15.6 cannot be parried. The character pays one stamina and rolls dodge dice against the attack's dodge difficulty. Success makes the attack miss, staggers the attacker, and allows a *riposte*; failure deals the full attack value with no block or resist roll and staggers the character (see §9.6).

**Phantom.** A *summon* or an *invader*: a model of the Phantoms expansion with a data card and behaviour cards. A summon is friendly and an invader hostile; a phantom is neither a character nor a boss (see §12).

**Physical damage.** Damage from an attack marked with the crossed swords icon. Physical damage is reduced by block.

**Pillar node.** One of the four terrain nodes printed on the mega-boss side of the Asylum Demon expansion's *mega-boss game board*, on each of which a *pillar token* is placed at the start of a boss encounter fought there (see §17.3).

**Pillar token.** A terrain token placed on each of the four pillar nodes of the Asylum Demon mega-boss game board. No model may *move*, *dodge*, or be pushed into its node until the pillar is destroyed (see §5.5, Pillars; §17.3).

**Poison.** A condition. A model may hold any number of poison tokens. At the end of each of its turns a model suffers one damage for each poison token it holds, which block and resist do not reduce, and then removes all of its poison tokens (see §8.5, Poison).

**Push.** Forced movement of a model from its node to an adjacent valid node. A push is caused by a push-attack, by a push-move, by the node model limit, or by a boss model moving onto the node of another boss model. A push is not a move and costs no stamina; a gravestone, a chest, and a pillar token block it, and a push with no valid node does not happen (see §8.4).

**Push-attack.** An attack that bears the push icon. A model it hits is pushed directly away from the attacker, whether or not the attack dealt damage (see §8.4).

**Push-move.** An enemy or boss movement that bears the push icon. Characters on the enemy's node are pushed aside before it moves, and characters on each node it enters are pushed directly away from the node it left; a push icon that carries a damage value also makes each entry a physical attack (see §8.4).

**PvP encounter.** An optional contest fought between the characters alone, without enemies, on the tile on which a boss has been defeated, after each boss's defeat and before the rest that follows it. It has no enemy turns and no aggro token, is neither *cleared* nor *failed*, awards no *souls* and no treasure, and death in it costs nothing (see §20.1).

**Range.** The maximum distance between an attacker and its target, counted in nodes along the shortest chain of adjacent nodes. Range 0 is the same node; range 1 is an adjacent node; range ∞ is unlimited. Range is never blocked by terrain or models (see §5.4).

**Reduced reward.** Half the full soul reward, rounded down, gained by every character when an encounter that has been cleared before is cleared again (see §7.6).

**Relevant stat.** The stat whose requirement is the highest of the four stat requirements printed on a weapon card; if two or more requirements are tied for the highest, the character chooses one of the tied stats. A weapon is *scaled* by comparing the character's relevant stat with that requirement (see §10.5).

**Repeat.** An icon that causes an entire attack option or behaviour card to be resolved the number of times shown. For a character's attack option the stamina cost is paid once and each resolution is a separate attack roll with its own target (see §10.3, Repeat).

**Reset.** The state of an encounter after the characters have rested at the *bonfire*: its encounter card is face down, its enemy models have been removed, and its terrain tokens remain in place. A *cleared* encounter keeps its black cube through a reset; a *failed* encounter has none (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution; §11.5).

**Resist.** The defence rolled against an attack that deals magical damage, resolved as block is with resist dice (see §9.4). For enemies, resist is the fixed value subtracted from a magical attack value.

**Rest (major action).** The character removes every stamina cube from their endurance bar; damage cubes, hollow cubes, and condition tokens stay. A character cannot *Rest* in a turn in which they have *run*, cannot *run* after resting in the same turn, and cannot *Rest* while holding a *frostbite* token (see §10.1).

**Resting at the bonfire.** The party action, costing one *spark* unless it follows the defeat of a boss (in campaign play, unless it follows the completion of a *section*; see §18.3), that revives dead and *lost* characters, removes every damage cube, stamina cube, hollow cube, and condition token, readies the heroic action, luck, and estus flask tokens, allows visits to the Firekeeper and Blacksmith Andre, and resets every encounter. It neither places nor removes an *ember token* (see §11.2).

**Revised card.** A card listed in Appendix B that takes the place of the printed card of the same name. Where a revised card differs from the printed text, the text given in Appendix B governs (see Appendix B).

**Riposte.** The attack a character may make at once against the attacker after a successful *parry*, using one attack option of the weapon in the right weapon slot and paying its stamina cost. A riposte cannot be parried, and it does not count against the attacks of the character's own turn (see §9.6).

**Round.** One pass of the encounter turn order: every enemy model takes a turn in descending threat order, then every character takes a turn in descending threat order (see §7.5, Encounter Turn Order).

**Run.** A minor action: the character spends one stamina and moves one node. It may be taken any number of times per turn. A character who has *run* cannot *Rest* in that turn (see §10.2).

**Scaled health.** An enemy's base health plus the increase that enemy scaling gives for the number of characters. An enemy is killed when the total value of its wound tokens equals or exceeds its scaled health (see §8.2).

**Scaled (weapon).** A weapon equipped by a character whose *relevant stat* exceeds the weapon's highest printed stat requirement by five or more. It is marked with a red cube and each of its attack options adds one to its attack value. Spells are never scaled (see §10.5).

**Scry.** To reveal one hidden card (the top card of a treasure deck, a face-down encounter card, or the top card of the next boss's behaviour deck) and either return it or send it away (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution).

**Section.** In campaign play, one list of tiles: a *bonfire* tile, a number of encounter tiles with their encounter levels, and the bosses to be faced. A section is completed when the last boss it lists is defeated or, for a section whose list ends in an encounter, when that encounter is *cleared*. The shop is restocked and a rest that costs no *spark* is granted only when a section is completed (see §18.1).

**Shaft.** A weapon icon. An attack option with the shaft icon cannot target a model on the attacker's own node (see §10.3, Shaft).

**Shield.** A one-handed weapon card that shows block or resist dice and has no attack option. A shield is a weapon for equipment slots and for two-handed attacks, and a shield for stocking the shop and for parrying (see §4.1).

**Shift.** An icon found on attack options and on summon behaviour cards. A character whose attack option bears it may move up to the number of nodes shown, before the target is chosen if the icon precedes the dice and after the attack's damage and effects if it follows them. A *shift* is a *move*, costs no stamina, and does not use the *Walk* or *Run* minor action (see §10.3, Shift; §12.1).

**Shop.** Blacksmith Andre's stock of face-up treasure cards, in a tier one shop and a tier two shop. A tier one card costs two *souls* and a tier two card four; a treasure card other than starting equipment sells for one soul (see §11.4). The tier one shop is stocked at setup and the tier two shop when the mini-boss is defeated; both are restocked only after a boss is defeated (in campaign play, after a section is completed), unsold cards going to the bottom of their deck first (see §6.3).

**Signature card.** One of Black Dragon Kalameet's two behaviour cards, Mark of Calamity and Hellfire Blast, that are included in every behaviour deck built for Kalameet (see §17.8).

**Souls.** The currency of the game, tracked by soul tokens on each character's dashboard. *Souls* are gained by *clearing* encounters, defeating bosses, and killing invaders, and are dropped on death. They are spent while resting at the *bonfire* and, as the one exception, to call a *summon* at the fog gate (see §7.6, §12.1). *Souls* cannot be transferred between characters. A character adventuring alone begins with six *souls* (see §6.1).

**Spark.** The *bonfire*'s charges, tracked on the spark dial. The party begins with six minus the number of characters, gains one for each boss defeated, and spends one each time the characters rest at the *bonfire* (see §7.4). If the characters are required to rest while the dial shows zero, the players lose the game (see §7.1). In campaign play the dial's maximum is one more than its starting value and a *spark* may be bought back for *souls* (see §18.3).

**Spawn node.** A node on which enemy models are placed at the start of an encounter. Boss tiles have a boss spawn node.

**Spell.** An equipment card or upgrade card whose art shows a scroll: a pyromancy, a sorcery, or a miracle. A character may use at most one spell attack option per turn, and spells are never scaled or two-handed (see §4.3).

**Stagger.** A condition. A staggered character subtracts one from the total of each attack, block, resist, and dodge roll; any other staggered model reduces its attack values, its block and resist values, and its dodge difficulty by one. The token is removed at the end of the model's next complete turn. Stagger is placed by attacks that bear the stagger icon, by stamina overflow, and by a parry (see §8.5, Stagger; §9.6; §10.1).

**Stamina cube.** A black cube. Each point of stamina a character spends places one stamina cube on the endurance bar, filling from the left.

**Starting equipment.** The three or four equipment cards a character begins with, marked with that character's class icon (see §6.2). Starting equipment cannot be sold (see §11.4).

**Stat cube.** A white cube that marks a character's *stat tier* on a stat progression track (see §3.3).

**Stat requirement.** One of the four values (STR, DEX, INT, FTH) printed on an equipment card or upgrade card; for a weapon listed in Appendix A, the values given there replace the printed values (see Appendix A). A character may equip a card only if each of their stats equals or exceeds the matching requirement of the card and of every upgrade attached to it (see §4.1).

**Stat tier.** A character's position on each stat progression track: the base tier, or stat tier one, two, three, or four. It is marked with a *stat cube* or, at stat tier four, with a red cube in the stat tier three space. A stat at stat tier four has the value 40 or the stat tier three value plus five, whichever is higher. Tiers are bought in sequence from the Firekeeper (see §3.3, §11.3).

**Strafe card.** A card from the Black Dragon Kalameet expansion revealed when a strafe icon is resolved. It shows the nodes whose characters the strafe attack targets and the landing node on which Kalameet's model is then placed; a character on the landing node is pushed to an adjacent node (see §17.8).

**Summon.** A friendly phantom called before a boss encounter. Each *embered* character may pay two *souls* to draw one summon data card at random, and the players choose one of the drawn summons. The summon acts after each character's turn, defends with the dice on its data card, and is removed when its health dial is reduced to zero or the encounter ends (see §12.1).

**Target.** The model at which an attack is directed. An enemy never targets another enemy; a character targets another character only in player versus player (see §8.1).

**Terrain.** Gravestones, barrels, chests, and pillars, placed on terrain nodes, and trap tokens, dealt to basic nodes. Terrain stays on its nodes when encounters are reset (see §5.5).

**Terrain node.** A node on which a terrain token or model is placed at the start of an encounter, as the encounter card directs. A terrain node that holds no terrain is used as a basic node (see §5.1).

**Threat.** The value that fixes turn order, printed on the character dashboard as its Taunt Level and on enemy, boss, and phantom data cards. Enemies act in descending threat before the characters, who then act in descending threat. The character with the highest threat takes the aggro token at the start of each encounter; the players settle ties (see §3.1; §7.5, Encounter Turn Order).

**Tier one treasure.** The treasure deck that holds common and class-specific treasure with every stat requirement below 25. Encounter rewards and chests draw from tier one until the mini-boss is defeated (in campaign play, until the *bonfire ascetic* is burnt) (see §6.2).

**Tier two treasure.** The treasure deck that holds every treasure card with a stat requirement of 25 or more, the transposed treasure and the five legendary cards. Encounter rewards and chests draw from tier two once the mini-boss is defeated (in campaign play, once the *bonfire ascetic* is burnt) (see §6.2).

**Titanite.** A weapon upgrade (Titanite Shard, Titanite Chunk, Titanite Slab, or Titanite Scale) that is fused permanently to the weapon it is attached to. A Titanite Chunk is attached only in place of an attached Titanite Shard, and a Titanite Slab only in place of an attached Titanite Chunk; the replaced card returns to the bottom of the treasure deck of its tier (see Appendix B). When that weapon is sold, the Titanite is returned with it to the bottom of the treasure deck of the weapon's tier (see §4.2).

**Track behaviour card.** One of the four behaviour cards of the Executioner's Chariot expansion, named Death Race and numbered 1 to 4, that direct the Chariot's turns in fixed order until it heats up. Each shows the node to which the Chariot moves and the nodes that its attack targets (see §17.9).

**Transposed treasure.** A class treasure card marked with a blue light. All transposed treasure is tier two.

**Transposition.** In campaign play, exchanging one boss treasure card and four *souls* for one of the five face-up legendary treasure cards while resting at the *bonfire*. It is available after the last boss of the first section is defeated, and the boss treasure card is returned to the game box (see §18.8).

**Trap token.** A token, blank or showing a dodge difficulty and a damage value, dealt face down to the basic nodes of a trapped encounter. It is turned face up when a character enters its node by a move, a dodge, or a push, and each character there must suffer the damage or *dodge* it; enemies neither trigger traps nor suffer them (see §5.5, Traps). In a *PvP encounter* one trap token is placed at the end of each character's turn, and a character who fails to dodge it dies (see §20.6).

**Treasure card.** Any card from a treasure deck or from Blacksmith Andre's shop: *starting equipment*, *common treasure*, *class-specific treasure*, *legendary treasure*, *boss treasure*, or an *ember card*. A treasure card that may be equipped is an *equipment card* (see §2.5; §4.1).

**Turn.** One model's opportunity to act. In each round the enemies take their turns, then the characters take theirs in descending threat order (see §7.5, Encounter Turn Order; §10.1). In a boss encounter the boss takes a turn before each character's turn, and the players choose the order of the characters (see §16.1).

**Twin Gargoyles.** The boss encounter set by a campaign *section* that lists the Gargoyle twice in succession: one mini-boss encounter in two parts, fought against one Gargoyle and then at once against a second, with no rest or reward between them. The rewards of a defeated boss follow only when the second Gargoyle is defeated, and a failure against either Gargoyle means both are fought again (see §17.1, Twin Gargoyles).

**Two-handed.** A weapon bearing the two-handed icon, or a one-handed weapon equipped while the other weapon slot holds no card; a shield in the other weapon slot is equipment in the other hand. A weapon wielded two-handed may reroll one die of each attack roll. Spells are never two-handed (see §10.4).

**Ultimate victor.** The character who wins the *PvP encounter* that follows the defeat of the final boss (see §20.8).

**Upgrade card.** A card that enhances a weapon or armour card. Blacksmith Andre attaches it beneath the card, up to the card's upgrade slots, and the character must meet its stat requirements to equip the card. Upgrade spells are instead attached as the *Attack* major action during an encounter and return to the inventory afterwards (see §4.2).

**Upgrade slot.** A space shown by the upgrade slots icon on a weapon or armour card; a card has zero, one, or two. Each attached upgrade card other than an upgrade spell occupies one slot (see §4.2).

**Upgrade spell.** An upgrade card whose art shows a scroll. It is attached to a weapon or armour card equipped to the same character as the *Attack* major action during an encounter, occupies no upgrade slot, and returns to the inventory when the encounter ends (see §4.2).

**Valid node.** A node that a model may enter by a given form of movement: an adjacent node not separated from the model's node by a wall and holding no terrain that blocks that form of entry. A node at its node model limit is a valid node (see §5.2).

**Victory bonus.** The reward for winning a *PvP encounter* that follows the defeat of a boss other than the final boss, marked by one wound token of value 1 placed beside the winner's dashboard. Each victory bonus adds one to the attack value of each of the character's attacks in every subsequent PvP encounter; victory bonuses are cumulative and are kept until the game ends (see §20.8).

**Walk.** A minor action, once per turn: the character moves one node without spending stamina (see §10.2).

**Weak arc.** A boss arc shown in red on the arc diagram of the boss's current behaviour card, the card it drew most recently. A character who attacks a boss from a weak arc adds one black die to each attack roll, however many weak arcs the character is in (see §15.6).

**Weapon.** An equipment card that bears the one-handed or two-handed weapon icon and whose art does not show a scroll. Shields, catalysts, bows, crossbows, and throwable weapons are weapons (see §4.1).

**Weapon slot.** One of the two dashboard slots, the left weapon slot and the right weapon slot, that each hold one equipped weapon card. A two-handed weapon requires the other weapon slot to be empty (see §3.2).

**Wind Up.** A behaviour icon printed on behaviour cards of the Asylum Demon and the Stray Demon. When it is resolved, each character may spend one stamina to move to an adjacent node before the next icon is resolved; the movement counts as a *Run* (see §17.3).

**Wound token.** A token of value 1, 3, or 5 that records the damage suffered by an enemy. Wound tokens with a total value equal to the damage are placed on the enemy model's base, and an enemy whose wound tokens equal or exceed its scaled health is killed and removed from the tile (see §8.2). Bosses, phantoms, and mimics use a health dial instead.
