#  INTRODUCTION

Dark Souls the Board Game Second Edition (DStBG 2E) is a cooperative dungeon crawl board game for one to four players. Each player will control one character of a party of one to four characters. The character’s goal is to defeat a mini-boss followed by a main-boss which are selected by the players before starting their adventure.

To succeed in their quest the characters must: learn their enemies behaviours, train their skills, seek out powerful weapons, and work together to further their progress through increasingly treacherous encounters.

When characters die, they lose part of their humanity. After death they arise weaker and *hollow*. The *hollow* can find their way back from the darkness by either using *sparks* at a *bonfire* or consuming fragments of humanity stored in *embers*.

Nameless accursed undead, seek strength, and prepare to die.

# SECOND EDITION NOTES

Dark Souls the Board Game was developed and published by Steam Forged Games (SFG) in 2017. The original Kickstarter was a great success and SFG had outlined an extensive release schedule. Unfortunately, likely due to the Kickstarter sales model, the game shipped in many small expansions leaving the overall rules fragmented. While the production value was high, the original rules had many glaring issues. The main issues were player downtime, long playtime due to grinding, unbalanced defensive options, and an unthematic store which sold players items they never wanted. These issues and others resulted in a gameplay experience that fell painfully short of its apparent potential. The core elements were inspired and the community rose to the challenge in creating not only better rulesets but entire expansion sets worth of original content. SFG had seen the community’s innovation and promised their own updated rule set that would be compatible with the original core set and expansions. Unfortunately, SFG had lied and issued two new core sets with partially reworked rules which were not easily compatible with any previous content. Worse, the new rules essentially worsen the original rules issues that had already been better addressed by the community. Shopping remained random, defensive options were eliminated, out-of-turn actions worsened game-flow, excess stamina decreased tactics, and the new encounter system added new ideas without fixing old issues.

This rule set is designed to be an answer to the community's request for a proper second edition of rules. The goal is to collect the original rules from all expansions, integrate the most commonly agreed upon fixes, and provide a comprehensive ruleset that stands on its own. Ultimately, I would love to produce a professional quality rulebook that new players could use to learn the second edition of the game without requiring referencing other texts.

This project started by cutting out any more radical additions that were present in my own playtested ruleset. Next, I attempted to integrate the most common rule changes present in publically available community and SFG rulesets. Now, I am extensively playtesting these new unified rules for balance. As such, it should be considered a work in progress. Finally, I would request the community to please review these rules for any sort of improvement that could be made. Once playtesting and community review is complete the rules can be finalised (layout, font, diagrams, art, etc). Until then, the rules will desist in google docs to facilitate public review. Please don’t think this is the final layout as obviously that will require much work after the rules texts are finalised. Please comment on any errors you find or issues with proposed rules. I would love to have this become the go-to ruleset that serves the most players. Thank you for reading this far.

For editors reading through this, please note that this rule-book will have many diagrams and pictures as well. But I have yet to decide if I should just rip the old ones from the original rule book or generate my own. I don’t think copyright would be an issue given this is a free fan work that will be released digitally.

Please note game terms that are written in *italics* will have quick reference definitions in a glossary at the end of the rule book.

# CHARACTERS

## Character Dashboards

Each character is represented by a unique model with their relevant information tracked by a dashboard sharing the same name.

The character dashboard for each character includes all the information specific to that character’s class:

1.  Name

2.  Illustration

3.  *Heroic Action*

4.  Equipment Slots

5.  Endurance Bar

6.  Stat Progression

7.  *Threat* Level

8.  *Heroic action* token slot

9.  *Luck* token slot

10. *Estus flask* token slot

11. *Ember* token slot

## Equipment Slots

Character dashboards have four equipment slots each. Each equipment slot may contain card(s) of the appropriate equipment type as described.

A single armour card, as noted by the armour icon, may be equipped to the armour slot.

Weapon cards, as noted by either the one or two-handed weapon symbol, may only be equipped to the weapon slots. If a two-handed weapon is equipped to a weapon slot, the other weapon slot must remain empty. If a one-handed weapon card is equipped with a weapon slot, the other weapon slot may contain another one-handed weapon card. Only a single weapon card may be equipped to each weapon slot at any given time.

The back-up slot may contain two treasure cards of any type and represent readily available equipment, which may be accessed during encounter, but is not considered equipped. Only equipment cards assigned to the armour or weapon slots are considered equipped.

Finally, characters may have an unlimited number of equipment cards in their inventory, which may be represented as a stack of face-down cards next to their character dashboard. Characters may freely exchange equipment cards between their character dashboard and the character inventory at any time except during an encounter. Once the encounter has started players should not interact with their character’s inventory cards. Players may exchange equipment cards one-to-one between character inventories, so long as both players agree on the trade, and the characters are not in an encounter. Characters may not exchange equipment cards in any way during an encounter.

## Stat Progression Track

The stat progression track indicates the character’s current strength (STR), dexterity (DEX), intelligence (INT), and faith (FTH) values. Each character has a unique array of stats which represent their unique progression of skills.

All characters begin with their base tier stats as indicated by four white cubes in the base tier space of each track. The characters may upgrade their stats while resting at The Bonfire by paying the appropriate amount of *souls* and moving the white cube one space to the right. Characters may only ever upgrade their stats while resting at The Bonfire and each ascending upgrade must be paid for in sequence (i.e., to upgrade strength from base tier to tier two, one must pay the cost in *souls* for the tier one upgrade, and then the tier two upgrade).

## Endurance Bar

A character’s stamina bar tracks their stamina and damage. The endurance bar begins empty, but is filled with black stamina and red damage cubes during play to represent the impact of battle. The endurance bar can be filled completely with black stamina cubes, red damage cubes, or any combination of the two. Black stamina cubes are added sequentially, starting at the left-most space. Red damage cubes are added sequentially, starting at the right-most space.

When a character spends one or more stamina, they add that number of stamina cubes to the endurance bar starting from the furthest left available space.

When a character suffers one or more damage, they add that number of damage cubes to the endurance bar starting at the furthest right available space.

When a character dies and is revived *hollow*, they block off the two furthest right spaces of the endurance bar with a marker such as white cubes. The effect of these *hollow* markers is to reduce the functional capacity of the endurance bar from a total of eight stamina/damage cubes to only six.

If a character recovers stamina, they remove the corresponding number of stamina cubes from the endurance bar starting with the black stamina cubes furthest to the right.

If a character recovers damage, also referred to as healing, they remove the corresponding number of damage cubes from the endurance bar starting with the red damage cubes furthest left.

If a character is ever forced to place a stamina cube on their endurance bar, but there is no space to do so, the character is instead marked stagger*ed* with the appropriate condition marker. A character may never elect to spend excess stamina at the cost of receiving a stagger*ed* condition marker.

If a character is ever forced to place a damage cube on their endurance bar, but there’s no space to do so, the character dies.

## Character Tokens

There are three different types of character tokens: *heroic actions, luck, and estus flask.* Each character token has a read and a used side. The ready side is coloured in brighter colours than the used side. The tokens effect is only available to the character while the tokens ready side is face up. The token must be flipped face-down when the character uses the related effect. Each character has one of each of the four following tokens on their character dashboard at any given time. The characters begin the game with the *heroic action*, *estus flask*, and *luck* tokens face up. The *ember* tokens are treated differently, and should be placed in the condition token supply at the start of the game..

### *Heroic Action*

A character may flip their heroic action token to the used side as a minor action to resolve the heroic action as listed on their dashboard. The specific wording of the heroic action overrides any other rules.

### *Luck*

A character may flip their luck token to the used side to reroll any attack or defence roll. Any or all of the dice may be rerolled all at once, but the results of the reroll must be kept.

### *Ember*

Ember tokens are placed face-up onto a character's dashboard when that player discards an ember card from their back-up equipment slot. An *ember* card may be discarded this way both in and out of encounters. Ember represents a character’s return to humanity and allows the character to discard any *hollow* tokens from the character dashboard. If a character with an *ember* token suffers three or more damage from a single attack, they may reduce the damage by one. If a character with an *ember* token dies, discard the token. Finally, players should be aware that *ember* tokens enable the character to summon phantoms, but also attract malicious invaders.

# EQUIPMENT

## Equipment Cards

Equipment cards include weapons, spells, shields, armour, and items which the character equips to improve their combat capabilities. Equipment cards with either a one or two-handed weapon icon are considered weapons unless the card depicts a spell as indicated by a picture of a scroll with an overlying spell icon in the centre of the card. Equipment cards are considered armour if they have the armour icon.

1.  Equipment name.

2.  Equipment type (common, class-specific, transposed, legendary, boss).

3.  Equipment slot (one-handed weapon, two-handed weapon, or armour).

4.  Icon to indicate what core or expansion set the card is from.

5.  Attack range.

6.  Minimum stat requirements to equip (STR, DEX, INT, FTH)

7.  *Attack options*

8.  *Block* value.

9.  *Resist* value.

10. *Dodge* value.

11. Upgrade slots available.

Each equipment card has four listed stat requirements which indicate what stat values on a character's dashboard are required to equip the item.

An equipment card’s actions represent an attack or special effect used during an encounter. Each line on the card represents a separate attack option that can be used as a character's main action. The specifics of using equipment cards offensively is explained in detail later on in the section title “**Character Attacks**”.

An equipment card provides defensive capabilities including block, resist, and dodge. The specifics of using equipment cards defensively is explained in detail later on in the section titled “**Enemy Attacks**”.

An equipment card may have a number of upgrade slots to permit improving the equipment with upgrade cards at Blacksmith Andre which is described later in more detail.

Some equipment cards have dice modifiers that add a fixed value to the die roll or subtract a fixed value from the die roll.

Players should include all equipment cards from expansions available in their games to increase variety.

## Upgrades

Upgrade cards are used to enhance equipment. There are two types of upgrade cards: those marked with a sword which may only be used to upgrade weapons, and those marked with play mail, which may only be used up grade armour. Upgrades can only be added to or removed from equipment by Blacksmith Andre while the characters are resting at The Bonfire. Upgrades are limited to the number of upgrades permitted by a piece of equipment’s upgrade limit. For example, a character could attach up to two weapon upgrade cards to a weapon card with an upgrade limit of two.

Upgrade cards with a picture of a magic scroll are considered spells and are not attached to equipment permanently by Andre. Instead, upgrade spells are attached to a character's equipment as a main action similar to casting an attack spell. Upgrade spells must be equipped to a character’s weapon slot or catalyst to be transferred to a weapon or armour card equipped to the same character. The upgrade spell card is attached to the target equipment for the rest of the encounter but returns to the character’s inventory after the encounter ends.

Aside from Titanite, all weapons and armour upgrade cards are returned to a character's inventory when the equipment card they are attached to is sold. Unlike the gems and relics of other upgrade cards, Titanite upgrade cards are fused permanently into the weapon to which they are attached. Therefore, any upgrade card, including the word Titanate (i.e., Titanite Shard) must be returned to the bottom of the tier one treasure deck along with the weapon it is attached to when sold.

## Spells

### Spells

- Spells are a type of equipment or upgrade card defined by the presence of a scroll as part of the equipment card’s art, usually with a coloured magic effect in the foreground.

- A character is always limited to one spell *attack option* per turn.

- Spells cannot be affected by scaling and do not receive scaling bonuses.

- Spells cannot be two-handed and do not receive two-handed bonuses.

- Spells are considered either Pyromancy, Sorcery, or Miracles.

  - Pyromancies display red or purple magic effects and have both INT and FTH requirements.

  - Sorceries display blue or black magic effects and have only INT requirements.

  - Miracles display yellow or pink magic effects and have only FTH requirements.

<!-- -->

- Spells stored within their respective *catalyst* may use their *attack options* as if equipped.

  - Pyromancer Flames may store three pyromancies

  - Staves and Catalysts may store three sorceries.

  - Talismans and Chimes may store three miracles.

  - Spell cards are considered stored in the *catalyst* card they are attached to.

  - Spells may be freely transferred between a characters’ catalyst and their inventory between encounters.

## Embers

*Ember* cards may only be purchased from the tier one shop and are in limited supply. Ember cards may be stored in a player’s inventory, but must be equipped to the backup slot to be available for use during an encounter. Discarding an *ember* card permits the character to place an *ember* token on their character dashboard with the aforementioned effects.

# TILES & NODES

## The Basics

All combat takes place during encounters which take place on encounter tiles and Boss tiles. All tiles have one or more doorways. Doorways allow characters to travel between tiles.

Every tile except for the *bonfire* tile has a number of circular symbols on it. These symbols, called nodes, are used for setup and movement during encounters and take the place of game board spaces used in other board games. Each node is one of three types: basic, spawn, or terrain.

Basic nodes are the most common type of node. All basic nodes that are adjacent to doorways are entry nodes and basic nodes. Entry nodes are where characters are placed when they enter the tile.

Spawn nodes are where enemy models are placed at the start of an encounter.

Terrain nodes are where terrain features are placed at the start of an encounter.

The mini-boss spawn node is where the mini-boss is placed at the start of the mini-boss encounter (mini-boss tile only).

The main-boss spawn node is where the main-boss is placed at the start of the main-boss encounter (main-boss tile only).

## Movement

During encounters, each model must always occupy a single node. When a character moves, they place their model on an adjacent node. An adjacent node is any node located directly next to another node: horizontally, vertically, or diagonally.

## Node Model Limits

Multiple models can share the same node. However, only three models can share a node at the same time, regardless of how many are friendly or enemy models. If there are already three models on a node and another model moves onto that node, the players must push one of the three models already on the node. characters must choose a friendly model to push from the overcrowded node if possible. Enemy models must push other enemy models for the over-crowded node if possible.

The exception to this rule is that nodes also have a boss limit. Only one boss model can ever be on a node. If a boss model moves onto a node that already contains a boss, the players must push the boss that was on the node ﬁrst. However, the boss does not count to the standard three model node limit. Therefore, a node may contain one boss and three characters, for example.

## Range

Attacks and eﬀects have a maximum range that is counted from one model to another model, whether friendly or enemy. Range 0 means the models must be on the same node. Range 1 means the models must be on adjacent nodes (or closer). Range 2 means the models can be up to two nodes apart, etc. Range ∞ (inﬁnity) indicates unlimited range and can aﬀect any model in the encounter.

## Terrain

### Gravestones

- Models cannot *walk*, *run*, *dodge*, or be pushed into nodes containing a gravestone.

- Upon successfully *clearing* an encounter containing a gravestone, the players may scry one card of their collective choosing.

- Scrying is defined as revealing a hidden card and then either returning to its place or sending it away.

- Treasure deck scrying allows the players to reveal the top card of either tier treasure deck. They then decide to return the card face-down to the top of the deck or place it on the bottom of that deck.

- Event scrying allows the players to reveal a single face-down event card. They then decide to return the card face-down to its encounter tile or replace it with the top card of the same level event deck.

- Encounter scrying allows the players to reveal a single face-down encounter card. They then decide to return the card face-down to its encounter tile or replace it with the top card of the same level encounter deck.

- Boss Behaviour scrying allows the players to reveal the top card of the upcoming boss’s Behaviour Deck. They then decide to return the card face-down to the top of the deck or place it on the bottom of that deck.

- When the characters rest at the bonfire, gravestone tokens are not flipped from their current side or moved.

### Barrels

- Models cannot *walk* or *run* into nodes containing barrels.

- Models may be pushed or *dodge* into nodes containing barrels.

- Barrels are destroyed, and the token removed, when a model dodges into or is pushed into that node.

- When the characters rest at the bonfire, barrel tokens are restored to their unbroken state but are not moved.

### Chests

- Models cannot move, dodge, or be pushed into nodes containing a chest.

- Chests always begin encounters with the closed side face-up and with one face-down treasure Card of the appropriate Tier beneath it.

- Upon successfully *clearing* an encounter containing a Chest, the players must flip the chest permanently to its open side and decide which character will take the revealed treasure. If they cannot decide, the treasure should be awarded to a randomly selected character.

- When the characters rest at the bonfire, chest tokens are not flipped from their current side or moved.

### Traps

- If an encounter card is marked with the trap symbol, then trap tokens must be deployed during encounter set-up.

- All trap tokens must be shuffled face-down and dealt out to all basic (yellow) nodes on the encounter tile besides nodes along a tile edge adjacent to another tile.

- Traps do not affect models’ movement.

- When a character is pushed onto or moves into a node containing a face-down trap token, flip the token face-up and resolve its effect on all models on that node.

- If an encounter is reset, face-up trap tokens are flipped face-down but remain on their original nodes.

- If multiple encounters have traps, there may not be enough trap tokens available. In this case, simply stop adding trap tokens when none are available. Players should fill the basic nodes furthest from the tiles entry with trap tokens first.

- When the characters rest at the *bonfire*, trap tokens are flipped face-down but are not moved.

# SETUP

## Character Setup

1.  Each player places their selected character dashboard(s) in front of them.

2.  Equip each character with the starting equipment with a matching icon.

3.  Place the white stat cubes in the base tier stat progression spaces.

4.  Place luck, heroic action, and *estus flask* tokens face-up on each character’s dashboard.

5.  Place an *ember* token beside the character’s board.

6.  Set the red damage cubes and black stamina cubes nearby.

7.  Assign the *aggro* token to the highest threat character.

8.  Characters adventuring alone start with six *souls* on their dashboard.

## Treasure Deck Setup

### Starting Treasure

Each character starts with three or four equipment cards equipped.

### Common Treasure

Common treasures are not marked as starting, class-specific, transposed, legendary, or boss. These treasures represent the most versatile equipment available. Common treasures are divided into tier one and tier two treasure decks during setup. Common treasure with any stat requirement equal or greater than 25 is considered tier two; those with lesser requirements are considered tier one.

### Class-specific Treasure

Class-specific treasures are marked with a tiny white emblem in the top right icon to suggest which class might benefit most from its use. Aside from this suggestion of ideal character class, there are no gameplay differences from common treasures. Class-specific treasure cards are divided into tier one and tier two treasure decks during set-up in the same way as common treasure cards.

### Transposed Treasure

Transposed treasures are marked with a blue light over the top-right of the card. They also feature a tiny white class emblem to suggest which class of character might benefit most from its use. Transposed treasure cards all have at least one stat requirement greater than or equal to 25 and are thus shuffled into the tier two treasure deck at set-up.

### Legendary Treasure

Legendary treasures are marked with an orange light under a treasure chest in the top-right of the card. These represent exceedingly rare relics that do not appear in every game and cannot be purchased. All legendary treasure cards are shuffled during set-up, after Andre's Shop is stocked, and five unrevealed legendary treasures are then shuffled into the tier two treasure deck. The remainder of unused legendary treasures are returned to the game box.

### Boss Treasure

Each boss (mini, main, or mega) has associated boss treasure cards indicated by their silhouette over an orange light in the top-right of the card. During set-up, players find the boss treasure cards associated with the selected bosses and return the rest of unused boss treasure cards to the game box. A single random boss treasure is awarded to the characters upon the defeat of the associated boss.

### Tier One Treasure Deck

1.  Include all common treasures without a required statistic =\> 25.

2.  Include all class-specific treasures without a required statistic =\> 25.

3.  Exclude starting equipment.

4.  Exclude ember cards.

### Tier Two Treasure Deck 

1.  Include all common treasures with a required statistic =\> 25.

2.  Include all class-specific treasures with a required statistic =\> 25.

3.  Include all transposed treasures.

4.  Exclude ember cards.

5.  Exclude boss treasure cards.

6.  Exclude legendary treasures until after the tier two shop is stocked.

### Using Expansion Treasure

DStBG has many expansion sets available of which many include new treasure cards. Players should include all treasure cards from expansion sets in their appropriate type of treasure deck as above.

## Shop Setup

### Start of Game Setup

1.  Shuffle the tier one and tier two treasure decks separately.

2.  Draw tier one treasure cards one at a time to stock tier one shop:

    1.  3 weapons, 3 armour, 2 shields, 2 upgrades, and 2 spells.

    2.  Add ember cards equal to the number of characters.

    3.  Reshuffle extra treasures back into the tier one treasure deck.

### After Boss is Defeated:

1.  Draw tier one treasure cards one at a time to restock tier one shop:

    1.  3 weapons, 3 armour, 2 shields, 2 upgrades, and 2 spells.

    2.  Reshuffle extra treasures back into the tier one treasure deck.

2.  Draw tier two treasure cards one at a time to stock tier two shop:

    1.  3 weapons, 3 armour, 2 shields, 2 upgrades, and 2 spells.

    2.  Reshuffle extra treasures back into the tier two treasure deck.

    3.  Shuffle five random legendary treasure cards.

## Bonfire Setup 

1.  Set the *sparks* dial equal to six minus the number of characters.

2.  Place each player’s chosen character model(s) on the *bonfire*.

3.  Place the tier one and tier two treasure decks beside the *bonfire* tile.

4.  Place the tier one shop cards face up in a pile on the table next to Andre.

## Encounter Setup

### Boss Selection

1.  Players collectively select a mini-boss and main-boss and collect their behaviour decks, health dials, and models.

2.  Players may now decide if they wish to pursue a longer adventure ending in an epic battle with a mega-boss if they like.

3.  For added challenge the players may forgo choice and instead randomly determine the bosses they will oppose by blindly shuffling available mini-boss data cards then revealing one at random. This would be repeated for the main-boss cards. If players want to pursue a mega-boss, this choice can also be randomised in the same way.

### Encounter tiles

1.  Draw four random encounter tiles and arrange them so they are connected in a two by six tile grid with the bonfire tile and mini-boss tile on opposite corners.

2.  Arrange the doorways of the encounter tiles so there are two open doorways that connect to an adjacent tile. This will create a linear route along four sequential encounter tiles between *bonfire* and *boss* tiles.

3.  Place the previously selected mini-boss’s model, data card, behaviour cards, and treasure cards on the boss tile.

### Encounter cards

1.  Encounter decks (level one, two, and three) can be constructed two ways:

    1.  Shuffle all available encounter cards of each level separately.

    2.  Shuffle only encounter cards from the same set of selected bosses.

2.  Deal one face-down encounter card of the appropriate level to each tile.

    1.  Encounter levels are determined by the mini-boss data card.

    2.  Encounter levels should increase from the tile adjacent to The Bonfire tile towards the mini-boss tile.

3.  Deal one face-down event card of the encounter card’s level to each tile.

4.  Deal one tier one treasure per character face-down to each encounter tile.

5.  If playing with optional mimics for increased challenge, deal one random face-down ambush card beside each encounter tile.

### Using Expansion Encounters

DStBG has many expansion sets available which include new encounter cards. By default, players should include only the encounter cards from the same set as the boss they will face next. For example, if the players selected the Gargoyle (mini-boss) and Grey Wolf Sif (main-boss) for their game, they would use only the encounters from the core setup until they defeat the Gargoyle and then only encounters from the Darkroot Basin expansion afterwards. However, players may elect to shuffle all encounter cards from available expansion sets together if they prefer more variety.

## Setup After the mini-boss

1.  Return any dropped souls to the characters.

2.  Return all previously used encounter and event cards to the game box.

3.  Clear remaining tokens from encounter tiles.

4.  Shuffle the four previously used encounter tiles, flip them to the opposite side, and arrange them so they are connected in a straight line originating from the *bonfire* tile and ending in the main-boss tile. Place the fog-gate as before.

5.  Place the previously selected main-boss model and its related data, behaviour, and treasure cards on the boss tile.

6.  Deal one face-down encounter card of the appropriate level to each tile.

    1.  Encounter levels are determined by the main-boss data card.

    2.  Encounter levels should increase from the tile adjacent to the *bonfire* tile towards the main-boss tile.

7.  Deal one face-down event card of the encounter card’s level to each tile.

8.  Deal one tier two treasure card per character face-down to each encounter tile.

9.  Ensure the tier two shop is stocked as previously described.

10. Add one *spark* to the *spark* dial.

11. Place all character models on the *bonfire* tile where they may now without spending a spark. They may visit the Firekeeper and Andre's Shop as usual after resting at the *bonfire*.

12. Resume encounters in the same way as before starting with the encounter tile closest to The Bonfire tile.

## Setup After the main-boss

1.  Return any dropped souls to the characters.

2.  Return all previously used encounter cards, event cards, encounter tiles, and boss tiles to the game box.

3.  Place the Level 4 encounter tile associated with the chosen mega-boss adjacent to The Bonfire tile with the fog-gate placed at the entrance.

4.  Deal one Level 4 card encounter card associated with the chosen mega-boss face down on the encounter tile. No event card is used for this encounter.

5.  Shuffle mega-boss Items and deal one face-down to the encounter tile.

6.  Add one *spark* to the *spark* dial.

7.  Place all character models on the *bonfire* tile where they may now rest without spending a spark. They may visit the Firekeeper and Andre's Shop as usual after resting at the *bonfire*.

8.  Characters then attempt to clear the Level 4 encounter as they have with previous encounters.

9.  If the characters successfully clear the Level 4 encounter they are presented one last chance to spend a *spark* to rest at the *bonfire* prior to challenging the mega-boss.

10. Flip the Level 4 encounter tile to the mega-boss side and place the mega-boss model on the marked node. Set the heat-up and data card next to the mega-boss tile. Shuffle the mega-boss behaviour cards.

11. The characters now begin combat with the mega-boss employing any special rules described in the mega-boss rule booklet. If a character dies they are permanently removed from play. If all characters die, the players lose the game. If any character survives to defeat the mega-boss, the players win the game.

# GAMEPLAY

## Objective & Loss Conditions

The players lose collectively if at any point, all characters are dead and there are no remaining sparks to revive them. The players win if any character survives to defeat the selected final boss. By default, the final boss will be a main-boss and the players share victory. Players may choose to extend their adventure by adding a mega-boss after the main-boss to serve as the final boss instead. Players may also choose to add a player versus player (PvP) encounter after the final boss is defeated to determine a single intimate victor. Players should agree on whether they will include a mega-boss and/or PvP encounter during set-up.

## Death & Hollowing

- Characters die whenever they cannot add a damage cube to their endurance tracker because it is already full. Upon death, a character is temporarily removed from play.

  - Drop all *souls* from the dashboard onto the current encounter tile.

  - Remove the *ember* token from the dashboard if present.

  - Place the character model on its side next to the bonfire.

- If any character survives to *clear* an encounter, then the characters who died in that encounter are revived in a *hollow* state.

  - Place two white cubes on the right side of the stamina bar.

  - *Hollow* status reduces the total endurance of the character by two.

  - *Hollow* status cannot be removed by rest actions or healing.

  - *Hollow* status is only removed by using a *spark* or an *ember* token.

- If all characters die before the end of an encounter, or any character with *hollow* status dies, then the characters are forced to immediately spend one *spark* and rest at the *bonfire*.

## Embering

- *Ember* cards may be discarded as an action to *ember* a character.

  - Remove all damage cubes.

  - Remove all *hollow* cubes.

  - Place the *ember* token on their character dashboard.

- *Embered* characters are affected in multiple ways:

  - If suffering greater than two damage from an attack, reduce damage by one.

  - Phantoms can be summoned before a boss encounter.

  - Invaders will attempt to kill the characters.

  - Characters cannot carry an *ember* card while *embered*.

## Sparks

- Players begin *sparks* equal to six minus the number of characters.

- Defeating a mini-boss or main-boss provides one additional spark.

- Sparks can not be gained by any other means.

- Resting at the *bonfire* costs one *spark*.

## Encounters

### Encounter Start

1.  Characters start with the encounter closest to the *bonfire* tile and advance tile-by-tile towards their ultimate goal of the boss tile.

2.  Characters may skip previously *cleared* encounters at the cost of two stamina cubes placed in the endurance bar of each character.

    1.  For example, if the first three encounters are marked *cleared* then the characters could skip to the fourth encounter but would all start with 6 stamina expended.

3.  Characters enter an encounter tile by placing their models on one of the tile’s entry nodes. The basic nodes along the wall adjacent to the previous encounter tile are considered entry nodes. Only three characters can start on the same entry node due to node model limits.

4.  Reveal the tile’s encounter card, if not already revealed.

5.  Reveal the tile’s event card, if not already revealed.

6.  Populate the tile with enemies and terrain per encounter card directions.

7.  Place one face-down treasure card from the appropriate treasure deck underneath any newly revealed chest tokens. Draw from the tier one treasure deck before the mini-boss is defeated and tier two treasure deck thereafter.

8.  Place the *aggro* token on the character with the highest threat value.

9.  Place the *first player* token on the character with the highest threat value.

### Encounter Turn Order

1.  Each enemy model takes a turn one-by-one in descending *threat* value.

    1.  If multiple enemy models share a *threat* value, the closest model to a character model takes its turn first.

    2.  If multiple enemy models of the same *threat* order are equidistant to a character model, the model with the most damage tokens takes its turn first.

    3.  If multiple enemy models of the same *threat* order and damage are equidistant to a character model, the players may decide which model takes its turn first.

2.  Each character takes their turn one-by-one in descending *threat* value.

3.  After all characters have taken their turn, place the *aggro* token on the character who placed the most damage tokens on enemy models.

    1.  If multiple characters inflicted the same number of damage tokens, then the *aggro* token remains on the same character.

4.  Continue alternating between enemies and characters until either all enemy or character models are defeated.

### Encounter Resolution

- Encounters end in one of three ways:

  - Encounters are *cleared* if all enemy models are defeated.

  - Encounters are *failed* if either all characters die.

  - Encounters are *failed* if any character with *hollow* status dies.

- Encounters *cleared* for the first time resolve as follows:

  - Characters who died must drop their *souls* on that encounter tile.

  - Characters who survived retrieve their dropped *souls* from that tile.

  - Characters gain *soul* rewards and one treasure card each.

  - Mark the encounter card *cleared* with a black cube.

  - *Cleared* encounters are reset when characters rest at the *bonfire*.

  - *Cleared* encounters may be skipped thereafter.

- Encounters *cleared* that have already been *cleared* resolve as follows:

  - Characters who died must drop their *souls* on that encounter tile.

  - Characters who survived retrieve their dropped *souls* from that tile.

  - Characters gain *soul* rewards but not treasure.

- Encounters *failed* resolve as follows:

  - Characters must all drop their *souls* on that encounter tile.

  - *Failed* encounters do not reward *souls* or treasure.

  - *Failed* encounters flip their encounter card face-down.

  - *Failed* encounters are reset when characters rest at the *bonfire*.

  - *Failed* encounters may not be skipped once reset.

  - *C*haracters are forced to rest at a bonfire at the cost of a *spark*.

### Encounter Recovery

- Characters who survive to *clear an* encounter recover:

  - Remove one damage cube from each character.

  - Remove all stamina cubes from each character.

  - Remove all condition tokens from each character.

  - *Estus flask*, *luck*, and *heroic action* tokens are not restored.

  - *Hollow* status is not removed.

- Characters who died in a *cleared* encounter are revived:

  - Place the character model upright with the surviving characters.

  - Remove all damage and stamina cubes from the character.

  - Remove all condition tokens from the character.

  - *Estus flask*, *luck*, and *heroic action* tokens are not restored.

  - Mark the character *hollow* with two white cubes on the rightmost spaces of the endurance bar.

- All characters must rest at the bonfire if an encounter is *failed:*

  - Spend one *spark*.

  - Place all character models upright on the bonfire tile.

  - Remove all damage and stamina cubes from all characters.

  - Remove all condition tokens from all characters.

  - Remove all *hollow* tokens from all characters.

  - *Estus flask*, *luck*, and *heroic action* tokens are restored.

## Souls

- Characters track their personal supply of *souls* by placing *soul* tokens on their character dashboard.

- *Souls* cannot be transferred between characters.

- Upon successfully *clearing* an encounter that has not been previously *cleared*, reward the full *soul* reward to each character.

- Upon successfully *clearing* an encounter that has been previously *cleared*, reward the reduced *soul* reward to each character.

- Successfully defeated invaders provide additional *souls* per player.

- When a character dies, drop all of the *souls* from their character dashboard onto the encounter tile they died on separate from other characters’ dropped *souls*.

- The character who dropped them by successfully clearing or mastering the encounter on which they were dropped can reclaim dropped souls.

- Dropped *souls* are lost forever if the character who dropped them dies before reclaiming the *souls*.

## Treasure Rewards

- Treasure is awarded to the characters when they have successfully *cleared* an encounter that has not been previously marked as *cleared.*

  - Characters are awarded one treasure each.

  - Characters receive their treasure card reward even if they died.

  - Draw cards from the Tier one treasure deck before defeating the mini-boss, and Tier two treasure deck thereafter.

- Treasure is awarded to the characters if they successfully *clear* an encounter with an unopened chest token.

  - If playing with *mimics*, determine if a mimic is encountered and fight immediately before encounter recovery is resolved.

  - Flip the chest token to the opened side.

  - Reveal the treasure card placed face-down under the chest token.

  - Players decide which character should receive this treasure. If the players cannot decide, the treasure will be awarded to the character who ended the encounter with the *aggro* token.

- If playing with the option of mimics, any character who survives to open a chest risks being ambushed by a mimic.

  - If there is at least one surviving character on an encounter tile with a chest they may decide to open the chest or leave it closed.

  - The characters do not recover stamina, remove damage, draw treasure, or collect souls until a decision is made.

  - If any surviving character wishes to open a chest after an encounter ends, they must reveal the ambush card beside that encounter. If they choose to leave the chest closed they recover normally and then continue on to the next encounter.

  - If the ambush card shows treasure, they collect one treasure card from the appropriate tier treasure deck. They then recover and proceed to the next encounter normally.

  - If the ambush card shows a mimic, set up a mimic mini-boss encounter immediately. If a mini-boss has not been defeated, use the hungry mimic. If a mini-boss has already been defeated, use the voracious mimic.

  - Characters remain in the nodes they ended the encounter in and the mimic attacks with combat proceeding the same as any other mini-boss encounter. See the detailed rules on mimics later in these rules for details.

- If playing with the option of *crystal lizards*, award a Titanite Chunk card to any character who killed a *crystal lizard* during the encounter.

- If a boss was defeated the characters are awarded one of the treasure cards marked with the defeated boss’s icon drawn at random. Players decide which character should receive the treasure. If the players cannot decide, the treasure will be awarded to the character who ended the encounter with the *aggro* token.

# COMBAT BASICS

## Target versus Hit

When a character or an enemy attacks, the model defending the attack is considered the attack’s target. Attacks have a single target by default but some attacks have special modifiers that allow them to target all models on a node or multiple nodes. Characters may only target other characters if playing a PvP encounter per the optional rules detailed later. Enemies never target other enemies.

A model is considered hit by an attack even if the attack deals zero damage so long as the attack was in range and the target model did not successfully dodge the attack. Therefore, targets who successfully *dodge* an attack are not considered hit and therefore are not pushed or afflicted with conditions associated with being hit by the attack.

## Enemy Data Cards

Each enemy type in the game has a data card breaking down how it works in the game. Enemy data cards give the information necessary to activate that enemy or to resolve a character’s attack against it.

Each enemy data card includes the following information:

1.  Name

2.  Threat level

3.  Attack range

4.  *Dodge* difficulty

5.  *Block* and *resist* values

6.  Behaviour icons

7.  Base health

8.  Enemy icon

9.  Set symbol

The behaviour icons are the core element of any enemy which determine how the enemy behaves during its turn.

An enemy’s threat level determines the order in which it takes a turn in combat with higher values taking their turn first. The attack range is the maximum range at which the enemy model can attack characters. The dodge difficulty determines how hard it is for characters to dodge the attack. The enemy’s block value reduces the damage it suffers from physical attacks, and its resist value reduces the damage it suffers from magical attacks.

An enemy’s base health is how much damage the enemy can suffer before being defeated in a single character game. When playing with more than one character, calculate a scaled health value by adding one health for each character (including dead characters) in play. When an enemy suffers damage, place one wound token on the base of that enemy model for each point of damage not prevented by the enemies *block* or *resist*. Once an enemy model has wounds equal to (or exceeding) its scaled health, it is considered killed and removed from the tile.

## Enemy Scaling

- Non-boss enemies gain benefits depending on the number of total characters in a game to ensure balance.

  - Increase each non-boss enemy’s base health by the number of characters (including dead characters) beyond one.

  - If playing with two or more characters, increase any non-boss enemy behaviour of one movement to two movements.

  - If playing with three or more characters, increase all non-boss attack values by one.

  - If playing with four characters, add *repeat to* each non-boss enemy's attack behaviour.

- Boss enemies (mini, main, and mega) do NOT modify their base health or receive scaling benefits otherwise.

## Pushing

Certain movements and attacks are labelled as push-movements and push-attacks (respectively). These special push effects may push a model from its current node to an adjacent node. Push movement does not cost stamina.

Boss models cannot be pushed by a character’s movement or attack. The only way a boss model can be pushed is if another boss model moves onto its node.

If the push icon appears on an attack, the model(s) hit by the attack are pushed. The pushed model(s) must move to the adjacent node most directly opposite of the attacking model. If there are multiple adjacent nodes equally opposite to the attacking model the players choose the node. A model being pushed directly into a corner remains on the corner node.If the attacker is on the same node as the model hit by the push attack, the players choose any adjacent node for the model to be pushed into.

If the push icon appears on an enemy’s movement, first immediately move each character on that enemy’s node onto an adjacent node chosen by the players. Then, the enemy moves one node in the direction dictated by the enemy or boss data card. If a push-movement causes the enemy to move onto a node that contains any character(s), the character(s) must move to the adjacent node most directly opposite to the direction from which the enemy model is push-moving. In other words, a character being pushed by an enemy's push-movement must move into the adjacent node further from the node the enemy started their movement on. If there are multiple valid nodes the players choose which node the character is pushed into. If an enemy performing a push-movement moves multiple times as part of their move, the above process is repeated separately for each single node moved.

Players should take note that only enemy behaviours that deal damage can be dodged. Therefore, only enemy behaviours with an associated attack value can be dodged to permit the character to avoid the associated push effect. Of course, characters who block or resist a push behaviour are pushed regardless of damage dealt.

## Conditions

Some attacks inflict conditions on their target. When an attack has a condition icon, place the corresponding condition token on the model(s) hit by that attack. When a model ends its turn, remove one poison token and any *frostbite*/*stagger* tokens. Each condition has its own rules and effects listed below.

### *Bleed*

- *Bleed* tokens are limited to one per model.

- *Bleed* tokens are not removed at the end of activations.

- *Bleed* tokens are only removed at the end of the encounter.

- If a second *bleed* token would be placed on a model, deal three damage and remove all *bleed* tokens from that model.

- *Bleed* damage is not affected by *block* or *resist*.

### *Poison*

- *Poison* tokens are not limited to one per model.

- A model suffers one damage for each *poison* token it has at the end of its activation, then removes only one *poison* token.

- *Poison* damage is not affected by *block* or *resist*.

### *Frostbite*

- *Frostbite* tokens are limited to one per model.

- *Frostbite* affected characters cannot recover stamina.

- *Frostbite* affected enemies reduce their movement values by one.

- *Frostbite* tokens are removed at the end of the model’s turn.

### *Stagger*

- *Stagger* tokens are limited to one per model.

- *Staggered* models subtract one from *attack*, *block*, *resist*, and *dodge*.

- *Stagger* tokens are removed at the end of the model’s turn.

### *Calamity (*Kalameet Expansion*)*

- *Calamity* tokens are limited to one per model.

- *Calamity* reduces a model’s *block*, *resist*, and *dodge* roll results by one.

- *Calamity* tokens are not removed at the end of a model’s turn.

- *Calamity* tokens are only removed at the end of the encounter.

### *Corrosion (*Gaping Dragon Expansion*)*

- *Corrosion* tokens are limited to one per model.

- *Corrosion* tokens reduce the affected model’s block roll results by one.

- *Corrosion* tokens are not removed at the end of a model’s turn.

- *Corrosion* tokens are removed if the affected character takes damage.

# ENEMY TURN

## Overview

Each encounter begins with each enemy model taking their turn before proceeding to the character’s turns. Enemy models take their turn beginning with the highest threat and ending with the model with the lowest threat. For enemies with equal threat levels, the enemy model closer to a character model goes first, if multiple enemy models are equidistant to a character model then the enemy model with more wound token’s goes first. If there are multiple enemy models with the same threat value, distance to a character, and number of wound tokens, then the players decide which model should go first.

Non-boss enemies follow the behaviour icons on their data cards. Bosses follow the icons on their behaviour cards instead. All behaviour icons are resolved left to right. An enemy’s behaviour can be as simple as making one attack or can have multiple moves and/or attacks. If there is no valid target or the enemy behaviour otherwise cannot be reasonably executed, that enemy behaviour is skipped and the next icon to the right on the enemy behaviour card is then resolved. An enemy model ends its turn once all of the icons on its behaviour card have been resolved.

## Enemy Movement

The enemy movement icon shows how an enemy model will move. Various smaller icons can appear around the movement icon, modifying how the enemy moves. This allows different enemies to move towards or away from characters in different patterns and at different speeds.

The four directions of the movement icon are oriented so the upward direction indicates movement toward the target character. The downward direction indicates movement away from the target character. The number in the direction arrow indicates how many nodes the enemy model will move in the indicated direction. The small icon in the top right of the movement behaviour icon indicates the target character. A small skull icon indicates that movement will target the character with the *aggro* token. A small ring icon indicates that movement will target the closest character.

If an enemy movement icon instructs an enemy to move towards a character it already shares a node with, then it simply does not move.

If an enemy movement icon instructs an enemy to move away from a character that is already at maximum distance, then it simply does not move.

When two different nodes would take an enemy closer to where it is trying to go, the players can choose which node the enemy enters.

### Towards aggro

The enemy moves the indicated number of nodes closer to the character with the aggro token.

### Away from aggro

The enemy moves the indicated number of nodes farther from the character with the aggro token.

### Towards nearest

The enemy moves the stated number of nodes closer to the nearest character. If two or more characters are tied for being nearest, the enemy moves towards the character with the aggro token. If the model with the aggro token is not tied for being the nearest, the enemy moves towards the character with the higher threat level. If the enemy movement covers multiple nodes, determine the nearest character at the start of the movement, not at each step.

### Away from nearest

The enemy moves the stated number of nodes away from to the nearest character. If two or more characters are tied for being nearest, the enemy moves away from the character with the aggro token. If the model with the aggro token is not tied for being the nearest, the enemy moves away from the character with the higher threat level. If the enemy movement covers multiple nodes, determine the nearest character at the start of the movement, not at each step.

### Push-move

If the Movement icon includes the push icon, the enemy’s movement pushes characters out of its way.

### Push-attack

If the push icon includes a number, characters pushed out of the way suffer that amount of damage during the push.

## Enemy Attacks

When resolving an attack icon as part of an enemy’s turn, determine which of the two types of attacks that enemy will make. Physical attacks cause physical damage and magical attacks cause magical damage. The crossed swords icon indicates physical damage and the white circle icon indicates magical damage. The number in the centre of the attack icon is the enemy’s attack value. If there is a condition token symbol, then characters hit by that enemy are marked with the matching condition token. Additional symbols beside the attack type icon modify the attack with special rules detailed later.

Whenever an enemy model attacks a character, that character must decide how to defend themselves. Characters may *block*, *resist*, *dodge*, *parry*, or do nothing. Since the enemy attack values are fixed, the character’s defensive dice rolls determine the ultimate result of enemy attacks. More on each defensive option listed below.

### *Block* & *Resist*

1.  Gather all dice of the appropriate type noted on the character’s currently equipped armour, weapon(s), and shield(s).

    1.  *Block* dice are used against physical attacks.

    2.  *Resist* dice are used against magical attacks.

2.  Roll *block* or *resist* dice permitted by equipment.

3.  Characters may flip their *luck* token face-down to reroll any or all dice.

4.  Determine the defence total by adding the number of sword pips on the highest two dice results. Additional dice beyond the two with the highest shown face value are ignored.

5.  Determine damage to the defending character.

    1.  If the enemy's attack value exceeds the character’s defence value, the character must place red damage cubes on their dashboard equal to the difference.

    2.  If the enemy’s attack value is less than or equal to the character’s defence value, the character takes no damage.

6.  Place any condition tokens indicated, even if no damage was dealt.

7.  Resolve any push effects.

### *Dodge*

1.  Pay one stamina to attempt to *dodge* an attack.

2.  Roll a number of *dodge* dice indicated by the character’s equipment.

    1.  If the enemy dodge difficulty is zero, the character does not need to roll dice and the dodge automatically succeeds, even if they have no dice to roll.

    2.  Characters may spend two additional stamina (total of three stamina cost) before rolling dice to add a single additional *dodge* die to their roll, up to a maximum of four *dodge* dice.

3.  Characters may use their *luck* token to reroll any or all dice.

4.  If the *dodge* total is equal to or greater than the enemy’s *dodge* difficulty, the attack does not hit.

    1.  No damage is taken.

    2.  No conditions are applied.

    3.  No push effects are applied.

    4.  The character must move to an adjacent node.

5.  If the *dodge* total is less than the enemy’s *dodge* difficulty, the attack hits.

    1.  Roll only the *block* or *resist* dice (depending on enemy attack type) provided by the character’s currently equipped armour. Do not include dice provided by equipped weapons, shields, or spells.

    2.  Determine damage to the defending character as above.

    3.  Place condition tokens associated with enemy attack, even if no damage was dealt.

    4.  Resolve any push effects.

### *Parry* & *Riposte*

1.  Pay one stamina to attempt to *parry* an attack.

2.  Determine if current enemy attack can be *parried*.

    1.  Characters must be equipped with a shield as their left weapon.

    2.  Characters must share the same node as the attacker.

    3.  *Push, area-of-effect,* and *boss-arc* attacks cannot be *parried*.

3.  Roll a number of *dodge* dice indicated by the character’s equipment.

4.  Characters may use their *luck* token to reroll any or all dice.

5.  If the *dodge* value is equal to or greater than the enemy’s *dodge* difficulty, the attack is successfully *parried*.

    1.  No damage is taken.

    2.  No conditions are applied.

    3.  Place a *stagger* token on the attacking model’s base.

    4.  The character may make one *riposte* attack out of turn against the attacker using any *attack option* on the weapon equipped to the right weapon slot after paying the associated stamina cost.

6.  If the *dodge* value is less than the enemy’s *dodge* difficulty, the attempt to *parry* fails.

    1.  The character takes full damage without the option to block*/*resist.

    2.  Place a *stagger* token on the defending character’s dashboard.

## Character Turn Order

## Character Movement

Characters have four methods of moving from node to node during an encounter. Characters may first *walk* as a minor action which allows them to move one space to an adjacent node. The character may then elect to *run*, which allows additional movement at the cost of one stamina per node. The character may repeat the *run* minor action as many times as they are able to pay stamina. The third way a character can move is by successfully *dodging* an attack targeting their model which forces the character to move one space to an adjacent node. Finally, some weapon’s *attack options* are marked with the *shift* icon, which allows the character to move one node during an *attack* major action. *Shift* movement is either resolved immediately before or after the attack is resolved, depending on whether the *shift* icon is before or after that *attack option’s* dice icons.

All character movement (*walk*, *run*, *dodge,* and *shift*) may only be performed if the character ends their movement in a valid node. A valid node is any node that does not contain terrain that would block movement such as a gravestone or chest. Additionally, characters are not permitted to *walk, run,* or *shift* to a node containing a barrel. However, characters may be pushed or *dodge* into a node with a barrel token, which causes the barrel to be destroyed. Characters may move into a node already at the node model limit but must push a friendly model out of the node immediately. Characters may not leave the encounter tile(s) noted on the current encounter card during their movement.

## Character Attacks

During the character’s turn they may make up to one attack with each weapon or spell equipped to the weapon slots on their character dashboard. A character makes an attack by selecting an *attack option* on one of their equipped weapons, paying the stamina cost, and then nominating a valid enemy model as their target within the weapon’s range value. Then roll the number and colour of dice associated with the selected attack option, count the number of sword icons, and apply modifiers to determine the attack’s attack value. At this point the player may elect to issue any re-rolls available to them by their *luck* token or otherwise. Once the final attack value is determined that number is compared to the enemy’s relevant defence value to determine damage dealt.

All weapons do physical damage by default. Certain weapons and most spells will instead do magical damage if the attack option selected is labelled with the magical damage icon. Final damage dealt by physical attacks is calculated by subtracting the enemy’s block value from the character's attack value. Final damage dealt by magical attacks is calculated by subtracting the enemy’s resist value from the character’s attack value.

Players then place a number of damage tokens onto the target enemy model’s base equal to the final damage dealt. If the total number of wounds received by an enemy model ever equals (or exceeds) the enemy’s scaled health value, that enemy dies and is removed from play.

Though many weapons simply deal damage, some have special abilities explained in text on their cards or with icons. This includes the magical damage icon explained above, the conditions explained previously, and the following weapon icons.

### *Range*

Some *attack options* specify a range that is different from the weapon’s default range listed on its equipment card. An *attack option* specific range replaces the weapon’s range for that attack action.

### *Shift*

When a character makes an attack with the *shift* icon, they may move up to the number of nodes shown. Shift icons that appear before the dice icons allow a character to move before rolling the dice. Those that appear after the dice allow the character to move after rolling the dice. Movement from *Shift* icons costs 0 Stamina and does not replace the character’s options to *Walk* and *Run* during their activation.

### *Area-of-Effect (AoE)*

Attacks with the *AoE* icon can target all enemies on a single node within range instead of targeting just one enemy. Make the attack roll once, and compare it to the block or resist value of each enemy on the node to determine final damage dealt to each enemy model.

### *Shaft*

Bows, polearms, and other unwieldy weapons are difficult to use effectively at short range. Weapons with the *shaft* icon cannot target enemy models on the character’s node.

### *Repeat*

The *repeat* icon allows the character to use that entire weapon option the number of times indicated in the circle. This represents a rapid flurry of attacks such as from an agile sword or a repeating crossbow.

## Two-Handed Attacks

Characters equipped with a two-handed weapon are decidedly on the offensive. Attack actions with two-handed weapons may reroll any single die after rolling to attack. Additionally, if a character equips a one-handed weapon without equipment to their other hand, that weapon is considered to be wielded as two-handed and gains the same reroll option.

## Stat Scaling

Weapons may provide additional attack damage if they are equipped by a character who’s stats far exceed the weapon’s stat requirements. If the character's current stat value exceeds an equipment’s highest stat requirement by ten or more points, the character should mark the equipped item with a red damage cube to indicate that it is scaled. If two requirements are tied, use either. A scaled weapon benefits from a +1 modifier to the attack value of all of its’ attack option*s*. Weapons are not provided additional stat scaling beyond the +1 benefits for excess stats beyond ten. Spells are not affected by scaling.

# RETURNING TO THE BONFIRE

## The Bonfire

The *bonfire* serves as a safe haven in an otherwise treacherous world. When the characters arrive, there are two strangers, who wish to help the characters on their journey. The first of which is the Firekeeper, a mysterious blindfolded woman, who can increase the characters’ stats in exchange for the *souls* of the fallen. The second stranger is Blacksmith Andre, a massive smith toiling endlessly at his forge. Andre runs a shop and will also upgrade the character’s equipment, if they bring him suitable materials.

The characters must collectively spend one *spark to* visit the *bonfire* to rest, improve stats, upgrade equipment, and shop. Resting at the *bonfire* is paramount to restoring the characters’ strength and keeping them from being *lost* to the *hollow.* Characters cannot spend their hard-earned *souls* without resting at the *bonfire*. *Sparks* are the most precious resource in this game and the players must carefully weigh the timing of each *spark* spent.

## Resting at The Bonfire

- Resting at the *bonfire* costs a *spark* and allows characters the following.

  - Remove all damage and stamina from the character dashboards.

  - Remove all *hollow* cubes from the character dashboards.

  - Flip *luck*, *estus flask*, and *heroic action* tokens face-up.

  - Pay the Firekeeper *souls* to increase character stats.

  - Purchase and/or sell treasure cards at Andre's Shop.

- Resting at the *bonfire* also revives *lost* characters who have been removed from play due to dying while *hollow.*

- Characters rest at the *bonfire* without needing to spend a *spark* after defeating a boss or completing player-versus-player (PvP).

##  

## The Firekeeper

Characters may only spend *souls* to improve character stats when resting at the *bonfire*. All character stats start at their base tier and must be increased to the next tier sequentially. Each stat tier requires payment for all lesser stat tiers. For example, to increase a character’s strength (STR) stat from base tier to tier two, they would first have to pay two souls to reach tier one before paying four additional souls to reach tier two. If a character reaches stat tier four they mark this on their character dashboard by replacing the tier three white cube with a red cube. Tier four stats are all considered 5 greater than the tier three stat value.

## Blacksmith Andre

Only when the characters spend a spark at the bonfire to rest, they may visit Blacksmith Andre to upgrade equipment or visit Andre's Shop.

Characters may attach any weapon upgrade cards from their inventory to equipped weapon cards and armour upgrade cards to equipped armour. However, this is limited by the upgrade slots of the equipment being upgraded. Additionally, characters can only upgrade equipment if they have met the minimum stat requirements of an upgrade card. characters cannot equip weapons or armour if they do not meet the minimum stat requirements of the equipment and all attached upgrades.

### Shop Rules

- Shops are only restocked after a boss is defeated.

- Boss treasures may be sold to Andre but never purchased.

- Starting equipment cannot be sold.

- Weapon upgrades aside from Titanite upgrades (i.e.: gems) that are attached to sold weapons are returned to the character.

- Armour upgrades attached to sold armour are returned to the character.

- Each character may only carry one *ember* card at a time.

## Resuming Exploration

Once the characters are restored to strength and no longer wish to spend souls they are nearly ready to resume their exploration of encounters. Prior to setting out, flip all cleared and failed encounter tiles face-down to reset them. If there are any remaining enemy models on the encounter tiles, set them aside. After the first encounter tile is resolved the characters continue towards the boss tile, one encounter tile at a time.

# SUMMONS

## Phantoms

*Embered* characters may summon a random friendly phantom at the fog gate prior to encountering a mini-boss or main-boss.

1.  *Embered* characters may elect to pay two *souls* to draw a phantom card.

2.  Draw a random phantom card of appropriate level.

    - Standard phantoms for mini-bosses

    - Advanced phantoms for main-bosses or mega-bosses

3.  Players must agree on only one of the drawn phantoms to summon.

4.  Shuffle all of the phantom’s behaviour cards to form the behaviour deck.

5.  Place the chosen phantom's model on the boss tile’s entrance node after the characters have placed their character models on their starting node.

6.  Phantoms take their turn after all character turns are resolved and before the first enemy turn begins.

7.  Each phantom turn, draw and resolve the top card of the phantom behaviour deck.i

8.  After each icon on the encounter card is resolved in left to right order, the phantom’s turn ends.

Phantoms are removed from play if their health dial is reduced to or the encounter ends.

## Invaders

### Invader Tokens

1.  *Embered* characters provoke an invader if no invasion has occurred yet.

2.  Randomly draw an invader token from the supply.

    1.  Standard invaders before the mini-boss.

    2.  Advanced invaders after the mini-boss.

3.  Shuffle enough blank invader tokens with the selected invader token so there are equal tokens to face-down encounter cards.

4.  Place an invader token face-down on each face-down encounter card.

5.  When the characters move their models onto an encounter tile with a face-down invader token, flip the invader token face-up before revealing the encounter card.

### Invader Encounter Setup

1.  If an invader token reveals an invader icon, an invader is added to the upcoming encounter.

2.  Shuffle the invader’s behaviour cards and deal a number equal to their starting decks deck size to create the invader behaviour deck. When the invader is reduced to its Heat Up value, add an unused invader behaviour card to the behaviour deck and shuffle.

3.  Set the invader heal dial to the invader’s starting health value. Invaders do not multiply their health by the number of characters like other enemy models.

4.  After the encounter card setup is complete, place the invader model on the encounter tile’s most central node. If the central node is blocked by train, then play the invader model in a node adjacent to the centre node of the players’ choosing.

### Invader Combat

1.  Characters enter combat with the encounter normally with the addition of an invader who takes a turn after each character’s individual turn.

2.  The invader takes a turn each time a character completes their turn, not in threat order with the other enemies. Thus, the invader will take many more turns than any other model in the encounter.

3.  On an invader’s turn, resolve the top card of the invader behaviour deck.

4.  After each icon on the encounter card is resolved in left to right order, the invader’s turn ends.

5.  If any character dies, the invader immediately vanishes without award.

6.  The invader dies if their health dial is reduced to zero. Immediately award *souls* and all invader treasures when the invader dies.

7.  Invaders do not reset or return once encountered regardless of outcome.

# MIMICS

Players with the Encounters expansion set available may choose whether they wish to include mimics in their game during setup. Groups with new players should default to leaving mimics out as they can be quite punishing. Experienced players should include mimics to ensure they are challenged. The following rules assume the play with the Explorers expansion set including mimics.

After defeating an encounter that contains an unopened chest, characters do not recover normally and remain in their current node. Characters must collectively decide whether they wish to open the chest or leave it be out of fear of encountering a mimic. If the characters cannot agree, the character with the *aggro* token makes the final decision. If the characters elect to leave the chest alone, they proceed to recover all stamina and two damage normally. The then are faced with the typical option of continuing onto the next encounter or returning to rest at the bonfire for the cost of one *spark.*

However, if the characters wish to open the chest, they must reveal one an *ambush* card to determine if the chest is a lurking mimic. If the *ambush* card shows treasure, the characters receive a total of one tier appropriate treasure which they must assign per normal treasure chest rules. If the *ambush* card shows a mimic, an additional min-boss encounter ensues before the party can claim their treasure. If the characters have already defeated their previously chosen mini-boss, then the Voracious Mimic’s data and encounter cards are used. If the characters have not yet defeated their previously chosen mini-boss, then the Hungry Mimic’s data and encounter cards are used.

Characters remain on the nodes they occupied at the end of the encounter containing the chest. The mimic will take its turn first. Refer to “**Boss Encounter**” rules for more details on how to manage the mimic’s behaviour cards. The only difference is that the mimic does not have specific *heat-up* behaviour cards and instead just shuffles in one unused normal behaviour card when it heats-up. If the characters kill the mimic, they gain two tier appropriate treasures (instead of one), and may mark the encounter *cleared.*. If the encounter is *cleared* then the characters may return to repeat the encounter again but do not encounter the mimic a second time. If the mimic kills all of the characters, the encounter is *failed*, and the characters will encounter the mimic next time they complete the normal encounter in which the mimic lives. However, once the mimic has been fought once, it must be fought again at the end of the encounter until it is killed.

# CRYSTAL LIZARDS

Crystal lizards are strange and glimmering reptiles that offer character’s powerful rewards if the lizard is slain before it can escape. Players should include crystal lizards as an optional game component if they own the Iron Keep expansion set. The following rules are assuming the players are playing with the Iron Keep expansion set and the included crystal lizards.

Whenever the character reveals an encounter card with the crystal lizard symbol, a crystal lizard appears. During encounter set-up, place a crystal lizard on the terrain node indicated by the encounter card. Some encounters contain multiple crystal lizards. Crystal lizards take their turns like any other enemy model in descending *threat* order. However, their behaviours dictate that they simply run from the characters which makes killing them challenging. Crystal lizards do not scale their health depending on the number of characters. Whenever a character kills a crystal lizard, they immediately place one Titanite Scale card into their inventory. When the last enemy of an encounter (beside crystal lizards) dies, any remaining crystal lizards are immediately removed from play. The crystal lizards removed from play at the end of an encounter have escaped and do not award characters any treasure.

If any number of crystal lizards die during an encounter, no further crystal lizards will spawn on repeated attempts of that encounter. Mark encounter tiles with an upside-down crystal lizard model if any crystal lizard dies during an encounter. Otherwise, if no crystal lizard died in a previous attempt, the crystal lizards will respawn on repeated attempts until one dies. Note that an encounter is still considered *cleared* regardless of any surviving crystal lizards.

# BOSS ENCOUNTERS

## The Fog Gate

Once a character has reached a boss encounter they must pass through the fog gate. The first time a character passes through the fog gate the fog gate tile should be removed from the boss-tile entryway and placed on the *bonfire* tile to serve as a shortcut. If the characters fail their first attempt at a boss encounter, they may use the fog gate to travel directly to the boss encounter from the *bonfire* tile to try again without having to fight any encounters. If the characters wish to forgo the fog gate to gain more souls and treasure they may proceed back to the boss encounter through their previous route encounter tiles.

## Boss Encounter Overview

Boss encounters are the heart of the character’s adventure and the bosses are the targets of their bloodlust. Bosses are single models that are not only large in stature, but formidable opponents with increased health, attack power, and special abilities. characters can expect to die often at the hands of these powerful enemies. Only with adequate preparation, astute tactics, and the favour of luck should the characters hope to fell a boss. There are three types of bosses: mini-bosses, main-bosses, and mega-bosses. The core game does not come with mega-bosses as they are optional objectives which players can attempt after defeating a main-boss should they want a more epic final battle. Boss encounters follow many of the same rules as other enemies as described previously, but there are key differences outlined as follows.

## Boss Encounter Tiles

While mini-boss encounters typically take place on the single mini-boss tile, main-boss and mega-boss encounters use special encounter tile rules. main-boss encounters require two boss encounter tiles to be placed adjacent to one another and are treated as one large tile for all purposes. Mega-boss encounters usually have a unique mega-boss encounter tile included with the mega-boss expansion.

## Boss Data Cards

Boss data cards include similar information to enemy data cards but are noticeably lacking behaviours to dictate how they act on their turns. Instead, bosses have variable behaviours determined by a special deck of behaviour cards. The specifics of boss data cards is detailed here and information about boss behaviour cards will follow.

1.  Name

2.  *Threat* level

3.  Behaviour deck size

4.  Heat-up point

5.  *Block* and *resist* values

6.  Special ability

7.  Encounter levels

8.  Starting health

9.  Boss type icon (mini, main, or mega)

10. Set symbol

Boss *threat, block,* and *resist* values are unchanged from previously described enemy data cards. Boss starting health is treated the same as well but instead of using dozens of wound tokens, players track the boss’s remaining health on a health tracker dial specific to each boss.

Boss behaviour deck size and heat-up point are used to determine the composition of the boss behaviour deck.

Boss special ability provides an overarching theme to the boss which often gives the boss unique powers.

Finally, boss data cards list the encounter levels leading up to that boss as detailed during the setup chapter of these rules.

## Boss Behaviour Cards

Each boss behaviour card includes information formatted in the same way.

1.  Name

2.  Attack range

3.  Dodge difficulty

4.  Heat-up symbol

5.  Behaviour icons

Bosses' behaviours are entirely dictated by the behaviour cards they reveal from the top of their behaviour deck during a boss’s turn. The behaviour icons on the cards are similar to enemy data cards but include a few new icons, take into account boss facing, and are generally more powerful. Each behaviour card will dictate the boss’s attack range and dodge difficulty until the next boss behaviour card is drawn.

Some boss behaviour cards have a Heat Up symbol signifying those cards as Heat Up cards. These behaviours are typically more powerful but are not placed into the boss behaviour deck initially. Heat Up cards are added into the behaviour deck once the characters have reduced the boss’s health to (or below) its Heat Up point.

To construct the initial boss behaviour deck, first set aside all of the Heat Up behaviours. Then, shuffle the normal boss behaviour cards and deal a number equal to the boss’s behaviour deck size face-down into a pile to form the boss’s initial behaviour deck. Return any unused normal boss behaviour cards to the box, face-down. Shuffle the Heat Up behaviour cards and place them in a face-down deck next to the boss encounter tile. When the boss’s health is reduced to (or below) its Heat Up point, take a random Heat Up behaviour card and shuffle it into the boss behaviour deck. Some bosses have special Heat Up rules which may allow them to Heat Up multiple times or otherwise override these rules.

## Boss Arcs and Defence

The base of a boss model is separated into four distinct arcs: front, left, right, and back. These arcs are a key part of what makes boss encounters unique, and they add an extra layer of tactics to fighting against bosses.

Bosses always directly face an adjacent node. For a model to directly face a node, the centre of the model’s front arc must line up with the centre of that node. This splits the tile into four areas defined by the boss's four arcs. Each node on the tile is in at least one of the four arcs. Nodes along the lines between arcs are considered to be in both arcs.

### Character Arc Movement

Character movement in boss encounters is supplemented with the following rules.

A character sharing a node with a boss must be placed on the boss model’s base in one of the four arcs.

A character moving onto the same node as a boss must remain in the same arc as the node from which they are moving. If this node is split between two arcs the player may select one of those two boss arcs to move into.

A character sharing a node with a boss may elect to move to an adjacent boss arc instead of moving off of the node. This may be repeated if the character can move multiple nodes (i.e.: *Run*) which can allow a character initially on the front-arc of the base (for example) to move to a side-arc of the base and then to the rear-arc of the base.

If a character moves out of a node shared with a boss, they must remain in the same arc. Thus, if the character elects to *Walk or Run* off of the boss’s base arc they must move to an adjacent node within that same boss arc. If the character is pushed off of the boss’s node, they must move to the centre-most node in the boss’s arc they originate from, if possible. Otherwise, the character is pushed to any valid node in that arc. If there are no valid nodes in that arc due to a wall, the character may move to any adjacent node touching that wall instead.

If a character successfully dodge*s* a boss’s attack they must move to an adjacent node as per the normal encounter rules. However, they are not required to remain in the boss’s arc when moving due to dodge. A character may move to any adjacent node or to any arc on the base of the boss’s model.

### Attacking Bosses and Weak Arcs

When a character attacks a boss, the most recently resolved boss behaviour card determines which arcs are considered weak. If the character is attacking a weak arc, the attack gains an additional black die.

Remember that a character can be in more than one arc if it is on a node along the arc line. This makes it easier to gain the weak arc bonus, but it also means the character will be targeted by attacks against either arc. Note that even if a character is attacking multiple weak arcs, they gain just one black die for attacking the boss in a weak arc.

# BOSS TURNS

## Boss Turn Overview

During a boss encounter, the boss always takes their turn first. Draw the top card from the boss behaviour deck at the beginning of every boss turn. The boss’s actions are then determined by resolving each symbol on the behaviour card from left to right. When the right-most symbol is resolved, the boss’s turn ends. After each boss turn, one character may take their turn, then the boss takes another turn, a second character, and so-on. characters must take an equal number of turns and cannot take a turn until every other character has taken an equal number of turns.

If there are no cards remaining in the behaviour deck at the start of a boss’s turn, flip the discard pile face-down without shuffling the cards to renew the boss behaviour deck. Then, draw the top card of the boss behaviour deck as usual. Players can gain an advantage over a boss by memorising the order in which the boss behaviour cards will appear each time the deck is renewed.

## Boss Movement & Symbols

Bosses abide by the same enemy movement and attack rules previously described with the following exceptions.

When a boss moves towards a target, it first rotates to align the centre of its front arc with the adjacent node closest to the target character. Once facing is resolved, the boss moves forward into the adjacent node it has just centred in its front arc. The boss repeats this for each node of movement instructed by the current behaviour card.

When a boss moves away from a target, it first rotates to align the centre of its back arc with the adjacent node furthest from the target character. Once facing is resolved, the boss moves backwards into the adjacent node it has just centred in its back arc. The boss repeats this for each node of movement instructed by the current behaviour card.

If a boss behaviour card instructs movement towards a target already sharing the same node, the boss remains in the same node but rotates to bring the target model into the front-arc of the boss’s base. The target character model should remain static while the boss model rotates underneath to change facing towards the target.

If a boss moves into a node occupied by a character, the character is placed on the boss’s base in the arc the character was in at the beginning of the boss’s movement. If the character was on a node split between two arcs the player may choose either.

If a boss move behaviour does not indicate a target it moves the number of nodes indicated in the direction shown without rotating to change facing.

If a boss *leaps* into a node occupied by a character, the boss maintains its original facing and the character(s) are pushed into any adjacent node of their choosing unrestricted by the boss arc.

The *turn* symbol indicates the boss turns 90 degrees to the left or right as indicated by the direction of the quarter-turn arrow.

The *rotate* symbol indicates the boss turns 180 degrees to face their rear.

## Boss Attacks

Boss attacks follow the same rules as enemy attacks but can contain additional symbols with more powerful effects. When resolving a boss behaviour attack note the four boss arcs coded as either normal (black), attack (green), or weak (red).

The *area* symbol indicates that the boss is attacking entire arcs instead of an individual character. Bosses do not change facing when they attack and instead deal damage to characters within range that occupy nodes in their attack arcs. If a node is split and only half in an attack arc it is still targeted by that attack. characters targeted by an *area* attack either block*, resist,* or dodge as they normally would.

The *repeat* symbol is similar to the character’s equipment symbol and allows a boss to repeat its entire behaviour card multiple times before ending its turn.

## Boss Aggro

Boss behaviours may indicate their target as the character with the *aggro* token on their dashboard. The *aggro* token is assigned to the character with the highest *threat* value at the beginning of the boss encounter. Similar to basic encounters, the *aggro* token moves to the character who dealt the most damage before the boss's next turn. Because the boss takes a turn after each individual character takes their turn, one can assume any damage dealt by a character will be the most damage prior to the boss’s activation. Therefore, simply move the *aggro* token to a character’s dashboard if they deal any damage to the boss during their turn. If no damage is dealt, the *aggro* token remains stationary for that turn.

## Boss Death

When the boss’s health dial is reduced to zero, the boss is slain and the encounter ends. If this was the final boss (main or mega), the characters are victorious and the players win. If not, determine boss treasures and collect soul rewards as previously described. Then, increase the spark dial by one and give each character an *ember* token to mark them as *embered.* Finally, the characters return to The Bonfire to rest at no spark cost. They may visit the Firekeeper and Andre if they wish.

This is often a reasonable time to end a game session if time is limited. Either today or another day the players may continue on with post-boss setup rules as described earlier in these rules.

# BOSS SPECIAL RULES

## Asylum Demon & Stray Demon

### Pillar Smasher

During the boss encounter setup, place one pillar token on each of the four pillar nodes found on the mega-boss game board. Pillars block movement.

When the Asylum Demon or Stray Demon makes a physical attack with an *area* icon, check to see if there are any pillar tokens within the affected area. characters within one node range of the pillar token immediately suffer one damage and the stagger condition. Then remove that pillar token from the encounter tile. Resolve the area attack itself (including any pushes or dodges) after resolving the pillar’s destruction. Depending on positioning, it is possible for a demon to smash through multiple pillars at once or even for a model to be hit by multiple shattering pillars at once.

Once a pillar is destroyed, pillar nodes are treated as basic nodes for the remainder of the encounter. When the party rests at the bonfire, return any destroyed pillars to their pillar nodes on the mega-boss game board.

### Wind Up

When the Wind Up behaviour icon is resolved, the characters may each spend one stamina to immediately move their model to an adjacent node. This movement is considered a *Run* for purposes of rules interaction.

#  CAMPAIGN PLAY

## Setup

The setup for a campaign is largely the same as the basic game. However, each section of a scenario has varying numbers of exploration tiles, may contain multiple bosses, and may have encounters with special rules. Campaigns provide a more challenging and longer play experience suitable for players who have played the basic game at least a few times..

Players might expect to meet over multiple play sessions to complete a campaign. A single section of a campaign might serve as a convenient amount of content to cover in a session. While campaigns can vary in length, the included campaigns are all three sections long.

When starting a campaign, the players must first choose one of the available campaigns. This rule-set includes three campaigns which require only the components available in the core set. Many of the game’s expansions also add additional campaigns for selection.

## The Fog Gate

The fog gate does not provide a short-cut to the boss encounter in campaign play as it does in the basic game. The fog gate token is purely aesthetic in campaign play.

## Sparks

The characters start a campaign with the same number of sparks used in the basic game (six minus the number of characters). The starting number of sparks also serves as the maximum number of sparks.

Each time a mini-boss, main-boss, or mega-boss is defeated, the characters gain one *spark* as in the rules for the basic game. However, characters no longer rest at the *bonfire* for free after defeating a boss except for the final boss of each section. Sparks cannot be purchased or gained otherwise. The players lose the campaign if all characters are lost to the hollow without a *spark* to revive them.

## Adding & Dropping Players

During campaign play, sometimes a new player would like to join the characters part way through or an existing player is required to leave. New players can join a campaign that is in progress at the cost of one spark each. The new player begins the game with starting character stats and equipment. The new maximum number of sparks is based on the new number of characters. If a player character leaves the characters, the characters gain one spark, the character’s equipment is added to the inventory, and the maximum number of sparks is changed to reflect the new number of characters.

## Encounter Soul Rewards

- Encounter *soul* rewards are reduced by one compared to the basic game.

- Encounters that have been *cleared* may be either skipped for stamina cost or repeated for *souls* rewards but not treasure rewards, just as in the basic game.

## Encounter Treasure Rewards

- Encounters still reward a single treasure card to each character after the first time an encounter is *cleared*, as in the basic rules.

- All encounters and chests reward tier one treasures initially, even after a mini-boss is defeated.

- All encounters and chests reward tier two treasures after tier two treasure deck is unlocked by paying 16 *souls* to burn bonfire ascetic.

## The Bonfire Tile

The rules for returning to the *bonfire* are essentially unchanged from the basic rules. Characters may return to the *bonfire* to revive, rest, improve their stats at the Firekeeper, and visit Blacksmith Andre (attach upgrades, sell treasure cards, and/or purchase treasure from Andre's Shop). The cost to level up stats at the Firekeeper is increased in Campaign play to slow the characters’ progression to scale better across a longer journey. Characters may now also transpose boss items and burn bonfire ascetic.

### Blacksmith Andre's Shop

Andre's Shop functions similarly to the basic rules with some changes to how the shop is stocked to better suit the progression of the campaign. Use the basic rules for Andre's Shop with the following modifications.

- Tier one and two shops are only restocked after each section is completed. This means that if a section has multiple bosses, only defeat of the section's final encounter will restock the shop.

- Tier two treasures are only available after the characters pay 16 *souls collectively* to burn bonfire ascetic and unlock tier two treasures.

- Never add legendary or boss treasure to either tier of treasure deck.

- Boss treasure is only obtained by defeating bosses.

- Legendary Treasures are now only available through transposition.

### Bonfire Ascetic

In campaign play, characters do not gain access to tier two treasures freely after defeating a boss. They must decide at what point their need for better equipment outweighs the cost of increased difficulty. Characters may pay a communal one time fee to burn the bonfire ascetic to gain access to tier two treasures. A total of sixteen souls may be paid from any combination of character contributions while resting at the bonfire. Once the bonfire ascetic has been burnt, characters immediately populate the tier two shop. Thereafter, any treasure cards earned through encounters or opening chests come from the tier two treasure deck. The tier one shop does not restock when burning bonfire ascetic; the characters must defeat a boss to restock the shop(s).

However, burning the bonfire ascetic invites stronger enemies. After the bonfire ascetic has been burnt, all non-boss enemies increase their attack damage by one for the remainder of the campaign. This effect is cumulative with stat scaling for character count. This does not affect invaders, mini-bosses, main-bosses, or mega-bosses.

### Transposition

After the final boss of the first section is defeated, characters may elect to transpose boss treasure to obtain legendary treasure. Transposition is only available after the characters spend a *spark* to rest at the *bonfire*.

First, players gather all available legendary treasure cards and shuffle them to create the legendary treasure deck. Second, five legendary treasure cards are dealt face-up near the *bonfire* tile. Third, characters may now remove a boss treasure in their inventory from the campaign, pay four *souls*, and obtain any of the five available legendary treasure cards. Fourth, deal another legendary treasure card face-up by the *bonfire* tile to keep five transposition options available.

# PLAYER VS PLAYER

- Decide during setup if PvP rules will be used or not.

- After each boss (mini, main, and mega) a PvP encounter will occur.

- Remove all enemy models and traps from the boss encounter board.

- Restore each character as if they rested at the *bonfire*.

- Place each character in a corner of the boss encounter board.

- Dead characters are excluded from PvP.

- Phantoms (Summons and Invaders) are excluded from PvP.

- *Aggro* token is not used.

- *First* *turn* token is given to the character with the highest threat value.

- Characters take turns in descending threat order.

- Character turn structure is unchanged from basic rules.

- Players place a trap token on an outermost available node after each turn.

- Characters that move onto a trap token may attempt to *dodge*.

- Characters that fail to *dodge* a trap immediately die.

- Dying in PvP does not cause any penalty.

  - Souls are not dropped.

  - Ember is not removed.

  - Hollow cubes are not placed.

- The last character alive is awarded victory.

- Victory on non-final bosses provides +1 attack damage in future PvP only.

- Victory on the final boss determines the ultimate champion.

- Dodge difficulty is based on an the attacker attack option*’s* stamina cost:

  - \[0\] -\> 4

  - \[1\] -\> 3

  - \[2\] -\> 2

  - \[3\] -\> 1

  - \[4\] -\> 1

  - \[5+\] -\> 0

**Weapons - Updated Stat Requirements**

The first edition of DStBG has many nonsensical weapon stat requirements which this edition aims to remedy. Specifically, many high strength requirement weapons from the video game series where given intelligence and/or Faith requirements. Weapons with intelligence and/or faith requirements that do not deal magical damage where assessed. If the assessed weapon had no intelligence and/or faith requirements in the source material, the stats were revised for this edition. Revisions typically involved removing inaccurate stat requirements and adding a strength or dexterity requirement if previously zero. If the weapon already had both strength and dexterity requirements, the weapons primary stat requirement was often increased to ensure overall balance remained roughly equal to the first edition.
