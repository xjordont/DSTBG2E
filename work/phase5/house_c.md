## 1. Hollowed Edition Extension - Boss scaling.pdf

Drive id `1srYxiUGdVwM152grQwhzhEzMwr4raNt0`. One page, marked "Master0.2", signed "@Santuric". Read in full.

1. **Round structure:** nothing.
2. **Scaling with the number of players:** nothing. The scaling here is between boss tiers, not player counts. Quoted in full because the formula style is the interesting part:
   - "Main to mini downscaling: Health: x, Heatup: x, Armor: -1, Resistance: -1, Damage: -1, Dodge: -1, Hand size: x"
   - "Mega to main downscaling: Health: -15, Heatup: -10, Armor: x, Resistance: x, Damage: -1, Dodge: x, Hand size: -1"
   - "Mega to mini downscaling: Health: -15, Heatup: -10, Armor: -1, Resistance: -1, Damage: -2, Dodge: -1, Hand size: -1"
   - "Reverse the above values in case of upscaling." "If you want to move a boss between mini and mega, just add both types of changes." "Note that dodge values on boss attacks cannot go below 1."
3. **Aggro / targeting:** nothing.
4. **Player versus player:** nothing.
5. **Simple and clever:** a boss is rescaled with a flat modifier line applied in the players' heads, "while still using the cards you already have": no new cards. The author admits the limit: "downscaled megabosses still retain their special mechanics and complexity", and for Four Kings and Executioner's Chariot "simply adjust their health up a little."
6. **Problem it solves (its own words):** "Boss scaling allows players to customize the order in which they face bosses ... Want to fight a megaboss early, or play a boss order that fits the video game?"

## 2. Hollowed Edition Extension - Endgame PvP.pdf

Drive id `1YsOOBM-vPYUCUtZm-YAs5M31vn9qsd6u`. One page, "Master0.2", signed "@Santuric". Read in full.

1. **Round structure (PvP only):** "The player who scored the killing blow on the boss goes first. Turns proceed clockwise as normal." Nothing on normal encounters or boss fights.
2. **Scaling with the number of players:** nothing (free-for-all among however many characters there are; "Place all characters in each corner of the recently used boss tile").
3. **Aggro / targeting:** nothing.
4. **Player versus player:**
   - **Setup:** "After defeating the last boss, all players are fully healed and embered. They have one last chance to adjust their equipment before the fight begins. Any contested claims on items are determined by coinflip." "Place all characters in each corner of the recently used boss tile."
   - **Turn order:** killing-blow player first, then clockwise.
   - **Allowed actions:** "Players may take any action they would normally be able to on their turn, except estus flasks or embers, as that would be dishonorable. Luck tokens and heroic actions may be used once."
   - **How attacks are defended:** "Dodge difficulty is determined by the stamina cost of an attack. 0-1 cost - 3 difficulty; 2-3 cost - 2 difficulty; 4-6 cost - 1 difficulty." (Cheaper, faster attacks are harder to dodge.) Nothing is said about block or resist; presumably the defender's normal defence applies, but the file does not say.
   - **How it ends:** "When only one player remains, your journey has come to an end."
   - **Rewards:** none beyond the title: the survivor is "the next Lord of Cinder".
5. **Simple and clever:** deriving a character attack's dodge difficulty from its stamina cost (three bands) gives every weapon card a dodge value without printing anything new. The file also flags the campaign effect of a known final duel: "Do you gimp yourself to support the party, or do you pursue selfish greed for a chance at winning the duel."
6. **Problem it solves:** "an optional endgame round of PvP after the last boss has been defeated, to determine who becomes the next Lord of Cinder."

## 6. DARK SOULS VG V.1.docx

Drive id `1Z3-5YIqZI1lrvSRijuDtJPXwUiiYi5p6`. Read on the first attempt. No author name is printed. The text returned covers "Section I: Standard game rules" (pages 4 to 32 by its own table of contents) and stops at the heading "UNDEAD ASYLUM SETUP" in the quest section; the cover lists "STANDARD (I) 4 players" and "HARDCORE (II) 3 players", but no Section II text was returned (only two "Hardcore" lines inside Section I), so the Hardcore variant is essentially unseen. Setup diagrams, if any, are images and were not returned.

1. **Round structure:**
   - Normal encounter ("ENCOUNTERS", p.25): "When entering an encounter the players roll one green die to see who goes first. Blank is enemies. Right side up, players. Encounters follow the standard order of the core game. All enemies/player 1/all enemies/player 2 etc. If the green die is rolled for the players to go first then the order is the opposite." So: the published alternating structure is kept; the only change is a dodge-die roll for who starts.
   - Boss ("BOSS ENCOUNTER", p.28): "Boss encounters remain the same as core game ... Bosses can't combo and always go first in the fight."
   - Character turn ("PLAYER ACTIVATION", p.7): "Players will make their move then attack or if the scenario allows it, attack and move. Players will recover the standard 2 stamina at the beginning of their activation."
   - Nothing on players choosing their order.
2. **Scaling with the number of players:**
   - Souls ("SOUL REWARD", p.5): "Encounter level 1 = 2 soul for each player; level 2 = 4 souls for each player; level 3 = 6 souls for each player. After completing an encounter, multiply the encounter level by the monsters defeated on the tile." Worked example (solo, level 2, four monsters): "level 2 encounter x 4 monsters defeated - 8 souls + tile completion of encounter level 2 - 4 souls; 8 + 4 - 12 souls total". (The example's own prose says "2 souls received for the tile" but its arithmetic uses 4; the file is inconsistent.)
   - Sparks (p.6): "Spark is only set to 1 (no matter the player count.)"
   - Estus (p.16): "Players share an estus pool of 4. This includes, solo, two, three, and four players. Resets only after resting at the bonfire. Heals both stamina and health." Hardcore: "Players share an estus pool of 4 ... Heals health only." "When estus is used by any player, the total amount of estus will change until next rest at the bonfire."
   - Shop prices (p.29): priced by the soul cost of the stat tier needed to wield; "If multiple players are playing then players will determine the price off the lowest stat (longest to level toward wielding)."
   - Levelling cost (p.4): tier 1 = 4 souls, tier 2 = 8, tier 3 = 16, tier 4 = 20 (not player-count dependent).
   - Enemy health, damage, number of enemies, number of activations: nothing.
3. **Aggro / targeting:** no aggro token is mentioned. Targeting is by proximity, and an enemy that has already attacked moves on: "An enemy will always exhaust their movement on their given data card. This can lead to the enemy performing their actions backwards or splitting the actions." Example: after attacking a player it failed to kill, on its next turn the Hollow Swordsman "will attack and now move one space toward the nearest player. In this case he moves away from the player he attacked and toward the next player. If you're playing solo, then the Hallow Swordsman will simply move away from the player one space in any direction." (p.17-18)
4. **Player versus player:** no rules for players fighting each other. The nearest thing is the "hallowed version of the one who died" (see 5).
5. **Simple and clever:**
   - **Dead character returns as an enemy** (p.6): "When the dial reaches 0, the party or player must conquer the tile they've failed to receive the lost spark. In doing so a hallowed version of the one who died will appear ... based on the equipment of the dead player": "Four moves toward the nearest player (goes first on enemy activation and exhaust movement)"; armour = "highest die value on armor" (the higher of armour or shield, e.g. blue die = 3); attack = "highest die value on weapon"; "Ten health points." Reward: "Players can reset their spark and all of their resources including Estus when defeating the tile. Also the players get to continue from the conquered tile."
   - **Enemy combo on big hits** (p.18): "if an enemy attacks you for 3 damage or more ... they get an attack every time the damage recieved is 3 or higher." Bosses do not combo.
   - **Player combo** (p.12): "If the player makes a successful attack, the player is given the option to make an additional attack at the cost of 2 stamina. This method can stack however many attacks the player's stamina will allow."
   - **Guard break** (p.11): "For two stamina, roll the die associated with the shield. The number rolled should be equivalent or higher than enemy's armor points" to negate armour; done after an attack it "stuns an enemy so that they can't move/attack on their next activation". Not on bosses.
   - **Dodge** (p.21): "Spend 2 stamina, roll green die and move character to any adjacent node. If the roll is unsuccessful, move character to any adjacent node. Then roll the die associated with the armor, not the shield."
   - **Parry / counter** (p.22): "roll a block equal to or higher than the enemy's attack", then "Spend 2 stamina. Roll [the] green di[c]e ... Dodge difficulty on an enemy applies the same difficulty on a parry chance", then make an attack out of turn. A failed parry that lets through 3 or more damage "allows the enemy a 2nd attack".
   - **Backstab** (p.24): "After a successful dodge roll ... move the character to an adjacent node with an enemy for a free attack that negates the enemy's armor." Not on bosses.
   - **Taunt = pass** (p.14): skip move and attack to "recover additional 2 stamina on top of the 2 stamina recovery".
   - **Two-handing** (p.9): stow the shield to "add one black die roll to an attack of your choice"; one-handing a two-handed weapon costs "two additional stamina".
   - **No free healing:** "Players do not recover health after an encounter. Only stamina."
   - **Loot per kill** (p.26): each player takes "one skull token for each monster they've defeated", then rolls "the number of green di[c]e equal to that of skull tokens ... For each face up die, collect one loot card." Three inventory slots per player; "Souls are still shared after an encounter."
   - **Shop** (p.29): "Draw 5 cards from the loot pile. Place them face up on the bonfire tile"; refresh all five for 1 soul; unrequired-stat items cost 2 souls; sell for 1 soul.
   - Souls are dropped on death: "Venture toward the original spot where slain to retrieve lost souls."
6. **Problem it solves:** "Darksouls VG brings an experience closer to that of the video game. With more moves and strategies reminiscent of the game, players can expect to conquer an encounter with more skill than luck."

## 8. Hollowed Edition v1.3 (base ruleset)

Local text `/home/user/DSTBG2E/work/text/Hollowed_Edition_v1.3.txt`. "Hollowed Edition, Version 1.3, by Santuric". Eight numbered pages plus a quick-reference page. Read in full.

1. **Round structure** ("Combat: Activations", p.7): the published alternating structure, restated:
   - "Turns are divided into activations. Enemies always activate first when entering an encounter. Invaders activate after enemies, if present. One player then activates, according to turn order, followed by a white phantom, if present. ... Then, repeat from monster activation. The player to the left of the active player is next to activate."
   - First player: "When first entering an encounter, the first player to activate is the one who holds the first activation token." "After finishing an encounter, give the first activation token to the player on the left from the current holder." Setup: "Choose a player to receive the first activation token."
   - Players cannot choose their order (fixed clockwise). Nothing separate is said for boss fights.
   - Character turn ("Actions", p.7): "a player can perform a major and a minor action, or two minor actions. Actions can be done in any order. Free actions can be done at any time during the activation, but only once each." Major: Attack; Rest ("Recover all of your stamina"). Minor: Run ("Move any number of nodes at the cost of one stamina for each node moved"); Estus ("recover all health, and cure all conditions"). Free: Walk ("Move one node in any direction, at no stamina cost"); Swap. "Gain 1 stamina passively on turn start."
2. **Scaling with the number of players:**
   - Sparks: "Turn the spark dial according to the amount of players." "The amount of sparks scale with the amount of players, and can only be increased by beating a boss or consuming an ember item ... cannot go above 5." (No table is given.)
   - Solo: "Solo players begin with 6 souls." (the only solo adjustment)
   - Encounter souls (p.4): "X+1 individual souls to each player where X is the level of the encounter."
   - Soul streak (shared souls for consecutive encounters without returning to the bonfire): "2x streak +1 shared soul per player; 3x streak +2 shared souls per player; 4x streak +3 shared souls per player."
   - Boss rewards: "Mini 10 shared souls +2 per player; Main 10 shared souls +4 per player; Mega 10 shared souls +6 per player", plus boss items "and 1 spark for the spark dial."
   - Invader rewards (p.6): "Standard 1 shared soul/player; Advanced 2 shared souls/player."
   - Summoned white phantom: "they gain only half the amount of souls rounded down."
   - Enemy health, damage, number of enemies, number of activations: nothing. (Because enemies still activate once per player activation, the published structure itself does the scaling.)
3. **Aggro / targeting** (p.7): "At the beginning of a player's activation, that player gains the aggro token." "No player holds the aggro token before the first player activates." Pushing: "If monster behaviour would cause it to move into a node with three models already on it, it must push another monster. If no monsters are on the node, the player with the lowest threat score is pushed."
4. **Player versus player:** none between players. Invaders (p.6): "Whenever any player becomes embered, place invader tokens on all undefeated encounters. All but one token must be empty. The remaining token is chosen at random, and indicates which phantom will invade." "Invaders spawn on the middle node of the board, and fight you alongside normal enemies. They always activate after normal enemies." "If any players are defeated during an invasion, the invader leaves. The invader does not come back." Quick reference: "Invaders trigger whenever a player is embered. Once per floor."
5. **Simple and clever:**
   - **Death does not end the encounter** (p.5): "If a player dies, the remaining players continue fighting. Only if all players die is the encounter lost. Dead players do get souls from the encounter. Once an encounter has been defeated, any dead players respawn."
   - **Hollowing instead of losing a spark:** "After the first death, place 2 black cubes on the right side of the bar. After the next death while hollowed, place 1 additional black cube ... for a total of three. If any player dies while on the second stage of hollowing, they are permanently removed from the game." "Hollowing can be reversed by becoming embered ... by consuming a spark while resting at The Bonfire."
   - **Ember buff:** "While embered, when taking 3 or more damage in a single hit, you may reduce that damage by 1."
   - **Full heal between fights:** "After defeating an encounter, all surviving players recover all health and stamina." Tokens rechargeable out of combat for souls: coin 1, pendant 1, estus 2.
   - **Dropped souls without a map marker:** "When a player dies, they drop their individual souls ... Dropped souls can only be regained by defeating another encounter without dying. If a player with dropped souls die[s] again before recovery, those souls are lost for good."
   - **Party wipe:** "If all players die in an encounter, the latest room to be defeated respawns. This room grants normal rewards ... Otherwise, encounters remain cleared, even when returning to the Bonfire."
   - **Block:** "roll all dice available according to your equipment. Choose any two rolled dice. These two dice represent the blocked damage." **Dodge:** "on a failed dodge roll a player may block damage from the attack using only the dice listed on their armor, and not weapons and shields."
   - **Shop:** three decks (Weapon, Armor, Magic); "Purchasing an item from any of the decks costs 1 soul." Levelling: 2 / 4 / 6 / 8 souls per tier; tiers 3 and 4 locked until the first boss is beaten ("Early Game" and "Late Game" phases).
   - **Conditions:** bleed "stacks up to 2 times ... the stack is consumed and the model takes 3 damage unaffected by armor"; poison "can stack indefinitely. At the end of a poisoned model's activation, it consumes all poison tokens, and takes damage equal to the amount"; stagger: player "-1 success on all attack rolls", enemy "damage values lowered by one"; frostbite: player "1 additional stamina to attack, and for each node moved", enemy "dodge and movement values lowered by 1". "All conditions are removed upon ending an encounter or dying."
   - **Procedural dungeon:** on entering each new room roll 4 dodge dice; fog door appears at 4 icons (first room), 3 (second), 2 (third), guaranteed (fourth).
   - **Summons:** one white phantom per tombstone found on the floor, at half souls.
6. **Problem it solves:** "to give players a more challenging, less grindy, and more rewarding dungeon crawling experience ... without radically overhauling the structure of the vanilla rules ... more focused on player decision making and resource management, instead of random chance and grinding, and allows for more time fighting bosses."

## 9. Extended Rules V45 ("Extended Ruleset")

Local text `/home/user/DSTBG2E/work/text/DS-Extended-Rules-V45.txt` (27 numbered pages, 1,655 lines; no author name seen). Searched for the brief's topics and read the matching passages (Revised Rules pp.2-3, the scaling tables, the relevant Encounter Events, the Weapon Arts, Scenarios and Combat Cards introductions). It was not read line by line: the bulk of it (pp.4-17 and 23-26) is flavour text and per-encounter special rules. Page numbers are the printed ones.

1. **Round structure:** nothing changes it. "All other rules from the core set that these rules do not override remain" (p.2), and no section overrides turn order; the text speaks of "the enemy turn", "each player's activation" and passing "the Aggro Token" as in the published game. Individual Encounter Events bend it for one encounter:
   - **Silent Tombs** (p.12): "Enemies do not activate on their turn. Each time a player moves to a node or completes an attack, check Dexterity. If the result of the roll does not meet or exceed the dodge value of an enemy's attack, that enemy activates immediately."
   - **Boggy Hollow** (p.7): "The enemies do nothing on the enemy turn until a Dexterity check has been failed. Before making an attack, the player must check Dexterity ... If the Dexterity check fails, the attack is cancelled and the enemies immediately activate."
   - **Shrine of the Torch** (p.12; Alonne Knights and a Captain): the knights "do not make their typical activations, but instead move one node closer to the gravestone on each turn ... The Captain's health and damage dealt are increased by the number of models on the card."
2. **Scaling with the number of players:**
   - **Encounter Soul Increase** (p.3; a difficulty dial that is also the player-count correction): "Increase core rules encounter soul yield by the number below for each player, except for Level 4 encounters: Game modes: Easy, Normal, Challenge, Impossible. 1-player: 15, 9, 6, 0. 2-player: 10, 7, 4, 0. 3-player: 7, 5, 3, 0. 4-player: 5, 3, 2, 0."
   - **Soul Rewards** (Revised Rules, p.2): "Players gain double the typical number of souls when completing encounters, except for Level 4 encounters and bosses."
   - **Sparks** (p.2): "Halve the number of sparks the party would normally gain, rounded up."
   - **Summons** (p.2): "the cost of a summon is two souls per player. This cost can be paid during any player's turn ... The summon is not removed from the game until it is dead or the party is dead, and does not heal at the beginning of encounters. There may only be one summon active per spark."
   - **Immediate Boss Fight** (p.3): for a mega-boss, "award the party 8 souls per player to use before facing the boss." Dungeon setup for main and mega bosses deals "each player 3 weapons, 2 armors, 1 shield, and 2 items" to choose 4 from.
   - Per-encounter examples: Withered Thicket (p.9): the brush barrel "has a health of 1 per player ... The chest has a number of treasures equal to the number of players." An event on p.11: "Models deal +N damage on attacks, where N is the total number of sparks used by the party since the beginning of the game."
   - Enemy health, damage or number of activations by player count as a general rule: nothing.
3. **Aggro / targeting:** unchanged in general. Two one-line exceptions: Weapon Art, **Mace** (p.20): "You may move the Aggro token to another character after this attack." Scenario rule (p.24): "A player that has the Dancer's Enchanted Swords and Dancer Armour equipped does not pass the Aggro Token."
4. **Player versus player:** nothing found (searches for PvP, duel, versus, invader, invasion found no player-versus-player rules).
5. **Simple and clever:**
   - **Heavy Armor: over-blocking costs stamina** (p.2-3): "Any Block or Resist roll result that is greater than the value of the attack being defended against costs the difference of the values in Stamina. Players may decline to roll any number of dice."
   - **Extended Actions** (p.2): each stat tier-up adds an "action cube"; spend one (it moves to the endurance bar, so it also costs a bar slot) to add a black die after an attack or block roll, or a dodge die after a dodge roll, if the gear used has a non-zero requirement in that stat. "At the beginning of an encounter, each player may elect to move one action cube from their endurance bar back ... they suffer one damage." Weapon Arts (pp.18-22) cost 1 cube (2 for legendary and boss treasures) and give each weapon a one-line special.
   - **Shortcuts** (p.2): "When an encounter is defeated, place the encounter card face up on the tile. When the party dies, they may take a shortcut to skip tiles that have face up encounter cards."

## Best ideas for an all-enemies-then-all-characters round

**First, the plain finding:** none of the eight files that could be read uses an all-enemies-then-all-characters round. Every one that describes a round keeps the published alternation (Hollowed Edition v1.3: "One player then activates ... Then, repeat from monster activation"; Dark Souls VG: "All enemies/player 1/all enemies/player 2 etc."; the Extended Rules leave the core order untouched). Likewise, none scales enemy health, damage or activations by player count as a general rule; they all leave that to the alternating structure and scale only rewards. So these files offer no tested precedent for the author's round, only parts that would help it. EmberEdition_v1.pdf could not be read and remains unknown.

The balance problem in such a round is that enemies act once per round whatever the party size, so a bigger party gets more character turns per enemy turn. The mechanisms below are the ones in these files that bear on it.

1. **Tie extra enemy activations to character actions, not to the round** (Extended Rules V45, Encounter Events "Silent Tombs" and "Boggy Hollow"; also Dark Souls VG's enemy "combo": "they get an attack every time the damage received is 3 or higher"). Enemies react when a character moves or attacks and fails a check, so the amount of enemy action grows with the number of characters by itself, with no table. Cost: a roll (or condition) on character actions; it adds dice-rolling to every turn and was only ever used as a one-encounter special rule.
2. **Scale the reward, not the enemy, with a small table** (Extended Rules V45 "Encounter Soul Increase": extra souls per player of 15/9/6/0 solo, 10/7/4/0 for two, 7/5/3/0 for three, 5/3/2/0 for four, by difficulty; Hollowed Edition v1.3: "X+1 individual souls to each player", bosses "10 shared souls + 2/4/6 per player", "Solo players begin with 6 souls"). Small parties are compensated through faster growth instead of weaker enemies; enemy cards stay untouched. Cost: one lookup at the end of each encounter; it balances the campaign, not the single fight.
3. **Fixed party-wide pools that do not grow with player count** (Dark Souls VG: "Spark is only set to 1 (no matter the player count)"; "Players share an estus pool of 4. This includes solo, two, three, and four players"). The advantage a larger party gets from more turns is offset because the same healing and retries are split among more characters. Cost: none in play (one shared counter); it is blunt and untested against an all-enemies round.
4. **A death does not end the fight; the party loses only when everyone is down** (Hollowed Edition v1.3: "If a player dies, the remaining players continue fighting. Only if all players die is the encounter lost ... any dead players respawn" with hollowing: 2 black cubes on the bar, then 1 more, removed by spending a spark to become embered). An all-enemies phase can pile every attack onto one character; this makes that spike a setback for a larger party rather than a wipe, and it is harsher on a solo character, which matches the direction the balance needs. Cost: a few cubes on the health bar and one rule for removal.
5. **One-line health multiplier, and a movable aggro token** (Covenants extension: "Your health and stamina is now doubled, but so is the health of all enemies"; Extended Rules: brush with "a health of 1 per player", Mace weapon art "move the Aggro token to another character after this attack"; Hollowed Edition: "At the beginning of a player's activation, that player gains the aggro token", invaders "always activate after normal enemies"). If enemies must be scaled directly, a single per-character health figure is the simplest form these files show; and under the Hollowed aggro rule an all-characters phase would leave aggro on whoever acted last, which becomes a player choice if characters may pick their order (that consequence is an inference; no file states it). Cost: one multiplication at setup per enemy; aggro needs no new component.
