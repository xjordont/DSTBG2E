# House-rules survey, report A

Ten fan-made rulesets for "Dark Souls: The Board Game", read from Google Drive. Each section reports only what the file says. Quotes are close to the source; where the text extraction scrambled a multi-column page, that is noted.

---

## 1. Vondy Souls Rule Book - v0.5.pdf

A two-page reference sheet for a heavily reworked game ("Vondy Souls", v0.5). The text extraction is scrambled by the multi-column layout, so sentence order was reconstructed; no page numbers survive, headings are given instead.

### 1. Round structure

- Heading "During Combat": "**Enemy Activation**: All enemies activate in order of threat number. Complete each enemy action fully before moving to next enemy. If multiple players are impacted by a single enemy action, each player responds simultaneously."
- "**Player Activation**: The player with the activation token activates. The activated player gains one stamina and can begin taking player actions. Once the player has decided on actions, they receive the aggro token and pass on the activation token to end their activation."
- "**Repeat**: Repeat the following steps until either Ending Combat scenarios is met."
- So this is the published alternating structure (all enemies, then ONE character, repeat), not all-enemies-then-all-characters. Turn order is fixed by passing the activation token to "the next player in the activation order"; no free choice of order.
- Boss fights: nothing separate is stated (the sheet says it covers "Combat Flow - Basic Enemies" only).
- A player turn is: one optional free action at the start (walk one node, swap equipment, or heroic action), one or two minor actions (run, rest, estus), one major action (attack, reaction, ember). "A player may not take two minor actions and a major action."

### 2. Scaling with the number of players

- Sparks (heading "Setup", step 5): "Set the bonfire sparks to X = 5 - Party Size + 1. Single player games are set to 5 with one shop improvement already made, as if a boss had been beat." (So 1 player 5, 2 players 4, 3 players 3, 4 players 2.)
- Souls (heading "Combat Rewards"). Two separate pools: "Party Souls" (spent on levelling and refreshing tokens, "only received if living") and "Player Souls" (each player gets this number, spent at the blacksmith, "counts all players regardless of death status").

  | Encounter | Party souls | Player souls (each) |
  |---|---|---|
  | Level 1 | 2 + 1 x soul streak | 2 |
  | Level 2 | 2 + 2 x soul streak | 3 |
  | Level 3 | 3 + 3 x soul streak | 4 |
  | Mini-boss | 5 x player count | 4 |
  | Main boss | 5 + 5 x player count | 5 |
  | Mega-boss | 10 + 5 x player count | 6 |

  Only boss party-soul rewards scale with player count; normal encounters scale with the "soul streak" instead.
- Treasure from a chest: "Draw 2 Tier One Treasures for each living player" or "Draw 1 Tier Two Treasures for each living player" (or, forgoing all other loot, one legendary treasure for the party).
- Entering a room: "If in a party of four, choose one player to push off the entrance tile."
- Enemy health, damage, number of activations, number of enemies: nothing.

### 3. Aggro / targeting

- At the start of combat: "Assign the threat token to the highest threat player and the activation token to the next player in the activation order."
- The aggro token moves to each player as they finish their activation ("they receive the aggro token and pass on the activation token").
- A "Protect" reaction (shield or great shield) lets a player "receive the threat token for X number of enemy activations", X being a roll of green dice by strength tier. The sheet uses "threat token" and "aggro token" for what appears to be the same token; it does not say so.

### 4. Player versus player rules

Nothing. (Invaders are summoned enemies: "Once per floor, if the player starting with the activation token is embered, an invader is summoned on any tile not adjacent to the bonfire.")

### 5. Unusually simple and clever

- **Soul streak:** "Each time you beat an encounter, increase this number by one then claim your reward. Resets if certain out of combat actions are taken" (talking to the Firekeeper, resting at the bonfire, visiting the blacksmith interrupt the streak). A push-your-luck reward for not resting.
- **Single endurance bar with Hollowing:** dying the first time adds two permanent stamina cubes to the right side of the bar (one more for each later death) until an ember, a spark at the bonfire, or a party wipe removes them. Death shrinks the bar instead of ending the character's game.
- **Death and revival:** players killed in combat are resurrected after a victory "with the appropriate Hollowing"; survivors "recover all stamina and two health".
- **Party wipe:** souls are dropped on the tile, players return to the bonfire, "Reduce sparks by two", the soul streak resets and the last completed encounter card flips back.
- **Chest gamble:** each chest has a hidden mimic card; the party chooses to loot or pass after the fight.
- **Reactions as major actions:** weapon-type-keyed set-up actions (Protect, Encourage, Parry, Mark, Throw, Expose, Dampen, Curse, Inspire, Rejuvenate) whose strength is a roll of "1 green die x stat tier".
- **Conditions apply to both sides:** bleed "at 2 stacks, take 3 damage" for enemies and players alike; stagger lowers enemy damage per stack.
- **Simultaneous defence:** "If multiple players are impacted by a single enemy action, each player responds simultaneously."

### 6. The file's own statement of its problem

None stated outright; it presents itself as "all relevant combat information not found in a standard rulebook", a replacement combat, reward and shop flow.

## 3. Classic_Dungeon_Crawl_Variant.pdf

A half-page official-style variant sheet (no author printed) with two variants: "Classic Dungeon Crawl" and "Boss Bash". One page.

### 1. Round structure

Nothing. The encounter round is left unchanged.

### 2. Scaling with the number of players

The only scaling is souls, in a table headed "Souls Per Encounter Per Player" (footnote: "Including Boss Encounters"):

| Players | Easy | Normal | Challenging |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 17 | 11 | 8 |
| 2 | 12 | 9 | 6 |
| 3 | 9 | 7 | 5 |
| 4 | 7 | 5 | 4 |

So the party total per encounter is: 1 player 17 / 11 / 8; 2 players 24 / 18 / 12; 3 players 27 / 21 / 15; 4 players 28 / 20 / 16 (totals computed here, not printed). A lone character gets roughly twice the per-head income of a character in a party of four. "Note: For a Nearly Impossible mode of difficulty, only gain 2 Souls per Encounter Per Player."

Enemy health, damage, activations, number of enemies, sparks, treasure: nothing (the spark dial is removed entirely).

### 3. Aggro / targeting

Nothing.

### 4. Player versus player rules

Nothing.

### 5. Unusually simple and clever

- **One life, no sparks:** "Ignore all references to the Spark dial. If a character dies, the players immediately lose the game." Removes the grind of re-running encounters.
- **Resources refresh only on a boss kill:** "Characters' Estus Flask Tokens and Heroic Action Tokens only refresh after they defeat a boss."
- **Difficulty as a single dial:** the whole difficulty setting is one number, souls per encounter per player.
- **Boss Bash:** "replace all of the encounters after the mini boss (that lead up to your selected main boss) with a mini boss or main boss you haven't faced yet."

### 6. The file's own statement of its problem

"The core element of gameplay in this variant is that the players have just one chance to defeat the encounters that lie before them."

## 4. Dark Souls The Better Board Game.pdf

A short bullet-list ruleset (no author printed). No page numbers in the extraction; headings are given. This is the one file so far that states an all-players-together turn.

### 1. Round structure

- Heading "Enemies": "Players must agree a turn order, this is kept for the whole game." and "**All players activate on a player turn**".
- So the round is: all enemies act, then all characters act in a fixed, agreed order (the order is chosen once by the players and never changes; it is not re-chosen each round).
- Boss fights: no separate structure is given; the same lines apply. In the player-versus-player finale, "Turn order is kept the same."
- Stagger: "Enemies that are staggered miss their turn."

### 2. Scaling with the number of players

- **Enemy health:** "Enemies have health * num players" (heading "Enemies"). Enemy damage and number of enemies are not changed.
- **Boss health:** "Bosses have more health depending on how many players there are. 3 players means the boss's gets 3 health dials worth of health."
- **Boss heat-up:** "Heat up is as close to half health as possible e.g 3 players means heat up is the heat up value of the middle dial, 4 players means heat up is when 2 dials have been used."
- **Souls, normal encounters:** per player, not shared and not scaled: "Lv1 encounter gives everyone 2 souls. Lv2 gives 4 souls Lv3 6 souls Lv4 8 souls." "Players earn their own souls, no sharing (unless for diplomatic reasons)." "Players start with souls equal to the number of sparks."
- **Souls, boss:** "Upon death, bosses grant souls equal to the real heat up value, shared between all players, the remainder is lost."
- **Number of activations, damage, number of enemies, sparks, treasure by player count:** nothing. (Treasure is bought: common 1 soul, rare 2 souls, legendary 4 souls.)

Net effect as written: the enemy side gets one activation per round whatever the party size; the party's extra attacks are paid for by multiplying enemy and boss health by the number of players.

### 3. Aggro / targeting

- "The last player who attacked gains aggro." (Aggro follows attacks, not turn order.)
- Crowding rules: "If a node contains more player characters then enemies, the enemy will always push a player character off first (players can choose), the enemy goal is to separate player characters, and gank them." "If a node contains more enemies than players, the enemy will locate the next nearest available target instead."
- "If an enemy is within range of its target, it will not move." "If an enemy is in the same node as a player and the enemy uses push attacks, the enemy does not move, instead the player is pushed away."

### 4. Player versus player rules

Headings "End game (Dark Souls Battle Royale)" and "PVP".

- **When:** after the game is beaten: "there can only be one winner. If there is more than 1 player, the final boss battle is each other."
- **Setup:** "If you have a mega boss tile then use it, otherwise just use a regular boss tile." "All sparks are destroyed before the battle, so no resurrections." "Players cannot use estus, coins, or abilities." "Players cannot heal other players."
- **Turn order:** "Turn order is kept the same" (the order agreed at the start of the game).
- **Defending against a player's attack:** the attack's dodge/parry difficulty is the stamina cost "dice inverted": "Look at the value on a dice and look at the opposite value. e.g a 1 stamina attack has difficulty 6, a 4 stamina attack has difficulty 3, a 6+ stamina attack has difficulty 1." Cheap attacks are hard to dodge, heavy attacks easy.
- **Riposte between players:** "the player rolls the dice on their armour to calculate their resistance (ignore left and right weapons). Then the Parryer rolls for damage (not forgetting the extra black die for the critical hit)."
- **Shrinking arena:** "At the end of every full player turn rotation, the board gets smaller Battle Royal style, the outermost nodes become dangerous, ending your turn on a dangerous node means you take 2 unblockable undodgeable damage. The battlefield gets smaller until there is only 1 safe node in the middle."
- **Conditions between players:** "Frostbitten players must pay 1 stamina to move, and 1 additional stamina for run, dodge, parry and attack." Health-healing spells also cure poison and bleed; stamina-healing spells also cure frostbite. "Players who initiate a push can choose the target of the push."
- **How it ends / rewards:** last player standing is the winner; no rewards stated.

### 5. Unusually simple and clever

- **Dodge and parry as levelled stats:** players start at level 1 in each; level 2 costs 2 souls, level 3 costs 4, level 4 costs 8. Level plus gear = number of dice rolled. Dodge "costs 2 stamina" and "can dodge on the same square, movement is optional".
- **Parry:** "Costs 1 stamina"; needs successes equal to the attack's difficulty + 1; failure means full damage. Ranged, push and multi-node attacks cannot be parried; bosses only if they wield a weapon. A parry interrupts the rest of a multi-part attack and grants a riposte: a free out-of-turn attack with +1 black die.
- **Death and sparks:** a dead player stays dead to the end of the encounter, loses all souls and earns none; "Before the next encounter, dead players may spend sparks to resurrect"; everyone resurrects free after a boss kill; a boss kill grants one spark and restores all tokens. "If everyone dies and there are no sparks left, everyone loses." Sparks can also be spent to restore everyone's tokens.
- **Ember:** "gained after killing a boss", "lost upon death".
- **Conditions on a timer:** poison and bleed last 3 enemy activations, frostbite 3 turns, stagger 1 turn (skip the enemy's turn).
- **Special abilities** are one-time use.

### 6. The file's own statement of its problem

None stated beyond the title ("The Better Board Game") and "Core changes"; the nearest statement of intent is "Encounters are easier with more people, so it's in the players' best interests to keep each other alive."

## 5. DARK SOULS BOARD GAME - (1 SPARK VARIANT) - FINAL.pdf

"1 Spark Variant for Shorter Play Times (for 1-4 players)", by Greema. Two pages (a cover of aims, then the rules); it says it was inspired by the published Dungeon Crawl variant, the "Guardian Dragon campaign rules", the "double souls / half sparks community variant" and other community variants. It changes the economy only.

### 1. Round structure

Nothing. The encounter round is left unchanged.

### 2. Scaling with the number of players

- **Sparks:** "Start with 1 spark regardless of number of players. Spark is consumed upon death or resting."
- **Treasure is unlinked from souls:** "Treasure cannot be purchased - treasure cards are earned by clearing a room... After a room is cleared, cannot earn treasure cards from it again." Treasure cards are per room (a party total, in two bands); souls are per player.

  | Encounter level | Treasure cards per room, 3-4 players (Normal / Challenging) | Treasure cards per room, 1-2 players (Normal / Challenging) | Souls per player |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | Level 1 | 5 / 4 | 4 / 3 | 1 + sparks remaining |
  | Level 2 | 6 / 5 | 5 / 4 | 2 + sparks remaining |
  | Level 3 | 7 / 6 | 6 / 5 | 3 + sparks remaining |
  | Level 4 | 9 / 8 | 8 / 7 | 8 (encounter does not reset) |
  | Mini-boss | boss treasure cards only | boss treasure cards only | "4 per player times number of sparks remaining (then reset sparks to 1)" |
  | Main boss | boss treasure cards only | boss treasure cards only | "8 per player times number of sparks remaining (then reset sparks to 1)" |
  | Mega boss | boss treasure cards only | boss treasure cards only | "Win condition met" |

- **Floor clear bonus** ("awarded if all rooms are cleared (excludes level 4 encounter)"): "3-4 player: Any combination of souls / treasure cards that total 6 (normal) or 4 (challenging). 1-2 player: Any combination of souls / treasure cards that total 4 (normal) or 3 (challenging)."
- **Solo head start:** "1 player game starts with 2 free treasure cards." "1 player game starts with 2 souls."
- **Stat upgrade gate scaled by player count:** tier costs as the core game (2, 4, 8 souls), but "All players must have 2 x # of players tier 2 purchased before upgrading to tier 3 (i.e. 4 players need 8 tier 2 upgrades purchased between them, 3 players need 6, 2 players need 4)"; for the last three tier 3 upgrades per player, "All players must have all remaining tier 2's purchased."
- Enemy health, damage, activations, number of enemies: nothing.

### 3. Aggro / targeting

Nothing.

### 4. Player versus player rules

Nothing.

### 5. Unusually simple and clever

- **Souls per player, treasure per room:** the stated reason is that this "ensures stat upgrade progress is balanced regardless of player count" and "ensures % of treasure deck revealed is balanced regardless of player count".
- **Unspent sparks pay out:** normal encounters pay "N + sparks remaining" souls per player and bosses multiply by sparks remaining, so not resting is rewarded; sparks reset to 1 after a boss.
- **Gravestones scout the boss:** "reveal the same number of boss behaviour cards as gravestones cleared starting from the top of the behaviour deck. After revealing, turn all the revealed cards face down but do not shuffle."
- **Tokens:** "Replenish when resting or upon death. Can replenish luck token between encounters without resting (1 soul). Replenish after boss defeated."
- **Harder rooms pay more:** treasure and souls rise with encounter level.

### 6. The file's own statement of its problem

"Faster play than core rules"; it "attempts to address some of the strange balancing issues with lower player counts."

## 6. DS-Prepare to Die. DS más difícil.docx

Despite the Spanish file name ("DS harder"), the text is an English forum post (no author printed) describing personal house rules "to make it more RPG and less board game". One page of bullet points; no headings.

### 1. Round structure

- "players choose which heroes activate next, and can be activated back to back."
- The post does not restate when enemies act; it keeps talking of single "activations" of heroes, so the enemy-after-each-hero-activation rhythm appears to be kept, with the change that the players freely pick which hero takes each activation, including the same hero repeatedly. (The file does not say "enemies act after each hero" in so many words.)
- "stamina does not regenerate on your activation but instead you gain 1 stamina at the beginning of any other heroes activation." This is the brake on activating one hero again and again: a hero only recovers while others act.
- "the only free action you get is to move, you must spend a stamina to attack, and then add the cost of the attack afterwords."
- Boss fights: nothing separate.

### 2. Scaling with the number of players

- "always play with 4 heroes". The player-count problem is removed by fixing the party at four (the author tested "playing all four players myself").
- Souls: "each player gets 2 souls" per room into a personal stash; "For every consecutive room cleared the players add +1 to the community stash starting at 0. this bonus is lost when players go back to the bon fire and level up, buy items, die, ect." "when a boss/miniboss is defeated 8 souls are added to the community stash, as well as each hero gaining 2."
- Nothing else scales.

### 3. Aggro / targeting

Nothing stated. (With free choice of who activates, the post does not say how aggro is assigned.)

### 4. Player versus player rules

Nothing.

### 5. Unusually simple and clever

- **No sparks:** "Simply play the game until you win, or give up / run out of time / go hollow."
- **Stamina as fatigue across the party:** recover 1 stamina when someone else activates, none on your own turn.
- **Attacks cost 1 stamina on top of the attack's own cost;** only movement is free.
- **Special ability:** "once per encounter, at the cost of 1 stamina."
- **Only stamina recovers after a room:** "only stamina is recovered after a room is cleared"; stamina spent on spells or estus between fights "is carried into the next room".
- **Shared estus timing:** "Estus flasks can be used by any hero during any other heroes activation as well as their own." "Estus flasks only clear health, not stamina."
- **Embers** "heal both stamina and health", are gained from a main boss, found in the item deck, or bought for 10 souls.
- **Consecutive-room bonus** (a streak): +1 soul to the shared stash per consecutive room, lost on returning to the bonfire.
- **Shop:** "pull 5 cards and lay them out"; prices: normal 1, class 3, class special 5, mini-boss 10, legendary 15, boss 15 souls.

### 6. The file's own statement of its problem

"I wanted to really tweak the rules to make it more RPG and less board game... to make it feel more like the video game." The author's own caveat: "With 4 players this may mean someone doesn't activate as often during a fight."

## 7. New tactic rules for DS.docx

A one-page outline (no author printed) that replaces the encounter round with a phased "all enemies, then all characters" round. Headings are given. This is the closest match to the author's target structure.

### 1. Round structure

Heading "Order of actions within an encounter (aka one 'phase')", quoted in full:

> 1. Chars may swap items between their backup slot and hand slots
> 2. Execute all offensive actions
>    i. Enemies (order according to threat level)
>    ii. Chars (order is of players' choice)
> 3. Execute all other (non-offensive) actions
>    i. Enemies (order according to threat level)
>    ii. Chars (order is of players' choice)
> 4. (Enemies switch Behavior Icon side for next phase)

- So each round is split in two passes: first every attack (all enemies, then all characters, **characters in an order of the players' choice**), then every non-attack action (all enemies, then all characters).
- **Each enemy does only half its card per round:** heading "Enemies": "Active action per phase: Alternates between left and right Behavior Icon (start with left, all Enemies use the same side; can be marked with First Activation Token to know which is active)". An enemy with a single icon "Either: Defends (= double Block and Resist Values) Or: does the one action it has in every phase. I'm unsure about this so pick one and stick to it."
- **Each character does one thing per round:** heading "Chars": "Choose one of the following actions each phase: Attack - offensive action; Move - non-offensive (not if dodged earlier); Use item - non-offensive (like Estus Flask); Rest - non-offensive (not if dodged earlier)."
- "First Activation Token is not used" (re-used as the marker for which icon side is active).
- Boss fights: nothing.

### 2. Scaling with the number of players

Nothing explicit: no number changes with player count. The balance is structural: every enemy acts once per round (one icon), every character acts once per round (one action), so the ratio of enemy actions to character actions is fixed by the encounter card and the party size and no longer multiplied by the number of player turns.

### 3. Aggro / targeting

- Setup: "give Aggro Token to char with highest Taunt Level [was: players' choice]".
- It moves on a successful hit: under "Attacking", "If damage is equal or higher then Block or Resist Value -> Char gets Aggro Token." So aggro goes to whoever last beat an enemy's armour, not to whoever last took a turn.

### 4. Player versus player rules

Nothing.

### 5. Unusually simple and clever

- **Dodging costs your non-attack action:** "Chars who choose to dodge one or more attacks cannot move or rest in same phase." Because enemies attack first, the dodge decision is made before the character's own choice and constrains it.
- **Death is checked at the end of the round, not mid-round:** a character dies "if Endurance Bar is full at end of a phase" or if the bar "has no more space for Damage Tokens or Stamina Tokens during a phase". "Note: a char does not die if it fills the bar during its action(s) and another char afterwards restores some space in it." Player-chosen order lets a healer act after the wounded character.
- **Rest is an action, not automatic:** "Rest action: Gain 2 Stamina [was: start of their activation]". There is no free stamina each turn.
- **Enemies dodge:** after a character's damage beats Block or Resist, "Roll as many Green Dodge Dice as enemy has Dodge Difficulty. At least one dodge symbol? -> no damage."
- **A blocked attack is not wasted:** if damage is below Block or Resist, or the enemy dodges, the character "may attack with next weapon or spell".
- **Push attack:** "Move + Push action: No damage dealt on target node if moved more than one node."

### 6. The file's own statement of its problem

None stated; the title is "New tactic rules for DS", and the changes are marked against the original with "[was: ...]".

## 8. The Shop Experimental Rules.docx

"Experimental Rules for Dark Souls: The Board Game" (no author printed): four short paragraphs of rules followed by price lists for every treasure card. It changes the treasure economy only.

### 1. Round structure

Nothing.

### 2. Scaling with the number of players

Nothing changes with player count. The only link to the party is that the shop stocks "common treasure and your class(es)' treasure", so the stock grows with the classes in play.

### 3. Aggro / targeting

Nothing.

### 4. Player versus player rules

Nothing.

### 5. Unusually simple and clever

- **A fixed-price shop replaces the treasure deck:** "Instead of a treasure deck, you have a shop that buys/sells items." Every card has a printed price and a sell price of about half. Price bands as listed: common weapons 3 to 10 souls, shields 4 to 8, armour 5 to 10, spells and ranged items 3 to 7, legendary treasure 5 to 10, class treasure 4 to 10, transposed class treasure 4 to 10; Titanite Shard 3, Ember 3 ("Can't Sell"), "All Gems" 5, "All Rings" 5. Starting equipment sells for 1 or 2; boss items sell for 3 to 7 and are not on sale.
- **Chests pay souls instead of cards:** "When you open up a treasure chest, gain 3 souls if it was found in a level 1 encounter, 5 souls for level 2, and 7 souls for level 3."
- **Stock unlocks with the first boss:** "The shop initially only sells common treasure and your class(es)' treasure. When you defeat the first boss, it sells your class(es)' transposed treasure and legendary treasure."
- **Stock is limited by the physical cards:** "The amount of cards the set has is how much of each item can be purchased. When sold back to the shop, the item can be bought again (weapon upgrades are split from the item and sold normally). Boss items and starting items cannot be bought back."
- **Upgrades raise resale value:** a Titanite Shard gives the attached weapon +1 sell price; a gem +2.

### 6. The file's own statement of its problem

"Instead of a treasure deck, you have a shop that buys/sells items" (removing the random treasure draw).

## 9. Documento de Google Keep.docx

Despite the Spanish file name ("Google Keep document"), the text is English: a set of notes (no author printed) for a "Standard (Quick mode, recommended for a party that includes newcomers)" campaign with a menu of optional modules. Headings are given.

### 1. Round structure

Nothing changes the round itself; the notes keep talking of "the next player's activation". The one relevant line (heading "Introducing Unconscious state"): "An unconscious player can not take its activation turn, does not gain the aggro token, nor count as the closest enemy and the turn is proceded to the next player's activation instead."

### 2. Scaling with the number of players

- **Sparks:** "A game starts with the bonfire spark count tracker set to 5, regardless the amount of players." Optional "Individual sparks": "Each player in the party starts with 5 sparks. A spark is consumed when a player dies or resets the setup, spending 1 to revive at the bonfire tile. If a player have 0 sparks, it stays dead."
- **Souls:** optional "Individual souls": "Each player in the party starts with 0 souls and gain an amount of souls upon defeating an encounter being equal to the level of difficulty" (level 1 gives each player 1 soul).
- **Boss loot is bought with sparks, per player:** after a mini boss, "Reveal all the treasure associated with the boss, including x class cards, where x is equal to the spark count. Each player may decide to draw a class item for the cost of 1 spark, class transposed for 2 or a legendary drop for 3." Optional "Overspark": each player gains "3 sparks, minus the cost of what each player looted".
- Enemy health, damage, activations, number of enemies by player count: nothing. (Enemy stats rise by repetition, not by party size: "The next encounter which includes the same kind of creature you have defeated before from a previous setup gains +1 to damage and defense to both magical and physical. Defeating this encounter rewards the party 1 spark.")

### 3. Aggro / targeting

Only the unconscious rule: an unconscious player "does not gain the aggro token, nor count as the closest enemy".

### 4. Player versus player rules

Nothing.

### 5. Unusually simple and clever

- **Fixed short campaign:** three setups of five tiles each: levels 1/1/1/1/2 then a mini boss; 2/2/2/2/3 then a mini boss; 3/3/3/3/3 then the main boss.
- **Unconscious versus dead, read straight off the bar:** "If your endurance bar would be filled with one stamina spent or more, you are unconscious. If it is filled with only red cubes, you are dead." An unconscious player regains no stamina and can be killed (green cubes replaced by red); "Receiving a heal from another player will bring you back up. Estus flasks can be used on other players if you are on the same node." "If all players in the party are unconscious, you lose the game."
- **Fleeing and carrying:** the party may flee by all leaving the room; a player can carry a dead or unconscious player for one additional stamina to run and "can not do anything else while carrying someone".
- **Revival** (individual sparks only): carry the body to the bonfire and spend a spark; all dead players there revive and "each place a black cube on the right side of their endurance bar" until the next rest.
- **Embers, two uses:** on entering an encounter, discard it and "damage taken is reduced by 1 for the duration of the encounter"; or it triggers on lethal damage: "destroy the ember token and be brought back up and have three endurance remaining instead."
- **Gravestone token:** spent at the next setup to reveal the encounter cards face up and "remove one creature sharing the same icon from an encounter card" that the party has defeated before.
- **Barrels:** on destroying one in combat, "roll a black die... and gain souls equal to each sword icon".
- **Combat loot:** chests, gravestones and barrels must be used during combat or they carry over; unlooted chests become mimics at the next setup.
- **Shop as six face-up piles:** the treasure deck is split into six typed decks (two-handed/dual; one-handed; shields/bows/ranged/thrown; armour/leather; robes/spells/staves; upgrades); the top card of each is for sale at 2 souls and is replaced "once per spark consumed". "Roll a 6-sided die if looting a chest, drawing one card from the appropriate deck."
- **Upgrades:** 1 soul to attach an upgrade, or 1 soul to strip the upgrades off an item, destroying the item.

### 6. The file's own statement of its problem

"Standard (Quick mode, recommended for a party that includes newcomers)": a shorter, modular campaign.

## 10. DS variants by Robert Small.pdf

"Variant by Robert Small at BGG." One page, core box only ("no idea how these rules would go with other expansions"). It replaces the activation structure with an all-enemies-then-characters round in which each character picks its own moment.

### 1. Round structure

- Heading "Encounters": "Characters have two options during the encounter - activation or defense. **Enemies and characters have one activation each before all the activations refresh**, but only characters have defense. A character may make a defense every time they are the target of an attack."
- Heading "Activating Models": "During an encounter, enemy models and characters take turns activating, starting with the enemy models. **Enemy models activate all together.** Characters have three choices of when to activate: 1. Before a non-boss enemy. See 'Rush'. 2. When they are the target of an attack. See 'Riposte'. 3. After all enemies. When all enemies and characters have activated, begin again with the enemy models."
- **Rush:** "A player may activate before any non-boss enemy by spending 2 stamina."
- **Riposte:** "an activation that may be chosen after an enemy has targeted the character, but before the enemy has dealt damage. The character forfeits their movement." Not usable out of weapon range or against an attack with the area or leap icon; a second one-handed weapon attack is forfeited. "1. Spend 1 Stamina. 2. Roll dodge." If the dodge icons equal or exceed the dodge difficulty, "no characters are hit by the enemy's attack. Furthermore, the character may immediately hit the enemy with an action that ignores the enemy's block or resist... The stamina for the action must still be spent, and actions with a range, node, shaft or repeat icon cannot be chosen. The enemy suffers a minimum of 1 damage." If it fails, "the attack is resolved as usual, except the character cannot use a defense."
- **Order among characters who act "after all enemies":** not stated.
- **Bosses:** the round is the same, but Rush is not available ("before any non-boss enemy"). Riposte against a boss is not excluded.
- A character's activation: "1. gain 2 stamina. 2. gain the aggro token. 3. may swap items between their backup slot and hand slots." "A character may do their movement before their attack or after their attack (but not both)." Walk: "0 stamina for one node, once / activation"; run: "1 stamina / node".

### 2. Scaling with the number of players

Nothing explicit: no number changes with player count. The balance is structural: one activation per enemy and one per character per round, so enemy activations no longer multiply with the number of characters. Each character may defend every time it is targeted.

### 3. Aggro / targeting

"When a character activates they... gain the aggro token." So aggro sits with the character who activated most recently; because characters choose when to activate (rush, riposte, or after the enemies), the party steers aggro by its timing. Nothing else.

### 4. Player versus player rules

Nothing.

### 5. Unusually simple and clever

- **Dodge:** "Spend 1 stamina / dodge. Roll dodge. If successful then the character may move up to 1 node in any direction, and they are not hit by the attack. If unsuccessful then the character may move up to 1 node in any direction but is hit by the attack and suffers damage - (1 for each dodge icon)." A failed dodge still reduces damage by the icons rolled.
- **Riposte is the parry:** it spends the character's whole activation for the round on a counter-attack that ignores armour, and a failed attempt leaves the character with no defence. One procedure covers parry, initiative and risk.
- **Rush is paid initiative:** 2 stamina to act before an ordinary enemy; never before a boss.
- **Block or resist:** "As per the rules."

### 6. The file's own statement of its problem

"Love the game, but like some others want to change the activations."

## Best ideas for an all-enemies-then-all-characters round

A caution first: across the nine readable files, **no ruleset gives a per-player-count number for enemy damage, enemy activations or number of enemies.** Where the round is "all enemies, then all characters", the designers balance it in one of two ways: structurally (one activation per model per round, so nothing needs scaling) or by multiplying enemy health by the number of players. File 2 (bgg226352.pdf) could not be read and may hold more.

1. **One activation per model per round; characters defend every time they are targeted** (file 10, Robert Small; also file 7). "Enemies and characters have one activation each before all the activations refresh." With every enemy acting once and every character acting once, enemy output stops multiplying with party size, so no scaling table is needed. Cost: almost none; it is a deletion. What it leaves open is that a lone character faces the whole encounter card with one activation, which neither file addresses.

2. **Enemy health multiplied by the number of players; a boss gets one health dial per player with heat-up at the halfway point** (file 4, "The Better Board Game", which also has "All players activate on a player turn" in a fixed agreed order). The party's extra attacks per round are cancelled by proportionally more health, and enemy damage is untouched. Cost: one multiplication per enemy and more health tracking; boss heat-up needs the "middle dial" rule. It does not correct for incoming damage being spread over more characters.

3. **Enemies resolve half their behaviour each round, alternating sides, and each character takes one action per round, in two passes (all attacks, then all other actions)** (file 7, "New tactic rules for DS"). This halves the enemy burst that lands before any character can answer, which is the main danger of letting all enemies go first. Cost: moderate; a two-pass round, a marker for the active side, and an unresolved case the author flags for enemies with a single icon.

4. **Let each character choose its moment: pay 2 stamina to act before the (non-boss) enemies, spend the activation as a riposte when targeted, or act after all enemies** (file 10, Robert Small). This gives back the interleaving and tactical choice that a strict enemies-then-characters round removes, and the stamina price self-limits it at every player count. Cost: moderate; two extra timing rules and a riposte procedure with several exclusions (range, area and leap attacks, second weapon).

5. **Aggro follows the last character to attack or to get through armour, and death is checked at the end of the round with characters acting in an order they choose** (file 4: "The last player who attacked gains aggro"; file 7: "If damage is equal or higher then Block or Resist Value -> Char gets Aggro Token" and a character "does not die if it fills the bar during its action(s) and another char afterwards restores some space in it"). When all characters act together, "last to take a turn" stops being a useful aggro rule; these two replace it with something the players control, and the end-of-round death check lets a healer save a character after the enemy volley. Cost: low; one sentence each. File 9's "unconscious" state (bar full with any stamina in it = down but revivable, full of damage = dead) is a second, slightly heavier way to soften the volley.

Supporting economy rules for player-count balance outside the round (not part of the five): souls per player with treasure per room, so that "stat upgrade progress is balanced regardless of player count" (file 5, Greema); a souls-per-encounter-per-player table that falls from 17/11/8 for one player to 7/5/4 for four (file 3); sparks set to "5 - party size + 1" (file 1).
