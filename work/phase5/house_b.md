# House-rules survey, report B

Each section reports only what the file itself says. Text was read through the Drive text extraction, so icons and symbols printed in the originals (dice, soul and spark symbols, player-count pips) are lost; where a number depended on a lost icon this is said.

---

## 1. Dark_Souls_Revised_Rules_v130_by_Gorus_eng.pdf

"Revised Rules ... by Gorus, v. 1.30", 8 pages. "This set of house rules is an addition to the base game and overwrites some of its norms."

**1. Round structure:** nothing. The file does not change who acts when. The only related wording is in "remember encounters" (p. 2): "The player with the first activation token must remember ..." and "Give first activation token to next player", so a first-activation token that rotates between players is assumed. Double Boss mode (p. 6): "Bosses move one behind the other, first establish which one has the initiative. Use two action decks at once and follow the rest of the rules normally."

**2. Scaling with the number of players:** the only place is the souls table on p. 2: "To speed up play the number of souls earned per encounter are doubled compared to the original game (see below). For defeating bosses, players earn double the amount below." The table shows four values, "= 4, = 12, = 8, = 16", each next to an icon that did not survive extraction (almost certainly the player-count icons, but the pairing of icon to number cannot be confirmed from the text). Nothing else scales with player count: no change to enemy health, damage, activations or number of enemies by party size. Difficulty instead scales with **progress**: "The difficulty of enemies level up every time a boss is defeated" (Difficulty Chart, p. 8, levels 1 to 8+): rising "Penetration" ("All enemy physical attacks win +1 damage to a blocking player, including bosses. Energy attacks do not receive this modifier"), "Extra life" ("a life bonus for all minions and bosses", +1 rising to +4), "Boss Ember", "Maximum Shields" ("Enemies and bosses with shields of level three or higher will be lowered to two"), and spark price rising "4 x, 4 x, 4 x, 6 x, 6 x, 8 x, 8 x, 12 x" (the multiplier's unit is a lost icon, probably per character).

**3. Aggro / targeting:** nothing.

**4. Player versus player:** nothing.

**5. Simple and clever:**
- **Remember encounters** (p. 2): a won encounter is never refought. After resting, its card is face down with a soul marker; on re-entering, the player with the first activation token must recall which enemies, how many of each, and whether it was trapped. "All players receive one effort cube for each encounter card not remembered correctly", applied in the next combat. Souls are collected and the shop renewed either way.
- **Souls doubled** per encounter; bosses pay double that again (p. 2).
- **Andre's Store** (p. 3): the shop is three face-up treasure cards; "Whenever you clean a room, keep one card and replace two". Buy item 2 souls, sell 1 soul, "Mulligan: -2 souls can draw 2 cards again, only once per spark", "Just for you: -4 souls, place an item in the shop chosen from the first 6 cards of the deck", "Buy spark: cost in difficulty chart. Limit once per bonfire." Inventory limit of eight items.
- **Knight block cap** (p. 3): "sum only the roll of the 3 highest defense dice".
- **Two-handed reroll** (p. 3): two-handed melee weapons may re-roll one die from the attack.
- **Parry** (p. 3): "If you haven't moved and attacked (healing is allowed) in your previous activation, your character is considered ready. You can parry the next attack, even projectiles." Roll the orange die (and a dodge die; a dodge symbol cancels enemy damage). "Riposte: If you roll at least a 2+ on the orange dice you can return a free light melee attack in range. If you roll a 3+ the attack can also be the strongest of the weapon. If you roll a 4 it will also ignore armor. Failure: Take full damage of the enemy attack, without being able to block or dodge it."
- **"This didn't happen!"** (p. 4): "You can spend a spark to turn a bad defense/dodge roll into 'the roll you need to survive'."
- **Encounter objects** (p. 2): barrel breaks for souls (empty means treasure); grave: "Go close and spend 1 energy to read it. +1 Boss card. +1 bleeding token to be used on an attack against that boss"; chest gives two treasures at the end of the encounter.
- **Campaign** (p. 4): "Encounters: Place maximum 3 for each boss"; "Gargoyle: 2 are too many for the first game. Just put one in."
- **Death** (p. 5): "As with the base game, if a single character is defeated the party is returned to the bonfire."
- **Endless Dungeon** (pp. 5-6): one spark, 20 souls each, four free mulligans; each new tile is rolled (1 to 3 = encounter of that level, 4 = random boss tile), at most four consecutive encounters before a boss; score table (level 1/2/3 encounter 5/10/15 points, mini boss 30, boss 50, mega boss 100).

**6. Problem it solves (own words):** "Fast Play. Less Grinding" (p. 2): cut the repetition of refighting cleared rooms and speed up soul income. It does not touch the round structure.

## 2. The Homebrewery - NaturalCrit.pdf

Untitled, unsigned homebrew ruleset laid out with The Homebrewery (about 3 pages; headings "Setup", "Characters", "Encounters", "Out of Combat Actions", "Mechanics Detailed", "Death"). No author is printed.

**1. Round structure** ("During Encounters"): "Repeat the below steps until the Encounter ends. 1. Enemy Activation - All Enemies activate in order of threat number descending. 2. Player Activation - On the first turn, the player with the First Activation token activates. Otherwise, Player Activation proceeds clockwise." Read literally this is the published alternation: all enemies, then **one** player, repeat, with players taking turns clockwise (no free choice of order). No separate boss-fight structure is given. After a win: "Pass First Activation - Pass the First Activation token to the Player who would have activated next." A player activation is: "Recover 2 Stamina. Gain the Aggro token", then, each "only once per activation" in any order: swap equipment, move (1 node free, then 1 stamina per extra node), "Attack - You may make an Attack with each weapon in your hand slots", drink Estus.

**2. Scaling with the number of players:**
- Treasure ("Normal Encounter Rewards"): "Draw cards from the current Treasure Deck equal to 4 + the number of players and add these cards to the Inventory. After defeating the Mini Boss and receiving its rewards, draw cards equal to 2 + the number of players instead. After defeating the Main Boss ... draw a card from the Legendary Treasure Deck instead."
- Souls: "Each player gains 2 Souls" (flat per player, so the party total grows with party size).
- Sparks: "Place the Spark dial on the Firelink Shrine tile and set it to 5" regardless of player count; but sparks are consumed **per death** ("When a player dies, they immediately consume a Spark"), so a bigger party drains the same pool faster.
- Enemy health, damage, activations, number of enemies: nothing.

**3. Aggro / targeting:** "Initial Aggro - The Party decides which player starts with the Aggro token." Then at the start of every player activation: "Gain the Aggro token." So aggro sits on the player who activated most recently.

**4. Player versus player:** nothing (Invaders are enemy miniatures: "the Invader spawns on the center node of the Encounter").

**5. Simple and clever:**
- **Death does not end the encounter:** "If all players die in an encounter, the Party is defeated"; a single dead player "immediately consume[s] a Spark ... and return[s] at the end of the Encounter with all of their tokens flipped to the ready side."
- **Hollowing instead of game over:** with no sparks left, a dead player returns with "a grey cube to the right side of their Endurance Bar (to a maximum of 3)", which lowers maximum endurance; dying with 3 grey cubes makes the player a "Husk" who sits out until a boss is defeated. An Ember card removes the grey cubes.
- **Short rest after every won encounter:** "Remove all black and red cubes from each player's Endurance Bars and flip the Pendant token to the active side." Resting at the bonfire (spend a spark) readies all tokens.
- **Three once-per-rest tokens per player:** Heroic Action, Estus Flask, and "Lucky Coin ... reroll 1 die in their attack, block, or dodge roll."
- **Ember:** "if they suffer 3 or more damage from an attack, reduce the damage by 1"; or discard it to summon an ally.
- **Dodge:** "you may spend 1 Stamina to move up to 1 node then roll the dodge dice".
- **Levelling:** flat prices "Base to Tier 1 1 Souls, Tier 1 to Tier 2 2 Souls, Tier 2 to Tier 3 4 Souls"; souls are spent only on stats, treasure simply comes as drawn cards into a shared party inventory (no buying).
- **Losing:** "the Encounter is reset and they return to the Firelink Shrine tile" (no refighting of cleared rooms is described).
- **Mimic:** chest check is one dodge-die roll; blank means the chest becomes a Mimic.

**6. Problem it solves:** not stated. The file is a rewritten compact ruleset with no design note.

## 3. Praise_the_Fun_v1_3_1.pdf

"Dark Souls - Praise the Fun", v1.3, 7 pages plus a cheat sheet. No author name is printed in the extracted text.

**1. Round structure** ("Encounters / Activations", p. 3): "During an encounter enemies and characters activate in separate groups, starting with the enemies. When all enemies and characters have finished their activation, begin again with the enemies." So this file **is** an all-enemies-then-all-characters round. "Character Activations: Each character completes their activation one at a time in one of two groups: 1. Before the enemies (see Rush) OR 2. After the enemies." The same structure applies to bosses (Counter has boss clauses; no separate boss round is given).
- Order among players: the First Activation token fixes who is first, but it can be handed on at will: "When a character activates, but before they Move or Attack: 1. Two Stamina tokens may be removed from their Endurance Bar. 2. If they have the First Activation token then they may pass it to another player. 3. They may freely swap equipment ... 4. They gain the Aggro token."
- **Rush** (p. 4), the price of acting before the enemies: "Only characters who Rush can activate before the enemies: 1. No character can Rush if the character with the First Activation token doesn't Rush. That character must activate first. 2. Before the first activation, place on the Endurance Bar 1 Damage token for the character and then Stamina tokens equal to other characters who Rush. Three characters decide to Rush. Each character has 1 Damage and 2 Stamina tokens added to their Endurance Bar." (Cheat sheet: "1 Damage + 1 stamina/Rush character".)
- "Dash Through": "If every character Rushes then the party may move to the entry nodes of a tile connected to the current one. The encounter is not considered to be defeated", i.e. the party may skip a non-boss encounter.

**2. Scaling with the number of players:**
- **Rush cost grows with the number of rushers:** each rusher pays 1 damage plus 1 stamina per *other* rushing character (solo 1 damage; two rushers 1 damage + 1 stamina each; three 1 + 2; four 1 + 3). This is the file's only brake on the whole party acting before the enemies.
- Souls ("Ending An Encounter", p. 4): "2 souls / character for non-boss encounters; 4 souls / character for each mini boss; 6 souls / character for each boss; 8 souls / character for each mega boss." Mimic: "1 soul / character".
- Treasure is **flat**, not per player: "For non-boss encounters place 2 cards from the Treasure Deck into the Inventory"; for bosses, the boss treasure plus "1 card from the Treasure Deck for each spark remaining on the Bonfire Dial" plus "1 soul ... and 1 card ... for each face-down Encounter card remaining".
- Sparks: "The Bonfire Dial begins on 5 sparks" for any party size.
- Enemy health, damage, number of activations, number of enemies: nothing changes with player count. Enemies activate once per round, so the file accepts that enemies act less often per character as the party grows.

**3. Aggro / targeting:** "Before beginning an encounter the First Activation and Aggro tokens must each be assigned to a character. One character may have both." Thereafter each character "gain[s] the Aggro token" when they activate (cheat sheet: "must gain the Aggro token"). So during the enemy group's activation the aggro sits on whoever activated **last** in the previous character group, which the party controls by choosing its order.

**4. Player versus player:** nothing.

**5. Simple and clever:**
- **Counter** (p. 4), one rule standing in for parry and backstab: only during Rush, only by the character with the First Activation token, one counter per enemy, one melee weapon; roll the dodge dice of one weapon or shield against the enemy's dodge difficulty "at the same time as the enemy's (first) attack". "If successful, the enemy can neither complete its Attack nor Move. The character may do one Attack at ⸢Damage x 1.5⸣ [rounded up], with a minimum damage of 2. After this, the character and enemy end their activations. If unsuccessful, the character does no damage and cannot Block, Resist or Dodge that enemy's attack." Bosses: character must be in the front arc; Leap and Push attacks cannot be countered.
- **Death** (p. 4): "An encounter immediately ends when all enemies are defeated or a character is killed." "If any member of the party is killed then the party immediately rests at the Bonfire. Place the souls in the Soul Cache on the node where the character was killed."
- **Sparks as a reward timer** (p. 2): the dial goes down one on each defeat or rest, and "Each decrease reduces the bonus Treasure reward for defeating the boss"; it returns to 5 after a boss.
- **Two encounter cards per tile** (pp. 2, 4): a tile can be fought at most twice; a cleared side is "passed through ... without having an encounter". Unused face-down cards pay a soul and a treasure at the boss.
- **Full recovery after each win:** "remove all Stamina and Damage tokens from the characters' endurance bar."
- **Stat "Overload"** (p. 2): each tier of a stat above what the equipment requires gives a small bonus (Strength: melee stamina cost -1; Dexterity: +1 dodge die and +1 bow range; Intelligence: spell stamina -1; Faith: Estus usable at any time). Heroic Action and Luck tokens can be re-readied at the Firekeeper for 1 soul.
- **Level prices** (p. 3): "Tier 1 = 2 souls, Tier 2 = 4 souls, Tier 3 = 8 souls", then 8 souls per tier up to Tier 6. Treasure: 1 card per 2 souls; trade in 2 cards for 1 soul.
- **Conditions** (cheat sheet): "Bleed: next damage +2. Frostbite: character movement +1 stamina, enemy movement -1. Poison: 1 damage at end of activation. Stagger: character actions +1 stamina, enemy attacks -1 damage."
- **Quests** (p. 5): eight optional self-imposed restrictions, each worth 1 treasure card.

**6. Problem it solves (own words, p. 1):** "One of the biggest weaknesses in the vanilla rules is waiting for your turn while just defending against enemies ... With Rush all players can cut to the chase by choosing to take damage without the wait."

## 4. DS final rules.pdf

Short, unsigned list of add-on house rules (headings "Level up", "Skill checks", "Merchant", "Barrels", "Gravestone", "Treasure chest", "Boss encounter", "Hazard die", "Spend extra stamina"). It ends with two unfinished notes: "Create a map where you can open a shortcut after two or three fights." and "Improved enemy AI".

**1. Round structure:** nothing. (It mentions "the next player activation" and enemy "activations" in passing but changes nothing.)

**2. Scaling with the number of players:** nothing.

**3. Aggro / targeting:** nothing.

**4. Player versus player:** nothing.

**5. Simple and clever:**
- **Skill checks from the stat value** ("Skill checks"): roll one die chosen by your score in the attribute and count successes: "< 10 no die allowed; >= 10 dodge die; >= 20 black die; >= 30 red die; >= 40 orange". Used for barrels (strength/dexterity check: "gain as much souls as you roll successes") and gravestones (intelligence/faith check).
- **Hazard die:** "Roll a Black die. If you roll a blank, one of the encounter enemies does condition damage or has equipment", then a d6: "1 item, 2 stagger, 3 frostbite, 4 bleed, 5 poison, 6 estus flask". An enemy with an item uses it if it does more damage, and drops it when killed ("you get that equipment card and may immediately equip it"). Estus enemy: the first time it is killed it heals to full unless it takes damage on the next player activation.
- **Spend extra stamina:** "You can always spend two extra stamina to roll an extra black dice for attack or to reroll one dodge die."
- **Merchant:** "The Black Smith always has three items for sale ... To buy an item pay two souls. After each successful encounter, you may redraw two cards."
- **Temporary extra cube:** buy one extra stamina cube and one extra health cube at "ten souls per cube"; it is lost first and "Reset this cube every new encounter."
- **Tier 5** for eight souls with a perk per stat (Strength or Intelligence: an extra dodge die for attacks; Dexterity: reroll one dodge die; Faith: luck token refreshes after each encounter).
- **Gravestone:** spend two stamina next to it while enemies remain, intelligence/faith check; one success reveals a boss card, two or more also lets the party discard and redraw a boss card.
- **Chest:** "Draw one Treasure card from the actual Tier deck and one from the Tier +1 deck."

**6. Problem it solves:** not stated.

## 5. Dark Souls Express Rules.docx

"Dark Souls Express Rules. (Game time, 60-120 mins)", one page of rules plus a page of reasoning. "The variation of rules is written/designed by Louis Castell".

**1. Round structure:** nothing. The file changes only the campaign flow (how many encounters, sparks, souls); combat is untouched.

**2. Scaling with the number of players:** souls only, in a table "Soul reward / Game Flow / and Encounters (ignore encounters levels on Boss card)". Values for 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 players:

| Step | What it is | 1 player | 2 players | 3 players | 4 players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upon spark use | "Trading spark is not counted but dying or choosing to rest at the bonfire is." | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Encounter 1 | random low-level encounter | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Encounter 2 | random low- or high-level encounter | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 |
| Encounter 3 | random high-level encounter | 32 | 48 | 48 | 32 |
| Spark trade | "Only available between Encounter 3 and 4. You may trade 1 spark for this many souls; this can only be done once per boss." | 6 | 12 | 18 | 24 |
| Encounter 4 | "Same encounter as 3 (Treasure and barrels are reset)" | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 |
| Boss | "On all retries of boss you must repeat encounter 4 for no reward." | sparks reset to 2; Luck, Heroic and Estus tokens restored | | | |

So almost every reward is simply **2 souls per player** (or a multiple). The Encounter 3 row is printed as 32 / 48 / 48 / 32, which does not follow the per-player pattern; it is reported as printed and may be an error in the source. "Note: Total number of souls possible is equal to the classic game. (in 1 player game you get 16 souls at start as well)".
- Sparks: "All groups start with 2 sparks" at every player count.
- Enemy health, damage, activations, number of enemies: nothing.

**3. Aggro / targeting:** nothing.

**4. Player versus player:** nothing.

**5. Simple and clever:**
- **Sparks do not reset encounters:** "All groups start with 2 sparks and sparks do not reset encounters so you only have to fight 4-6 encounters per boss."
- **A spark pays souls when used:** "Upon using a spark either by choice or death: you gain 2 souls per player." "You may use a spark before any encounter or boss."
- **Spark trade:** once per boss, between encounters 3 and 4, one spark may be sold for 6 souls per player; "to keep the decision of having more souls or an extra chance for the boss".
- **Fixed four-encounter track per boss:** low, low/high, high, then the same high encounter again; "Low Level Encounter is 1 on mini boss and 2 on main boss. High Level Encounter is 2 on mini boss and 3 on main boss."
- **Boss retry tax:** each boss retry requires refighting encounter 4 for no reward.
- After a boss, sparks reset to 2 and all tokens are restored.

**6. Problem it solves (own words):** "the main flaw of the game is it can take up to 6hrs for a game. So these rules are designed to shorten the game while preserving the difficulty spikes of the original game."

## 6. Fanmade ruleset by Thibault.pdf

"My personal rules variant, for 4 players campaign mode. By Thibaut G". Headed "Dark Souls The Boardgame 4 players campaign rules modifications".

**1. Round structure:** nothing. Only a "player at his turn" and a "first activation token" are mentioned.

**2. Scaling with the number of players:** none; the file sidesteps the question by fixing the party at four characters: "these rules are made to be used by a party of four characters. Of course can be used by four players but I tested it with just one other player, and we were using two characters each." Souls are paid **per enemy killed**, not per player ("Souls reward for each enemy"): "Hollow = 1 soul, Silver knight = 1 soul, Large Hollow = 2 souls, Sentinel = 5 souls, Mini Boss = 10 souls, Main Boss = 20 souls". Sparks: "At their first rest at bonfire, the spark counter is turned at 5."

**3. Aggro / targeting:** nothing.

**4. Player versus player:** nothing.

**5. Simple and clever:**
- **Death removes one character, not the party** ("Player's death"): "When a player dies, he goes on the bonfire tile and the rest of the party tries to end the encounter without him. If they succeed, souls and treasure are acquired then the whole party move to the bonfire and uses a spark to revive the dead. If the player with the first activation token dies, the whole party dies."
- **Refill by waiting:** "If no run or action is used by a player at his turn, he removes every black cubes from his endurance bar."
- **Backstab:** "On a foe that lands on the same node as one player, another player who comes on that node may use a backstab with an attack of 0 range and 0 stamina (or the less powerful on his weapon) +1 blue dice. Backstab can not be used against boss because of weak points system."
- **Counter-attack:** "On a block of equal or superior value to the attack, a player equipped with shield can use a counter-attack immediately with an attack of 0 range and 0 stamina (or the less powerful on his weapon) +1 blue dice."
- **Mastering an encounter:** "If the party solves an encounter without anyone being hurt, without using any token, the encounter is mastered. The card remains on the room and every time the party use a spark, the amount of souls of this encounter is immediately added to the soul cache" (no refight needed).
- **Gravestone:** "can be used to reboot the room when the encounter is done, without using a spark, or it can be used to get a token back."
- **Levelling priced by the stat gain:** Tier 1 costs as many souls as the points gained in that stat, Tier 2 double, Tier 3 triple.
- **Fixed store:** a set of cards drawn at the start (3 weapons, 3 shields, 3 armours, 3 others), not replaced until the end of the scenario. "Buying: 3 souls = 1 common treasure, 6 souls = 1 legendary treasure. Selling: 4 common treasures = 1 soul, 2 legendary treasures = 1 soul."
- **Ember:** become human: clear all cubes and conditions, "-1 stamina for every action and get -1 damage for every hit equal or superior to 3."
- **Boss reward:** "+1 spark, +2 loot cards of the boss that are added to the store, every player not dead in the boss fight become humans."
- **Heroic actions that grow with tier:** each class's heroic action is rewritten with Tier 1/2/3 upgrades (for example Cleric: "Every character at range 1 remove 2 damages; Tier 1: range 2, -3 damages; Tier 2: range 3, -4 damages; Tier 3: range 4, -5 damages").

**6. Problem it solves:** not stated as a problem; the author describes it as personal modifications for a long, slow-levelling four-character campaign, and says he looked for a use for gravestones because seeing boss cards early "never helped my party".

## 7. More variety to fighting trash mobs.docx

One-page forum-post variant, unsigned (it links to the author's "push your luck faster play variant" thread on BoardGameGeek, which is not in this file).

**1. Round structure:** nothing changes; it refers to "enemy activation" and "a player's activation" as given.

**2. Scaling with the number of players:** nothing. Enemy behaviour scales with **encounter tier**, not party size: "The die rolled depends on the tier of the encounter. Black: tier 1, Blue: tier 2, Orange: tier 3."

**3. Aggro / targeting:** each rolled action names its own target, either "target nearest" or "target aggro" (for example Hollow Soldier "Stab: target nearest", "Leaping Strike: target aggro"). Who holds aggro is not addressed.

**4. Player versus player:** nothing.

**5. Simple and clever:**
- **Rolled enemy actions:** "During enemy activation, roll a die to determine the action of each model." The number of sword symbols rolled picks one of four actions per enemy type, each a short stat line. Full table as printed:
  - Hollow Soldier: 1 sword "Stab: target nearest: move 1: attack P4: dodge 1"; 2 "Barrage: target nearest: move 1: attack P2: repeat 2: dodge 1: stamina drain 2"; 3 "Leaping Strike: target aggro: move 2: attack P4: dodge 2"; 4 "Fire Bomb: target aggro: move 0: range 2: attack M4: push: node AOE: dodge 1".
  - Silver Knight: 1 "Combo Strike: target nearest: move 1: attack P3: repeat 2: dodge 1: stamina drain 2"; 2 "Charging Thrust: target aggro: move 2: damage P5: push: dodge 2"; 3 "Shield Bash: target nearest: move 1: dodge 2: stamina drain 3"; 4 "Crushing Blow: target nearest: move 0: attack M5: dodge 2: stamina drain 2".
  - Large Hollow Warrior: 1 "Swing: target nearest: move 1: attack P4: dodge 1"; 2 "Charge: target nearest: move 1: push P5: dodge 1"; 3 "Overhead Smash: target nearest: move 1: attack P4: push: node aoe: dodge 2: stamina drain 2"; 4 "Leaping Strike: target aggro: move 2: attack P5: dodge 2".
  - Sentinel: 1 "Vertical Swing: target nearest: move 1: attack P6: range 1: dodge 1"; 2 "Thrust: target aggro: move 0: attack P5: push: range 2: dodge 1"; 3 "Wide swing: target aggro: move 1: attack P5: push: target all nodes in range (except rear): range 1: dodge 2"; 4 "Shield Slam: target nearest: move 0: attack M6: node aoe: dodge 1: stamina drain 2".
- **Enemies can whiff:** "If a blank is rolled on a black die, the mob does nothing that activation."
- **Tells:** "During a player's activation they may choose one model and roll its action before the player makes a decision about what action to take."
- **Stamina drain** as an attack effect, "a new concept that shouldn't be used unless you are using variant stamina recovery rules. Possibly apply stagger instead."
- Stated cost: "This variant adds about 3 minutes to each encounter".

**6. Problem it solves (own words):** "This variant is intended to add a little more variety to fighting trash mobs."

## 8. Dark_Souls_-_1_pager_rules_variant.pdf

"Dark Souls - Additional rules for a random campaign comprising a mini, main and mega boss." One page, unsigned; it thanks "kenhawkins13 and santuric for their posts on variants."

**1. Round structure:** nothing. One line touches it: "Speed run: You can run through an encounter after all enemies have activated."

**2. Scaling with the number of players:**
- Sparks: "Half sparks: Start the game with half the sparks rounded down (1/2 player = 2; 3/4 player = 1)".
- "Starting souls: 1 player = 8; 2 player = 4 per player; 3 or 4 player = 2 per player."
- "Encounter souls: Level 1 = 3; level 2 = 4; level 3 = 5; level 4 = 8; mini boss = 6 x number of sparks; main boss = 8 x number of sparks. Souls are per player and not shared."
- Barrels: "2 souls per player".
- Estus: "Start with 2 flasks per spark" (so indirectly 4 flasks at one or two players, 2 at three or four).
- Enemy health, damage, activations, number of enemies: nothing.

**3. Aggro / targeting:** nothing.

**4. Player versus player:** nothing. (Invasion is mentioned only as a state: "If any player is Hollowed you cannot summon or be invaded.")

**5. Simple and clever:**
- **Souls are personal:** "Souls are per player and not shared", with a fixed amount by encounter level (3 / 4 / 5 / 8).
- **Boss souls depend on sparks left:** "mini boss = 6 x number of sparks; main boss = 8 x number of sparks".
- **Hollowed:** "If you die you become hollowed. Add two black cubes to the right of your endurance bar. You need to use an ember at a bonfire to reverse being hollowed."

- **Chests:** deal one card from three normal and one mimic card.
- **Merchant:** appears after clearing an expansion encounter, sells four revealed cards at 2 souls each and always the same items. "Random loot costs 2 souls; 2 loot items can be sold in exchange for 1 soul."
- **Random campaign:** three encounters before each boss (level 1, 2, 2 before the mini boss; 2, 3, 3 before the main boss), boss chosen at random on entering the fog gate. "Spark: Gain a spark after defeating a boss."

**6. Problem it solves:** not stated beyond the title (rules for a short random campaign of mini, main and mega boss).

## 9. Dark_Souls_Board_Game_-_Grindless_v1.2.pdf

"Dark Souls Board Game: Grindless Variant", 8 pages, unsigned; a write-up of a BoardGameGeek thread ("grindless variant") with house-rule appendices partly "taken from Hallowed Variant".

**1. Round structure:** nothing. "All core rules outside these areas and not explicitly overwritten by these variant rules remain the same" (section 1); the four changed areas are "treasure and loot drops, movement between tiles and bonfire actions, acquisition of souls, and effects of triggered traps".

**2. Scaling with the number of players:**
- **Shop size** (rule 1, Table 1): "the game starts with a fixed shop with a number of treasure cards based on the number of hero characters being played": 1 character = 5 cards, 2 = 6, 3 = 8, 4 = 10. "This is also the number of cards added to the shop whenever a boss is defeated." Items cost 1 soul each.
- **Souls per encounter** (rule 13, Table 3), souls **per character**, chosen by a difficulty setting: Easy 5, Normal 4, Hard 3, Extreme 2. "For example, in a normal difficulty game with 3 played characters, each encounter is worth a total of 12 souls." "difficulty level only affects the total number of souls obtainable from each tile encounter, not the difficulty of enemies or encounters".
- A Mimic spawned mid-encounter raises the encounter's souls "by 1/2 (rounded up)".
- Enemy health, damage, activations, number of enemies, sparks: nothing changes with player count.

**3. Aggro / targeting:** nothing, except "Enemies do not follow characters moving between tiles" (rule 7; "they de-aggro").

**4. Player versus player:** nothing.

**5. Simple and clever:**
- **Souls only once** (rule 15): "Souls cannot be gained from previously killed enemies or previously defeated tile encounters, even after resetting encounters".
- **Enemy loot drops** (rule 2, Table 2): when an enemy dies roll dodge dice; "If at least 1 dodge success is obtained, then the enemy drops a random treasure card that is immediately added to the inventory." As far as the scrambled table can be read: 1 die for Hollow Soldier, Crossbow/Firebomb Hollow, Scarecrows, Silver Knights, Demonic Foliage, Alonne Sword/Bow Knight; 2 dice for Large Hollow Soldier, Stone Guardian/Knight, Mushroom Child, Alonne Knight Captain, Ironclad Soldier; 3 dice for Sentinel, Mushroom Parent. Barrel: 1 dodge die, success = 1 treasure card (rule 3).
- **Cleared tile is a safe place** (rule 12): "players can freely perform bonfire actions without having to move to the bonfire or use bonfire sparks (bonfire sparks are only used in the event of character death)".
- **Running past** (rule 6): the party may go from one uncleared tile to the next, but "characters do not recover their stamina or health as normal between encounters" and gets no souls or loot from the tile left behind.
- **Traps hit enemies** (rules 16-17): a triggered trap affects enemies on its node, who "cannot dodge the trap"; only characters trigger traps.
- **Resets** (Appendix B): "Only the last failed encounter ... and all skipped encounters are reset due to party death. Any cleared encounters are not reset"; "Using a bonfire spark does not reset any cleared encounters. However, it still resets all health, stamina, Estus Flasks, Luck Coins, and Hero Abilities".
- **Death** (Appendix C, optional): "The party is not defeated until all played characters are killed ... If the surviving characters are able to defeat the encounter, any killed characters are instantly revived once the encounter is defeated, without needing to use any bonfire sparks." Also: "A character is not killed unless his endurance bar is full and takes additional damage or attempts to use stamina" (a hidden extra box). And: "During an encounter, any undefeated character can resurrect a killed character at the cost of 1 spark from the bonfire. However, the resurrecting character can take no other actions (not even movement) during the round".
- **Token reset for souls** (Appendix C.3): "At the end of an encounter, each player can reset their coin token, pendant token, or estus token for the cost of 1 soul for each token reset."
- **Per-enemy souls** (rule 14b, optional): split the encounter's souls among enemies with the highest tier "worth at least half the total".
- **Procedural dungeon** (Appendix D): lay tiles as you go; after each new tile "Roll 4 dodge dice"; the fog gate appears on 4 icons after the first tile, 3 after the second, 2 after the third, always on the fourth.
- **Individual souls** (Appendix E): each character has their own pool; "If a character is defeated during an encounter, they immediately drop their souls. They can recover their souls by surviving the next encounter without being defeated ... If the character is defeated again before they recover their souls, then the souls are lost."

**6. Problem it solves (own words, section 1.1):** "a steady and rapid character progression, eliminating the need to grind encounters and significantly shortening playtime from the original rules."

## 11. Faded_Embers_Dark_Souls_Overhaul.doc

"Faded Embers: The First Total Dark Souls Rules Overhaul. By Phantasmical. Version 1.00". Lettered sections A to R; section R gives the author's reason for every change. This is the one file in this batch that both adopts an all-enemies-then-all-characters round **and** rebalances it for player count.

**1. Round structure** (H4, "Turn Order"): "Resolve activations in the following order each turn: 1. All enemies activate (enemies with a higher threat value activate before enemies that possess a lower threat value). 2. The player with the first activation token activates. Then the remaining players activate, one after another, in clockwise order. 3. After all players have activated, resolve end of turn effects. The last player to attack an enemy gains the aggro token. The player with the first activation token hands it to the player on his/her right."
- Player order is fixed (clockwise from a rotating first-activation token); players do not choose it.
- **Before the first enemy activation** (H2): "After players enter a new tile and before enemy figures activate for the first time, each player may choose to perform any character activations excluding Rest, Drink Estus, and Attack." Reason (R, H2): "At first, I playtested a variant where players always went first, but that just resulted in players murdering the most annoying enemy before it could activate. I feel the new rule is a good compromise: players can't kill an enemy outright, but they can position themselves as necessary before enemies first activate."
- **Boss fights** use the same order, with (L2): "During each boss activation, the boss resolves 2 behavior cards one after another." Reason (R, L2): experimental; because stamina and health are separated in this overhaul "enemy (especially boss) damage needs to be increased to compensate".
- Reason for the round (R, H4): "to reduce individual player downtime slightly and make the game feel more fast-paced. Now each player in a four-player game doesn't have to wait through 7 activations before they can react. Sadly, I do not expect this to significantly shorten overall game time."

**2. Scaling with the number of players:**
- **Enemy health x players** (H3): "If playing with more than 1 player, multiply the total health of all enemies encountered by the number of players (with 3-4 players it is recommended to assign a boss dial to each enemy in order to keep track of health). All other enemy stats remain unchanged." Reason (R, H3): "Increased enemy health was the easiest way to scale the game's difficulty alongside player numbers, since I got rid of the current scaling mechanism of increasing enemy activations."
- **Boss health and heat-up x players** (L1): "All Mini and Main Bosses have their health multiplied by the number of players participating in the game session (just like all other enemies). Use multiple health dials ... Also multiply a boss's Heat Up Point by the number of players." Reason (R, L1): "multiplying a boss's health makes up for its reduced activations".
- **Health is not reduced when a character dies** (H6): "Enemy health is NOT reduced if a character is dead, even if that character died during a previous encounter. Therefore, it is possible to challenge a boss that has 4 times its normal health with a single character".
- **Sparks fixed at 4, one spent per dead character** (A2, E4, H6): "the dial displaying 4 sparks, regardless of the number of players"; "The group must pay 1 spark per dead character." Reason (R, E4): "a spark now being consumed upon each player death ... This preserves the same number of runs per player (1 player has 5 chances, 4 players have 2, etc.)."
- **Souls are per player and personal** (H5, E3, L3): each player gets "Level 1: 2 souls, Level 2: 3 souls, Level 3: 4 souls" per encounter (only when the tile still yields a treasure card, which caps each tile at two paying clears), and "Each mini boss and main boss gives 8 souls to each player once defeated. The number of sparks possessed by the group has no bearing". "a lone player still starts with 0 souls" (O1).
- **Treasure** is per tile, not per player: three face-down cards per tile, two drawn on the first clear and one on the second (D3; H5 says one per clear, the two passages disagree).
- **Solo patch** (O1): "The player naturally regenerates 4 stamina at the beginning of each turn (up from 2), performing the Drink Estus action does not prohibit the player from performing any other actions during that turn, and the player is immune to all poise damage." Reason (R, O1): "When playing alone, a player will naturally end up taking hits during almost all enemy activations."
- Enemy damage, number of enemies, number of enemy activations: unchanged by player count.

**3. Aggro / targeting:**
- A character activation begins "after gaining 2 stamina, receiving the aggro token" (J1), and at end of turn "The last player to attack an enemy gains the aggro token" (H4.3). So when the enemies act, aggro sits on whoever attacked last in the previous round.
- Threat is removed from characters (C3): "Characters no longer possess a threat value. Any time that enemy behavior would be determined by characters' threat levels, the group instead decides how to resolve that enemy's action."

**4. Player versus player:** nothing.

**5. Simple and clever:**
- **Individual death** (H6): "If a player's character dies during an encounter, that encounter continues normally until all enemies die, every player's character dies, or each member of the group leaves the tile." Afterwards the group returns to the bonfire and pays one spark per dead character; "Successfully killing a boss instantly resurrects all characters". "Dead characters are still awarded full souls for completed encounters".
- **Dropped souls** (H7): a dead player's souls lie on the node of death; regained "by ending its activation on the node where its souls reside. Souls are never discarded from map tiles".
- **Retreat and dash through** (H8, H9): a character leaves by reaching the exit (or entry) node and spending 1 stamina; no souls or treasure; no recovery when dashing forward. The fog gate "fully prevents retreating" (H10).
- **No dice** (C4): each die colour is a fixed number ("Black: 1, Blue: 2, Orange: 3") for attacks, blocks and resists.
- **Armour wears down** (C5): an attack that deals no damage lowers the enemy's block or resist by the amount absorbed until it next takes damage, so weak weapons can always get through.
- **Stamina separate from health** (C1): red cubes on the bar (death at 10), black cubes kept beside it (maximum 10, no death from stamina).
- **Rest action** (J1): "Once per activation, a character may remove up to 4 black cubes from its board and reset its poise", with only a Walk allowed that turn. Attacks cost 1 stamina.
- **Three Estus, no heroic action or luck** (C2): the three tokens become three Estus uses per rest; drinking removes red cubes only and allows only a Walk that turn.
- **Parry as a last resort** (J5): roll one dodge die; a dodge symbol means no effect and the attacker is staggered; a blank means "double the normal damage". Not against ranged, undodgeable or push attacks.
- **Dodge without dice** (J3): move one node and pay 4 stamina; succeeds if the character's dodge value is high enough on a fixed chart (1 dodge beats difficulty 1, 3 beats difficulty 2; each point in between saves one stamina).
- **Block by paying stamina** (J4): pay stamina equal to the damage blocked, up to the block or resist rating.
- **Conditions build up** (I1): a condition applies only once the buildup exceeds the target's block (bleed) or resist (poison, frostbite). Bleed: 3 extra damage on the next hit. Stagger on a boss: "place the top card of that boss's behavior deck into the discard pile".
- **Leaps** (M1): maximum leap range 1; attacks after a leap cannot be dodged.
- **Resting is free** (E1): "Resting at the bonfire does not consume a spark if all players are still alive"; levelling up requires resting, which resets encounters.
- **Shop** (D2): players "may freely examine the entire deck and choose any treasure card to purchase. Each card in the Shop costs 1 soul."
- **Chest** (G1): 2 cards of the encounter's level or 1 card of the next level up.
- **Entry** (H1): the whole party enters on the single middle entry node (it may hold 4 models at that moment).

**6. Problem it solves (own words):** "to more faithfully replicate both the feel and mechanics of the Dark Souls videogames"; and on the round specifically (R, L1): "players also no longer go at least 5 minutes every round without making any real decisions."

## Best ideas for an all-enemies-then-all-characters round

Overview of what the eleven files do. Only two use an all-enemies-then-all-characters round: **Faded Embers** (file 11) and **Praise the Fun** (file 3). One (file 2, NaturalCrit) keeps "all enemies, then one player, clockwise". The other eight, including the Gorus Revised Rules (file 1), do not touch the round at all; they change souls, sparks, treasure and grind. Across all files the usual thing that scales with player count is **souls per player**; only Faded Embers scales the enemies. No file contains player-versus-player rules.

1. **Multiply enemy health by the number of players; everything else stays.** Faded Embers H3 and L1: all enemy and boss health, and the boss heat-up point, are multiplied by the player count; damage, enemy count and activations are unchanged; health is not reduced when a character dies. The author's stated logic: enemies act once per round instead of once per player, so their survival time is scaled instead. Cost: one multiplication, but real bookkeeping (the file itself recommends a health dial per enemy at three or four players), and one-health enemies stop dying in one hit.

2. **Sparks as the player-count valve: a fixed pool, one spark per death, and a death does not end the encounter.** Faded Embers A2, E4, H6: 4 sparks at any party size, "1 spark per dead character", the fight goes on until all are dead or have left; the author notes this gives "1 player ... 5 chances, 4 players ... 2". Supported by file 2 (5 sparks, one consumed per death, the dead return after the encounter), file 6 (the rest finish the fight, then one spark revives; the party dies only if the first-activation holder dies) and file 9 Appendix C (party defeated only when all are dead). Cost: very low; one sentence, and it removes the published "one death ends everything" rule that punishes large parties hardest in a simultaneous enemy phase.

3. **A priced option to act before the enemies, whose price rises with the number who take it.** Praise the Fun, "Rush": a character may activate before the enemy group by taking 1 damage plus 1 stamina for each *other* rushing character; the first-activation holder must be among them. The party-size cost is built into the formula with no table. Cost: one extra decision each round and one small sum; it also brings the Counter rule along if wanted, which is a second rule.

4. **Free positioning, but no attacks, before the first enemy activation.** Faded Embers H2: on entering, every character may take any activation "excluding Rest, Drink Estus, and Attack" before enemies act. The author reports testing players-first and rejecting it because "players [were] murdering the most annoying enemy before it could activate". Cost: almost none; it applies once per encounter and needs no numbers. It softens the opening enemy volley for any party size but does not itself scale.

5. **Aggro decided by the players' own turn, set once per round.** Two variants: Faded Embers H4.3, "The last player to attack an enemy gains the aggro token" at end of round, with the first-activation token passing on each round and clockwise order; or Praise the Fun, where each activating character takes the aggro token (so the last to act holds it through the enemy phase) and the first-activation holder may pass that token to anyone, letting the party set its order. Cost: none beyond the published token; the Praise the Fun version gives players a choice of order, the Faded Embers version keeps order fixed and is easier to adjudicate.

Supporting patches worth noting with the above: Faded Embers O1 gives a solo character 4 stamina per turn instead of 2 (a one-line fix for the single character who takes every attack), and Faded Embers L2 has a boss resolve 2 behaviour cards per activation at every player count (flat, not scaled; the author calls it experimental and warns that some card pairs "might just immediately murder a player").
