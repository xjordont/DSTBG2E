# Dark Souls: The Board Game — web evidence for a rules redesign

Method note: searches used only the game's name, rule terms and site names. Fetched pages were read through a summarising fetch tool, so quotations are as relayed by that tool. A few were trimmed or checked only against search snippets, and are flagged. Where evidence is thin the report says "not found".

## 1. Complaints about the 2017 rules (ranked by how often they recur in what could be reached)

Ranking is by the number of independent sources raising each point. It is not a count of forum posts, because the forums were largely blocked.

1. **Grind (re-fighting reset encounters for souls and treasure).** This is the most frequent complaint, raised by every review fetched.
   - Tabletop Gaming: the game fails to balance "grinding through battle after battle to earn souls" and dice with "meaningful decisions or rewarding gameplay" (https://tabletopgaming.co.uk/reviews/dark-souls-the-board-game-review).
   - CelJaded calls it a "necessary 'grind'", "a bit of a slog" in replays. Souls are the same for every regular encounter tier, which pushes players to replay the easiest ones (https://www.celjaded.com/review-dark-souls-the-board-game/).
   - Fuzzy Llama: "there is SO MUCH repetition in this game" (https://fuzzyllamareviews.com/2019/07/23/dark-souls-the-board-game/).
   - Steamforged's own designer blog says the original "encouraged farming souls and treasure before each boss" and that this caused fatigue (https://steamforged.com/blogs/brands/dark-souls™-the-board-game-the-new-campaign-system).
2. **Any death sends the whole party back to the bonfire, and all enemies reset.**
   - Fuzzy Llama: "if even 1 person in your party dies, you all die" (https://fuzzyllamareviews.com/2019/07/23/dark-souls-the-board-game/).
   - CelJaded: a defeat resets the party and "adds to the grind"; the reviewer finds the spark-risk trade-off "a bit too 'video gamey'" (https://www.celjaded.com/review-dark-souls-the-board-game/).
   - Zatu: a death empties unspent souls, and enemies respawn, so "failed attempts are punishing" (https://zatu.com/en-us/blogs/reviews/dark-souls-review). This is the fetch tool's paraphrase, not a verified quotation.
3. **Length.**
   - The box says 90–120 minutes. CelJaded reports 4–6 hours with two experienced players, "interminably long", and longer with four (https://www.celjaded.com/review-dark-souls-the-board-game/).
   - Tabletop Gaming: up to "double or even triple the two hours suggested" (https://tabletopgaming.co.uk/reviews/dark-souls-the-board-game-review).
   - Fuzzy Llama: about 2.5 hours, "it takes forever to level your characters up" (https://fuzzyllamareviews.com/2019/07/23/dark-souls-the-board-game/).
4. **Random treasure.** Zatu says the equipment deck is so large that unsuitable draws are common, and each draw costs a soul, so finding the right gear is expensive (https://zatu.com/en-us/blogs/reviews/dark-souls-review). The Game Rant house-rule list gives the same diagnosis: buying from the blacksmith is "pure luck" (https://gamerant.com/dark-souls-board-game-house-rules/).
5. **Luck over skill, few meaningful decisions.** Tabletop Gaming: "endless dice rolls", with luck often outweighing skill (same URL as above). CelJaded takes the opposite view, praising the fact that players roll only for their own attacks and that fixed behaviour cards make turns feel like a puzzle (https://www.celjaded.com/review-dark-souls-the-board-game/). Opinion is split.
6. **Downtime and rules clarity.**
   - Steamforged: "we heard players felt they had too much downtime" (https://steamforged.com/blogs/brands/dark-souls-the-board-game-working-with-the-community).
   - Fuzzy Llama says the rulebook is "so long and tiring to read through", with too few examples, and boss movement and attack cards are the hardest part (https://fuzzyllamareviews.com/2019/07/23/dark-souls-the-board-game/).
7. **Player-count difficulty.** Fuzzy Llama says difficulty rises "substantially" with each added player. CelJaded says bosses get harder because fewer nodes are safe at higher counts. Two sources, no deeper data.
8. **Bosses.** These are mostly praised (see section 5). The complaints are that the boss deck repeats in a fixed order once exhausted, so patterns are learnable (CelJaded), and, from a 2017 buyer relayed in a search snippet, that bosses give little reward. That snippet could not be traced to a page.
9. **Sparks, levelling costs, enemy activation before each character turn.** No source found that complains specifically about these. Levelling slowness appears only in the length complaints (Fuzzy Llama).

## 2. What the 2022 revision changed and how it was received

Steamforged's own explanations:
- **Campaign system.** The campaign blog describes a dashboard with a soul cache, bonfire, blacksmith, firekeeper, an NPC tracker and ten encounter slots. Players move left to right, drawing from each slot's encounter deck. Tiles are packed away after each encounter. Shortcuts and an extra bonfire start (after the mini-boss) let a party bypass early encounters. "For those of you that like the grind, it's still there." Block, dodge and stamina recovery were "subtly revised" (https://steamforged.com/blogs/brands/dark-souls™-the-board-game-the-new-campaign-system).
- **Encounters.** The original used a fixed bonfire plus four enemy tiles. Revised encounter cards each have their own layout and a non-kill objective: survive a timer, activate levers, assassinate a target, carry an item. Some have optional "trial" objectives. Harder encounters give bigger rewards, and the list includes souls, extra draws, token refills and shortcuts. A player draws two level-one cards, plays one and returns the other (https://steamforged.com/blogs/brands/dark-souls™-the-board-game-exploring-new-encounters). That post does not describe player-count scaling.
- **Rules tweaks.** Block and dodge are combined into "a single compound roll". Players may move during other characters' turns to cut downtime. Stamina recovery was altered because two-handed weapons were uncompetitive. Terrain was streamlined and wording tightened. The designers say the changes were run past community members (https://steamforged.com/blogs/brands/dark-souls-the-board-game-working-with-the-community). The FAQ post lists "additional stamina recursion", out-of-turn movement, combined defence rolls and revised terrain, and says it revised "with an eye on the first-edition rules" (https://steamforged.com/blogs/brands/dark-souls™-the-board-game-answers-to-our-most-frequently-asked-questions).
- **Backward compatibility.** Existing cards stay usable, and a free rules download is promised (icv2 and Board Game Quest announcements, snippets only: https://boardgamequest.com/steamforged-games-releasing-two-new-dark-souls-core-sets).

Reception: only one independent review of the revised set was reachable.
- Board Game Quest on Painted World of Ariamis praises the objective variety ("not all simply: 'Kill X'") and says the campaign "FEELS like Dark Souls". The 30 event cards "are not thematic" but are a good change. Some encounters are auto-wins or auto-losses. The set supports one to three players only, so four players need a second box. The reviewer says it "addressed some issues while bafflingly retaining old issues and creating new ones", and counts grinding as a strength, "at the expense of time" (https://boardgamequest.com/dark-souls-the-board-game-painted-world-of-ariamis-review). That reviewer never played the 2017 game, so there is no direct comparison.
- Not found: any Steamforged text on the four-card shop, on printed rewards beyond the post above, or on scaling by player count. The event-card details were deferred in the FAQ post. No poll or forum verdict on whether the revision "fixed" the grind was reachable.
- Not found: a published account of the 2017 official "campaign rules" and FAQ changes. The April 2017 FAQ exists as a PDF (https://gamers-hq.de/media/pdf/25/6d/cc/Dark_Souls_The_Board_Game_FAQ.pdf), but its contents could not be extracted. Miniature Market's review reportedly argues that the rulebook's campaign rules "suggest skipping parts of the game" (search snippet only; the page returned 404 on fetch).

## 3. Most-recommended house rules

- **Double souls / half sparks.** This is Game Rant's "easily one of the most popular" rule. Each cleared encounter pays double ("four per player instead of two"), and the maximum number of sparks is halved, rounded down. It solves the grind. The downside is that a four-player game is left with one spark, "too harsh" for some (https://gamerant.com/dark-souls-board-game-house-rules/). A Steam commenter said double souls "halved the play time" (search snippet).
- **Farming rooms.** A room cleared earlier without spending Heroic Actions, Luck tokens or Estus can be skipped while still paying out. The caution is that spark spending could farm the whole treasure deck (https://gamerant.com/dark-souls-board-game-house-rules/).
- **Separate treasure decks (weapons/shields, armour, spells, upgrade materials) so players buy what they need.** It fixes treasure luck, at the cost of setup time and faster power growth (same Game Rant page).
- **16 starting souls.** It speeds up early levelling and is already the rulebook's rule for solo play (same page).
- **Death does not fail the encounter.** The party continues while consuming a spark. This is a Reddit megathread suggestion, seen only in a search summary (https://lr.sudovanilla.org/r/DarkSoulsTheBoardGame/comments/ed09nz/house_rules_megathread_wave_3_edition/ff3202j/?context=3).
- **One-spark variant.** A three-page fan document, "1 Spark Variant – Final". Per search snippets, sparks replenish on resting or death, Luck can be refilled between encounters for 1 soul, and stat upgrade progress is meant to stay balanced regardless of player count. The text itself could not be extracted.
- **Other variants.** BGG hosts an "Extended Rules by Martinious v3.0", updated for the 2022 sets, with three treasure decks and other rules (https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2883275/dark-souls-the-board-game-extended-rules-by-martin). It was blocked, and only the snippet was seen. A Scribd "Variant Rules v2" lists "Git Gud", "Unpredictable Enemies", Classic Dungeon Crawl and Boss Bash (https://www.scribd.com/document/418185797/Dark-Souls-Board-Game-Variant-Rules-v-2). Steamforged itself published a Classic Dungeon Crawl variant in a 2017 backer update (https://darksouls.backerkit.com/hosted_preorders/project_updates?page=10).
- **Not found:** any trace of "Bare Essentials", "Hollowed Edition", "Jolly Souls", "Faded Embers", "The Better Board Game" or "Praise the Fun". Searches on those names returned nothing relevant. Also not found: individual hollowing in place of a party wipe, all enemies acting once per round, estus changes, or boss changes, as named, tested house rules.

## 4. Hard data

- Sparks by player count, from a rulebook summary: 1 player 5, 2 players 4, 3 players 3, 4 players 2 (https://rulespal.com/dark-souls/rulebook). Fuzzy Llama gives 4 for two players and 2 for four, which is consistent.
- Play times: 2.5 hours (Fuzzy Llama), 4–6 hours for two experienced players (CelJaded), two to three times the stated two hours (Tabletop Gaming). All are individual reports.
- Dice Tower scores: Tom Vasel 4.0 and Bryan Drake 8.5. The scale was not stated (https://www.dicetower.com/board-game-review/5607). That polarisation fits the split in opinion noted above.
- **Not found:** win rates, spark usage per campaign, re-run counts, variant polls.

## 5. Design commentary

What works:
- The merged stamina and damage "Endurance track": "a huge win" (CelJaded) and "a clever way to introduce more strategic decisions" (Tabletop Gaming).
- Boss behaviour decks and heat-up routines: "a highlight" (Tabletop Gaming) and "the most enjoyably tense moments" (CelJaded).
- Zatu calls the combat "tight, tactical and exciting".
- Predictable enemy behaviour cards make turns a puzzle (CelJaded).
- Miniatures are praised throughout.

What undermines it:
- Grind and party-wide reset, treated as the same problem.
- Costly random gear draws.
- Boss patterns that become learnable once the deck cycles.
- Poor rulebook examples, especially for bosses.
- Reviews placed the length well beyond the stated time.

Not found: Shut Up & Sit Down and No Pun Included reviews. Dice Tower pages show only scores, and the GamesRadar review text could not be read.

## What this implies for a redesign

- **Decouple soul income from repetition.** Grind heads the complaints (section 1, items 1–2), and the most popular fixes (double souls, skipped rooms) both attack it. Pay out souls from a campaign or encounter-reward structure rather than from re-fighting.
- **Keep an optional grind only as a safety valve.** Steamforged itself kept "for those who like the grind" (campaign blog), and Board Game Quest counted it as a strength, so make it opt-in.
- **Replace the party wipe with an individual penalty.** Evidence: Fuzzy Llama, CelJaded and Zatu on the wipe; the "death does not fail the encounter" suggestion. No tested individual-hollowing ruleset was found, so this needs playtesting.
- **Make treasure purchasable or partly face-up.** Treasure luck is a stated complaint, and the split-deck house rule is its direct response.
- **Give every encounter its own objective and a visible reward.** The revision's non-kill objectives were the best-received change (Board Game Quest).
- **Avoid fixed spark pools that collapse at four players.** Halved sparks leaves one spark at four players ("too harsh"). The rulebook's 5/4/3/2 shows the scaling already rewards smaller parties, so test player-count scaling explicitly.
- **Cut downtime between turns.** Steamforged's own change was out-of-turn movement, and the question of when enemies act remains open. No evidence was found on the activation rule, so verify it separately.
- **Protect the Endurance track and boss decks.** They are the consistently praised core, so redesign around them rather than through them.
- **Add variety to prevent boss deck memorisation** (CelJaded). For example, vary the starting deck order, or add modifiers like the revised game's events.
- **Design for a stated length per player count.** Reported times run two to three times the box time, and one review calls the length impossible to estimate.
- **Write a rulebook with worked examples, especially for bosses** (Fuzzy Llama).
- **Collect your own numbers.** There is no public win-rate, spark or re-run data, so playtest logging will be the only quantitative evidence.
