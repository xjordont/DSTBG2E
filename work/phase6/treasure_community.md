# Dark Souls: The Board Game - community treatment of treasure decks and shops

Research date: 16 November 2026. This builds on `treasure_web.md` (15 November 2026) and does not repeat its findings, except where they are counted in the table.

## Method and reliability

- BoardGameGeek (BGG) pages and the official XML API returned errors (Cloudflare challenge; `xmlapi2` returned HTTP 401). The unofficial JSON endpoints behind the site did work: `https://api.geekdo.com/api/forums/threads?objectid=197831&objecttype=thing&pageid=N` for thread lists and `https://api.geekdo.com/api/articles?threadid=ID` for post text. I read about 220 threads from the game's forums and searched them for treasure, shop, loot and Andre. Thread links below use the form `https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/ID`, which is the page for the thread I read through the API. I did not see the pages themselves.
- The Jolly Souls rulebook PDF was downloaded from its public Google Drive link and read directly.
- Hollowed Edition, Faded Embers and the Extended Ruleset documents could not be downloaded (BGG file pages blocked; file IDs not resolvable). Their treasure rules are therefore known only from forum posts about them, and are marked as second-hand.
- Reddit could not be read (see the final list). Reddit items are search-engine summaries only.
- Forum posters are not named. "Poster" below means a BGG forum user. Quotes are verbatim from the post text unless I say "paraphrase".

## 1. Treasure schemes people use, and what they say about them

### 1.1 Two tiers with a static shop, 2 and 4 souls (closest to option A)

Source: BGG, "Dark Souls the Board Game: Second Edition (Community Project)", https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2947691 (posted after the 2022 core sets were announced).
- A poster says a "static tiered shop is more reflective of the video game, predictably balanced, and provides more choice of usable items without cycling through the deck too quickly."
- Their scheme: "My current rule set populates a shop with a set number of each type of item from one of two tiers; the second tier selections become available after the mini-boss."
- Prices: "I prefer 2 souls for tier 1 items, 4 souls for tier 2 items, 1 soul to sell an item, and legendary treasure from loot only." They add that "Costs of 1 soul for all items from mundane to legendary is inherently nonsensical and imbalanced."
- Statistics: by their own spreadsheet, the proposed rules give "ONLY ~7 treasure purchases from the shop per Floor", and the party would "only [need] to expose ~25% of available Treasure Cards per game".
- The post does not say how items are assigned to a tier (stat requirement or otherwise). The same thread's author also says a Black Knight Great Axe card had stat requirements that "make no sense" and that a community treasure update set might be better.
- Note: this thread is the only source I found that matches the 2 and 4 soul price pair in option A. It reads as one poster's work in progress, not a tested consensus.

### 1.2 Tiers set by stat requirement (the criterion in option A)

- Hollowed Edition, as described by users. BGG thread "Hollowed Edition v1.3 - Big update, streamlining, and the Hollowed Collection!", https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2213779 (second-hand; the ruleset itself was not read). A 2019 commenter asks about "Tier decks. If we play with all expansions do we add up all treasure items with stats 24 and below for Tier 1 + all basic class items (regardless of stats) half of embers and titanite shards. Is that normal? Coz my Tier 1 is massive with slim chance to get any good weapon or ember." This shows that an earlier Hollowed version put cards with stat requirements of 24 or less, plus class items, in tier 1. The v1.3 notes (7 November 2020) then say: "The shop has been reworked into three decks based on item type (weapons/armor/magic). This should be way easier to set up and understand." So Hollowed moved from stat tiers to type decks.
- "My Dark Souls: The Board Game - Bare Essential Rule Changes", https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3518733 (May to June 2025). The author runs three item decks. Quote: "My 3 item decks are based on 'Faded Embers' ideas, this is to decrease the chance of getting high tier item that you can't use, also it is in the same way you get items in the source material (items are getting better when enemies are tougher). The specific stat at '24' is also to spread out all the gem stones." The thread also credits Faded Embers, Jolly Souls and Hollowed Edition v1.3 as its sources. A second poster: "I've also been playing with three decks for a while. Yes, loot has become more predictable, but I won't say it's gotten better. It was most annoying to reassemble the decks every time."
- Same author, "My Dark Souls The Board Game - Choose Your Own Adventure by ChatGPT", https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3535639: "Level 1, 2, 3 Treasure deck (based on character stat requirement to equip, level 1 need the least stat requirement)". Encounters name which level of deck to draw from. This is the same author and scheme as the previous item, so it is not independent.
- Jolly Souls, a 2-page rulebook (v6, BGG thread https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2487637, read from the PDF). Recommended market, verbatim: "All unused boss items go into a separate 'Boss' deck. All transposed items, embers, armor upgrade items and weapon upgrade items and items with 48 or more stat requirements go into a 'Treasure' deck. All items below a total stat requirement of 48 and all unused starter items go into a 'Market' deck. A completed encounter reveals 4 items from 'Market' at the bonfire, available to purchase for 2 souls each. For each boss defeated, 1 of the bonfire cards can come from 'Treasure'. Chests always give 1 'Treasure' Item and 1 item that depends on how many bosses you have beaten: 0='Market' 1='Treasure' 2+='Boss'." Anything can be sold for 1 soul. The thread's update note says the market rules are "designed for players who want access to all their expansions gear and not have to sort the decks every playthrough." Note the threshold is the total of stat requirements (48), not the highest single requirement (24 or 25).
- Faded Embers (BGG thread https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1787693, May 2017). The announcement lists "Treasure tiers and a shop". I did not read the rules. The author later recommended raising the cost of buying treasure from 1 to 2 souls and giving 4 treasures per map tile. A commenter said the shop design would "reduce replayability and fun by locking away almost all the high-level treasures", leaving players "significantly under-geared when they face the main boss". The author replied that restricting high-level gear "increases replayability". This is the one direct objection I found to locking high-tier cards away.
- A poster in "Which rules?", https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3590496: "I sometimes use a tiered item deck variant to reduce getting random items can't use without spending loads of souls on stats." The tier criterion is not given.
- Tiers by quality, not stated stat: in "Elden Ring Board Game Announced", https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2935748 (Sept 2022), a poster says "the loot deck is far too large and diluted to make getting loot any fun. The equipment is designed in tiers, and yet the random nature of it means that you might not find mid-level equipment until you've almost cycled through the entire deck." Their fix: "tiered loot decks (break the loot deck into three piles based on the perceived quality of the equipment, that you draw from in order - exhaust the low tier loot before moving on to the mid tier deck)".

### 1.3 Sorting by item type

- "My House Rules", https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3223612 (Jan 2024). Two decks. "Deck 1: Weapons, spells, half of the gems (randomly selected). Deck 2: Armor, shields, embers, rings, and the other half of the gems. Players may spend 2 souls to purchase the top card from either deck." The poster says it was to "mitigate getting screwed on items" and compares it to "encounter vendors" in the video game. Replies against it: "I also don't like splitting the deck of items. Again, it all boils down to the fact that you are looking for the right item the whole game", and another found it "too 'gamey'". Another reply argues the game is a roguelike and guaranteed gear makes it "far too easy". So in one thread: one supporter, three critics.
- Hollowed Edition v1.3: three decks (weapons, armour, magic), as quoted above, https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2213779. A user with Iron Keep and Explorers cut the common cards to 60, sorted them into the three decks and reported that "We barely got into any of the three decks at all (I think we pulled 3 class treasures, in a two player game)". They did not say how many cards they saw overall.
- "I created my own camping Dashboard to play with the original core", https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2984503: with the 2022 four-slot shop, the poster created four categories and reveals "cards from the deck until one card of each category is revealed", or alternatively separates the cards as Hollowed 1.3 does. They note embers have no home in Hollowed's three decks. A commenter objects that filling one slot per category "slows the game down".
- "Why such hate?", https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3062964: a poster says most people "seem to prefer only the most basic house rules like 'double souls' or 'different loot piles'", and that most regular players use the rules as written.
- "Dark Souls Core v1.0 House Rules for Player Agency", https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3032429: the author calls "loot type piles" one of the usual options ("OK") but says they, like double souls, "don't quite manage to keep the decision making in the players hands". Their own scheme is draw 3, keep 1.
- Gamerant, "Dark Souls board game house rules" (https://gamerant.com/dark-souls-board-game-house-rules/) and a 2017 r/DarkSoulsTheBoardGame megathread comment (via search summary only) are already in `treasure_web.md`: four piles (weapons and shields, armour, upgrade materials, sorceries).

### 1.4 Sorting by kind of card (option B), and rewards-only legendary and boss cards

- No community source proposes B exactly. The official rules already do most of it (see `treasure_web.md`, section (a)).
- Partial matches: Jolly Souls keeps boss cards in a separate deck that appears only through chests and bonfire reveals after bosses; the two-tier shop in 1.1 sells legendary cards "from loot only"; the "1 Spark Variant" (in `treasure_web.md`) gives boss treasure only from bosses.
- No community source I found proposes a second shop that opens for transposed cards after the mini boss. The nearest is the tier 2 shop that "becomes available after the mini-boss" (1.1).
- Unused boss cards in the 2017 core: a BGG user in "What happens to the 'purchased' treasure cards when a mini-boss is defeated?", https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2748412, confirms that after the mini boss "You only shuffle in each characters 'transposed' cards as well as 5 random 'legendary' cards into whatever remaining cards are in the treasure deck", and that acquired cards are kept. Hollowed 1.3 and the New Game Plus thread (https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3196875) both aim to give unused boss treasure a use ("so they actually see play, like, ever"; "Mega Bosses with loot that nobody can use").

### 1.5 Other schemes

- One universal deck. https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3518733: a poster, tired of preparing decks, built "one universal deck" of about 100 items from the base game, expansions and every character, except the starting equipment, with 5 to 7 extra embers and a sharpening stone each to restore their share. More than two dozen games played; setup "much faster". Another poster uses a deck of all expansion cards (about 136 cards) in https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3064109, and a reply warns that "adding all cards ... dilutes the pool".
- Cut or rebuild the deck to a fixed size (dilution control). https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3203446: "Having too many common treasure cards will dilute the deck and make it more difficult to draw useful equipment within the normal time constraints of a campaign." Suggestions: curate the deck to 45 cards, or keep 60 and "draw 5-6 cards during a shop trip instead of 4"; adding 15 expansion cards means removing 15. A poster in https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3064109 advises the same: replace 15 cards, do not add.
- Draw more, pick one. https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3075536: "The 4 card shop could be good when learning the game, or you could draw 2, pick 1. Return the other to the bottom of the deck." https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3032429: draw 3, keep 1. A poster in https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3396907 uses the official four-card limit with the original rules; another objects that the 2022 rules "limited the player options of drawing from it", comparing "20+ cards or ... 4 cards".
- Early and late split. Hollowed 1.3 "codified game phases, early and late" (https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2213779, truncated note). The 2017 Beasts of War comment and 2019 r/ megathread comment are in `treasure_web.md` and the search summary of this pass.
- Trading row and "Tiny Shop" (2017 r/ megathread, search summary only; see `treasure_web.md`).

## 3. Statistics and opinions on card counts and stat requirements

- Cards seen per game, official 2022 rules, from players. In https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2957619 a poster estimates a four-player party gets "2 Sparks + 1 after the mini boss = 12 Treasure cards in total" from the shop. In https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2945425 a commenter notes the four-card shop does not scale with player count: one-player games have "an enormous number of treasure opportunities and relatively few for 4 player games". Both are arithmetic from the rules, not measured play.
- Two-tier design target (https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2947691): about 7 shop purchases per floor and about 25 percent of the treasure cards exposed per game.
- Chest frequency, from the Faded Embers author (https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1787693): level 1 encounters have chests 6 of 12, level 2 3 of 12, level 3 5 of 12. Another poster there said legendary access of 1 to 2 per main boss session "sounds about right".
- Usable loot depends on party size. https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/1787693: "If you play a 3-4 player game nearly every single piece of loot will at least be considerable for one of your characters", while solo play needs "a whole lot" of grinding. https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3396907 agrees: "More players also reduces the issue with the deck giving you loot you can't use."
- Stat requirements. https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2945768 ("Seeing as the games overhauled - Did they change any of the item cards?", Oct 2022): "the items and their stat requirements often made no sense", described as "a semi common complaint". The 2022 community-project thread (2947691) makes the same point about one card. https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3590496 uses tiers to avoid items "can't use without spending loads of souls on stats". A review cited in the same family of threads (https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/3105883, "The Cantankerous Crow Reviews", a blog review reposted on BGG) says the "abundance of garbage in the treasure deck ... is so important", and that the loot constrains who levels first because "it costs fewer experience points to let a knight use a powerful piece of armor". That reviewer wants the randomness; they dislike only that the loot stack is large to shuffle.
- Set-up burden. The same review calls sorting the cards "twenty or more unique decks" "incredibly obnoxious". Two BERC thread posters say the same about reassembling tiered decks.
- Early lucky draws and snowballing: I found no post that complains about snowballing from one early draw. The complaint found instead is the opposite, a party with no useful draw for long periods ("there was no good treasure in the treasure deck", https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2967762; "it is entirely possible that during any play, you will get not a single usable upgrade", https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2935748). Class cards for absent classes: only the official 2017 and 2022 rules (leave them out), no complaint found.

## 5. How comparable games sort loot

These are brief and, apart from the ones marked with a source, written from my general knowledge of the games and not checked in this pass.

- Descent: Journeys in the Dark, 2nd edition. Shop item cards are divided into Act I and Act II decks; expansions add their own cards to the matching deck. The FAQ erratum says to "Shuffle the Shop Item cards and reveal five Shop Item cards" when shopping (search result for the FAQ at https://cdn.1j1ju.com/medias/72/5e/86-descent-journeys-in-the-dark-second-edition-faq.pdf, summary only). Reason, as I understand it: a time-gated tier keeps early gear weak and stops a late item appearing before the party can afford it, while a small random window keeps each campaign different. I did not read the rulebook.
- Gloomhaven. A fixed shop of numbered item cards; each town prosperity level adds item numbers to the supply, and found item designs add every copy to the supply (https://rules.dized.com/gloomhaven/rule/items/available-items, search summary). Reason: progress gates availability, and the player sees exactly what is for sale, so there is no randomness about what is on offer.
- Massive Darkness 2: Hellscape. Three rarity decks (common, rare, epic); a treasure token drawn from a bag selects the deck, and the bag mix shifts as the game progresses, which raises the chance of better loot; Heavenfall adds a legendary deck (search result, https://rulespal.com/massive-darkness-2/rulebook, summary). Reason: the bag controls the rate of better loot without a shop. I did not check the original Massive Darkness.
- Sword & Sorcery. The treasure deck is shared; expansion cards are shuffled in when the matching quest is played, and cards marked "Quest" are held back until quest rules release them (search result for the Questbook and rulebooks, summary only). Loot is random and campaign-gated, with no shop.
- Bloodborne: The Board Game. The base game has separate reward, consumable and upgrade card decks (60 upgrade cards in the component list, from the search result for the CMON rulebook). I could not confirm how they are drawn.
- Kingdom Death: Monster. Gear comes from settlement innovations and monster-specific gear decks, not an open shop. Not checked in this pass.

## 6. What I could not reach

- Reddit: `old.reddit.com` search pages and `.json` forms returned HTTP 403 ("blocked due to a network policy"). The privacy-mirror pages for the 2017 and 2019 house-rules megathreads returned 404 and 503 on two attempts each. All Reddit content here is search-engine summaries, and is marked as such.
- BGG XML API (`xmlapi2/forumlist`, HTTP 401) and BGG web pages (Cloudflare challenge). The JSON endpoints noted above worked, but I did not systematically read the whole forum: about 220 threads were fetched, and the first one to four pages of replies in each.
- Hollowed Edition, Faded Embers, Extended Ruleset, BERC, Combined Edition and the Forbidden Lore library: the actual rules documents. The BGG file pages were blocked and the file IDs did not map to the documents.
- Facebook groups and Discord servers: not attempted beyond a web search; no public mirror was found. I did not log in anywhere.
- Steam Workshop descriptions and YouTube descriptions: not found.
- Comparable games: rulebooks not opened; descriptions rely on search-result summaries or general knowledge.
- No source found for "Praise the Fun", "Grindless", "Dark Souls Revised Rules", "The Shop Experimental Rules" or "Dark Souls the Board Game Remastered" by those names.
