# Treasure deck organisation: official rules, manuscript, house rules

Purpose: evidence for and against splitting treasure three ways (1: common plus class; 2: transposed; 3: legendary plus boss), set against the manuscript's two tiers split at a stat requirement of 25.

Sources read: work/text/DS-Core-Rules-lores.txt (2017 core), work/text/DS-TG-Rulebook-Compressed_compressed.txt (2022 Tomb of Giants core), the other rulebook extractions in work/text, work/phase5/house_a.md, house_b.md, house_c.md, work/phase6/house_matrix.md, official_diff.md, web_research.md, the manuscript chapters named in the brief, and tools/sim/gear.json. Page numbers are the printed ones, taken from the extractions or from official_diff.md. Where I could not find something, it says "Not found".

Short version of the findings:

- No official edition and no house ruleset surveyed keeps transposed treasure apart from legendary treasure. Where both appear, they arrive together at the same moment, after the first (mini) boss.
- The manuscript's tier split at 25 has one direct precedent: the Extended Ruleset (C9), which splits at the same figure and also adds transposed and five legendaries to the second deck. The manuscript differs from it on class cards (see section 6).
- The proposed three-way split would leave a legendary-plus-boss pile of roughly seven to nine cards in the standard game, too few to stock a shop by category.

## 1. Official 2017 core set

**Contents** (Core, p. 5, "Treasure Deck"): 13 starting equipment, 40 class-specific treasure, 60 common treasure, 10 legendary treasure, 18 boss treasure. Each character has "ten class-specific treasure cards". The manuscript (§2.5) and the 2017 character boxes show these as five class treasure plus five transposed per character.

**Initial deck** (Core, p. 8, step 6): "Find the common treasure cards and each chosen character's five class treasure cards. Shuffle them together to form the treasure deck, and place it face down on the Treasure Deck section of the Bonfire tile." Boss treasure is set aside with its boss at step 3 ("boss treasure cards ... set aside"). Transposed and legendary cards are held back (official_diff.md, citing p. 6 and p. 9). So the starting deck is one deck: common plus the party's class cards. The extraction does not say which printed line sets transposed aside; official_diff.md records this as the rule.

**After the mini boss** (Core, p. 9, "Setup after the Mini Boss"): "Instead of setting up the treasure deck in step 6, you will add treasure to the deck. Find the five transposed treasure cards for each character. Then find the legendary weapon cards, shuffle those, and randomly select five of them. Add the transposed treasure cards (five per player) and the five selected legendary weapon cards to the treasure deck and shuffle it. From now on, the characters have a chance to gain these more advanced items when they draw a card from the treasure deck." So transposed and legendary enter together, in one step, into the same single deck. Five of the ten legendaries are used.

**Boss treasure** (Core, p. 28): "After the players defeat a mini boss encounter, place that boss's treasure cards in the inventory." All of the mini boss's cards go to the inventory; the Core book says a boss has "between two and four treasure cards" (p. 4). Main boss: "The main bosses do have their own treasure cards, but these are used only in campaign play (see 'Campaigns Rules', p. 32)." The Twin Gargoyles' treasure is awarded only after two Gargoyle encounters back to back (scenario note in the campaign section).

**Buying and chests** (Core p. 14 and the encounter rules): 1 soul buys the top card of the deck, drawn blind; "The only limit to the number of times players may purchase treasure is the number of souls in the soul cache." A chest gives two cards from the treasure deck (encounter rules). Campaign (p. 33): cost rises to 2 souls and unwanted treasure sells for 1 soul.

**Expansions (2017)**
- Darkroot and Explorers: remove fifteen random common cards from the core deck and replace them with fifteen common cards from the expansion, at step 6 (both rulebooks). Both then say: "After completing Section 1, players augment the treasure deck by adding the transposed and legendary treasure cards as described" in "Setup After the Mini Boss". Iron Keep, Phantoms, Executioner's Chariot (EC), The Last Giant (TLG), Manus (MFA) and Vordt of the Boreal Valley (VotBV) use the same wording ("After completing Section 1 [or 2], players augment the treasure deck by adding the transposed and legendary treasure cards").
- Boss expansions (the AD, BDK, EC, Four Kings, GD, GaD, MFA, OIK, TLG and VotBV rulebooks): after the main boss, "add the main boss treasure cards to the inventory". The same text says there are no changes to the character boards, treasure deck or tokens after the main boss.
- Characters expansion (DS-Characters-Rules-lores.txt): adds 10 common and 10 legendary armour cards. Each new class has "five class treasure cards, and five transposed treasure cards". Its optional armour sets: players "can add basic versions of their treasure cards to the treasure deck at the start of the game, or ... more advanced versions ... after they defeat the mini boss". "Add one to five common treasure cards" at step 6; or "one to five legendary armour cards ... at the same time they add legendary weapon cards to the treasure deck after defeating the mini boss", with a warning that "some of these legendary armour sets have very high stat requirements".
- Not found: any 2017 expansion rule that creates a separate deck.

## 2. Official 2022 core (Tomb of Giants) and what changed

Only one 2022 rulebook is in work/text: DS-TG-Rulebook-Compressed_compressed.txt ("TG"). No other 2022 set was found.

**Contents** (TG p. 5): 30 class-specific treasure (three characters, ten each; the picture shows class and transposed for Cleric, Pyromancer and Thief), 45 common, 5 legendary, 5 boss (Black Knight and Gravelord Nito).

**Initial deck** (TG p. 14): "Take the class specific treasure cards for the characters being used, and shuffle them together with the common treasure cards to create the starting treasure deck. Place this deck face down on treasure deck slot ... Place the transposed, legendary, and boss treasure cards, and enemy and boss cards face up to one side for the time being. These will be used later in the game." Same shape as 2017: one deck, three groups held back. The 2022 text gives no per-character count of class cards (official_diff.md).

**After the mini boss** (TG p. 31, Black Knight): "add the Black Knight treasure cards (Black Knight Halberd, Black Knight Shield, and Blue Titanite) to the inventory. Then, shuffle the legendary treasure cards, Gravelord Nito treasure cards, and transposed treasure cards for the characters in the party into the treasure deck. Increase the spark dial by 1, then each character gains an ember token."

What changed from 2017:
- No tile reset: the cards are shuffled into the deck in place, mid-campaign (2017 reset the play area).
- All five legendaries are shuffled in (2017: five of ten).
- The main boss's treasure cards (Nito) are shuffled into the treasure deck. In 2017 main boss treasure went to the inventory in campaign play only. So in 2022 boss treasure for the main boss is in the deck, not awarded.
- Mini boss treasure still goes straight to the inventory.
- Chests: a character spends an action to draw one card into the inventory (2017: two cards, on winning).
- Shop (TG p. 27): draw the top four cards, place them face up; buy any number at 1 soul each; return the rest to the bottom; once per visit. This is a face-up four-card shop drawn from the one deck, with no categories. 2017 sold blind draws at 1 soul. The 2017 campaign sell-back and 2-soul price are not found in 2022 (official_diff.md).
- New reward types (TG p. 19): Draw, and Search, which finds a named card in the treasure deck, places it in the inventory and reshuffles; if the card is not in the deck, "this reward cannot be claimed". This matters for any split: a Search reward can name a card that sits in a different pile.
- Gravestones (TG p. 12) let a character peek at the top card of the treasure deck.

Not found: any 2022 text on shop stock by category, on a second treasure deck, or on class cards of characters not in the party.

## 3. The author's manuscript: two tiers

All references to the manuscript use its own section numbers.

**Decks (§6.2).** "Two treasure decks are built at setup: the tier one treasure deck and the tier two treasure deck." Type and stat requirement decide the deck.
- Starting equipment: neither deck.
- Common: "A common treasure card with any stat requirement of 25 or more is tier two"; all below 25 is tier one.
- Class-specific: "sorted into tier one or tier two by its stat requirements exactly as common treasure is."
- Transposed: "All transposed treasure is tier two."
- Legendary: "cannot be purchased, and no legendary treasure card enters a treasure deck at the start of the game." Five random legendaries are shuffled into the tier two deck the first time the tier two shop is stocked (§6.3 step 5); the rest go to the box. Drawn legendaries are set aside during shop stocking (§6.3 step 4.1), so they never appear in the shop.
- Boss: not in a deck. Each selected boss's cards sit with the boss; "one of them is awarded at random when the boss is defeated" (§6.2, §16.5 step 2: "Shuffle the boss's boss treasure cards, draw one at random, and award it to the party"; the others return to the box).
- Ember cards, invader cards, Titanite Scale: neither deck. One ember per character goes in the tier one shop at the start (§6.3).
- Expansion cards are sorted into the tiers by the same rule (§6.2, Expansion Treasure), subject to Appendix B.
- Tier one is therefore every common and class card with all requirements below 25. Tier two is every common and class card with a requirement of 25 or more, plus every transposed card (§6.2).

**Shops (§6.3, §11.4).**
- Two face-up shops. Tier one is stocked at the start; tier two is first stocked when the mini boss is defeated; both are restocked after each boss (§6.6, §6.7).
- Stocking by category: three weapons, three armour, two shields, two upgrades, two spells (12 cards per shop). A card goes into the first category that describes it; unmatched cards are set aside and shuffled back. A category the deck cannot fill stays short.
- Prices (§11.4): tier one 2 souls, tier two 4 souls; sale value 1 soul; boss treasure sells for 6 souls and cannot be bought. Legendary and boss treasure are not in the shop and cannot be bought.
- Encounter treasure: one tier one card per character dealt face down to each tile before the mini boss (§6.5), one tier two card per character after it (§6.7).

**Campaign (§18.5 to §18.8).** The tier changes when the bonfire ascetic is burnt, not when a boss dies. Legendary and boss treasure enter neither deck nor shop. Legendary treasure is gained only by transposition: return one boss treasure card and pay 4 souls to take one of five face-up legendaries (§18.8). Shop prices in campaign: boss treasure sells for 4, legendary for 4. Campaign restock happens only when a section is completed (§18.6).

**Cross-check on the 2017 text.** The manuscript's earlier draft sentence "Transposed treasure cards all have at least one stat requirement greater than or equal to 25" (change log, around line 1317) holds on the gear.json data: all 20 transposed cards have a highest requirement of 28 or more (section 5).

**Differences from the official rules, as the manuscript stands**
- Boss treasure: official 2017 awards all of the mini boss's cards to the inventory; the manuscript awards one at random and discards the rest. I found a ruling "CONFIRM as written" for boss-039 and a related one for core-035, but did not find a stated reason for choosing one at random.
- Legendary count (five) and transposed arrival (after the mini boss) match 2017.
- Class cards: the official deck contains only the chosen characters' class cards. Manuscript §6.2 says "every ... class-specific treasure card" and does not state that only the party's classes are used. This could be read as putting all four (or more) classes' cards in the decks. Flagged for the author; I did not find a clarifying sentence.

Are transposed and legendary stronger than common in practice? Judged by requirement, yes: the transposed median (35) is 14 above the common median (21), and every transposed card needs 28 or more. Legendary median is 30.5 and every card needs 27 or more. The legendary range is lower and narrower than transposed (max 37 against 40). Class cards sit between common and transposed (median 28.5). Requirement is a proxy for power; the file carries attack data, but I did not analyse damage. Counting by requirement only: 16 of 53 common cards (30%) overlap the transposed and legendary range of 25 or more.

Stat tracks give context (gear.json "classes"): a stat's base value is about 8 to 16, tier one about 15 to 23, tier two about 23 to 32, tier three about 30 to 40. A requirement of 25 therefore corresponds to roughly tier two in the stat in question.

**Manuscript tier sizes applied to this data** (the manuscript's sort, gear.json items, no expansions):
- Tier one: 43 cards (37 common plus 6 class, the class cards being all requirements below 25).
- Tier two: 50 cards (16 common, 14 class, 20 transposed), plus 5 of 10 legendary added later.
- By type (the file has only armour, upgrade and "hand", the last covering weapons, shields and spells, so shop categories cannot be counted exactly): tier one has 10 armour, 7 upgrade, 26 hand; tier two has 8 armour, 8 upgrade, 34 hand.

**Three-way split sizes applied to the same data and the rulebook's per-party rules.** Using the official rule that only the party's characters' cards are used:
- (1) common plus class: 53 common (printed 60) plus 5 per character: about 58 to 73 cards in the file's terms, so ample.
- (2) transposed: 5 per character, so 5 cards for one player and 20 for four.
- (3) legendary plus boss: 5 legendary (2017 official count) plus the boss's 2 to 4 treasure cards if boss treasure were put in the pile rather than awarded: about 7 to 9 cards in the standard game. If all 10 legendary cards are used, 12 to 14.

## 6. Assessment

All of this is grounded in the evidence above and flags where the evidence is silent.

### Arguments for the three-way split

1. **It matches the card types the printed rules already distinguish.** Official set-up holds three groups back (transposed, legendary, boss) and starts with common plus class (Core p. 8; TG p. 14). Group (1) is exactly the official starting deck. Group (2) is exactly what the official rules add for each party character. Group (3) is exactly what the official rules hold back at the start and treat by special rules.
2. **Class cards are in group (1), as in the official starting deck, C8, C9 and A8.** The manuscript instead sorts class cards by requirement. gear.json shows 14 of 20 class cards need 25 or more, so under the manuscript most class cards land in tier two. The three-way split puts every class card in the first deck, as the official game does. A8, C8 and C9 take the same view (class from the start).
3. **Separate transposed pile gives the author a clean lever.** Transposed cards are the only group tied to specific characters and all require 28 or more (gear.json). A separate pile means a party can see which of its own class cards are the second tier, and the shop for that pile never offers a card for a class that is not playing.
4. **Boss and legendary are both unbuyable in the manuscript** (§11.4: "Boss treasure and legendary treasure are not placed in the shop and cannot be bought"). Grouping them as non-shop reward cards is consistent with that. A6 prices them alike (15 and 15), A8 withholds boss items from sale.
5. **Chest and encounter rewards from a legendary pile can be an explicit event** and avoids the manuscript's "five random legendaries shuffled blind into the tier two deck" (§6.3 step 5), which makes a legendary appear only by chance and never in the shop.

### Arguments against the three-way split (and for the manuscript's two tiers)

1. **No precedent for a transposed pile on its own.** In both official editions transposed and legendary arrive together at the mini boss (Core p. 9; TG p. 31). In A8, C8 and C9 they also arrive together. The only ruleset that treats them separately is A9, and it does so for boss loot priced in sparks, not for a deck. B2 and C5 isolate legendary only, leaving transposed unaddressed or with class cards. So a split between (2) and (3) departs from every source read.
2. **Requirement tracks power better than card type for common and class cards.** 16 of 53 common cards need 25 or more, and common cards such as Zweihander (35) and Greataxe (32) sit inside the transposed range (28 to 40); class cards in the file include 14 at 25 or more, ten of them at 30 or above. A pure type split puts these high-requirement cards into the opening deck and opening shop, where a character with tier-one stats (about 15 to 23) cannot equip them. C9 splits at 25 on exactly this ground ("less than 25", "at least one stat requirement of greater than or equal to 25"). C6 prices by stat tier needed. The manuscript's 25 split is the only one with the same logic.
3. **Pile sizes.** The legendary-plus-boss pile would be about 7 to 9 cards in the standard game (5 legendary plus 2 to 4 boss cards of the selected mini boss). The manuscript's shops want 12 cards by category (3 weapons, 3 armour, 2 shields, 2 upgrades, 2 spells, §6.3). That pile cannot stock a shop by category, and gear.json shows all 10 legendaries are "hand" items, so it would hold no armour, upgrade or spell at all. A transposed pile of 5 to 20 cards (5 per party character) can fill the weapons slot but would rarely hold 3 armour (gear.json has 4 transposed armour cards across all four classes, so a one-player party sees 1 at most). Category stocking from a three-way split would regularly leave categories short, which §6.3 allows but which makes shops dull. The two-tier decks (43 and 50 cards in the file's terms) are large enough.
4. **Class cards are useless to a party without that class.** A single pile of common plus class cards is fine only if the deck contains the party's class cards alone, as the official rules do. The manuscript's §6.2 text does not say so (see section 3). If the author adopts group (1) as "common plus class", the same clarification is needed there. This problem is neutral between the two schemes, and the same rule fixes both.
5. **Boss treasure does not suit a deck.** Official rules award a mini boss's cards directly (Core p. 28). TG shuffles Nito's cards into the deck, which is the only official case of boss cards in a deck. Grouping boss cards with legendary in a pile conflicts with the manuscript's random single award (§16.5) and with the campaign transposition rule (§18.8, boss card plus 4 souls for a legendary), which depends on boss cards being held as inventory items.
6. **Expansions.** The Characters expansion adds legendary armour and common armour; Darkroot and Explorers add 15 common cards each, and later campaign sets say to add transposed and legendary cards at the mini boss. Common cards are the growing group. A three-way split sends them to pile (1), the largest, and leaves piles (2) and (3) small; the manuscript's sort by requirement spreads expansion cards over both decks. The Characters expansion's own text ("some of these legendary armour sets have very high stat requirements") is itself a requirement-based warning.
7. **Shop economics.** The manuscript prices by tier (2 or 4 souls). A requirement split keeps price proportional to what the card demands. A three-way split would need a third price or would put 25-plus common cards at the tier-one price.

### Alternatives found in the evidence

| Option | Source | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Two decks split at stat requirement 25; class in deck 1; transposed and five legendaries in deck 2 | C9 | Same split as the manuscript but class cards stay in deck 1 regardless of requirement. |
| Two stages: common plus class first, transposed and legendary together after the first boss | Official 2017 and 2022; A8; C8 | Cheapest change; no extra deck. |
| Three decks by item kind (weapon, armour, magic), stage gate for transposed and legendary | C8 | Fixes "wrong kind of card" luck; costs set-up time (Game Rant). |
| Six typed piles | A9 | More granular; each pile small. |
| Legendary as its own deck, drawn after the main boss | B2 | Isolates legendary only. |
| Legendary locked until earned | C5; the manuscript's own campaign rule (§18.8) | Treats legendary as a reward, not as stock. |
| Priced bands by card type | A4, A6, A8, B6 | Keeps one deck; price does the sorting. |
| Price by stat tier required | C6 | Close in spirit to the manuscript's 25 split, without a second deck. |
| Boss loot bought with sparks in three levels (class 1, transposed 2, legendary 3) | A9 | Three-level structure by card type, applied to boss loot only. |

### Net reading

On the evidence the three-way split is a type-based reading of the official hold-back groups, and the manuscript's split is a requirement-based reading that has one direct house-rule precedent (C9). The main weaknesses of the three-way split are the lack of a precedent for isolating transposed from legendary, a legendary-plus-boss pile too small and too weapon-only to stock a shop by category, and boss treasure being awarded rather than drawn. Its main strengths are fidelity to the printed card types and class cards staying in the first pile. A hybrid fits the evidence best: keep the two-stage structure (transposed and legendary together, after the first boss), keep boss treasure as an award, and decide the class-card question explicitly (all class cards in deck 1 as in C9 and the official game, or sorted by requirement as now), restricting class cards to those of characters in the party.
