# Issues — chapter 18 (Campaign Play)

- Campaign soul rewards (§18.5): two Gate 1 answers disagree. OQ-21's recommendation reads "keep rewards at 3/4/5/6 and the doubled costs" (B-011, first option), which read literally makes the campaign full rewards equal to the standard ones. OQ-05, CMP-0010, and the Preserve list give the campaign reduced column as 1/1/2/2 and "soul rewards one lower", which presuppose a full column of 2/3/4/5. The chapter follows the manuscript's table and the lead's instruction: full 2/3/4/5, reduced 1/1/2/2. B-011 is therefore not applied. If the author intended 3/4/5/6 in campaign play, the table becomes identical to §7.6 and the reduced column 1/2/2/3.
- Bonfire ascetic fee (§18.7): B-034 (approved PROPOSED: eight souls per character, and only after the first section's final boss) is not applied. The chapter keeps the manuscript's one-time communal fee of 16 souls, payable during any rest, as the chapter instructions, the glossary, and the Preserve list state. The author must confirm.
- Spark buy-back (§18.3): B-013 says "may buy one spark" without a limit per rest. The chapter allows one spark per rest, requires every character to pay four of their own souls (no pooling), allows the purchase during a free rest, and forbids it at the maximum. The author must confirm the limit and whether a character who cannot pay blocks the purchase.
- Spark dial range (§18.3): the maximum for a lone character is six. Whether the physical spark dial shows six is unconfirmed (A-047 raised the same doubt for the standard game).
- PvP encounters in campaign play (§18.1): no source says whether they are played. The chapter applies the optional rule after each boss, as §20.1 does, and names the campaign's final boss for the ultimate victor. For a boss whose defeat does not complete its section, no rest follows, so the chapter restores the participants as at the start of the PvP encounter and returns the dead to the tile; this rule is new. §20.9 names only §6.6 and §6.7 as the setup that follows; it needs a campaign cross-reference to §18.1.
- Mid-section bosses (§18.1, §18.3): the manuscript provides for a section with more than one boss, but in the twelve campaigns every boss ends its section (the doubled Gargoyle is one encounter under §17.1). The chapter states the general rule (spark and rewards, no free rest, no restock, exploration continues). §16.5 must defer to §18.1 and §18.3 in campaign play: the spark is capped at the dial's maximum, the free rest and restock follow only a completed section, and the defeat of a mega-boss that is not the campaign's final boss does not win the game.
- Section completion without a boss (§18.1): CMP-0008 grants a free rest and a restock but no spark; A-048's proposed fix granted a spark as well. CMP-0008 and the chapter instructions govern.
- Section setup details (§18.1) inferred, not in any source: the tiles for each section are drawn at random from all of the encounter tiles and placed with either side face up; the encounter decks are built from the sets the campaign names; the used encounter cards return to the game box as in §6.6. §19 must name the encounter sets for every campaign.
- Level 4 encounters in sections (§18.1): the chapter uses the game board and Level 4 cards of the mega-boss the section lists, or of a mega-boss expansion the campaign requires when the section lists none (Into the Depths, Section Four, where a Level 4 encounter precedes Ornstein & Smough). Two Level 4 encounters in one section (Call of the Abyss, Into the Depths) are fought in turn on the one board. Each awards one mega-boss treasure card as in §6.7; the author must confirm that reward for a section with two Level 4 encounters or with no mega-boss. A cleared Level 4 encounter is crossed at no stamina cost; confirm.
- Victory (§18.1): the campaign's final boss is the last boss of the last section; a campaign whose last section ends in an encounter (The Gauntlet) is won when that encounter is cleared. Big Game Hunting (sections Two A, B, and C in any order, won when all three are completed) and Demon Hunting (Section One has no bonfire tile) need their own statements in §19.12 and §19.6; §18.1 gives campaign rules precedence.
- Adding and dropping players (§18.4): changes are made on the bonfire tile between encounters, not only during a rest as F-039 proposed, so that joining costs one spark and not two. The join threshold of two sparks follows B-013 (A-105 proposed one). Not in any source and to be confirmed: the new character begins with no souls; treasure cards on uncleared tiles are topped up or reduced by one; the departing character's cards, including starting equipment, pass to the remaining characters; the party is limited to four characters; whether a campaign reduced to one character grants the six souls of §6.1.
- Sold legendary treasure (§18.6): the campaign table gives a sell value of four souls. The chapter places a sold legendary card on the bottom of the legendary treasure deck (its "respective deck" in campaign play); A-077 proposed the game box. Confirm.
- Transposition timing (§18.8): the manuscript says "after the final boss of the first section is defeated"; the chapter writes "last boss", since final boss is a defined term for the campaign's last boss. Three campaigns have a first section that ends in an encounter (Facing the Abyss, Playing with Fire, The Gauntlet); for these the chapter opens transposition when the first section is completed. A-051 is applied: any rest qualifies, whether or not it cost a spark.
- Invaders in campaign play (§18.1): the chapter limits invasions to one per section, mapping OQ-28's "one per leg". No source says which invader tokens (standard or advanced) are used in a section; §12.2 keys them to the mini-boss's defeat. §12.2 or §19 must state the campaign rule.
- Bonfire ascetic exemptions (§18.7): the manuscript exempts invaders and bosses. The chapter follows §8.3 and exempts every enemy that is not scaled (boss, mimic, phantom, crystal lizard). No component marks the ascetic as burnt; a campaign record sheet or a marker needs a line in §2.8 if the author wants one.
- Glossary: Fog gate is not amended here, because the chapter 15 delta already carries its campaign clause ("aesthetic only"); §18.2 says the token marks the boss doorway, is not moved, and gives no shortcut, which the lead may wish to reflect in that entry. The amendments to Final boss (§7.1), Level 4 encounter, Encounter level and Mega-boss (§6.5, §6.7), Resting at the bonfire (§11.2), and Legendary treasure (§6.2, also amended by the chapter 06 delta) add only a campaign clause to entries owned by other chapters. The lead must merge them with those chapters' deltas.
- C-103 (qualify the campaign headings "Stat Tier Costs", "Shop Price Table", "The Fog Gate", "Sparks", "Setup") is not applied because the section map fixes the titles; cross-references carry the section number, which keeps them unambiguous.

Resolved at integration (lead editor): campaign soul rewards follow Gate 1 OQ-21 (standard rewards kept, doubled stat costs retained; CH-18-038). The author must confirm this, because OQ-05's illustrative campaign figures (1/1/2/2) and the Preserve list ("soul rewards one lower") assumed the manuscript's table of 2/3/4/5.
- Boss defeated before the end of a section (§18.1): the dead are revived without hollow cubes, because §16.5 embers every character at a boss's defeat. No published campaign lists two bosses in one section; confirm the ruling for campaigns of the players' own design.
