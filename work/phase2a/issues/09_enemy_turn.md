# Issues — chapter 09 (Enemy Turn)

- Nearest-character tie-break (§9.2, §9.3): the chapter instruction read "ties: players choose". The manuscript's rule for movement, and D-008's approved fix for attacks, is deterministic: the tied character who holds the aggro token, otherwise the tied character with the higher threat. The chapter keeps that rule and adds the players' choice only as the last step, for tied characters of equal threat. Replacing the whole tie-break with a free choice would alter a complete rule outside the five signed-off mechanical changes. The lead editor must confirm; a free choice needs only the second and third sentences of the **Nearest character** definition in §9.2 and the glossary ADD changed.
- B-002 and B-033 (both approved PROPOSED) are not applied. B-002 raises the block cap to three dice with a shield and B-033 cuts the extra dodge die to one stamina; both alter rules on the preserve list (two highest dice; two further stamina for one die), and the chapter instructions restate the preserved values.
- Parry slot (§9.6; chapter 03 issue): settled as the preserve list states it, a shield in the left weapon slot and the riposte with the weapon in the right weapon slot. A-068's wider fix (shield in either slot) is not adopted. §3.2's sentence and the glossary entries **Parry** and **Riposte** agree with §9.6; no change to §3.2 is needed.
- Riposte weapon (§9.6): the manuscript says "the weapon equipped to the right weapon slot". A spell is not a weapon (§4.1), so as drafted a spell equipped in the right weapon slot cannot riposte, and neither can a spell stored in a catalyst there. The author must confirm.
- Pushes (§9.2 and §8.4): the chapter instructions gave §9.2 the push-move and the "push-attack as movement", while chapter 08, drafted in parallel, states the per-node push steps in §8.4. To keep one statement of the rule, §9.2 defines the icon, fixes the target before any character is pushed, has the enemy follow the target's current node, and cites §8.4 for the push steps and directions. Terms follow §8.4 and the glossary: a push-attack is an attack icon that bears the push icon, and the damaging movement is "a push-move with a damage value" (the manuscript's sub-heading "Push-attack" is retitled). Two statements are made only in §9.2 and §8.4 must not contradict them: the attack of a push-move with a damage value cannot be parried, and a character pushed onto a node the enemy enters afterwards is attacked again there. If the lead editor prefers §9.2 to own the steps, they move from §8.4 unchanged.
- Push-move damage and scaling (A-025, OPTIONAL, applied): §9.2 and the draft of §8.3 agree that the damage value of a push-move is scaled as an attack value is.
- Repeat icon (§9.3; B-005): Gate 1 (OQ-07) removed Repeat 2 from enemy scaling, so no rule in this book now adds a repeat icon to an enemy. §9.3 limits a printed repeat icon to the attack icon beside it, while the draft of §8.2 says it "shows the number of times the behaviour is resolved"; the two must be aligned. "The target is determined afresh each time" has no manuscript source and must be confirmed.
- Stagger (§9.3, §9.6): the chapter cites §8.5 for the effect of a stagger token on an enemy and for its duration (OQ-16). The first parry example (block reduced from 1 to 0) agrees with the draft of §8.5.
- Fixed defence modifiers (§9.4; D-033, A-120): the chapter applies every fixed modifier of the equipped cards after the two highest dice are added, whether or not a die of the modified card is among the two highest, and treats a defence total of less than zero as zero. Neither detail is in the manuscript; the author must confirm.
- Failed dodge (§9.5): the dice of upgrade cards attached to the armour card are rolled with the armour card's dice, because §3.2 treats attached upgrade cards as equipped with their card. The manuscript says only "armour". The author must confirm.
- Dodge and parry with a full endurance bar (§9.5, §9.6): following §3.4, a character who has no empty space for the stamina cube cannot choose to dodge or parry. §5.5, Traps offers "suffer the damage or attempt to dodge"; such a character suffers the damage.
- "Do nothing" (§9.3, Defence): the manuscript lists doing nothing as a fifth defence. The chapter omits it, because a block or resist with no dice has the same result (the full attack value, and the character is hit).
- Unreachable target (§9.2, Paths): as instructed, an enemy with no path of valid nodes to its target's node does not move, even where a move would bring it nearer. The author may prefer "moves as near as it is able".
- Summons (§9.1): the chapter states that enemies target only characters. If a boss or an invader may target a summon, §12.1 and §16.3 must say so; no enemy rule here allows it.
- Boss chapters: §15.6 must state which boss attacks may be parried (the manuscript says "boss-arc attacks cannot be parried"). A-018 (aggro after a riposte damages a boss) belongs to §16.4 and A-042 (heat-up caused by a riposte during the boss's own turn) to §15.5; neither is stated here.
- Player versus player: the chapter instruction says the riposte is one "which the enemy cannot dodge or parry". The draft of §20.4 lets a character targeted by a riposte block, resist, or dodge it, but not parry it. §9.6 therefore states the rule for enemies only and cites §20.4 for a riposte against a character; the glossary entry **Riposte** keeps "cannot be parried", which holds in both cases.
- Icons: the manuscript calls the nearest-character icon "a small ring icon"; this chapter and the draft of §8.2 name it by its function. `ast-0058-push-affected-enemies` is attached to the push icon, as the draft of §8.4 attaches it, although the manifest describes it as a character-attack icon. The extracted set has no slug for the skull (aggro) icon or the crossed swords icon. The glossary calls the magical damage icon "the white circle icon" while §4.3 and §9.3 call it "the magical damage icon"; the glossary owner must align the wording with the printed icon.
- **Dodge difficulty** is defined in §9.5 because the dodge, the parry, and the trap all use it; the draft of §8.2 lists it as a data card value and cites §9.5.
- Glossary: the ADD lines for **Push-attack** and **Push-move** are copied verbatim from chapter 08's delta, so that the merge is idempotent whichever chapter is integrated first; if chapter 08 revises them, this file must follow. No AMEND is filed for **Attack value**, which chapter 10's delta also amends; its enemy clause is already correct.
- Examples: a named enemy appears only where its values match the example in the draft of §8.3 (Hollow Soldier, movement value 1, attack value 4). Every other example says "an enemy", so that no example is read as the data of a printed card.
