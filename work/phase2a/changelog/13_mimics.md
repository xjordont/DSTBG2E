# Change log — chapter 13 (Mimics)

### CH-13-001 · §13 Mimics
- Type: restructure
- Before: "# MIMICS" followed by four prose paragraphs and an empty heading used as a page break
- After: "# 13. Mimics" / "## 13.1 Chests and Ambush Cards" / "## 13.2 The Mimic Encounter" / "## 13.3 Mimic Rewards" / "## 13.4 Failed Mimic Encounters"
- Rationale: The chapter owns the whole chest-and-mimic procedure, which the manuscript split between three chapters. It is divided into the decision, the encounter, its rewards, and its failure; the empty heading is deleted and an intro without rules is added.
- Source: CMP-0014, C-089, C-098, C-104, C-120

### CH-13-002 · §13 Mimics
- Type: cut
- Before: "Players with the Encounters expansion set available may choose whether they wish to include mimics in their game during setup. Groups with new players should default to leaving mimics out as they can be quite punishing."
- After: "*Mimics* are an optional rule. They require the Explorers expansion. The party decides during setup whether mimics are in play."
- Rationale: The advice to groups and the scope disclaimer are replaced by the fixed optional-rule formula. The expansion is the Explorers expansion; "Encounters" named a product that does not exist.
- Source: F-023, F-051, C-018, C-040, A-101, C-124, C-121

### CH-13-003 · §13.1 Chests and Ambush Cards
- Type: completion
- Before: new text
- After: "When *mimics* are in play, one *ambush card* is dealt face down beside each encounter tile at setup (see §6.5; §6.6). An ambush card shows Treasure or Mimic on its face (see §2.6)."
- Rationale: The chapter used the ambush card without saying where it came from or what it shows. The dealing itself is owned by the setup sections and is cited.
- Source: CMP-0014, A-092

### CH-13-004 · §13.1 Chests and Ambush Cards
- Type: clarification
- Before: "After defeating an encounter that contains an unopened chest, characters do not recover normally and remain in their current node."
- After: "The characters remain on their nodes. No character recovers, no dead character is revived, and no *souls* or treasure are collected."
- Rationale: The deferral left open whether dead characters were revived before the chest was opened and whether rewards were collected first. Resolution and recovery are both deferred until the chest is settled.
- Source: A-060, CMP-0014

### CH-13-005 · §13.1 Chests and Ambush Cards
- Type: clarification
- Before: "Characters must collectively decide whether they wish to open the chest or leave it be out of fear of encountering a mimic. If the characters cannot agree, the character with the *aggro* token makes the final decision."
- After: "The party then decides whether to open the chest or to forgo it. If the players cannot agree, the player whose character holds the *aggro token* decides."
- Rationale: Three passages gave three decision rules. The party is the collective actor, the players disagree, and the aggro holder settles it, as everywhere else in the book. The flavour clause is cut.
- Source: A-059, F-024, F-086, C-087; Gate 1 — OQ-29

### CH-13-006 · §13.1 Chests and Ambush Cards
- Type: mechanical change
- Before: "If the characters elect to leave the chest alone, they proceed to recover all stamina and two damage normally."
- After: "If the party forgoes the chest, the encounter is resolved and the characters recover in the ordinary way, except that the chest is not opened (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution)."
- Rationale: The manuscript healed two damage here and one in Encounter Recovery. Gate 1 fixed the amount at one damage cube; the chapter cites §7.5 and states the amount once, in §13.3.
- Source: Gate 1 — OQ-12; A-061, D-035

### CH-13-011 · §13.2 The Mimic Encounter

### CH-13-012 · §13.2 The Mimic Encounter
- Type: completion
- Before: "Characters remain on the nodes they occupied at the end of the encounter containing the chest."
- After: "Remove the chest token from its node and place the mimic model on that node." / "A character who died in the cleared encounter stays dead and takes no part in the mimic encounter."

### CH-13-013 · §13.2 The Mimic Encounter
- Type: style
- Before: "The mimic will take its turn first. Refer to "**Boss Encounter**" rules for more details on how to manage the mimic's behaviour cards."
- After: "The mimic takes the first *turn*. The mimic and the characters then alternate turns as a boss and the characters do (see §16.1), and the aggro token moves as it does in a boss encounter (see §16.4)."
- Rationale: Present tense replaces the future. The quoted heading named no section; the reference is to the numbered boss sections, without an imperative addressed to the reader.
- Source: C-082, C-084, C-122, F-056, F-074, F-088

### CH-13-014 · §13.2 The Mimic Encounter
- Type: clarification
- Before: "The only difference is that the mimic does not have specific *heat-up* behaviour cards and instead just shuffles in one unused normal behaviour card when it heats-up."
- After: "When a mimic's health is reduced to or below the *heat-up* point ... on its data card, one of its unused behaviour cards, taken at random, is shuffled into its behaviour deck in place of a heat-up card (see §15.5)."
- Rationale: The heat-up is worded as the invader's is. It was not the only difference: a mimic has no arcs, so no attack against it gains the weak-arc die.
- Source: C-097, C-057, A-044

### CH-13-015 · §13.2 The Mimic Encounter
- Type: completion

### CH-13-016 · §13.3 Mimic Rewards

- Rationale: The two cards are the chest's own card and one more, in place of the chest's one. The deferred resolution and recovery of the encounter follow. The double full stop and the punctuation inside the italics are removed.

### CH-13-020 · §13.4 Failed Mimic Encounters
- Type: completion
- Before: "If the mimic kills all of the characters, the encounter is *failed*"
- After: "A *mimic encounter* is *failed* when every character has died, or when a *hollow* character dies and is *lost* (see §7.2). The *encounter* that contained the *chest* is then failed, although its enemies had been killed."
- Rationale: The manuscript did not say whether the original encounter's rewards survived a lost mimic encounter. They do not: the encounter is resolved as any failed encounter, under §7.5.
- Source: A-060, CMP-0014

### CH-13-021 · §13.4 Failed Mimic Encounters
- Type: clarification
- Before: "the characters will encounter the mimic next time they complete the normal encounter in which the mimic lives. However, once the mimic has been fought once, it must be fought again at the end of the encounter until it is killed."
- After: "Each later time that encounter is *cleared*, the party again decides whether to open the chest (see §13.1). Opening the chest begins a new mimic encounter, set up afresh (see §13.2)."
- Rationale: The two sentences were incompatible. The party keeps its choice, since it may always decline to open the chest, and the chest yields nothing until the mimic is killed.
- Source: CMP-0014

### CH-13-026 · §13.2
- Type: ruling
- Before: "The characters begin the mimic encounter as the cleared encounter left them. Each character keeps their damage cubes, stamina cubes, and condition tokens, and the *aggro token* stays with its holder."
- After: "Before the mimic's first turn, each character removes every stamina cube from their *endurance bar*. Nothing else is recovered. Each character keeps their damage cubes and condition tokens, no token is turned to its ready side, and the *aggro token* stays with its holder."
- Rationale: [MECHANICAL CHANGE] The ambush stays dangerous because damage carries over, but the fight no longer opens with characters who cannot act, and the party has no reason to stall on the last enemy to shed stamina before opening a chest.
- Source: final rulings, phantom-E19 (`06_final_rulings.md`); Audit finding B-035 (approved at Gate 1, not applied: CH-13-022); official Explorers booklet removes all cubes before the mimic (DS-Explorers-Rules-Web.txt lines 464-467); Hollowed Edition: 'Players heal as normal' before opening (lines 378-380).

### CH-13-027 · §13.2
- Type: ruling
- Before: "The Hungry Mimic is fought if the mini-boss has not been defeated, and the Voracious Mimic if it has."
- After: "The Hungry Mimic is fought if the mini-boss has not been defeated, and the Voracious Mimic if it has. In campaign play, the Hungry Mimic is fought while the current tier is tier one, and the Voracious Mimic once it is tier two (see §18.5)."
- Rationale: [MECHANICAL CHANGE] Same anchor as for invaders: a campaign has no single mini-boss, and the mimic's extra card is drawn from the treasure deck of the current tier, so the stronger mimic guards the stronger treasure.
- Source: final rulings, phantom-E20 (`06_final_rulings.md`); §13.3 step 2, §18.5 and §18.7 of this book; official Explorers booklet: 'a mini boss' (lines 476-482).

### CH-13-030 · §13.3 (example)
- Type: ruling
- Before: "The Hungry Mimic replaces the chest token, takes the first turn, and attacks the Knight, who still carries all seven cubes. The characters kill the mimic. The party gains the chest's treasure card and one card drawn from the tier one treasure deck. Each character then gains four souls and one of the treasure cards dealt to the tile, and the Knight removes all four stamina cubes and one damage cube."
- After: "The Hungry Mimic replaces the chest token, and the Knight removes the four stamina cubes. The mimic takes the first turn and attacks the Knight, who still carries the three damage cubes. The characters kill the mimic. The party gains the chest's treasure card and one card drawn from the tier one treasure deck. Each character then gains four souls and one of the treasure cards dealt to the tile, and the Knight removes the stamina cubes placed during the mimic encounter and one damage cube."
- Rationale: [MECHANICAL CHANGE] The example now computes from §13.2 as amended: stamina cubes are removed before the mimic's first turn, damage cubes are kept.
