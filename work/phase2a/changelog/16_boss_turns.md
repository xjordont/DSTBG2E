# Change log — chapter 16 (Boss Turns)

### CH-16-001 · §16 Boss Turns
- Type: completion
- Before: new text
- After: "A boss does not take its place in the line of lesser foes. It answers every blow, and each card it turns shows a new way to die."
- Rationale: The chapter had no intro; a 50-word intro in voice is added, with no rule inside it. The H2 titles follow the section map ("Overview", "Boss Movement and Symbols").
- Source: C-104, C-112

### CH-16-002 · §16.1 Overview
- Type: style
- Before: "During a boss encounter, the boss always takes their turn first. … After each boss turn, one character may take their turn, then the boss takes another turn, a second character, and so-on."
- After: "The *boss* takes the first *turn* of a boss *encounter* (see §15.2). After each boss turn one character takes a turn, and the boss then takes its next turn."
- Rationale: Filler ("and so-on") and "always" are removed; the alternation of boss and character turns is unchanged.
- Source: C-123; preserve list (bosses alternate boss and character)

### CH-16-003 · §16.1 Overview
- Type: correction
- Before: "characters must take an equal number of turns and cannot take a turn until every other character has taken an equal number of turns."
- After: "No living character takes a second turn in a round until every other living character has taken one. A dead character takes no turn and is not counted."
- Rationale: A dead character can take no turns, so the literal equal-turns rule blocked every living character after a death. The chapter also states that the players choose which living character takes each turn, which "one character may take their turn" implied.
- Source: A-019, A-022, C-114

### CH-16-004 · §16.1 Overview
- Type: correction
- Before: "Phantoms take their turn after all character turns are resolved and before the first enemy turn begins." (Summons chapter)
- After: "If a *summon* is in play, it takes a turn after each character's turn and before the boss's next turn (see §12.1)."
- Rationale: The summon's cadence is fitted to the alternating turns of a boss encounter; §12.1 owns the rule and this section cites it.
- Source: A-020, D-002; Gate 1 — OQ-32

### CH-16-005 · §16.1 Overview
- Type: restructure
- Before: "Draw the top card from the boss behaviour deck at the beginning of every boss turn. The boss's actions are then determined by resolving each symbol on the behaviour card from left to right. When the right-most symbol is resolved, the boss's turn ends."
- After: "Draw the top card of the behaviour deck and place it face up beside the deck. This card is the boss's current *behaviour card* (see §15.5, Card Anatomy)."
- Rationale: The boss turn becomes a five-step procedure: renew the deck if empty, draw, resolve left to right, repeat, discard. The renewal rule itself is owned by §15.5 and cited.
- Source: C-120, F-132, A-041

### CH-16-006 · §16.1 Overview
- Type: clarification
- Before: "The *repeat* symbol is similar to the character's equipment symbol and allows a boss to repeat its entire behaviour card multiple times before ending its turn."
- After: "If the card bears the *repeat* icon {{icon:ast-0061-repeat-behaviour-or-attack}}, resolve the whole row of icons again from left to right, until the row has been resolved the number of times the icon shows."
- Rationale: The repeat rule moves from Boss Attacks to the turn sequence, states the count, and is distinguished from an enemy's repeat icon, which repeats one attack (§9.3). Targets are determined again at each resolution.
- Source: A-024, C-027

### CH-16-007 · §16.1 Overview
- Type: completion
- Before: new text
- After: "If the boss is defeated during its own turn, as a *riposte* may cause, the turn ends at once and no further icon is resolved (see §16.5)."
- Rationale: States what happens to an icon that cannot be resolved (cited to §9.1), that conditions affect a boss, and the timing of a heat-up or a defeat that falls within the boss's own turn. A worked example of the turn order and of deck renewal is added.
- Source: A-042, A-018

### CH-16-008 · §16.1 Overview
- Type: cut
- Before: "Players can gain an advantage over a boss by memorising the order in which the boss behaviour cards will appear each time the deck is renewed."
- After: deleted
- Rationale: Tactical advice is not a rule; the unshuffled renewal it relies on is stated in §15.5.
- Source: F-033, C-121

### CH-16-009 · §16.2 Boss Movement and Symbols
- Type: clarification
- Before: "Bosses abide by the same enemy movement and attack rules previously described with the following exceptions."
- After: "A *boss* *moves* as an enemy moves (see §9.2), with the differences stated in this section."
- Rationale: The prose cross-reference becomes a numbered one. The section adds that boss movement is not scaled, that every node entered must be a valid node, and how a movement icon names its target.
- Source: F-078, C-084, A-082

### CH-16-010 · §16.2 Boss Movement and Symbols
- Type: restructure
- Before: "When a boss moves towards a target, it first rotates to align the centre of its front arc with the adjacent node closest to the target character. Once facing is resolved, the boss moves forward into the adjacent node it has just centred in its front arc."
- After: "1. Turn the boss so that the centre of its front arc faces the next node of its path. 2. Move the boss forwards onto that node."
- Rationale: Movement towards and away from a target become two-step procedures tied to the path rules of §9.2, so that a boss goes round blocking terrain and stops when no node is open to it, as §5.5 requires this section to state.
- Source: C-120, A-082; cross-chapter dependency from §5.5

### CH-16-011 · §16.2 Boss Movement and Symbols
- Type: correction
- Before: "the boss remains in the same node but rotates to bring the target model into the front-arc of the boss's base. The target character model should remain static while the boss model rotates underneath to change facing towards the target."
- After: "The boss turns in place until the arc in which the target is placed is its front arc: 90° towards a target in a side arc, or 180° for a target in the back arc."
- Rationale: "should" is replaced by a rule, and the turn is stated in the quarter turns that keep the boss facing an adjacent node.
- Source: C-019, F-089

### CH-16-012 · §16.2 Boss Movement and Symbols
- Type: clarification
- Before: "If a boss move behaviour does not indicate a target it moves the number of nodes indicated in the direction shown without rotating to change facing."
- After: "Each node it enters is the *adjacent node* at the centre of its front arc, its back arc, its left arc, or its right arc. If that node is not a valid node, the boss stops, and the rest of the movement is lost."
- Rationale: Names the node a boss enters when it moves in a direction and what happens when that node is blocked.
- Source: A-082

### CH-16-013 · §16.2 Boss Movement and Symbols
- Type: clarification
- Before: "the character is placed on the boss's base in the arc the character was in at the beginning of the boss's movement. If the character was on a node split between two arcs the player may choose either."
- After: "Each character is placed against the boss's base in the arc in which the node lay immediately before the boss entered it."
- Rationale: A boss turns before each node of a movement, so the arc is read at the moment of entry; the choice for a node in two arcs is kept.
- Source: F-130; preserve list (same-arc movement)

### CH-16-014 · §16.2 Boss Movement and Symbols
- Type: completion
- Before: new text
- After: "A character *pushed* off the boss's own node before the boss moves must be pushed within the arc in which the character is placed (see §15.6, Characters and Arcs)."
- Rationale: §8.4 resolves a boss's push-move as an enemy's "with the differences stated for boss movement"; this section states the three differences (turning, the arc limit, and the wall fallback for a character who cannot be pushed directly away) and the dodge difficulty used against a push-move with a damage value.
- Source: A-036; cross-chapter dependency from §8.4; preserve list (same-arc pushes)

### CH-16-015 · §16.2 Boss Movement and Symbols
- Type: completion
- Before: "If a boss *leaps* into a node occupied by a character, the boss maintains its original facing and the character(s) are pushed into any adjacent node of their choosing unrestricted by the boss arc."
- After: "**Leap.** A boss movement marked with the leap icon. The boss is placed directly on the node of its target, at any distance."
- Rationale: The leap icon was used but never defined. The chapter defines it, gives its three steps, and settles a leap onto the boss's own node and a character with no valid adjacent node.
- Source: A-038, C-033, A-082

### CH-16-016 · §16.2 Boss Movement and Symbols
- Type: restructure
- Before: "The *turn* symbol indicates the boss turns 90 degrees to the left or right as indicated by the direction of the quarter-turn arrow."
- After: "The turn 90° right icon {{icon:ast-0034-turn-90-right}}, the turn 90° left icon {{icon:ast-0035-turn-90-left}}, and the turn around icon {{icon:ast-0036-turn-around}} turn the boss in place on its node."
- Rationale: The turn icons are defined once, in §15.6, and cited here with their icon names. Two worked examples (a push-move to the wall and a turn towards a target on the boss's node) are added.
- Source: F-133, C-116

### CH-16-017 · §16.3 Boss Attacks
- Type: clarification
- Before: "Boss attacks follow the same rules as enemy attacks but can contain additional symbols with more powerful effects."
- After: "A *boss* attacks as an enemy attacks (see §9.3), with the differences stated in this section."
- Rationale: States where the attack value, range, and dodge difficulty of a boss attack are found, that they are not scaled, and that a boss does not turn to attack.
- Source: C-084, B-036 (no change to boss scaling)

### CH-16-018 · §16.3 Boss Attacks
- Type: completion
- Before: new text
- After: "The target is attacked in whatever *boss arc* they stand. If the target is not within the card's range, the icon is skipped, and no other character is attacked in the target's place (see §9.3, Targets)."
- Rationale: The manuscript described only area attacks; attacks on the aggro holder, on the nearest character, and on a node are stated by reference to §9.3, with a summon counted as a character as §12.1 requires.

### CH-16-019 · §16.3 Boss Attacks
- Type: clarification
- Before: "The *area* symbol indicates that the boss is attacking entire arcs instead of an individual character. Bosses do not change facing when they attack and instead deal damage to characters within range that occupy nodes in their attack arcs."
- After: "An *area attack* targets every character who is within the card's range of the boss and who is in an *attack arc* {{icon:ast-0040-attack-boss-arc}} of the arc diagram beneath the icon (see §15.6, Arc Diagrams)."
- Rationale: The area attack becomes a defined term with its icon; a node in two arcs and a character on the boss's own node are both covered.
- Source: C-027, C-114

### CH-16-020 · §16.3 Boss Attacks
- Type: clarification

### CH-16-021 · §16.4 Boss Aggro
- Type: style
- Before: "Because the boss takes a turn after each individual character takes their turn, one can assume any damage dealt by a character will be the most damage prior to the boss's activation. Therefore, simply move the *aggro* token to a character's dashboard if they deal any damage to the boss during their turn."
- After: "At the end of each character's *turn*, if that character inflicted damage on the boss during the turn, the aggro token moves to that character."
- Rationale: The reasoning is replaced by the rule it derived; "one", "simply", "Therefore", and "activation" are removed. The mechanic is unchanged.
- Source: F-034, F-087, F-091, F-090, C-039, C-123; preserve list (boss aggro moves on any damage)

### CH-16-023 · §16.4 Boss Aggro
- Type: clarification
- Before: "If no damage is dealt, the *aggro* token remains stationary for that turn."
- After: "If a riposte inflicts damage on the boss, the aggro token moves to the character who made it at the end of that boss turn, after the behaviour card has been resolved."
- Rationale: Covers damage dealt out of turn by a riposte, an attack reduced to no damage, the death of the holder, and a summon's damage (cited to §12.1). A worked example is added.

### CH-16-024 · §16.5 Boss Death
