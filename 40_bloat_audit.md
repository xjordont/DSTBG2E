# 40 — Bloat Audit against the Author's Manuscript (Class A)

Date: 2026-11-12. Question put by the author: how did the rules text end up with so much bloat and excess wording, measured against the original manuscript, and what should come out.

Method: word counts by script; every chapter of the final text read against the author's chapter by four independent readers, who classed each passage and traced each added rule to the change-log entry that introduced it (`work/phase4/bloat_core.md`, `bloat_combat.md`, `bloat_bosses.md`, `bloat_campaign.md`); glossary, F.A.Q. and appendices measured by the lead editor. Source and final figures are counted. "Needs" figures are the readers' estimates.

## 1. Verdict

The author wrote 20,500 words. The book is 77,500. A tight version of the same game, including the boss entries and campaigns the author asked to have added, needs about 29,000. **Roughly 48,000 words, more than 60% of the book, should not be there.** The author's complaint is correct, and the cause is the editorial process, not the source.

Five-sixths of the growth was in the first draft, before the author's later requests.

## 4. How it happened

1. **No length limit, and every incentive pointed up.** The drafting brief called the author's text "baseline, not ceiling". The gates counted findings closed, examples verified and glossary coverage. Nothing counted words. Of 1,032 logged changes, 36 were cuts, for a net 479 words removed.
2. **485 audit findings, each "resolved" by adding text.** 347 were rated Minor, Note, Clean-up or Optional. A rare case that one sentence would settle, or that the table could settle for itself, got a paragraph (for example "a character who adventures alone is therefore never revived hollow").
3. **"State a rule once, cite it elsewhere" became "summarise it everywhere".** Each chapter was drafted on its own and made self-sufficient: a one-sentence digest of the owning rule plus a bracketed pointer wherever the rule is touched. §16.3 restates chapter 9; §11 restates §6 and §7; the glossary restates everything.
4. **Copies had to be kept in step, and each fix added a clause.** Once a rule lived in four places, every ruling was mirrored four times, and the verification passes settled disagreements between copies by adding a qualifier to one, never by deleting the copy. The hollow marker alone went through six entries in four sections; the author's text was "a marker such as white cubes".
5. **Rulings bred rulings.** Parry from either slot needed three follow-ups and two more in verification. The attack sequence needed four clarifications in the last review. The heat-up timing needed six echo sentences in four chapters and changes nothing in play.
6. **Templates applied to everything.** A flavour introduction for every chapter; worked examples by standing instruction (42 in five combat chapters); a three-sentence formula for every optional rule; Components, Setup, Special ability, Heat-up and Unique icons for every boss, including bosses with nothing to say under a heading; numbered procedures "one action per step" where the author had a sentence.
7. **Coined terms.** Move, Valid node, relevant stat, next complete turn, victory bonus, ultimate victor. ("Lost" and the left and right weapon slots, first listed here, are the author's own terms; see `41_rules_workshop.md`.) Each needed a definition, some needed rules of their own, and Move produced a bug that took two fixes.
8. **The house voice.** Bullets became full third-person sentences with the actor named and the condition first. That alone roughly doubles a terse rule.
9. **Rules were changed inside bulk approvals.** Several of the author's rules were reversed or extended in the 33 Gate 1 answers and the final rulings, which were approved or delegated as a block. The cost in words and the departure from the author's rule were never put in front of the author one by one. Section 5 does that now.

## 6. What a trim would do

Target: about 29,000 words, near 50 to 60 pages in the present design against 124.

1. **The author's wording is the ceiling for each section**, plus a short list of fixes that close real holes (about 1,500 words in all: boss encounter setup, how a summon is attacked, how a character defends in player versus player, die faces, trap resolution, the zero-spark loss).
2. **One owner per rule, no digests.** A cross-reference only where the reader must turn the page to act.
3. **Examples: about a dozen**, where the rule is hard without one (push direction, boss arcs, scaling).
4. **Chapter 2 as a packing list. Chapter 19 as one compact table per campaign. Chapter 17 with one deck table** in place of nine setup procedures, no component lists, and the node-targeted attack defined once.
5. **Glossary: one line per term and a section number**, or an index only. **F.A.Q.: dropped**, or the ten answers a player would not guess.
6. **Coined terms and templates removed**; the rules in section 5 restored or cut as the author decides.
7. **A word budget per chapter in the build's QA**, and verification by checking that every rule of the author's survives, not by looking for gaps to fill.

What is lost: the lawbook completeness. Rare corner cases go back to the table, as in the author's manuscript and the published game.
