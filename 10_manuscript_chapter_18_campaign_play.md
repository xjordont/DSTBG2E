# 18. Campaign Play

A campaign is a long road between many fires. The party carries its *souls*, its equipment, and its scars from one *section* to the next, and each *bonfire* it reaches lies deeper in the dark than the last.

## 18.1 Setup

Campaign play follows the standard game except as this chapter states. Where a *campaign* states a rule of its own, that rule takes precedence over this chapter (see §19).

**Campaign.** A game played through a fixed list of *sections*, taken in order, in which the characters keep what they have gained from one section to the next.

**Section.** One list of tiles within a campaign: a *bonfire* tile, a number of encounter tiles with their *encounter levels*, and the bosses to be faced.

The party chooses one of the 16 campaigns (see §19). Two of them, The First Journey and The Coiled Sword, require only the core set (see §19.1, §19.2). Each of the other campaigns states the expansions it requires. A campaign has three to six sections, and each section lists its tiles in order outward from the bonfire tile.

Each character keeps their *stat tiers*, *treasure cards*, *souls*, and tokens from one section to the next. Death and hollowing follow the standard game in every *encounter* of a campaign, including every *mega-boss* encounter (see §7.2).

### Campaign Setup

At the start of a campaign the party carries out the setup of the standard game (see §6), with the following changes.

- The party does not select bosses, and random boss selection is not used (see §6.5, Boss Selection). The bosses are those the campaign lists.
- *Legendary treasure* cards are set aside and enter neither treasure deck at any time (see §18.8).
- The spark dial is set as in the standard game, and its maximum is fixed at the same time (see §18.3).
- The tiles, *encounter cards*, and treasure cards are laid out for the campaign's first section, in place of the encounter setup of the standard game (see §18.1, Section Setup).
- The optional rules of the standard game are chosen during setup (see §6.5, Optional Rules), with one exception. A mega-boss is not chosen as an optional rule and is faced only where the campaign lists one.

### Section Setup

The first section is set up at the start of the campaign. Every other section is set up when the section before it is completed, by the setup that follows the *mini-boss* in the standard game (see §6.6). In both cases the section's list takes the place of the standard route, as follows.

- As many encounter tiles as the section lists encounters are drawn at random from all of the encounter tiles, in place of the four of the standard game. Each is placed with either side face up, as the party chooses.
- The bonfire tile, the encounter tiles, and the tile of each boss are arranged as a single route in the order the section lists them (see §6.5, Encounter Tiles).
- Each encounter tile is dealt one encounter card of the level the section lists for its position, in place of the levels printed on a boss's data card. Where the section names an encounter, that encounter card is placed face down on the tile in place of a dealt card.
- The encounter decks are built from the encounter cards of every set the campaign requires, shuffled together by level, in place of the set that supplies the next boss (see §6.5, Expansion Encounters). Where a campaign names the sets for its encounter decks, only those sets are used.
- Each encounter tile is dealt one treasure card per character from the treasure deck of the current tier (see §18.5).
- A mini-boss is fought on the mini-boss tile, or on its expansion's game board when it is the Asylum Demon (see §17.3), and a *main-boss* on the two *boss tiles* joined as one (see §15.3). A mega-boss is fought on its game board (see §18.1, Level 4 Encounters and Mega-Bosses).
- A section that lists the Gargoyle as fought twice sets the Twin Gargoyles encounter, which is one boss encounter (see §17.1, Twin Gargoyles).
- The *fog gate* token is placed on the *doorway* to the boss's tile (see §18.2). A section that lists no boss has no fog gate.
- The *shop* is restocked as campaign play directs (see §18.6, Blacksmith Andre's Shop), and no *spark* is added during the setup of a section (see §18.3).
- If *invaders* are in play, no more than one invasion is provoked in each section (see §12.2). The invader token drawn for an invasion is a standard invader token until the *bonfire ascetic* is burnt and an advanced invader token from then on (see §18.7).

### Section Completion

A section is completed when the last *boss* it lists is defeated (see §16.5). A section whose list ends in an encounter instead of a boss is completed when that encounter is *cleared* for the first time (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution).

When a section is completed by the defeat of a boss, the boss's defeat is resolved as in the standard game (see §16.5), with two changes. The party gains its spark at once, up to the dial's maximum (see §18.3). The setup that follows the boss is the setup of the next section (see §18.1, Section Setup), and the characters then rest at the bonfire without spending a spark.

When a section is completed by clearing an encounter, the encounter is resolved as any cleared encounter (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution). The party then sets up the next section, and the characters rest at the bonfire without spending a spark. No spark is gained (see §18.3).

When the last section of the campaign is completed, the campaign ends, and no setup and no rest follow (see §18.1, The Final Boss).

When a boss is defeated and its section is not yet completed, the party gains the boss's rewards and its spark (see §16.5, §18.3). Each surviving character recovers, and each character who died in the boss encounter is revived, as after any cleared encounter, except that the ember token of the boss's defeat keeps a revived character from becoming *hollow*, so no hollow cube is placed (see §7.5, Encounter Recovery; §16.5). The exploration then continues. No section is set up, the shop is not restocked, and no rest is granted.

### Level 4 Encounters and Mega-Bosses

A *Level 4 encounter* listed in a section is fought on the Level 4 encounter side of a *mega-boss game board*. The game board takes the place of an encounter tile at that position in the section's route. The game board and the Level 4 encounter cards are those of the mega-boss the section lists. If the section lists no mega-boss, they are those of a mega-boss expansion the campaign requires.

The Level 4 encounter is set up and fought as in the standard game (see §6.7, Level 4 Encounters). It is rewarded as any other encounter of its section: every character gains the soul reward of a Level 4 encounter (see §18.5), and one treasure card per character of the current tier is dealt to the game board with its encounter card. No mega-boss treasure card is dealt to a Level 4 encounter in campaign play; a mega-boss's treasure cards are gained only by defeating that mega-boss (see §16.5). Where the section names the Level 4 encounter, that encounter card is used. A cleared Level 4 encounter is not fought again and is not counted as a skipped encounter when the party next travels the route (see §7.5, Encounter Start).

When a section lists two Level 4 encounters, both are fought on the same game board, one after the other. When the first is cleared, the party removes every token, model, and card from the game board and sets up the second Level 4 encounter on it.

A mega-boss listed in a section is fought on the mega-boss side of its game board, which is set up when the section's last Level 4 encounter is cleared (see §6.7, The Mega-Boss Encounter). The game board keeps its place in the route. A mega-boss that is not the campaign's *final boss* is fought under the same rules as any other boss. Its defeat grants the rewards of a boss (see §16.5) and does not end the campaign, which continues with the next section.

### The Final Boss

The campaign's final boss is the last boss listed in its last section. The players win the campaign when the final boss is defeated (see §7.1). A campaign whose last section ends in an encounter has no final boss, and the players win it when that encounter is cleared. The players lose a campaign as they lose the standard game (see §7.1, §18.3).

### Player versus Player Encounters

Player versus player (PvP) encounters remain an optional rule in campaign play (see §20.1). When they are in play, a *PvP encounter* follows the defeat of each boss whose defeat completes a section. A boss defeated before its section is completed is followed by no PvP encounter. The PvP encounter that follows the campaign's final boss decides the *ultimate victor* (see §20.8).

The setup and the rest that follow the PvP encounter are those of the completed section (see §18.1, Section Completion; §20.9).

## 18.2 The Fog Gate

In campaign play the *fog gate* token marks the *doorway* through which the characters enter a *boss* *encounter* and has no other effect. It is placed during the setup of a *section* on the doorway to the boss's tile, as in the standard game (see §15.1). The boss encounter begins when the characters pass through it (see §15.2).

The token is not moved to the *bonfire* tile when the characters first pass through it, and it gives no shortcut. After every rest the party travels the route from the first encounter tile, and each encounter on the route is fought or skipped (see §7.5, Encounter Start).

An *embered* character may call a *summon* at the fog gate, as in the standard game (see §12.1).

## 18.3 Sparks

The *spark* dial is set at the start of a *campaign* to six minus the number of characters, as in the standard game (see §6.4, §7.4). The dial's maximum is that starting value plus one. The dial cannot be turned past its maximum, and a spark gained while the dial shows its maximum is not added.

| Characters | Starting *sparks* | Maximum *sparks* |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 5 | 6 |
| 2 | 4 | 5 |
| 3 | 3 | 4 |
| 4 | 2 | 3 |

Each time a *mini-boss*, *main-boss*, or *mega-boss* is defeated, the party gains one spark, up to the maximum (see §16.5). The Twin Gargoyles are one boss and grant one spark (see §17.1, Twin Gargoyles). Clearing an *encounter* grants no spark, even when it completes a *section*. The spark for a boss is added when the boss is defeated, and the setup of the next section adds no other.

A rest at the *bonfire* costs one spark, as in the standard game (see §11.2). A rest is free only when it follows the completion of a section (see §18.1, Section Completion). The defeat of a boss that does not complete its section grants a spark but no free rest.

While the party rests, it may buy back one spark. The cost is four *souls* for each character in the party, paid to the supply by any combination of the characters, and the spark dial is turned up by one. The spark is not bought unless the full cost is paid. This cost and the cost of the *bonfire ascetic* are the only costs that the characters pay together (see §18.7); souls are never passed from one character to another (see §7.6).

The party may buy no more than one spark during each rest and cannot buy a spark while the dial shows its maximum. The spark is bought after the rest's own spark has been spent, and it may be bought during a free rest. Sparks are gained in no other way, except when a player leaves the campaign (see §18.4).

If the characters are required to rest while the spark dial shows zero, the players lose the campaign (see §7.1). The party cannot rest while the dial shows zero, so no spark is bought to prevent that loss.

*Example.* The Knight, the Herald, and the Assassin begin a campaign with the spark dial at three, and its maximum is four. The party has rested once, and the dial shows two. After clearing the second encounter of the first section, the party returns to the bonfire tile and rests again, and the dial is turned down to one. The Knight holds six souls, the Herald four, and the Assassin five. A spark costs 12 souls for three characters. The Knight pays six souls, the Herald two, and the Assassin four, and the dial is turned up to two. The Knight is left with no souls, the Herald with two, and the Assassin with one.

## 18.4 Adding and Dropping Players

A player may join or leave a *campaign* in progress. Either change is made only while the characters are on the *bonfire* tile and no *encounter* is in progress. A player joins or leaves a standard game in progress under the same rules; the steps that set the spark dial's maximum apply only in campaign play. The party has no more than four characters (see §1).

The party spends one *spark* for each player who joins. A player cannot join while the spark dial shows zero. When a player joins, the party carries out the following steps.

1. Turn the spark dial down by one.
2. Set up the new character as at the start of the game, with *starting equipment*, every stat at the base tier, and no *souls* (see §6.1).
3. Place the new character's model on the bonfire tile.
4. Set the spark dial's maximum for the new number of characters (see §18.3).
5. If the spark dial shows more than the new maximum, turn it down to the new maximum.
6. Deal one *treasure card* of the current tier face down to each encounter tile that still holds its treasure cards (see §18.5).

The party gains one spark when a player leaves. When a player leaves, the party carries out the following steps.

1. Give every treasure card the departing character holds to the remaining characters, as the players choose. Each card is placed in the *inventory* of the character who receives it.
2. Return the departing character's souls, and any *dropped souls* of that character still on a tile, to the supply.
3. Return the departing character's model, dashboard, and tokens to the game box.
4. Set the spark dial's maximum for the new number of characters (see §18.3).
5. Turn the spark dial up by one, unless it shows the new maximum.
6. Remove one treasure card at random from each encounter tile that still holds its treasure cards, and place it on the bottom of the treasure deck of its tier.

From the moment a player joins or leaves, the new number of characters is used for enemy scaling (see §8.3), for the spark dial's maximum, and for every cost and reward counted per character. No *ember card* is added to the *shop* when a player joins (see §18.6, Blacksmith Andre's Shop).

*Example.* The Knight, the Herald, and the Assassin stand on the bonfire tile with the spark dial at three. The Warrior's player joins the campaign. The party turns the dial down to two and sets up the Warrior with starting equipment and no souls. The campaign now has four characters, so the dial's maximum falls from four to three, and every enemy placed from then on is scaled for four characters.

## 18.5 Encounter Rewards

*Encounters* are rewarded as in the standard game (see §7.6, §7.7). The *souls* awarded are those of the standard reward table: the full reward and the *reduced reward* for each *encounter level*, and the rewards for *invaders* and bosses, are not changed in campaign play (see §7.6).

An encounter already marked *cleared* is skipped or fought again as in the standard game (see §7.5, Encounter Start). Fought again, it awards the reduced reward and no treasure.

The first time an encounter is cleared, each character takes one of the *treasure cards* dealt to its tile, as in the standard game (see §7.7). In campaign play the current tier is tier one from the start of the *campaign* until the *bonfire ascetic* is burnt, whatever bosses have been defeated, and tier two from then on (see §18.7). Every treasure card dealt to an encounter tile or placed beneath a *chest* {{icon:ast-0009-treasure-chest}} comes from the treasure deck of the current tier (see §5.5, Chests). Treasure cards already dealt when the bonfire ascetic is burnt are not replaced.

*Boss treasure* is gained only by defeating bosses (see §16.5). A *Level 4 encounter* awards treasure cards of the current tier, as any other encounter does, and no mega-boss treasure card (see §18.1, Level 4 Encounters and Mega-Bosses). *Legendary treasure* enters neither treasure deck and is gained only by *transposition* (see §18.8).

## 18.6 The Bonfire

The *bonfire* rules of the standard game apply in campaign play (see §11), with the changes of this section. While the party rests, it may also buy back a *spark* (see §18.3), burn the *bonfire ascetic* (see §18.7), and transpose *boss treasure* (see §18.8).

### Stat Tier Costs

The *Firekeeper* raises stats as in the standard game (see §11.3), at the following costs.

| *Stat tier* bought | Cost in *souls* |
|---|---|
| Stat tier one | 4 |
| Stat tier two | 8 |
| Stat tier three | 16 |
| Stat tier four | 20 |

The value of a stat at stat tier four is the same as in the standard game (see §3.3).

### Blacksmith Andre's Shop

Blacksmith Andre's *shop* follows the standard game (see §11.4), with the following changes.

The shop is restocked only when a *section* is completed, during the setup of the next section and before the rest that follows (see §18.1, Section Completion). The defeat of a boss that does not complete its section does not restock the shop. Before the shop is restocked, its unsold cards, other than *ember cards*, are placed on the bottom of their treasure decks (see §6.3, Restocking After a Boss).

The tier two shop is closed until the bonfire ascetic is burnt, and the defeat of a *mini-boss* does not open it. It is stocked at once when the bonfire ascetic is burnt (see §18.7). From then on it is restocked with the tier one shop each time a section is completed.

No *legendary treasure* card and no boss treasure card enters either treasure deck or either shop. The step of the standard restock that shuffles legendary treasure into the tier two treasure deck is not carried out (see §6.3, Restocking After a Boss). Boss treasure is gained only by defeating bosses (see §16.5), and legendary treasure only by *transposition* (see §18.8).

No ember card is added to the shop after the setup of the campaign, whether a section is completed or a player joins (see §6.3, §18.4).

### Shop Price Table

| Card or service | Purchase cost in *souls* | Sell value in *souls* |
|---|---|---|
| Tier one card | 2 | 1 |
| Tier two card | 4 | 1 |
| *Boss treasure* card | - | 4 |
| *Legendary treasure* card (*transposition*) | 4 and one boss treasure card | 4 |
| *Spark* | 4 for each character, paid by the party | - |
| *Bonfire ascetic* | 16, paid by the party | - |

A boss treasure card sells for four souls in campaign play, in place of the six of the standard game, and is returned to the game box when sold (see §11.4, Sales). A legendary treasure card sells for four souls and is placed face down on the bottom of the legendary treasure deck (see §18.8). An ember card is priced as a tier one card. Every other price, and every other rule of purchase and sale, is that of the standard game (see §11.4).

## 18.7 Bonfire Ascetic

In campaign play the defeat of a boss does not open tier two treasure. The party opens it by burning the *bonfire ascetic*.

The party may burn the bonfire ascetic once in a *campaign*, while resting at the *bonfire* (see §11.2). The cost is 16 *souls* in all, paid to the supply by any combination of the characters. This payment and the cost of a bought *spark* are the only exceptions to the rule that souls cannot be pooled (see §7.6; §18.3). The bonfire ascetic is not burnt unless the full 16 souls are paid.

When the bonfire ascetic is burnt, the party stocks the tier two *shop* from the tier two treasure deck, in the way the tier one shop is stocked at the start of the game and without *ember cards* (see §6.3, Stocking at the Start of the Game). The tier one shop is not restocked at that time. Both shops are next restocked when a *section* is completed (see §18.6, Blacksmith Andre's Shop).

From the moment the bonfire ascetic is burnt, the current tier is tier two for the rest of the campaign. Every treasure card dealt to an encounter tile or placed beneath a *chest* {{icon:ast-0009-treasure-chest}} from then on comes from the *tier two treasure* deck (see §18.5). Treasure cards already dealt are not replaced.

From the same moment, every enemy that is subject to enemy scaling has its base health increased by two for the rest of the campaign (see §8.3). The increase is cumulative with the health increase for the number of characters. The bonfire ascetic does not change enemy attack values or movement values.

A *boss*, a *mimic*, a *phantom*, and a *crystal lizard* are not affected by the bonfire ascetic (see §8.3).

*Example.* The Knight, the Herald, and the Assassin rest at the bonfire and burn the bonfire ascetic. The Knight pays eight souls, the Herald five, and the Assassin three, 16 in all. In the next encounter the party meets a Hollow Soldier, whose data card shows a base health of 1 and an attack value of 4. Its health is 5: the base health of 1, plus two for the bonfire ascetic, plus two for three characters. Its attack value stays at 4.

## 18.8 Transposition

*Transposition* exchanges a *boss treasure* card for a *legendary treasure* card. It is the only source of legendary treasure in campaign play.

Transposition is available once the first *section* of the *campaign* is completed (see §18.1, Section Completion). From then on a character may transpose during any rest at the *bonfire*, including a rest that costs no *spark* (see §11.2).

When transposition becomes available, the party prepares the legendary treasure cards as follows.

1. Gather every legendary treasure card and shuffle them face down to form the legendary treasure deck.
2. Place the legendary treasure deck beside the bonfire tile.
3. Deal five legendary treasure cards face up beside the bonfire tile.

During a rest, a character transposes as follows.

1. Return one boss treasure card that the character holds to the game box. The card takes no further part in the campaign.
2. Pay four *souls* to the supply.
3. Take any one of the five face-up legendary treasure cards and place it in the character's *inventory*.
4. Deal one card from the legendary treasure deck face up beside the bonfire tile, so that five cards are again face up.

A character may transpose any number of times during one rest. Each transposition requires its own boss treasure card and its own four souls, and the character pays with their own souls (see §7.6). If the legendary treasure deck is empty, step 4 is not carried out, and fewer than five cards remain face up.

A legendary treasure card is equipped and traded as any other treasure card, and it sells for four souls (see §18.6, Shop Price Table). *Transposed treasure* is a kind of tier two treasure card and is not gained by transposition (see §6.2).
