# 18. Campaign Play

## 18.1 Setup

A campaign is set up and played as the standard game, except as this chapter states. Campaigns are longer and harder than the standard game and suit players who have played it a few times. A campaign is played over several sessions, and one *section* is a convenient length for a session.

The party first chooses a campaign (see §19). Two campaigns need only the core set; each of the others names the expansions it needs. A campaign is a list of sections, played in order. Each section lists its encounters by level, in order from the *bonfire*, and the boss or bosses the party meets. A rule given with a campaign replaces the rules of this chapter.

Set up as in the standard game (see §6), with these changes.

1. Choose no bosses. The bosses are those the campaign lists.
2. Build the encounter decks from the *encounter cards* of every set the campaign needs, unless the campaign names the sets to use.
3. Draw one encounter tile for each encounter in the first section and lay them in a line from the bonfire tile to the boss tile.
4. Deal each tile a face-down encounter card of the level the section lists for it. Where the section names an encounter, place that card instead.
5. Deal treasure cards to the tiles from the *tier one* deck (see §18.5).

A Level 4 encounter is fought on the Level 4 side of a *mega-boss* game board, which takes the place of an encounter tile (see §6.7). The board and the Level 4 encounter cards are those of the section's mega-boss. Where the section has no mega-boss, they are those of a mega-boss expansion the campaign needs. The board is dealt treasure cards as any other tile, and no boss treasure card. Two Level 4 encounters in one section are fought on the same board, one after the other. The mega-boss is fought on the other side of the board once the section's last Level 4 encounter is *cleared*.

A section ends when its last boss is defeated. A section that lists no boss ends when its last encounter is first cleared. The party then restocks the *shop* (see §18.6), sets up the next section as it set up the first and *rests* without spending a *spark*. A boss that is not the last of its section is followed by no setup: the party plays on.

The characters keep their stats, treasure cards and *souls* from one section to the next. The players win the campaign when its last section ends.

## 18.2 The Fog Gate

In a campaign the *fog gate* gives no shortcut to the boss. The fog gate token is placed for show only.

## 18.3 Sparks

The party starts a campaign with *sparks* equal to five less the number of characters, as in the standard game. That number is also the most sparks the party may hold.

Each time a *mini-boss*, *main-boss* or *mega-boss* is defeated, the party gains one spark, never above the maximum. The party *rests* without spending a spark only when a *section* ends (see §18.1). After a boss that is not the last of its section a rest costs a spark. Sparks cannot be bought and are gained in no other way, except when a player leaves (see §18.4).

## 18.4 Adding and Dropping Players

A new player may join a campaign in progress at the cost of one *spark*. The new character begins with starting stats and *starting equipment*. The maximum number of sparks is then set for the new number of characters. When a player leaves, the party gains one spark, each of the departing character's cards goes to the *inventory* of another character, as the party chooses, and the maximum number of sparks is set for the new number of characters.

## 18.5 Encounter Rewards

- Every soul reward is one lower than in the standard game.
- A *reset* encounter is fought again or skipped as in the standard game (see §7.5).

### Campaign Soul Reward Table

Each reward is paid in *souls* to each character.

| | First clear | Reset encounter fought again |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1 encounter | 2 | 0 |
| Level 2 encounter | 3 | 1 |
| Level 3 encounter | 4 | 2 |
| Level 4 encounter or boss | 5 | - |
| Standard *invader* | 2 | - |
| Advanced invader | 3 | - |

### Encounter Treasure Rewards

- Each encounter tile holds two treasure cards, as in the standard game.
- Treasure cards for tiles and *chests* {{icon:ast-0009-treasure-chest}} come from the *tier one* deck, even after a *mini-boss* is defeated.
- Once the *bonfire ascetic* is burnt (see §18.7), they come from the *tier two* deck.
- A defeated boss gives the party one of its own *boss treasure* cards, drawn at random.

## 18.6 The Bonfire

The *bonfire* works as in the standard game (see §11). In a campaign stats cost more at the Firekeeper, so that the characters grow at the pace of a longer journey, and the *shop* is stocked differently. While it *rests*, the party may also burn the *bonfire ascetic* (see §18.7) and transpose *boss treasure* (see §18.8).

### Stat Tier Costs

| | Cost in *souls* |
|---|---|
| Stat tier one | 4 |
| Stat tier two | 8 |
| Stat tier three | 16 |
| Stat tier four | 24 |

### Blacksmith Andre's Shop

The shop follows the standard game, with these changes.

- The *tier one* and *tier two* shops are restocked only when a *section* ends. In a section with several bosses, only the last boss restocks them.
- The tier two shop is stocked only once the party has burnt the bonfire ascetic.
- No *legendary treasure* or boss treasure card is ever added to a treasure deck.
- Boss treasure is gained only by defeating bosses.
- Legendary treasure is gained only by *transposition*.
- Every card sells for one soul, boss treasure and legendary treasure included.

### Shop Price Table

| | Purchase cost in souls |
|---|---|
| Tier one shop card | 2 |
| Tier two shop card | 4 |
| Legendary treasure card | 4 and one boss treasure card |
| Bonfire ascetic | 16 |

## 18.7 Bonfire Ascetic

In a campaign, defeating a boss does not open *tier two* treasure. The party opens it by burning the *bonfire ascetic*, and must judge when better equipment is worth stronger enemies.

- The bonfire ascetic is burnt once in a campaign, while the party *rests* at the *bonfire*.
- It costs 16 *souls* in all, paid by the characters in any combination.
- The tier two *shop* is stocked at once. The *tier one* shop is not restocked; both are next restocked when the *section* ends.
- From then on, treasure cards dealt to tiles and placed under *chests* come from the tier two deck.
- From then on, every attack of a non-boss enemy has +1 damage for the rest of the campaign, on top of the scaling for party size (see §8.3). *Invaders*, mini-bosses, main-bosses and mega-bosses are not affected.
- Invader tokens are standard until the bonfire ascetic is burnt and advanced afterwards.

## 18.8 Transposition

Once the first *section* has ended, a character may transpose *boss treasure* to obtain *legendary treasure*. A character may transpose only during a *rest*.

1. The first time, gather every legendary treasure card and shuffle them to form the legendary treasure deck.
2. Deal five legendary treasure cards face up beside the *bonfire* tile.
3. The character removes one of their boss treasure cards from the campaign, pays four *souls* and takes any one of the five face-up cards.
4. Deal another legendary treasure card face up, so that five are again on offer.
