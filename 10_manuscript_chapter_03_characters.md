# 3. Characters

Each character is one of the undead who answer the *bonfire*'s call. The character is a model on the tiles and a dashboard beside the player, and the dashboard records what the character carries, learns, and suffers. This chapter describes the dashboard and its parts: the equipment slots, the stat progression track, the endurance bar, and the character tokens.

## 3.1 Character Dashboards

Each character is represented by a model and by the *character dashboard* of the same name. The model stands on the tiles and occupies a *node* (see §5.1). The dashboard records everything particular to the character's class, and it carries the cards, cubes, and tokens that change during play.

The character dashboard shows the following.

- The name and the illustration identify the character.
- The character's *heroic action* is printed in full (see §3.5, Heroic Action).
- The four equipment slots hold the character's *armour* and weapons: the armour slot, the left weapon slot, the right weapon slot, and the *back-up slot* (see §3.2).
- The stat progression track records the character's four stats (see §3.3).
- The *endurance bar* records the stamina the character has spent and the damage the character has suffered (see §3.4).
- The *threat* value {{icon:ast-0059-taunt}} fixes the character's place in the turn order.
- The four token slots hold the heroic action, *luck*, *estus flask*, and *ember* tokens (see §3.5).

**Threat.** The value that fixes a model's place in the turn order. Characters act in descending order of threat, and the character with the highest threat takes the *aggro* token at the start of each encounter (see §7.5, Encounter Start; §7.5, Encounter Turn Order). The dashboard prints the character's threat as its Taunt Level. Enemies and *phantoms* also have a threat value, printed on their *data cards* (see §8.2, §12).

The character's *souls* are kept on the dashboard as soul tokens (see §7.6). The character's inventory is kept beside the dashboard (see §3.2).

## 3.2 Equipment Slots

The dashboard has four equipment slots: the *armour* slot, the left weapon slot, the right weapon slot, and the *back-up slot*. Each slot holds only cards of the kind stated below. A character must meet an *equipment card*'s stat requirements to equip it (see §4.1).

**Armour slot.** The slot that holds one armour card, marked with the armour icon {{icon:ast-0047-armour}}. An armour card cannot be equipped in a weapon slot.

**Weapon slots.** The left weapon slot and the right weapon slot: the weapon slot on the left of the dashboard as its player reads it, and the weapon slot on its right. Each holds one weapon card, marked with the one-handed weapon icon {{icon:ast-0043-one-handed-weapon}} or the two-handed weapon icon {{icon:ast-0045-two-handed-weapon}}. A one-handed weapon may be equipped in either weapon slot, and the other weapon slot may hold a second one-handed weapon. A two-handed weapon may be equipped in either weapon slot, and the other weapon slot must then be empty. A one-handed weapon equipped while the other weapon slot is empty is wielded *two-handed* (see §10.4).

Shields are weapon cards and occupy a weapon slot (see §4.1). A shield in either weapon slot permits a *parry*, and the *riposte* is made with the weapon in the other weapon slot (see §9.6). Spell cards are equipped in a weapon slot or stored in an equipped *catalyst* (see §4.3).

**Back-up slot.** The slot that holds up to two *treasure cards* of any kind. Cards in the back-up slot are carried but not equipped. During an encounter, a character may exchange cards between the back-up slot and the armour and weapon slots only by the *Equip* minor action (see §10.1). An *ember card* must be in the back-up slot to be used (see §4.4).

**Equipped.** A card is equipped while it is in the armour slot or in a weapon slot. Cards in the back-up slot and in the inventory are not equipped. A character attacks only with the attack options of equipped cards (see §10.3) and rolls only the *block*, *resist*, and *dodge* dice shown on equipped cards (see §9.4, §9.5). Upgrade cards attached to an equipped card are equipped with it (see §4.2).

**Inventory.** A character's stack of unequipped treasure cards, kept face down beside the dashboard. The inventory has no limit. Outside an encounter, a character may move cards between the inventory and the dashboard's slots freely. During an encounter, the inventory cannot be accessed.

Outside an encounter, two characters may trade treasure cards between their inventories, one card for one card, if both players agree. During an encounter, characters cannot trade or give cards in any way.

*Example.* The Knight has a one-handed sword in the right weapon slot, a shield in the left weapon slot, armour in the armour slot, and a two-handed axe in the back-up slot. During an encounter the Knight takes the Equip minor action, moves the sword and the shield to the back-up slot, which now holds two cards, and equips the axe in the right weapon slot. The left weapon slot stays empty while the axe is equipped.

## 3.3 Stat Progression Track

The stat progression track records the character's four stats: strength (STR), dexterity (DEX), intelligence (INT), and faith (FTH). Each stat has its own track with a base space and spaces for *stat tier* one, two, and three. Each space is printed with the stat's value at that tier, and each character has its own values. The character's current value in a stat is the value printed in the space that holds that stat's *stat cube*.

At setup, one stat cube is placed in the base space of each of the four tracks (see §6.1). A stat rises one tier at a time, from the base tier to stat tier one, from stat tier one to stat tier two, and so on. When a stat rises one tier, the character moves its stat cube one space to the right.

Characters raise stats only while resting at the *bonfire*, by paying *souls* to the Firekeeper (see §11.3). Each tier must be bought in sequence, and each tier has its own cost (see §11.3; for campaign play, see §18.6, Stat Tier Costs).

The track has no space for stat tier four. When a stat rises to stat tier four, the character replaces the stat cube in the stat tier three space with a red cube and returns the stat cube to the supply. A stat at stat tier four has a value of 40, or the stat tier three value plus five, whichever is higher. Stat tier four is the highest tier.

A character must meet an equipment card's stat requirements to equip it (see §4.1). A weapon whose highest stat requirement the character's value in that stat exceeds by five or more is *scaled*; a weapon whose stat requirements are all zero is never scaled (see §10.5).

*Example.* The Warrior's strength stat cube is in the base space. While the characters rest at the bonfire, the Warrior pays the Firekeeper two souls for stat tier one and then four souls for stat tier two, and moves the strength stat cube two spaces to the right. The Warrior's strength is now the value printed in the stat tier two space.

## 3.4 Endurance Bar

The *endurance bar* is a row of 10 spaces along the bottom of the dashboard. It records both the stamina the character has spent and the damage the character has suffered. *Stamina cubes* (black) are placed from the left end of the bar and *damage cubes* (red) from the right end. The spaces not yet filled are the character's remaining capacity to spend stamina and to suffer damage.

The endurance bar is empty at the start of the game and is emptied when the characters rest at the *bonfire* (see §11.2). The bar may hold stamina cubes only, damage cubes only, or both.

When a character spends stamina, the character places one stamina cube for each point spent, filling the leftmost empty spaces first. When a character suffers damage, the character places one damage cube for each point of damage, filling the rightmost empty spaces first.

When a character recovers stamina, the character removes one stamina cube for each point recovered, taking the rightmost stamina cubes first. When a character recovers from damage, the character removes one damage cube for each point recovered, taking the leftmost damage cubes first. A recovery that finds no cube of the right kind to remove has no effect. A character recovers one stamina at the start of each of their turns (see §10.1).

When the two kinds of cube meet, the bar is full. If a character must place a damage cube and no empty space remains, the cube is not placed and the character dies (see §7.2). If a character must place a stamina cube and no empty space remains, the cube is not placed and the character is *staggered* {{icon:ast-0066-stagger}} instead; a character cannot choose to spend more stamina than the bar holds (see §10.1).

When a character is revived *hollow* (see §7.2), two *hollow cubes* are placed in the two rightmost spaces of the endurance bar. Those spaces hold no stamina or damage cubes while the hollow cubes remain, so a hollow character's endurance bar has eight spaces. The hollow cubes are removed only when the character stops being hollow (see §7.2).

*Example.* The Warrior has three stamina cubes at the left of the endurance bar and four damage cubes at the right, so three spaces are empty. A Hollow Soldier hits the Warrior for four damage. The Warrior places three damage cubes in the three empty spaces and cannot place the fourth, so the Warrior dies.

*Example.* The Herald is hollow, so two hollow cubes fill the two rightmost spaces of the endurance bar and eight spaces remain. The Herald has five damage cubes and two stamina cubes, leaving one empty space. The Herald chooses an attack option that costs one stamina rather than one that costs two, and fills the last empty space with a stamina cube.

## 3.5 Character Tokens

There are four types of character token: *heroic action*, *luck*, *estus flask*, and *ember*. Each character has one token of each type, and the dashboard has a slot for each.

The heroic action, luck, and estus flask tokens each have a ready side and a used side. The ready side is printed in brighter colours than the used side. A token's effect is available only while the token shows its ready side. When a character uses a token's effect, the character flips the token to its used side.

The heroic action, luck, and estus flask tokens begin the game ready, in their slots on the dashboard (see §6.1). They are flipped back to the ready side only when the characters rest at the *bonfire* (see §11.2), so each is used at most once between rests. Clearing an encounter does not ready them (see §7.5, Encounter Recovery).

The ember token marks a character who is *embered*. It begins the game beside the dashboard, not in its slot, and is placed in its slot only when the character becomes embered (see §7.3).

### Heroic Action

Each dashboard prints a heroic action particular to the character's class. A character may flip the heroic action token to the used side as a *minor action* (see §10.1) to resolve the heroic action as printed. The printed wording of a heroic action overrides any other rule with which it conflicts.

### Luck

After rolling the dice for one of their own attack rolls or defence rolls (a *block*, *resist*, *dodge*, or *parry* roll), a character may flip the luck token to the used side to reroll any or all of those dice. The character chooses every die to be rerolled at once and rerolls them together. The new results stand.

### Estus Flask

A character may flip the estus flask token to the used side as the Drink Estus *major action* (see §10.1) to remove all damage cubes from their endurance bar. Stamina cubes are not removed. Hollow cubes are not removed. Condition tokens are not removed.

### Ember

The ember token marks an embered character. When a character becomes embered, the character places the ember token in its slot on the dashboard; when an embered character dies, the ember token is removed from the dashboard (see §7.3). A character becomes embered by using an *ember card* (see §4.4). The effects of being embered, and when it ends, are stated with the embering rules (see §7.3). An embered character may summon a *phantom* before a boss encounter and attracts *invaders* (see §12).
