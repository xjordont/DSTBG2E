# 13. Mimics

Greed has warped certain creatures into the likeness of treasure chests, and they wait with a hunter's patience for a hand upon the lid. A party that has bled for its victory finds one more enemy where its reward was promised. This chapter describes the chest that bites.

*Mimics* are an optional rule. They require the Explorers expansion. The party decides during setup whether mimics are in play.

## 13.1 Chests and Ambush Cards

When *mimics* are in play, one *ambush card* is dealt face down beside each encounter tile at setup (see §6.5; §6.6). An ambush card shows Treasure or Mimic on its face (see §2.6). It stays face down until the party chooses to open the *chest* {{icon:ast-0009-treasure-chest}} on its tile.

When an *encounter* that contains an unopened chest is *cleared*, its resolution and the characters' recovery are deferred (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution). The characters remain on their nodes. No character recovers, no dead character is revived, and no *souls* or treasure are collected.

The party then decides whether to open the chest or to forgo it. If the players cannot agree, the player whose character holds the *aggro token* decides.

If the party forgoes the chest, the encounter is resolved and the characters recover in the ordinary way, except that the chest is not opened (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution). The chest stays closed, and its ambush card stays face down. Each later time that encounter is cleared, the party again decides whether to open the chest.

If the party opens the chest, the ambush card beside the tile is turned face up.

- Treasure: the chest is opened, and the *treasure card* beneath it is awarded as a chest's card is (see §7.7). The encounter is then resolved and the characters recover in the ordinary way.
- Mimic: the chest is a mimic. A *mimic encounter* begins at once, before the recovery and the rewards of the cleared encounter (see §13.2).

In the campaign A Hollow Pursuit, one chest is a mimic without an ambush card (see §19.8).

## 13.2 The Mimic Encounter

**Mimic encounter.** The encounter fought against a *mimic* on the tile of a *cleared* encounter whose *chest* the party has opened.

A mimic encounter is fought as a *mini-boss* encounter, with the differences stated in this section (see §15; §16; §17.6). The Hungry Mimic is fought if the mini-boss has not been defeated, and the Voracious Mimic if it has. In campaign play, the Hungry Mimic is fought while the current tier is tier one, and the Voracious Mimic once it is tier two (see §18.5). The mimic encounter is set up as follows.

1. Take the *data card*, the *behaviour cards*, and the model of the Hungry Mimic or the Voracious Mimic, and the mimic *health dial*.
2. Set the health dial to the starting health {{icon:ast-0029-starting-health}} printed on the data card.
3. Shuffle the mimic's behaviour cards.
4. Deal a number of behaviour cards equal to the behaviour deck size {{icon:ast-0027-behaviour-deck-size}} on the data card, face down, to form the mimic's *behaviour deck*.
5. Set the remaining behaviour cards aside, face down.
6. Remove the chest token from its node and place the mimic model on that node.
7. Keep the chest token and the treasure card that lay beneath it beside the tile.
8. Leave every character model on the node it occupies.

Before the mimic's first turn, each character removes every stamina cube from their *endurance bar*. Nothing else is recovered. Each character keeps their damage cubes and condition tokens, no token is turned to its ready side, and the *aggro token* stays with its holder. A character who died in the cleared encounter stays dead and takes no part in the mimic encounter.

The mimic takes the first *turn*. The mimic and the characters then alternate turns as a boss and the characters do (see §16.1), and the aggro token moves as it does in a boss encounter (see §16.4).

A mimic differs from a boss in three ways.

- A mimic has no *boss arcs*. Its behaviour cards show no attack arcs and no *weak arc*, and no attack against a mimic gains the weak-arc die (see §15.6).
- A mimic has no *heat-up cards*. When a mimic's health is reduced to or below the *heat-up* point {{icon:ast-0030-heat-up-point}} on its data card, one of its unused behaviour cards, taken at random, serves as its heat-up card, and the mimic heats up as a boss does (see §15.5, Heat-Up).
- The killing of a mimic is not the defeat of a boss. It grants no boss soul reward, no *boss treasure*, no *spark*, no embering, and no rest (see §16.5).

A mimic's health is not scaled. No *summon* is called for a mimic encounter, and no *invader* takes part in one (see §12).

## 13.3 Mimic Rewards

A *mimic* is killed when its *health dial* is reduced to zero, and the *mimic encounter* ends. The following steps are then resolved in order.

1. Remove the mimic model from the tile.
2. Award the party two *treasure cards*: the card that lay beneath the *chest*, and one card drawn from the treasure deck of the current tier (see §6.2).
3. Resolve the *encounter* that contained the chest as a *cleared* encounter, omitting the opening of the chest (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution).
4. Return the chest token to its node, open side up, and the *ambush card* to the supply.

The two treasure cards are the reward that the mimic's data card calls Loot the Body. They take the place of the single card that the chest held, and they are awarded to the party as a chest's card is (see §7.7).

A mimic has no soul reward of its own. The *souls* and the treasure that the characters collect in step 3 are the deferred rewards of the encounter that contained the chest (see §7.6; §7.7).

The recovery deferred since the encounter was cleared takes place at the end of step 3. Each surviving character removes all condition tokens, all stamina cubes, and one damage cube, and each character who died in either encounter is revived *hollow* (see §7.5, Encounter Recovery).

A killed mimic does not return. Its chest stays on its node as an opened chest (see §5.5, Chests).

*Example.* The Knight and the Herald clear a Level 2 encounter that contains a chest, before the mini-boss is defeated. The Knight carries three damage cubes and four stamina cubes. The party opens the chest, and the ambush card shows Mimic. The Hungry Mimic replaces the chest token, and the Knight removes the four stamina cubes. The mimic takes the first turn and attacks the Knight, who still carries the three damage cubes. The characters kill the mimic. The party gains the chest's treasure card and one card drawn from the tier one treasure deck. Each character then gains four souls and one of the treasure cards dealt to the tile, and the Knight removes the stamina cubes placed during the mimic encounter and one damage cube.

## 13.4 Failed Mimic Encounters

A *mimic encounter* is *failed* when every character has died, or when a *hollow* character dies and is *lost* (see §7.2). The *encounter* that contained the *chest* is then failed, although its enemies had been killed. It awards no *souls* and no treasure, and it is resolved as any failed encounter (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution).

When a mimic encounter is failed, the *mimic* returns to its chest.

1. Remove the mimic model from the tile.
2. Return the chest token, closed side up, to its node, with its *treasure card* beneath it.
3. Leave the *ambush card* face up beside the tile.
4. Return the mimic's data card, behaviour cards, and *health dial* to the supply.

Each later time that encounter is *cleared*, the party again decides whether to open the chest (see §13.1). Opening the chest begins a new mimic encounter, set up afresh (see §13.2). The chest yields no treasure until the mimic is killed.
