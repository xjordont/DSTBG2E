# 12. Phantoms

Not every spirit that crosses into the characters' world comes as a foe, and not every foe belongs to it. White phantoms answer the call of the embered and stand beside them against the great bosses. Red phantoms come unbidden, drawn by the same flame. This chapter describes both.

**Phantom.** A *summon* or an *invader*. A summon is a friendly phantom that fights beside the characters in one boss encounter (see §12.1). An invader is a hostile phantom that joins the enemies of one encounter (see §12.2).

Every phantom has a model, a *data card*, and *behaviour cards* (see §2.4). A phantom is neither a character nor a *boss*. A rule that names characters or bosses applies to a phantom only where this chapter says so.

## 12.1 Summons

*Summons* are an optional rule. They require the Phantoms expansion. The party decides during setup whether summons are in play.

A summon is a friendly *phantom* that *embered* characters call to fight beside them in one *boss* encounter. No more than one summon takes part in an *encounter*. Standard summons are called before a *mini-boss* encounter, and advanced summons before a *main-boss* or *mega-boss* encounter (see §2.4).

### Summoning

Each time the characters are about to pass through the *fog gate* into a mini-boss, main-boss, or mega-boss encounter (see §15.1), the embered characters may call a summon as follows.

1. Each embered character may pay two *souls*. A character pays no more than once.
2. For each character who pays, draw one summon *data card* at random and turn it face up: a standard summon before a mini-boss encounter, an advanced summon before a main-boss or mega-boss encounter.
3. Choose one of the drawn data cards. The summon on that card joins the encounter.
4. Return every other drawn data card to the supply.
5. Take the chosen summon's model, its four *behaviour cards*, and the summon *health dial*.
6. Set the health dial to the starting health {{icon:ast-0029-starting-health}} printed on the data card.
7. Shuffle the four behaviour cards and place them face down as the summon's *behaviour deck*.
8. When the character models have been placed on the *entry nodes* of the *boss tile* (see §15.2), place the summon model on an entry node that holds fewer than three models.

The players choose the summon together in step 3. If they cannot agree, the drawn data cards are shuffled face down and one is drawn. Souls paid in step 1 are not returned, whichever card is chosen. This payment is an exception to the rule that souls are spent only while resting at the *bonfire* (see §7.6). If no character pays, no summon joins the encounter.

*Example.* The Knight and the Herald are embered, and the Assassin is not. Before the Gargoyle encounter the Knight and the Herald each pay two souls and draw Eygon of Carim and Sword Master. The players choose Eygon of Carim. The data card of Sword Master returns to the supply, and all four souls stay paid.

### Summon Turns

In a boss encounter the boss and the characters alternate *turns* (see §16.1). The summon takes a turn after each character's turn and before the boss's next turn. The summon takes no turn after a boss's turn.

On each of its turns the summon acts as follows.

1. If the summon's behaviour deck is empty, turn its discard pile face down, without shuffling, to form a new behaviour deck.
2. Draw the top card of the behaviour deck.
3. Resolve each icon on the behaviour card in order from left to right.
4. Place the behaviour card face up on the discard pile.

The players make every choice that a behaviour card leaves open. An attack on a behaviour card is resolved as a character's attack is, with the *range*, dice, and modifiers shown on the card, and costs no stamina (see §10.3). A summon that attacks a boss from a node in the boss's *weak arc* {{icon:ast-0041-weak-boss-arc}} adds one black die {{icon:ast-0051-black-dice}} to the attack roll (see §15.6).

When a summon resolves a *shift* icon {{icon:ast-0057-shift}}, it may *move* up to the number of nodes shown (see §10.3, Shift). A shift icon printed before the dice is resolved before the attack roll. A shift icon printed after the dice is resolved after the attack roll.

**Distract.** An icon found only on summon behaviour cards. During the boss's next turn the summon is treated as holding the *aggro token*; the token itself is not moved.

*Example.* The Assassin and the Knight fight the Gargoyle with Eygon of Carim as their summon. The Gargoyle takes its turn, then the Assassin, then Eygon. The Gargoyle takes its next turn, then the Knight, then Eygon again.

### Summons in Combat

A summon's data card shows its *threat* {{icon:ast-0026-threat-level}}, its starting health, its *block*, *resist*, and *dodge* dice, and a special ability. A summon has no *endurance bar* and pays no stamina. It has no *boss arcs* and no *heat-up* point, and its health is not scaled. A summon does only what its behaviour cards and its data card direct. It takes no *minor action* and no *major action*, and it cannot *parry*.

The boss's behaviours treat a summon as a character. A summon is counted when a behaviour *targets* the nearest character or compares threat (see §16.3). A summon that is *hit* is *pushed* and receives *conditions* as a character does (see §8.4; §8.5).

A summon never holds the aggro token. Damage that a summon deals to the boss does not move the token (see §16.4).

When an attack targets a summon, the players choose its defence and roll the dice shown on its data card as for a character (see §9.4; §9.5).

- Block or resist: the summon rolls its block dice against physical damage or its resist dice against magical damage. Its *defence total* is the sum of the two highest dice.
- Dodge: the dodge costs the summon nothing. If the roll equals or exceeds the attack's *dodge difficulty*, the summon is not hit and must move to an adjacent *valid node* chosen by the players. If the roll is lower, the summon suffers the attack's full damage.

Each point of damage that a summon suffers lowers its health dial by one.

A summon's special ability applies as printed on its data card. An ability that is used once per encounter may be used at any moment the players choose. When it is used, a *wound token* is placed on the data card to mark the ability as used.

Summons neither trigger traps nor suffer them (see §5.5, Traps).

### Removal of a Summon

A summon is removed from play when its health dial is reduced to zero or when the encounter ends. Its model, data card, and behaviour cards are returned to the supply.

A summon is not a character. The encounter is not *failed* by the death of a summon, and no character becomes *hollow* because of it. An encounter in which every character has died is failed even if the summon is still on the tile (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution).

## 12.2 Invaders

*Invaders* are an optional rule. They require the Phantoms expansion. The party decides during setup whether invaders are in play.

An invader is a hostile *phantom* that joins one *encounter* and hunts the characters. Standard invaders appear before the *mini-boss* is defeated, and advanced invaders after it (see §2.4). In campaign play, standard invaders appear while the current tier is tier one, and advanced invaders once it is tier two (see §18.5). Except where this section says otherwise, the rules for enemy models apply to an invader.

### Invasions

**Invasion.** The placing of *invader tokens* that brings one invader into the game.

*Embered* characters provoke invasions. An invasion is provoked at either of two moments.

- At setup: when the *encounter cards* are dealt to the tiles (see §6.5; §6.6), if one or more characters are embered.
- During the exploration: when a character becomes embered by discarding an *ember card* (see §7.3), if one or more face-down encounter cards that carry no black cube lie on the encounter tiles (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution).

No more than one invasion is provoked before the mini-boss is defeated, and no more than one after it, however many characters are or become embered. The embering of every character at a boss's defeat provokes no invasion at that moment (see §16.5). The invasion that follows the mini-boss's defeat is provoked when the encounter cards of the next setup are dealt (see §6.6). In campaign play, no more than one invasion is provoked in each *section* (see §18.1).

### Invader Tokens

When an invasion is provoked, the invader tokens are placed as follows.

1. Draw one invader token at random and keep it face down: a standard invader token if the mini-boss has not been defeated, an advanced invader token if it has.
2. Add blank invader tokens, face down, until there is one token for each face-down encounter card that carries no black cube. If the blank tokens run out, make up the number with invader tokens of the kind not drawn in step 1; such a token is treated as blank when it is revealed.
3. Shuffle these tokens face down.
4. Place one token face down on each of those encounter cards.

An invader token stays face down on its encounter card until the characters fight that encounter, and it stays in place when the encounters are *reset* (see §11.5). Invader tokens still on the tiles when the mini-boss is defeated are returned to the supply unrevealed (see §6.6).

The token on an encounter card is revealed during Encounter Start, after the enemy models and *terrain* have been placed (see §7.5, Encounter Start). A blank token is returned to the supply, and the encounter is fought without an invader. A token that shows an invader brings that invader into the encounter; the token is returned to the game box and is not used again during the game.

Invader tokens are placed only on encounter cards. No invader appears in a *boss* encounter, and none takes part in a mimic encounter (see §13). Some campaigns place named invaders in listed encounters and use no invader tokens (see §19.3; §19.4).

*Example.* The Herald discards an ember card while two face-down encounter cards, neither of which carries a black cube, lie on the tiles ahead, before the mini-boss is defeated. The party draws one standard invader token, adds one blank token, shuffles the two face down, and places one on each card. The token on the next tile is blank and returns to the supply. The token on the tile after it shows Maneater Mildred, who joins that encounter.

### Invader Setup

When a revealed token shows an invader, the invader is set up as follows.

1. Take the invader's model, *data card*, *behaviour cards*, and *treasure card*, and the invader *health dial*.
2. Set the health dial to the starting health {{icon:ast-0029-starting-health}} printed on the data card.
3. Shuffle the invader's behaviour cards.
4. Deal a number of behaviour cards equal to the behaviour deck size {{icon:ast-0027-behaviour-deck-size}} on the data card, face down, to form the invader's *behaviour deck*.
5. Set the remaining behaviour cards aside, face down.
6. Place the invader model on the central node of the tile.
7. If terrain blocks the central node (see §5.5), place the model instead on a node adjacent to the central node that no terrain blocks, chosen by the players.

The invader is placed directly on its node. No model enters a tile through a *doorway* during an encounter (see §5.2).

### Invader Turns

An invader does not take its *turn* with the enemy models (see §9.1). The invader takes one turn after each character's turn (see §7.5, Encounter Turn Order).

On each of its turns the invader acts as follows.

1. If the invader's behaviour deck is empty, turn its discard pile face down, without shuffling, to form a new behaviour deck.
2. Draw the top card of the behaviour deck.
3. Resolve each icon on the behaviour card in order from left to right, as the icons of a boss's behaviour card are resolved (see §16.2; §16.3).
4. Place the behaviour card face up on the discard pile.

An invader has no *boss arcs*. Its behaviour cards show no attack arcs and no *weak arc*, and no attack against an invader gains the weak-arc die (see §15.6).

An invader has no *heat-up cards*. When an invader's health is reduced to or below the *heat-up* point {{icon:ast-0030-heat-up-point}} on its data card, one of its unused behaviour cards, taken at random, serves as its heat-up card, and the invader heats up as a boss does (see §15.5, Heat-Up).

The invader's special ability applies as printed on its data card.

### Combat with an Invader

Characters *target* and attack an invader as they do an enemy model (see §10.3). The invader's *block* and *resist* values {{icon:ast-0028-block-resist-shield}} are printed on its data card. Damage to an invader is recorded on its health dial, not with *wound tokens*, and counts when the *aggro token* is reassigned (see §7.5, Encounter Turn Order).

An invader is not scaled. Neither enemy scaling (see §8.3) nor the *bonfire ascetic* (see §18.7) changes its health or its attacks.

If any character dies while an invader is on the tile, the invader vanishes at once. Its model is removed from the tile, and it awards no souls and no treasure. The encounter continues against the remaining enemy models, and the character's death is resolved as any other (see §7.2).

### Invader Rewards

An invader is killed when its health dial is reduced to zero. Its model is removed from the tile at once, even if other enemy models remain.

When an invader is killed, every character gains *souls* equal to the invader reward at once, in addition to the reward of the encounter (see §7.6). The invader's treasure card is awarded to the party at once, and the players decide which character receives it as for any card awarded to the party (see §7.7).

### End of an Invasion

An invader is an enemy model, and its encounter is not *cleared* while it is on the tile (see §7.5, Encounter Resolution). An invader leaves the tile only when it is killed or when it vanishes.

An invader appears once, unless a campaign states otherwise (see §19.4; §19.15). Whether it was killed or vanished, its model and cards are returned to the game box. It is not placed again when its encounter is reset (see §11.5).
