- Scry count (§5.5, Gravestones): the manuscript grants one scry for "an encounter containing a gravestone". The chapter states one scry per cleared encounter however many gravestones the tile holds. The author must confirm that two gravestones on one tile do not grant two scries.

### Chapter 08 — combat basics (16)

- Frostbite duration (§8.5, Frostbite): Gate 1 OQ-16 names stagger only, but the approved fix of A-078 (REQUIRED) covers "a stagger or frostbite token". The chapter removes both at the end of the affected model's next complete turn. The outcome differs from the manuscript only when a frostbite token is placed during the model's own turn (a riposte with a frostbite weapon). The author must confirm the frostbite half.
- Conditions on bosses (§8.5; B-038): the chapter states that any model, including a boss and a phantom, may hold condition tokens, so a second bleed token deals three damage to a boss. B-038 offered the opposite ruling (bosses immune to bleed) as an alternative. The author must confirm.

- Push direction (§8.4): the manuscript's "most directly opposite" node is kept, with A-085's fallback. A model is pushed only to a valid node farther from the attacker than its own node, measured by range, so a model on a corner node (and any model with only sideways nodes free) is not pushed. - Push-move damage (§8.4): per the chapter instructions §8.4 owns the damage of a push-move (the satellite glyph), which A-023 had placed under Enemy Attacks. §9.2 and §9.3 must cite §8.4 and not restate it, and §9.3 must own the rest of A-023 and D-008: the skull, nearest-character, and node icons, the tie-breaks, and the out-of-range miss. §8.1 states only that an out-of-range target is not hit.

The mimic is inferred from its health dial and its mini-boss encounter (§13 must agree). §18.7 must state that the bonfire ascetic raises the health only of enemies that are subject to enemy scaling, so that invaders, mimics, and crystal lizards are unaffected, and §18.4 must state from when a joining or leaving character counts (A-027).

### Chapter 09 — enemy turn (20)

- Nearest-character tie-break (§9.2, §9.3): the chapter instruction read "ties: players choose". The manuscript's rule for movement, and D-008's approved fix for attacks, is deterministic: the tied character who holds the aggro token, otherwise the tied character with the higher threat. The chapter keeps that rule and adds the players' choice only as the last step, for tied characters of equal threat. Replacing the whole tie-break with a free choice would alter a complete rule outside the five signed-off mechanical changes. The lead editor must confirm; a free choice needs only the second and third sentences of the **Nearest character** definition in §9.2 and the glossary ADD changed.
- B-002 and B-033 (both approved PROPOSED) are not applied. B-002 raises the block cap to three dice with a shield and B-033 cuts the extra dodge die to one stamina; both alter rules on the preserve list (two highest dice; two further stamina for one die), and the chapter instructions restate the preserved values.
- Parry slot (§9.6; chapter 03 issue): settled as the preserve list states it, a shield in the left weapon slot and the riposte with the weapon in the right weapon slot. A-068's wider fix (shield in either slot) is not adopted. §3.2's sentence and the glossary entries **Parry** and **Riposte** agree with §9.6; no change to §3.2 is needed.
- Riposte weapon (§9.6): the manuscript says "the weapon equipped to the right weapon slot". A spell is not a weapon (§4.1), so as drafted a spell equipped in the right weapon slot cannot riposte, and neither can a spell stored in a catalyst there. The author must confirm.
- Pushes (§9.2 and §8.4): the chapter instructions gave §9.2 the push-move and the "push-attack as movement", while chapter 08, drafted in parallel, states the per-node push steps in §8.4. To keep one statement of the rule, §9.2 defines the icon, fixes the target before any character is pushed, has the enemy follow the target's current node, and cites §8.4 for the push steps and directions. Terms follow §8.4 and the glossary: a push-attack is an attack icon that bears the push icon, and the damaging movement is "a push-move with a damage value" (the manuscript's sub-heading "Push-attack" is retitled). Two statements are made only in §9.2 and §8.4 must not contradict them: the attack of a push-move with a damage value cannot be parried, and a character pushed onto a node the enemy enters afterwards is attacked again there. If the lead editor prefers §9.2 to own the steps, they move from §8.4 unchanged.
- Push-move damage and scaling (A-025, OPTIONAL, applied): §9.2 and the draft of §8.3 agree that the damage value of a push-move is scaled as an attack value is.
- Repeat icon (§9.3; B-005): Gate 1 (OQ-07) removed Repeat 2 from enemy scaling, so no rule in this book now adds a repeat icon to an enemy. §9.3 limits a printed repeat icon to the attack icon beside it, while the draft of §8.2 says it "shows the number of times the behaviour is resolved"; the two must be aligned. "The target is determined afresh each time" has no manuscript source and must be confirmed.
- Stagger (§9.3, §9.6): the chapter cites §8.5 for the effect of a stagger token on an enemy and for its duration (OQ-16). The first parry example (block reduced from 1 to 0) agrees with the draft of §8.5.

- **Mon Ring.** "One use per floor" is read as once between rests at the bonfire (CMP-0004). The author to confirm.
- **Chloranthy Ring.** The two notes are merged as CMP-0004 proposes: at the start of the wearer's turn, every character on the wearer's node recovers one stamina. The alternative reading (each character on the node recovers one more at the start of their own turn) is not adopted. The interplay with frostbite (§8.5) is not stated in any source.
- **Black Leather Armour and Eastern Armour.** CMP-0004 keeps the dice and requirements of one printing; those values are in no supplied source, so the appendix says only that the printing carrying the stated text is used.
- **Printed names (OQ-17).** "Four-Pronged Plough" follows the manuscript; the printed card may read "Plow", which `tools/style_check.py` would flag unless the name is whitelisted. "Sellsword Twinblades" and "Onikiri and Ubadachi" follow CMP-0004, not the source ("Sellsword Twinblade", "Onikiri And Ubadachi"). All three need a check against the cards.
- **Cross-chapter pointers.** §4.1 (Stat Requirements), §6.2, and §10.5 do not cite Appendix A, and §4.2 (Titanite) states that attached Titanite cannot be removed without citing the replacement rule of Appendix B. One cross-reference in each is recommended at merge. §17.7 and §17.10 call the cards "replacement" cards; Appendix B calls them "revised cards" that replace the printed cards. The sense agrees; the wording may be aligned.
- **Treasure totals.** §2.5 counts 534 treasure cards. Whether that total includes the 34 additional treasure cards is not stated in any source.
- **A-066.** The rule that equipment with a highest requirement of zero cannot be scaled is not in the committed §10.5. It does not affect Appendix A (no listed weapon has a highest requirement of zero) but remains open for §10.5.
