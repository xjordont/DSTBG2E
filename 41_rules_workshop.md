## 1. What the house rules tell us

All four attack the same three things in the published game: the grind of re-clearing rooms, defence that is all block and no skill, and bookkeeping. Your manuscript already answers each one, usually the same way they do:

| Problem | Your manuscript | House rules that do the same |
|---|---|---|
| Grind | treasure pre-dealt to tiles; cleared encounters can be skipped; reduced reward for repeats | Bare Essentials (pre-dealt treasure by level, dash through); Zed (never re-clear) |
| Slow rounds | all enemies act, then all characters | Bare Essentials and Combined Edition (the same round shape) |
| Flat defence | parry and riposte; failed dodge still blocks with armour; pay for an extra dodge die | Jolly Souls and Bare Essentials (parry; armour-only block after a failed dodge) |
| Flat progression | treasure tiers by stat requirement (25 and over is tier two) | Bare Essentials (same idea, three levels) |

So the design does not need new mechanics from them. What they do better than our book is **brevity**: the Combined Edition states a whole game in 18 pages.

## 2. Proposed ground rule

**Your rule stands unless a player would hit a hole in it in their first game. Where there is a hole, the fix is one sentence.** Under that rule the decisions fall into four blocks.

## 7. What to take from the Combined Edition's format (after the rules are settled)

- **Order of play as the order of the book**: setup, before an encounter, the tile, the encounter, enemy turn, party turn, after the encounter, bosses.
- **Pictures carry the anatomy**: a card or dashboard with numbered call-outs and the list beside it, in place of paragraphs.
- **The icon sits in the margin beside the rule it governs.**
- **Small framed tables** for every set of numbers; examples only as a captioned picture.
- **Expansion rules flagged by a margin symbol**, so the core reads clean.
- **One page of icons at the back.**
- About 300 words a page, 18 pages. Our book at the same density, with your campaigns and the boss entries, would be near 50.

### 8.1 Block A, rule by rule

Sources: 2017 = core rulebook and its expansion booklets; 2022 = Tomb of Giants rulebook; CE = Combined Edition; JS = Jolly Souls; BE = Bare Essentials; Zed = Zed Mod.

**A1 Campaign sparks.**
Yours: maximum = starting number; never bought; free rest only after a section's last boss. 2017: +1 per boss; may be bought at 2 souls per character up to the starting number. 2022: no campaign mode. House: Zed and JS drop sparks; CE gives them one use.
**Decision: yours.** Sparks are the clock. A price turns the clock into a shop item and souls into retries, which is the grind every house rule set out to remove.

**A2 Parry.**
Yours: one stamina; shield in the left slot; attacker on the same node; not against push, area or arc attacks; roll dodge dice against the dodge difficulty; success = no damage, no condition, attacker staggered, riposte with the right-hand weapon; failure = full damage with no block, and stagger. 2017, 2022: no parry. House: JS and BE allow any weapon or shield, rolling only that item's dodge dice.
**Decision: yours, with your amendment: any one weapon or shield in the left slot.** The dice stay as you wrote them (all equipped dodge dice, as for a dodge). I did not take the house version's "that item's dice only": few cards carry dodge dice, and parry would almost never be possible. What this gives: a character wielding a weapon two-handed has nothing in the left slot and cannot parry, which is the right price for two hands. Dodge is the safe defence (a failure still blocks with armour); parry is the gamble.

**A3 Mimic.**
Yours: no recovery before the fight; a mimic once fought must be fought again every time until killed. 2017 (Explorers): full recovery first; after a defeat the party "may choose to engage the mimic again". 2022: none. House: CE fights it when the card is drawn.
**Decision: no recovery (yours); the rematch is the party's choice (2017).** No recovery is the better design: opening a chest while hurt is a real gamble. The forced rematch is the one place I depart from you. A failed encounter cannot be skipped, so a forced rematch with no recovery can wall the only route with a fight the party has already lost once. A choice costs no extra words and keeps the decision. Say the word and it stays forced.

**A4 Crystal lizard.**
Yours: the character who kills a lizard takes a Titanite Scale at once. 2017 (Iron Keep): one Scale to the shared inventory if every lizard dies before the encounter is won. 2022: none.
**Decision: yours.** It rewards the chase and needs no shared inventory.

**A5 Invader token.**
Yours: flipped on entering the tile, before the encounter card is revealed. 2017 (Phantoms): flipped after the encounter is set up. Book: as 2017.
**Decision: yours.** Nothing is decided between the two moments, so it is a true tie.

**A6 Titanite.**
Yours: fused to its weapon; sold with it, both to the bottom of the tier one deck. 2017, 2022: upgrades come off freely. Book: separated at sale.
**Decision: yours.** Permanent reinforcement is the theme, and a commitment is a better decision than a free swap.

**A7 Traps.**
Yours: dealt to basic nodes except along an edge that touches another tile; sprung when a character moves or is pushed in; hits every model on the node. 2017: the same placement in other words; damage or a dodge, never blocked; no effect on enemies. 2022: one use; dodge dice only; no effect on enemies. House: Zed clears only the doorway entered.
**Decision: yours, including "every model on the node".** Stepping onto an enemy's node and taking the trap together is a real tactic, and it is how traps behave in the source games. One clause from 2022 is added: a character who dodges a trap stays where they are (your dodge moves the character, which would walk them onto the next trap).

**A8 Push-move with a number.**
Yours: pushed characters suffer that damage (the 2017 wording). 2017 F.A.Q.: it is an attack on the characters on each node the enemy enters. 2022: pushed characters suffer an attack after the push. Book: 2017's reading, so a character pushed along the path is hit on every node.
**Decision: each character pushed is attacked once per push-move, and defends as usual.** Your scaling turns one-node moves into two at two or more characters, so without "once" a double hit is routine.

**A9 Heat-up.**
Yours: the heat-up card joins the deck when health reaches the point. 2017, 2022: one random heat-up card is shuffled into the deck at that moment. Book: delayed to the boss's next turn, in five steps.
**Decision: yours and both official editions.** One sentence: when a boss's health first reaches its heat-up point, shuffle one random heat-up card with its deck and discard pile to make a new deck.

**A10 Aggro.**
Yours: after all characters have acted, to the character who dealt the most damage; a tie leaves it. 2017, 2022: the acting character takes it at the start of their turn (enemies act between character turns). House: BE gives it to the last character to attack; CE passes it clockwise.
**Decision: your amendment. The last character to deal damage holds it; if no one dealt damage it stays.** No counting, and it is the same rule in a boss fight, where the boss acts after each character.
**One consequence needs your call.** You have characters act in descending threat. Under the new rule the lowest-threat character who lands a hit ends every round holding aggro, and the Knight never can unless everyone after him holds back. BE pairs this aggro rule with "the players decide the order". I recommend that: **characters take their turns in any order the party chooses each round.** Who strikes last, and so who the enemies turn on, becomes the main decision of the round. Threat still sets the first aggro holder and breaks ties among targets.

**A11 Poison.**
Yours: tokens stack; at the end of its turn a model suffers one damage per token and removes one. 2017, 2022: one token, one damage, then gone. Book: n damage, then remove all.
**Decision: yours.** It is the only version in which poison is worth building around, and it matches your bleed (the second token bursts for three). One sentence each.

**A12 Campaign economy.**
Yours: soul rewards one lower, stat costs doubled. 2017: costs doubled (4/8/16, tier four 20), rewards unchanged. Book: as 2017.
**Decision: yours.** Your standard rewards are already above the official ones, and a campaign has three times the encounters. True tie on design; yours.

**A13 Bonfire ascetic.**
Yours: after it is burnt, non-boss enemies have +1 attack. Official: no such item. House: JS resets encounters with a harder one and an invader. Book: +2 health.
**Decision: yours.** The price of better treasure should be danger. Extra health only makes fights longer.

**A14 Summon's turn.**
Yours: once a round, after the characters. 2017 (Phantoms): after every character's turn. Book: as 2017.
**Decision: 2017, in boss encounters.** Your boss acts after each character. A summon that acts once a round is worth a quarter of what it costs in a four-player game and full value solo; acting after each character keeps it level with the boss at every player count, and its deck was built for that pace. It costs no extra rule: "the summon takes a turn after each character's turn."

**A15 Joining and leaving.** Yours is the 2017 text. **Decision: yours, four sentences.**

**A16 Disputed treasure.** **Decision: the aggro holder decides, everywhere** (your chapter 7; your chapter 5 said a random character).

**A17 Phalanx.** A fact, not a design choice: please check the Painted World box. Is it five Phalanx models and one Phalanx Hollow, or the reverse?

**A18 Player versus player.** No official version. **Decision: your rules, without the added stalemate rule.** See 8.3 for whether it stays in the book.

### 9.2 Event cards (final wording of the rule, in substance)

Used when a 2022 core set is on the table.

1. Shuffle the event cards. Deal one face down to each encounter tile.
2. When the characters first enter a tile, reveal its event card. An immediate card is resolved and discarded. A consumable card is taken by one character. A rendezvous card stays on the tile and applies to its encounter.
3. A gravestone lets the party look at one face-down event card and keep it or replace it from the deck.
4. Where a card names something this game does not have: the soul cache is the characters' souls, shared as the party chooses; the inventory is a character of the party's choice; the next encounter is the tile the card lies on. A card that still cannot be applied is discarded.

### 9.4 Enemy scaling: the decision left

**What scaling has to do.** In your round the enemies act once and then every character acts. So as characters are added, the party's damage and its health both grow, and the enemies' do not. Health is easy to fix. Enemy damage is the hard part, because whatever is added lands on one aggro holder in one enemy phase: that is why the repeat at four kills.

**Recommended: a second enemy phase, and nothing else.**

> Each enemy has +1 health for each character beyond the first.
> The enemies act at the start of the round, and again after every second character's turn while a character has yet to act.

| Characters | Round |
|---|---|
| 1 | Enemies, A |
| 2 | Enemies, A, B |
| 3 | Enemies, A, B, Enemies, C |
| 4 | Enemies, A, B, Enemies, C, D |

- **Simple:** no +1 to remember on every attack, no repeated attacks, no changed movement. The cards are played as printed at every player count.
- **Balanced:** enemy attacks per character per round run 1, ½, ⅔, ½ for one to four characters. Under the manuscript's table the same figure is 1, ½, ⅓, ½, with the four-player half arriving doubled on one character.
- **Intuitive:** "the enemies act after every two of us." It is the official game's shape (enemies between character turns) at half the frequency, so rounds stay short.
- **Tactical:** with the new aggro rule, the party chooses which pair acts first and who strikes last in each pair, so two characters share the tanking in a large party.
- What goes: the manuscript's movement bonus at two characters, +1 attack at three, and repeat at four.

**Fallback: numbers only.**

> Each enemy has +1 health for each character beyond the first. With three or four characters, each enemy attack value is +1.

One line shorter, and closest to your table. But four characters are then safer than three, and every extra point lands on the one aggro holder.

Bosses are not scaled in either version: they already act after each character.

## 10. Round four (2026-11-13): no optional rules at all, and scaling settled from the house rules

### 10.1 Player versus player

### 10.2 What 31 house rulesets do about the round

The author's shared folder holds 31 files. 28 were read (`work/phase5/house_a.md`, `house_b.md`, `house_c.md`); two image-only files returned no text and one stops part-way.

- **Only three use "all enemies, then all characters":** Faded Embers, Praise the Fun, and The Better Board Game. Robert Small's variant and "New tactic rules" use close relatives. Everything else keeps the published alternation and changes only souls, sparks and treasure.
- **Not one of them scales enemy damage, enemy activations or enemy numbers by player count.** There is no precedent for the manuscript's +1 attack, its repeat at four, or a second enemy phase.
- **The two that scale at all scale health only:** Faded Embers and The Better Board Game multiply enemy health by the number of players. Faded Embers' author, who wrote a rationale for every change, says enemies now act once a round, so their survival time is scaled instead; and warns that at three or four players each enemy needs a health dial.
- **The valve for party size is sparks, not the fight.** Faded Embers: a fixed pool, one spark per death, the fight goes on until all are down; its author notes this gives "1 player ... 5 chances, 4 players ... 2". That is the manuscript's six minus the number of characters. Several others do the same.
- **Aggro to the last character to attack,** in both Faded Embers and The Better Board Game; Praise the Fun lets the party set its order. Both of the author's round-two directions have tested precedent.
- **Players-first was tried and rejected** by Faded Embers ("players murdering the most annoying enemy before it could activate"). The manuscript's enemies-first stands.
- **Player versus player:** two files only. The Better Board Game has a last-one-standing finale on a shrinking arena; the Hollowed Edition's extension sets dodge difficulty from the attack's stamina cost. Both are what the manuscript already does.

### 10.3 Enemy scaling, settled

> Each enemy has +1 health for each character beyond the first.
> With three or more characters, each enemy attack has +1 damage.

That is the manuscript's own table with two things taken out: the movement bonus at two characters and the repeat at four.

**Why this and not the others.** A rough simulation (`tools/sim_scaling.py`; it compares rules with one another and is not a forecast of a real table) of a Level 2 and a Level 3 encounter, 8,000 fights each:

## 11. Round five (2026-11-13): scaling calibrated against sparks, win rate and game length

> **Withdrawn on 2026-11-14.** This section's model assumed its own difficulty. Section 12 replaces it with a simulator that plays the printed cards, and reaches a different result.

**Author's targets.** Level 3 encounters of four to seven rounds; the chance that someone dies in step with the number of sparks; a similar length of game and a similar win rate with one, two, three or four characters.

**Section 10.3 is withdrawn.** It was judged on single fights. Played through a whole game, it is badly unbalanced: where a lone character wins 59% of games, two, three and four characters win essentially every game.

### 11.1 How it was tested

`tools/sim_game.py` plays the settled ruleset from the first bonfire to the main boss: eight encounters and two bosses, sparks, rests, hollowing, recovery, skipping, souls and progression. `tools/sim_search.py` searches the scaling rules. 3,000 to 5,000 games per figure.

What the model assumes, since these cannot be read off the rules:
- **A lone character clears a Level 3 encounter in about seven and a half rounds.** Character damage is set to give that. If your table is faster or slower, every round count below moves with it.
- **Overall difficulty** is one dial (the share of enemy attacks that connect). It is set so that a lone character wins about 60% of games, and every rule was checked again at an easier setting (about 80%) and a harder one (about 45%).
- It leaves out movement, dodging, parrying, conditions and class abilities, and the players follow one fixed policy. It compares rules with each other. It does not forecast a real table.

### 11.2 The calibrated rule

> For each character beyond the second, each enemy has +2 health.

- **Win rate:** within about seven points of the middle at each of the three difficulty settings; four characters are the lowest and two and three the highest.
- **Level 3 length:** four to seven and a half rounds. A lone character is the slowest because it takes one turn a round.
- **Sparks:** every party size spends about seven-tenths of the sparks it will ever have. A larger party sees a death more often and has fewer sparks to answer it, but a death there does not end the fight.
- **Game length:** a larger party plays fewer rounds and repeats fewer fights, so the count of turns falls as characters are added; with more people at the table the clock time comes out closer than the turn count suggests.

### 11.3 Why it has this shape

All the enemies act once a round however many characters there are. So each added character adds a full turn of damage to the party's side and nothing to the enemies'. Health alone cannot answer that: it only makes fights longer. To keep the odds level, **what the enemies deal has to rise with every character added**, and a bonus to attack is the only way to do that without adding enemy turns. That is why every milder rule fails the same way:

| Rule | Games won, 1 / 2 / 3 / 4 characters | Level 3 rounds |
|---|---|---|
| **Calibrated** | 59% / 65% / 68% / 55% | 7.5 / 5.6 / 4.7 / 4.0 |
| One line: +1 health and +1 attack per extra character | 60% / 52% / 66% / 70% | 7.5 / 5.8 / 4.7 / 3.7 |
| Round four: +1 health each, +1 attack at three or more | 59% / 100% / 100% / 100% | 7.6 / 6.0 / 4.2 / 2.9 |
| Manuscript: the same, with attacks repeated at four | 59% / 100% / 99% / 82% | 7.5 / 6.0 / 4.2 / 3.9 |
| Health multiplied by the number of characters (Faded Embers) | 58% / 94% / 100% / 100% | 7.5 / 9.1 / 8.5 / 7.6 |
| No scaling | 60% / 100% / 100% / 100% | 7.6 / 5.5 / 2.7 / 2.0 |

The one-line rule is the simplest to say and is nearly as level; its cost is a two-character game that is harder than the rest and four-character fights under four rounds.

### 11.4 What to watch when it is played

1. **Blocking against +3.** With four characters a Hollow Soldier hits for 7 and a Sentinel for 9. A block will rarely stop all of it, so four-character play leans on dodging and on choosing who holds aggro. The model's characters only block, so a real table that dodges well will do better than these figures. If four-character play feels like a coin-flip, the first thing to try is +2 attack and +6 health at four.
2. **Two characters** are the count most sensitive to the attack bonus: +1 moves the win rate from near-certain to level with a lone character.
3. **The assumption about fight length** (first bullet of 11.1). Three or four timed Level 3 fights with a lone character would confirm or correct it.

Figures are from the model after two corrections on 2026-11-13 (a dead character's turn no longer earns the boss a turn; a boss that has been killed no longer attacks once more). The first moved no win rate by more than five points and the second none by more than two; neither changed a conclusion. The lone character's row is the same game under every rule, so its spread (58% to 60%) shows the run-to-run noise.

### 12.1 What the new simulator plays

`tools/sim/` plays the game move by move on the real components:

- **The tile:** the 13 nodes of an encounter tile, with range and movement counted node to node.
- **Enemies:** the six enemy cards of the 2017 core set with their printed health, block, resist, damage, dodge difficulty, threat, range, movement and targeting (nearest or aggro, node attacks, pushes), and the 36 core encounter cards with their printed line-ups.
- **Bosses:** four mini-bosses and the Dancer of the Boreal Valley, each with its printed health, heat-up point, block, resist and full behaviour deck (damage, dodge difficulty, repeats and conditions of every card); the deck is built, cycled and heated up as the rules say.
- **Characters:** Knight, Warrior, Herald and Assassin with their printed stat tracks, taunt values, heroic actions and starting equipment, and 116 treasure cards of the core set (weapons, shields, spells, armour, upgrades, class, transposed and legendary cards) with their printed dice, stamina costs, ranges and requirements.
- **A turn:** the endurance bar, stamina recovery, walking and running, each weapon's attack options, block or resist, dodge (with extra dice where the ruleset has them), parry and riposte, luck, the Estus flask, embers, stagger, frostbite, poison and bleed.
- **A game:** bonfire, four encounters, mini-boss, four encounters, main boss; sparks, rests, resets, skipping, the fog gate, souls, dropped souls, treasure, Andre's shop, the Firekeeper and levelling.

Every rule that differs between rulesets is a switch (`tools/sim/rules.py`), so the official 2017 rules, the official 2022 rules, the manuscript and any variant are played by the same engine on the same cards.

**Card data.** Enemy, boss and encounter values are from the public DSBG-Shuffle data set; behaviour icons were read from the card faces. Equipment and class values were compiled from two independently maintained community card databases and checked against the card images printed in the official rulebooks where those are legible (13 cards checked, all matched). One card rests on a single source: the Knight's starting shield (one black block die).

**What it does not play.** Terrain, traps, chests, event cards, invaders, phantoms, mimics, boss arcs drawn on the table, support spells, most upgrade rings, and the Tomb of Giants enemies (the 2022 rules are played on the 2017 enemy cards, with the 2022 rewards and shop). Boss arcs and positioning finer than a node are represented by a skill setting (12.2).

### 12.2 How it was checked

1. **Against hand calculation.** `tools/sim/test_sim.py` (56 checks, all passing): dice distributions, dodge odds, the geometry of the tile, attack arithmetic, and a fight small enough to solve by hand (a Knight with only his light attack against one Hollow Soldier: 1.5 enemy attacks expected, 1.58 damage; the simulator gives 1.58 within sampling error).
2. **Rules as written.** Scaling tables, spark counts, enemy activations for each character turn (one each in the official rules, one for the whole party in the manuscript), the death rule of each ruleset, stat costs and the size of the treasure deck are each asserted.
3. **Invariants** over whole games of every ruleset and party size: no dead model acts, no bar overfills, no rest without a spark, the same seed gives the same game.
4. **Sampling error.** Win rates are given with a 95% interval; the final tables use 600 to 1,200 games a cell.
5. **The players.** They are a written policy, not a search: they choose block, dodge or parry by expected damage and risk of death, choose attacks by what a kill is worth against the stamina it costs, step out of reach after attacking when it pays, save the flask for danger, buy and level whatever adds most strength for the souls, and choose when to rest. For each ruleset and party size they are allowed to pick the resting policy that suits that ruleset best, on separate games from the ones measured.
6. **Skill.** What the model cannot act out (boss arcs, reading a boss's fixed card order, positioning finer than a node) is one setting from 0 to 1. All figures are at 0.5 unless stated; the proposal was run again at 0.25 and 0.75.

**What cannot be checked: the absolute level.** No record exists of how often real tables win. The simulated players are weaker than practised players, above all against bosses, and they do not grind well. Read every win rate as a comparison between rulesets and between party sizes, not as a forecast.

**The author was right, and section 12's win rates and its proposal are withdrawn.** The engine and the card data stand. What was wrong was the players in it.

### 13.1 What was wrong with the simulated players

Logged official-rules fights showed three faults in how the simulated players played, none in the rules:

1. **They shopped badly.** After a mini-boss's worth of souls, one character had spent most of the cache and another still wore starting equipment; an Assassin had swapped to a bow that could not hurt the boss. The valuation of equipment over-rated range and weak hits, and the shared cache had no notion of a fair share. Both are rewritten: equipment is valued against light, medium and heavy armour and against a spread of attacks, defence counting for more than offence; each character gets an equal share of the cache, the weakest going first.
2. **They passed turns** with a free attack in reach. Fixed.
3. **A boss's attack caught bystanders too often** (35% for each other character near the boss; now 20%).

With those fixed, what remains is the one setting for skill the model cannot act out (reading a boss's fixed card order, positioning finer than a node). Section 12 ran it at the middle of its range. It is now set by the anchor the author gave.

**The 2022 rules cannot be checked the same way.** The model holds only the 2017 enemy cards. ## 14. Round eight (2026-11-14): every rule decided, working outward from the official game

**Author's direction.** "Boss turn order should be after each character activation like in the official 2017 and 2022 rules. ... sort out exactly which rules to use to create an amazing board game experience that most closely matches the goals as previously determined (tactical, balanced, interesting, fun, intuitive, simple). Ideally, the 2017 rules would have been excellent so no house ruling would be needed but they were 'grindy', uninteresting, unintuitive, and lacked tactical decision making. ... if a rule from either the 2017 or 2022 rules works well then it should be considered closely as our working rule with my original house rule manuscript as a close third default. Consider all the elements in the many variable house rules ... You can also search the internet for further discussion."

### 14.1 Method

1. **Order of preference for every rule:** the official rule (2017, then 2022) if it serves the goals; otherwise the manuscript's; otherwise the best idea from the house rules. A rule is replaced only when there is evidence that it fails a goal.
2. **Evidence gathered this round** (all in `work/phase6/`):
   - `web_research.md`: what reviewers, Steamforged's own designer notes and published house-rule lists say works and does not. Forums that refuse automated reading are listed there as not reached.
   - `house_matrix.md`: the 28 readable house rulesets of round four, tabulated rule area by rule area with counts.
   - `official_diff.md`: the 2017 and 2022 rulebooks compared rule by rule, with page numbers.
3. **Testing.** The calibrated simulator of section 13 (official 2017 game won 44% to 62%), changed one module at a time starting from the official 2017 rules. Besides the win rate it now reports: fights in a game, how many of them repeat an encounter already cleared (the grind), enemy activations for each character turn (waiting), and how the players' choices are spread (how often a defence is a dodge or parry and not a block, how often an attack is a heavy one, how often a character steps out of reach).
4. **Correction to section 13.** The 2022 rules had been played with the 2017 defence and stamina rules. The 2022 rulebook has one combined dodge-and-block roll and three stamina a turn; both are now modelled. The 2022 rules on the 2017 enemy cards then win 30% / 38% / 27% / 11%. That is still not a test of the 2022 sets, whose enemies, objectives, rewards and events are not modelled.

### 14.9 What is not settled by this

1. **The level is set by the author's recollection of the official game** (section 13.2) and every figure inherits it. The final ruleset is somewhat easier than the official game under the same players (58% to 65% against 44% to 62%). To make it harder, give a lone character 4 souls and add one to each damage step; the steps are coarse, about 20 points of win rate each.
2. **A lone character's fights are long** (8 rounds at Level 3), as they are in the official game (10).
3. **The gravestone's power to look at a face-down encounter card** has nothing to look at once cards are drawn on entry. It should look at the top card of an encounter deck.
4. **Whose souls** is the closest call in 14.5: the official shared cache is simpler and would also work with a different scaling table.
5. **Not modelled,** as before: terrain, traps, events, invaders, phantoms, mimics, the expansions' enemies, and the 2022 sets.
